An interlude that opens a block of three "insert" chapters between the novels "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]" and "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]". The action is moved far to the south, in the white stone [[lajem|Laem]] - the city of tea houses, white-blooded ladies and the althes trade. ([[alter|Alter and Altessa]]) The story is conducted through the eyes of the talkative Laem matchmaker Onoriko-Meysor, who tried to improve his affairs at the expense of a rich and speechless foreigner. The reader, familiar with the first novels, gradually guesses who this merchant is "from the Lands of the Crown"; directly his personality is revealed in the interlude "[[poleznye-lyudi|Useful People]]".

## Plot
### The Cursed Ball
In Laem Square, master hypnotist [[gortenzij|Hortensius]], who sold water-heating stoves for white-blooded ladies, vainly invited the townspeople to ride on his "heavenly ship" for one silver gloria. ([[vozdushnyj-shar|Balloon]]) It was a snow-white cloth ball forty feet across, painted by the sun and clouds. His neck was placed on an iron tripod over the fire. Passers-by walked around the ball side, and in three months of persuasion Hortensius did not find a single customer. He invented the balloon a year ago, noticing that a sheet of paper thrown into a fire takes off before it flares up. However, the means of transportation was meaningless. The ball lifted barely two people, at height it was cold and scary, and he flew only to where the wind blew. It was impossible to descend at will until the air cooled. The local bishop joked in a sermon that the ball was a fiction for those in a hurry to get to the Star. The last nail in the coffin of the invention was killed by the merchant Fidel-Corel. He spent an hour crying over the city, and a week later demanded three hundred gold from Hortense, claiming that he had lost his male strength from fear. The inventor was mired in debt: one cloth cost him eight gold, sewing nine and a half, basket four.

### Architect of Happiness
Onoriko-Meysor, who called himself the "architect of happiness" (and behind his eyes - a pimp), picked up rich citizens of white-blooded brides and violass. At the ball, he noticed a bearded foreigner of about forty years old in simple sailing clothes, but with a heavy purse and without an engagement bracelet. From the way the guest looked at the ball, Rico realized that he was in Laem for the first time. He led the stranger away from the "curse" and dragged him to the expensive tea room of Valerion, where he paid for the entrance of Gloria. In other teahouses, he has long been known as a debtor. The stranger introduced himself as merchant Horam Polina Robert of Crown Lands and said he had brought the goods for sale. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]])

Rico showed visitors: the planter Obessin-Limar with a white-blooded wife, merchant-shippers Marion and Diaz-Ramiro, Petty Officer of the carpenter's guild Leonard-Misa. Everyone came with women. Rico's rival, Timeret's pimp, sat surrounded by four violasses. A man without a woman in Shimmery, according to Rico, is like a knight without a horse. Horam noticed that the northerners also have white skin: once he saw the “infant of the House [[dom-oridzhin|Origin]]”. Rico explained with indignation that northerners are “cold as the tops of mountains.” Before Timeret left, Rico faked it: he did not appear at the auction of Groza’s Shavan, because he has no money or customers, and the cream has already sold out. ([[ganta-groza|Ganta Thunderstorm]])

### Dinner at Vanessa's
On the way, Rico secretly sent a beggar from Elena's church porch to warn his wife and twice traveled around the block to assess the neighborhood of the baths, the post office and the mansion of the mayor's cousin. The guest’s home was met by the white-blooded Vanessa-Lilitha of the Pramateri Ludmila family, in a white dress and a necklace of black pearls. She knelt down in front of her husband and helped him to take off. The guest was presented with children, a baby Siren and a plucked Aldo, who poked Horam with a toy sword: “Is there money, miserable?” Horam spoke of the Lands of the Crown, the capital, and the northeastern duchy, which "by someone's foolishness" was called the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. When Vanessa asked why he was not married, Horam replied that he loved the girl and wanted to get married, "but she died."

The couple explained to the guest why althes: for conversations, for going out into the world, for a variety, to help his wife. Horam asked Rico directly what he would do. Rico talked about trade. [[shavany|Shavans]] of the western counties - Reysa, Melnitz, Holliwell - in skirmishes seized women from each other and, unable to support them or return them to their relatives, sold them on ships in Shimmery. ([[rejs|Reis (County)]]; [[melnitsy|The Mills]]; [[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) A cheap Westerner cost a dozen ephes, a tolerable one - fifty, a pretty one - a hundred, the best - up to three hundred. It was customary for the Shavans to sell the goods in a week, and by the last day of trading, prices were falling. To Horam's astonished question whether slavery was forbidden by imperial law, Rico was outraged: it was not slavery, girls were finding "new families" in the South.

### Controversy
As soon as the guest left, Vanessa caused her husband a scandal. She was outraged by the poor messenger. The guest seemed to her a poor man: his clothes are “not worth the money,” and he is single, in her opinion, simply because he does not have money for ransom. The best wine was worth two flories. She had only three decent dresses, which everyone in the city knew by heart, and on her shoulders there was already a tan, because there was no money for an indoor crew. Rico swore he'd get the money by the end of the moon. Then he admitted that he dreams of althess: on the ship of the Thunder, he saw a captive named [[niza|Niza]], and she settled in his heart. She was wearing a red ribbon. Vanessa replied that she was not jealous of the "horse": it's all about the money. Until she has a covered crew, dozens of outfits and babysitters, you can not stutter about althess.

### Search for money
Friends agreed to give Rico only agate or gloria forever, but not in debt. The moneylender offered two eels for trinkets and a clock. To mortgage the house, which, according to Rico, was not less than two hundred gold, it was impossible: it belonged to Vanessa since virginity, and in the city he was called “the house of Vanessa”. The moneylender noticed that the only value of Rico is Vanessa herself: if she was a captive althessa, he would give four hundred gold for her.

Rico's calculation was simple. Horam will buy a trap of coins for one hundred and fifty, Groza will pay Rico a tithe, it will be possible to redeem Nisa, and later to match the merchant with a white-blooded bride for a thousand ephes of redemption. Horam's been a long time coming. He said that he did not deserve the deceased loved one to look at him from the Star: “did too much rubbish.” Finally, he asked to take him to the last auction.

### Trades in tea Exila
The auction took place in the Exila teahouse, a flower-shaped building on a terrace above the sea: the petal beds surrounded a round platform under spark lanterns. Along the way, Horam admired the Laem bridges and galleries, which are closed from the sun for the sake of white-blooded ladies. The doorkeeper did not recognize Rico, and Horam led them inside for a large coin: "We were called by a white lady, here is her portrait." Timeret and Lisa-Margo, the mistress of the althess school, sat in the neighboring lodges.

Thunder in a leather sleeveless sleeve with sheets briefly announced: “I do not sell dirt.” Of the forty prisoners brought, six remain. Melana of [[litlend|Litland]], almost white-blooded, cost two hundred and fifty ephes and enjoyed the attention. Eliya, a thirty-year-old Western woman with tiger plastic, was worth two hundred. Also present were two dancing sisters, a frightened fifteen-year-old girl from [[darkvoter|Darkwater]], and Niza. The performance began: Elia played an instrument similar to a lyre and pumpkin, the sisters danced, Melana sang. Eliya then sang a ballad about the seven ships so that Horam's eyes flashed. I don’t know why people try to please their customers. Eliya explained: Merchants fight for women with a coin, like warriors with a sword, and “the best of us will get the best of you.”

There was nothing to show, she was sent to deliver tea and wine to the guests. When asked what she could do, she replied:
> “Living.”

When Timeret grabbed her, Nisa tipped the jug over his head, and after getting a crack, spit under his feet. Groza promised her a dozen lashes, but Timeret agreed to forget the offense if Melana sings to him alone. Horam reproached Shavan that he allowed to spoil the goods. Thunderstorm showed submissive Eliya: he never hit her, because she remembers that she is a woman. Eliya said that Groza had killed her husband, a "fool and braggart", that she herself had killed twice and that she was a good archer. Shavan gave her to Horam for one hundred and ninety ephesas instead of two hundred and told her to show herself; the tunic fell at her feet. Rico had already counted his nineteen tithe ephemera, but Horam got up and left. Elia bought Lisa-Margo for two hundred and ten Hilt, and Melana, according to Timeret, he has already taken himself.

### Buying the bottom
On the street, Horam explained that everything here is “not human”: a woman is sold, and she is happy. Rico furiously reproached him for his northern stinginess, and Horam accused the matchmaker of looking after Nisa for himself and counting on his tithe. Horam added that Rico should rejoice: the unsold Niza Shavan will simply let go. Rico replied that the proud woman would be thrown overboard a mile from the shore. Horam returned and bought Nisa "not for the bed", and for what - "my business". The storm gave her almost for nothing: “Pay for an empty place.”

Drunk Vanessa met her husband affectionately. Rico at first assured that Horam bought the "most wonderful" of the girls, but his wife drew the truth from him: the tithe was four silver eels, that is, half-ephesa, which means that five ephesas were paid for the entire purchase, and Niza was bought. In anger, Vanessa predicted that the whole city would laugh at the matchmaker, who taught a foreigner the cheapest girl, and they had debts for thirty ephes. Rico burned out a crazy plan: to buy a celestial ship, fly to the capital and sell it to the emperor. Vanessa, who had already heard that he was trying to mortgage the house, put her husband out until he learned to earn and pay off his debts.

### Sky
Three days later, after a night out, Rico waited for Horam at the bottom of the hotel, where the gatekeeper did not let him. Horam came out in white silk and a hat with an ostrich pen. Next to him was Nisa, but kept a “parallel course”, as if behind an ice wall that grew between them immediately after the auction. Rico asked for twenty gold, then ten. Horam refused: he does not give in growth, and in debt to a friend - especially, but asked honestly to tell why money is needed. Rico admitted that his wife kicked him out, including for stupidity about the ball. With the words “sky ship” in the eyes of Niza, life first broke out.

Rico explained that the emperor loves inventions and, if you lower the ball right in the palace in [[faunterra|Faunterra]], will pay any money. But he honestly admitted that the idea is a fairy tale. Fidel-Corel lies about powerlessness: Rico swated him a third altessa and knows that he is “such” for two years. However, the ball flies only in the wind, it lasts in the sky no longer than two hours, once it was carried into the sea, and more than ten miles it did not fly, and about a thousand to the capital. She whispered, “Flying through the sky...holy gods!” Looking at her hope, Horam concluded:
> “Maybe you won’t get there. But we can try.”

That he really bought the ball as a gift to Niza, it is reported already in the interlude "[[poleznye-lyudi|Useful People]]".

## Significance in the Series
The interlude continues [[kharmon|Harmon]]'s line after the events of the first novels: the merchant who replaced the Light Sphere and fled with the original to the south, lives under a false name and is tormented by guilt. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) The dead girl he is talking about is [[polli|Polly]], accidentally killed by him in the first novel. Later, Harmon admitted that he saved Nisa and bought a celestial ship “to calm his conscience.” According to Useful Men, [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] learned from a captain in [[marengo|Marengo]] that the fugitive had called himself the "Glorious Horam" and sailed to Laham, where the whole city remembered how he bought the cheapest slave, and her as a gift - a heavenly ship. ([[poleznye-lyudi|Useful People]])

In the novel Yanmei Hunt, Horam, along with Hortense, Niza, and Rico, built a ball in the estate under Melison. ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]) The Labelin men, led by Buckley, beat Timeret to come out on the trail of Horam, and then brought Rico, Vanessa, and the children to Melison. On Moe's orders, mercenary Rodrigo cut Rico's throat. Harmon and Vanessa and the children were locked in the basement, and Nisa saved everyone. The ship, called the "Heavenly Sphere", rose into the sky with Harmon, Niza, and Hortense; The second of the Five sought to descend upon him into the [[bezdonnaya-propast|Bottomless Abyss]]. Harmon later sent four hundred Hilt to Laem "for Vanessa-Lilith, the widow of Onoriko-Meysor." Shavan Groza returned in the main story as Moran's right hand of the Steppe Fire in "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]". ([[ganta-groza|Ganta Thunderstorm]]; [[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]]) In addition, the chapter introduces the reader to the life of [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] - althes, white-blooded, trade prisoners of the West.
