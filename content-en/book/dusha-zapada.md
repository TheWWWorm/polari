Becca Litland, the empire’s best rider, is used to not looking inside things. A friend from the North taught her to look for the hidden essence behind the outside. Now a friend disappeared without a trace in the monastery, and Western nomads come to the Litland country estate. Becca has to understand the riddle of the farewell letter, and in the politics of her own family.

The whole text is an appeal to a friend to “you”. Becca tells her about the events of several days, argues with her, guesses her objections (“You think: no, Becca, no...”) and admits that she learned from her “double bottom”. The name of the interlude is taken from the words that Becca says about herself near the end: in it the blood of Yangmei, "but the soul of the West." ([[pramater-yanmej|Yanmei the Merciful]])

## Plot
### Tractor with horses
The story is told on behalf of Becky and is addressed to a friend she knows as [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria Northwood]]. In fact, this is [[minerva|Minerva]] Stagforth, who lived in the capital under a false name. Becca recalls believing as a child that a rail tractor was pulling horses hidden inside. When she was ten, her uncle the Duke appointed her father as a representative to the House, and the family moved to the capital. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) The father, wishing to please his daughter, paid the machinist Gloria, and he showed her a working car from the inside. The chaos of buzzing details frightened the girl. Since then, she’s decided to never look inside and has lived like this for nine years.

The change in her happened at the rostrum of the games, when the emperor called the name of his bride: “Minerva Gemma Alessandra”. ([[adrian|Adrian]]) Under this name was a real Gloria. Becca convinced herself that she did not hope for anything, but the words of the emperor burned her, "like a brand." And then she suddenly saw politics as a mechanism whose gears set each other in motion: rail reform, universal suffrage, the House, the Ayden conspiracy, the Erwin coalition, the influence of the feudal lords. She realized that the emperor could not say otherwise, and “the stigma was removed.”

### Guests and Father's Promise
After the games in the capital house of the Litlands, every day guests come with congratulations and delights, and Becca feels like a wheel of a car. She asks her father for one thing: when he wants to marry her, she will choose the “horseman” herself. The father responds with his usual "What are you, baby!" and hugs her instead of answering. Then Becca says, as a friend would say, it is reasonable: it is better to wait a year for the sisters C or Molly to give birth, gossip about the family will be forgotten, and more will be given for it.

### Gloria's Letter
Secretary [[itan|Ethan]] comes not for Becca, but for a friend: he asks for himself, and not for the emperor, where Lady Gloria was “taken”. Becca shows him a friend's farewell note. In it, she writes that the illness has receded and she goes to the monastery, apologizes for not saying goodbye, because “the last meeting would be too sad,” recalls the ball and a walk in the forest and signs “Gloria Sybil Dorina.” Becca's note seems strange in everything: and the signature with the name of the mother, and the pathos "the sun of my worldly life has set", and the very refusal to say goodbye.

### Strangeness of Litlands
Becca tells her friend about the “empowerment” of everyone in the family. The mother is obsessed with grandchildren and knows all the means for fertility; every month, Becca receives a letter from her with another one. Uncle, Duke of Willas, is a scribe. He has read a thousand and two hundred books, almost leaves the ancestral castle of Bassifor and rules, according to him, like a spider in the center of the web, receiving pigeon reports from all over Litland. In the old tower, he holds Gordon's spider, black, with a white cross on his abdomen. However, one day a stallion was taken from Bassifor, and everyone knew about it except the Duke. Becky's father is a Lord Representative in the House and, unlike other Lords, likes not to give advice, but to ask for it. His chief adviser is Lord Brandon Coss, the second representative of Litland.

On Coss's advice, the family moves to Lacey's estate, ten miles from [[faunterra|Faunterra]]. There is a spacious house, twenty acres of fields, a grove with an ice stream and a stable for thirty heads. Among the horses are the red Maylitland twins Paul and Gilles, Becky's favorites, and the smart cowling Moon of the Holliwell breed. Walking with the Moon, Becca ponders aloud: Ethan said “taken away”, not “left”. Gloria usually refers to her mother as "Lady Sybil" and has now signed her name. So the monastery is the Countess's decision. The father refuses to make inquiries and forbids his daughter to talk to [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] "the voice of Litland": the Great House will not fit into the Northwood family affairs.

### Count Reis
The guests are Count Damir [[rejs|Reis]] and his men. Becca compares their arrival with a wolf leader, who is drunk by dogs with water right on the psarna. Litland meets six Westerners on horseback, according to the custom of nomads. Among the guests, Becca remembers only two: the count himself, an iron body, but with rotten through one teeth, and the thin blue-eyed warrior Moran of the Steppe Fire, the count's right hand. ([[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]]) At the table, the count says that the Crown is "married to the bears" and Shayland to the Northern Princess, and that in a year the rails will reach the Bear Fang and the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]. ([[klyk-medvedya|Bear's Fang]]) He demands a direct answer from Litland about the alliance: the northerners are more fierce enemies, "just you fight worse than them." The father responds evasively. When Becca demonstratively passes behind the guests, Moran grabs her hand and silently calls at dawn to the stables.

### The race for apples
Becca decides to sleep the meeting, but wakes up at dawn and comes. They jump through the meadow, grove, orchards and river to the apple orchard and shoot at the horse. Becca knocks the apple with the first arrow, Moran - with fifty steps into the cutting. Moran cuts a ripe apple on the fly, takes a spoiled one and leaves. To Becky's mockery that she "did nothing special" he replies, "It's true." Then he puts the apple on his top. Becca shoots, and he catches her arrow on the fly. When asked why all this, he answers: "I like you ... You're not Litland."
> “You have a free soul, as if you were from the West.”

### Paper on the table
Back, Becca runs into the office where her father and Coss confer. The father hastily covers one paper with another, and Becca sees only the edges of the map, under which the text probably went. At the door, she hears her father say, “It changes everything. “Tell the Duke.” Coss replies that "and there's something to talk about with the savages." At lunch, the Litlands hold noticeably tighter. Becca catches herself thinking, said as if by the voice of a friend: Moran charms her to reach her father through the “bloodline”.

### Night with Moran
While walking with the Moon, Becca unravels a hint of a note. “The sadness of the last meeting” is not sorrow: a friend was sent to serve Ulyana Sad, the “sister of death”, that is, to a monastery. Moran finds her and kisses her. She says no, and he retreats, not out of fear. All night, they drive silently through the meadows under the Star. The father, who returned at dawn, locks his daughter in a room under guard, promising that "the savages will remain alive."

Beneath the castle, Becca questions the guard, Ser Gage, about Moran. He explains that Reis is "a piece of earth infested with raiders" now ruled by two iron hands: Count Damir and Moran. Moran is almost equal to the count with will and courage, but is loyal to him, and he received the nickname for his burrows, "like a fire in the steppe." Becca asks Gage for books about the monasteries and compiles a list: twenty-four monasteries of Ulyana Sad throughout the empire, from Belomorya in Origina to Aligeir in [[shimmeri|Shimmery]]. ([[belomorye|Belomorye]]) There is not a single one in the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]], so the word "rolled" does not indicate the ground. Drive them all - six thousand miles, a year in the saddle. Remembering his childhood in the border fortress in Shepherd's Meadows, Becca realizes that he does not see much difference between himself and the nomads.

### Auction
On the fourth day, it seems, the father and Coss, satisfied, tell Becky about the Westerners' concession. The flight offers a guarded road from Litland via Shepherds Meadow to the west coast, where ships from the North go. This is a trade route bypassing the Crown and the South Road with a “ridiculous” duty. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) But, according to Coss, “something else is better”: there is an auction going on for Litland’s friendship, and Reis should add something else to the cup. An outraged Becca asks if the world itself is not a value. She asks her father for half of his fifty warriors to look for Gloria, and explains that mentioning the ball and walking in the woods is a hint of help. The father threatens to burn the letter and replies that he only sympathizes.

### Pigeons
Becca is released and put at the common table as a “live hint” of the thaw. The Count and Moran ask her to say what the letter, which brought the courier from Faunterra on the day of their arrival. Becca honestly says she doesn't know. In the evening, two pigeons fly to Lacey with the response of the Duke of Willas, and at night, the father sends eight: a pair to each of the four fortresses near the fords of the sacred Holliwell River, separating Litland from Reis. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) Becca realizes that Litland is preparing for war.

### Dinner
At dinner, Count Damir interrupts Coss's health and demands a direct response. Coss replies that the proposal is only a "formal concession", and demands that Litland be given half of the Shepherd Meadows, a common possession since [[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]]. Moran yells, "Rebecca, what was in the letter?" Now Becca understands everything: what the northerners bought the Duke's uncle, why an offensive demand was made and why there were a dozen soldiers at the table. Moran reads this on her face, and Becca screams at the guests:
> "Run!"

There's a swivel crossbow under the countertop at Coss's place. When the count snatches the dagger, the bolt hits him in the stomach. Seven Shavans and a dozen knights hurl themselves at each other. After a few breaths, fifteen people lie in the blood. Of the Litlands, four remain on their feet: a father with a sparkle sword, Coss, one knight, and Gage, who overshadowed Becca. Only Moran has ambassadors on his feet. He fights Gage, passes Becky, and is thrown out the window. Koss targets him from the crossbow, but Becca hits bottom on the bed, and the bolt goes to the sky. She then beats Coss to the knee with all her might (Lacy's ambush). ([[zasada-v-lejsi|Ambush at Lacey]])

### After
Gage dies the same evening, Count Damir two days later: the doctors are powerless. Moran escapes the chase, although his father sends out pigeons to be intercepted. They say he killed five in the fight. Becca admits that if the count had not been hit by a bolt, the Litlands would have lost, and that would have made her even worse. The father does not talk to his daughter: she thwarted their plan. The Duke said to accept “that other offer” that was in the letter. Becca, with bitter irony, retells the "visionary" plan: to piss off the savages, to kill the ambassadors at the table and leave Reis without both leaders for years of turmoil, and then, perhaps, the Origin marines will get what remains. Coss had a cracked kneecap, "at least something nice." Becca admits to herself that her beloved father acted "like a complete bastard." She does not leave the question of what the friend meant by "sunset".

## Relationship with other books
The interlude shows what happened between the first and second novels: the emperor chose the “Minerva”, that is, the replaced Gloria, as the bride. Becky's friend is in the convent, and Becky is smart enough to crack her code. In "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]", Ethan reports that Minerva left Rebecca a note with a cipher and she realized the meaning: a northern woman in the monastery of Ulyana Sad in the west of Almera. It also says that her father, realizing her plan, locked Becca under the lock, and Ethan admitted: you can take the girl from the monastery only on the orders of the emperor. However, rumors of imprisonment reached [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]: he solved the substitution, found Mira and took her to Waymar to blackmail the Northwoods. What happened in the monastery itself is told in the "Plax". ([[plaksa|Crybaby]])

The main thing is that the perfidy of the Litlands in Lacey became the spark of the Western war. In "Only One Star", the Shavans of Reis are accused of the emperor that Count Damir was shot at the Litland table with his connivance. [[moran|Moran]] leads the West and raises a raid on Litland. Acquaintance in Lacey is continued: Moran remains in love with Becca, and in "[[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]]", Becca jumps to him in Rei-Roy to keep him from allying with heretics. [[paul|Paul]] immobilizes Eternity first Moran, and then her, and Erwin finds both frozen at the gates of the city. There, Adrian admits that someone forged a letter on his behalf, which gave the Litlands the Halliwell fords to provoke a war. Apparently, it is about the same story, but it is not said directly that this letter came to Lacey with a letter. In the interlude itself, the content of the letter is not disclosed. Becca saw only the edge of the map and realized only that “the northerners bought the uncle-duke.”

## Trivia
- Becca calls herself by the full name "Rebecca Eleanor Agatha of the Janmay family, Lady Litland" when Moran says she is "not Litland". According to the author, Becca is actually Sophia's family, and "Janmei's family" is a mistake of early editions.
- The horse Luna is “three times younger” than Becky and, according to her, much wiser: it is in conversations with her that Becca solves a note.
- According to Becky, Minerva she knew only two months, while Sybil knows her daughter seventeen years.
- The double bottom motif runs through the entire text: an apple with a worm, Moran's love, pigeons, Becca herself at the table as "an incentive apple for the horse."
