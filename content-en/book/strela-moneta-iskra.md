In the spring of 1774, from the Descent in the [[polari-world|Empire of Polari]], the young emperor [[adrian|Adrian]] conceived to build rail roads with money from the universal land tax, and the Great Houses are looking for ways to prevent him or how to profit from it. The novel follows three heroes who at first know almost nothing about each other. A traveling merchant undertakes to secretly sell a sacred relic. The heir to the northern duchy, sent by his father on a dangerous expedition outside the empire, sees there what makes him doubt the ruler himself. A young noblewoman, whose father was killed, goes to the capital to find the killers. The chapters are named after the code names of the narrators: "Coin", "Arrow" and "Spark".

## Title and publication
The novel is called “Arrow, Coin, Spark”, that is, by the code names of three storytellers. This is what the author himself calls him: in the preface to Fantasia, he writes that “the whole novel Arrow, Coin, Spark” arose without any commercial purpose, from the pure flight of imagination. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]])

The novel's overarching thought is in the last, 47th chapter. When a dying father offers him the title, Erwin mentally says, "Without your help, father," and immediately corrects himself: his father helped him by believing. These words echo Agatha's saying, which two chapters earlier quoted Harmon as [[otets-david|Father David]]: everyone carries the "burden of choice" alone, and this is the mortal's right and his curse. (In the early media, David quoted Scripture, and the saying explicitly stated that a mortal makes decisions “without our help,” that is, without the help of the gods — hence the echo in Erwin’s words.)

Each code name is explained in the text. Erwin compares himself to an arrow in chapter eight: his father, like an archer, pulled a string and sent him on a flight, not caring if the arrow would hit the target. The heads of Harmon are related to money and trade. The world in the tenth chapter, waking from its stupor after the death of his father, says to himself: “I am alive. I'm smart. I am a spark."

By the release of the printed version of the novel, a special collector's edition was published with Lady [[karen|Karen]]'s remarks after each chapter. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) Karen writes as a contemporary of events: mocks the mistakes of the "author" in armor, titles and laws and explains how everything was "really".

## Structure
The chapters alternate in a constant order: “Coin”, “Arrow”, “Spark”. Closer to the end, the order is lost in places.
- Coins: Chapters 1, 4, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 27, 30, 33, 36, 38, 40, 42, 45.
- Arrows: Chapters 2, 5, 8, 11, 13, 16, 19, 22, 24, 26, 29, 32, 35, 39, 44, 47.
- Iskra: Chapters 3, 6, 10, 14, 17, 20, 23, 25, 28, 31, 34, 37, 41, 43, 46.

The novel opens with the head of Harmon (March 21) and ends with the head of Erwin (August 30). The chapters of the Arrow are often interrupted by memories of the events of the autumn and winter of 1773 in the capital. From them, the reader learns how Erwin formed a coalition in support of reforms.

The convoy of Harmon is small: two vans and an open cart. He's got a Stable Whirlpool with his wife Louise and the kids, Doxet and the grim deserter Snape. ([[vikhor|Whirlwind]]; [[luiza|Louisa]]; [[snajp|Snipe]]) The morning after Smallden, Harmon deliberately leads the train through the Six Mile Forest. The priest predicts a war from which "the underworld will tremble and the Dynasty will shake", and invites Harmon to join the brotherhood. He refuses. David quotes Agatha about the “burden of choice” and says goodbye to the vial of poison, claiming that Harmon did not poison and that he should not be afraid of David. Harmon leaves the hotel in a hurry anyway. At sunset, Harmon runs south, still with the Sphere (see Sale of the Bright Sphere). ([[lzhe-sfera|Sale of the Radiant Sphere]])

## Plot: The Arrow is an Explorado for the River

On March 22, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Sophia Jessica]], heir to the Origins, returns from the capital to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]] for Iona's sister's wedding to Vittor Shayland (see Iona and Vittor's wedding). ([[brak-vittora-i-iony|Wedding of Iona and Vittor]]) The poverty of the city even pleases him: it confirms his calculation. Erwin meets with Iona in the greenhouse and with his mother, Duchess [[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica]]. After [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard]]’s older brother disappeared into the sea, his mother became interested in Sacred Objects. Castelian Arthur Hyroc calls Erwin to his father, the Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]]. ([[artur-khajrok|Arthur Highrock]]) Erwin sets out the plan. Adrian wants to replace the feudal military service with a universal land tax and build rail roads with this money (see [[reformy-adriana|Adrian's Reforms]]). ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]]) A vote in the House of Representatives is scheduled for September. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) Erwin behind his father gathered a coalition of Great Houses in support of reforms and wants to demand a generous payment from the crown for it. Desmond sees this as a trade in loyalty. He makes his son repeat the vassal oath, reminds of the Julian law on the punishment of rebellion and sends Erwin to the exploration in the Beyond as a punishment. ([[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]; [[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) Erwin must find a place on the great River for a spark dam. ([[reka|The River]])

A detachment of forty men is led by Captain Theobarth: eleven kairs, each with two grays. ([[teobart|Theobart]]) They are accompanied by the imperial observer Baron [[filipp-loufert|Philip Lowfert]], without whom, according to Constantine's law, it is impossible to go beyond the borders of the empire, and the mechanic [[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]] of Otmel. ([[otmel|The Shoal]]) In the [[kristalnye-gory|Crystal Mountains]], Erwin accidentally knocks down a young Grey Buck's horse, and he breaks his leg. Kair [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]] doesn't want to let the wounded go home, but Erwin overdoes it. The heir is cold, does not hold well in the saddle, and the Greeks called him "gentle". An avalanche passes a hundred paces from the camp beside the Maid's Hem glacier. The memoirs take Erwin to September 2, 1773, at a feast at the Pera Palace and the Sword, where he speaks to Duke [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]. ([[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]]) In the village of [[spot|Spot]] on the isthmus, the chief takes Philip for lord. ### The River and the Lodge of Dar (chapters 19–24)
At the end of May, the detachment reaches the River, about three hundred and eighty miles from the border of the empire. The river is a quarter of a mile wide, the east bank is cut short by a steeple of three hundred feet, and Erwin immediately sees that the dam cannot be built here. The memories of these chapters return him to the deals of last fall and winter: on October 10, 1773, he persuaded the Countess [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] to join the coalition, and on December 12, he negotiated with Duke [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]] in [[nadezhda|Hope]]. The scouts confirm that there is no place for the dam. Philip proposes to return, but Erwin out of stubbornness decides to cross to the east bank. On 6 June, the group crosses the River on rafts. Colemon and Kid remain on the west bank with Erwin's letters to his father.

Beyond the River, scouts find a dried-up corpse under the ash: around it, grass and bark turned to gray dust. A little further there are seven dugouts, where twenty or thirty people wintered. Erwin concludes that people live in the Beyond, although no imperial treatise mentions this. From the top of the hill, the detachment sees a huge failure about three hundred steps across with walls like scorched by fire. This is a bed of the previously unknown XVIII Gift of the Gods. The steps down are already cut by someone, at the bottom there are only human bones: the heart of the Gift is empty. At dinner, Erwin raises the spirits of the soldiers with a speech. The gods, he says, sent the Gift to the northerners, and the land he calls the "Blessed Land" must be declared part of the Origin. At night, Louis calls him to the cliff to look at the shimmering blue spot. During this time, the camp is attacked, and Louis stabs Erwin under his left collarbone. Erwin breaks into failure, but survives on the ledge. From the grotto, he hears Louis report on the "lord" to a man with a light foreign reprimand, who is called "sir". He tells the mechanic that Erwin should have been killed before the River, and causes him pain: "Fragile, fragile man."

### Survival and Jemis (chapters 26–35)
On June 9, Erwin is elected upstairs. The camp is full of mutilated bodies: Oswald, Philip, Frederick, Matthew, Tommy. Among the dead is a stranger in a mail and a scarlet shirt with a piece of spark spear. The captain tells Erwin to write everything down, nail a note to a tree, and shoot himself, but Erwin replies, "I'll survive." He gives the captain his dagger, and Theobart stabs him. Erwin picks up the doctor's chest with ointments, a crossbow and food and crawls into the dugout. In the fever he dreams of [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia]], sister and deceased brother. The juice of the poisonous snake-grass that Kid showed him, he pours directly into the cut dagger wound. He's got Agatha in his head. One day, waking up at the meadow of the snake-grass, he sees a gray Shepherd Sagittarius and the exiled Jemis.

Jemis went after the heir to kill him and wash away the shame of losing the cloak, but instead grooms him. He sews up the wound, feeds Erwin, and buries the fallen. They talk to each other and talk about girls. Erwin compares the marks of the attackers (red shirts, boots with lacing, double-toothed spark spears) and concludes that this is an imperial spark infantry.

### Fort and Graveyard (Chapters 39–44)
On July 6, Erwin and Jemis track down the enemy. A few miles from the lodge stands a log fort: a moat, a shaft, a twenty-foot wall smeared with clay from fire arrows, six towers, and four windmills that power spark machines. The garrison, according to Erwin's calculations, has one and a half or two hundred people. Twelve emaciated prisoners are taken out of the gate. High commander [[paul|Paul]] puts on his hand a smooth pinkish-smoky stone, which spreads along the forearm, and burns a person with one flash of heat. Soldiers of two "learn", firing fire at the fleeing prisoners. The last bald girl with a burned head, Paul breaks an invisible whistling force. Erwin fires a crossbow from about two hundred yards away, and the bolt gets stuck in Paul's shoulder. The fugitives recognize in the arms of the Ring of Wilhelm, which the Forefather Wilhelm, according to legend, drowned in the ocean. ([[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]) Jemis leads the chase to the bed, and Erwin leaves along the creek to the River.

Erwin reflects on Wilhelm, the first ruler of Polaris: he defeated the barbarian horde with the Persians, and before his death he himself drowned them in the ocean to protect his descendants. Turning objects into weapons is the greatest sin. At the River, he meets two half-naked soldiers and pretends to be Arthur the Thin's new recruit, sent by Paul. One of them is alerted: Erwin does not know their words, in the detachment of prisoners they are called a "scot". Erwin distracts the soldiers by calling out his raven Rain tied nearby and kills both. The second shouts before his death: "We serve the emperor Adrian." At sunset, Erwin swims across the River and goes to the cape, where two or three hundred decaying bodies lie, among them women and children. They were burned, melted, broken, refreshed or dried. He is soon caught up by Jemis with the Sagittarius. He tells how he led the chase to the bed, killed one of the pursuers and wounded another, and the invisible force of the wounded broke his sword and dislocated his shoulder. Jemis compares the graveyard to the ruined pirate town of Hoyt in the White Islands. Erwin swears that there will be no fort, no red shirts, no Objects behind the River, and the one who commands them is not here.

On the way back through the Forest of Shadows, Erwin exercises every day with Jemis on wooden swords "to the fifth bruise". In the Cathedral of the Agatha the Radiant Erwin prays in front of the fresco "The Decision of the Agatha the Radiant" and "hears" the holy images. Agatha shows him the burned girl, and he realizes that the saint is not in offense to him, but in horror. The main enemy is far superior to any northern lord. In the castle, Erwin learns that Desmond was broken by a [[kamennaya-khvor|stone disease]]: his skin petrified, he whispers and reads a book fixed in a special holder. The Duke demands abdication. Erwin refuses the title until he is heard, and in the presence of Earl Lilliday, Baron [[stetkhem|Statham]], Colonels Horton and [[blekberri|Blackberry]], mother, Iona and Vittor, tells of the lodge, fort and graveyard. Jemis confirms what he said. Erwin announces that Adrian has made the Objects speak and is about to crush the Great Houses, and Louis has been sent to kill him. He demands from his father a choice: to judge him as a rebel or to transfer power to him so that he overthrows the emperor. Iona stands at his right shoulder. Desmond recalls two cases when the Great Houses forced the lord to abdicate in favor of the heir, takes an oath from his son that he does not doubt the guilt of Adrian, and proclaims Erwin the reigning Duke of Origin.

## Story: Iskra - Peace in the Capital

The novel lays the foundation of the whole cycle. Later, in "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]", this conclusion is questioned.
