"Those We Forgive" is an interlude of several scenes dated May 1775. Each is shown through the eyes of a secondary character: a palace cupbearer, a storekeeper, a guard captain, a major, and a little girl. The overarching theme is forgiveness. [[minerva|Minerva]] was tired of forgiving those who had wronged her, and yet she was forced to decide who to forgive.

## Plot
### The Day of Return from Negotiations
The position of the palace cupbearer was hereditary in the Vigam family, but only the one who had a son was entitled to fill the cup of the lord himself. Ronald Wigam was granted this right in October, on the eve of Abundance Day, and the next day [[adrian|Adrian]] embarked on a campaign from which he did not return. Crowned soon "northern" Wigam despised: she did not distinguish mianti from kuado and once admitted that she drank kosukha with soldiers. Since the spring, he served her a "mixture": a cheap Mareng amino diluted with water, with a pinch of red pepper and a drop of "Scarlet Tears". Instead of the Leed horde, he brought twice cheaper Belomorye, and sold the wines he saved. When Minerva longed and felt sorry for herself, she drank the orge, and when she rejoiced, wine.

On the day Minerva returned with a bloodless victory from the [[moran|Steppe Fire]] negotiations, she asked for an Orja instead of wine. In the corridor Wigama stopped the first lady Leyla Talmir, twenty years holding the inn in the back. ([[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]]) She told me to pour her a glass and drank it, apparently for courage. Through the fireplace of the nearby coffee room, Wigam overheard her talking to the empress.

Leila asked for five thousand ephes. Minerva readily agreed: her venture with paper money brought an unexpected profit, and the next day Conto's bank had to pay the amount. ([[konto|Konto]]) Then Leila asked for another small linen possession, a two- or three-shielded allotment, not purchased, but granted on behalf of the Crown, and as soon as possible. Minerva replied that she did not know how to dispose of the lands of the Crown, which are in charge of the Ministry of Lands. Leila refused to name the reason for the rush: “This is my secret, Your Majesty.” She recalled that she helped Minerva twice, and heard in response: “I’m not your daughter.” Minerva suspected blackmail or bribery, and Leila shouted: “You will grow up — you will find out how it happens!” Then the empress quietly replied that the lady would receive neither land nor money and would remain in service only until the moment when she raised her voice again. Delighted by someone else’s humiliation, Wigam carried in genuine twenty-year-old Leed ordzh, but in the corridor the Lord Chancellor himself took the tray from him.

### Chest in the pantry
While Minerva and the Lord Chancellor were away, dealing with the leader of the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] [[salem|Salem]], the keeper of the palace warehouses Bertie Crane and his assistant Lisette took an "inspection". Sheepy Minister of the yard and Leila Talmir allegedly checked whether there were enough supplies for the holiday of victory over the rebels. In the vegetable cellar stood a locked chest left by the Lord Chancellor's men. Leila opened it with two keys, and inside was the body of a woman killed by a blow to the heart. Leila persuaded Bertie not to call the guard: the northerners apparently keep the body in the coldest place of the palace, and the Duke will be upset if the deceased is buried. Let Bertie, when Origin comes back, hint to one of the kires that the chest smells like mold. She locked her chest and spent three minutes alone with her body. Two days later, the Duke returned to the palace as evil as hell. ([[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]) Bertie turned to Robert, "the quietest of the northerners", and the same evening the chest was taken away. ([[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]]) Lisette in those three minutes peeked into the crack of the door behind the frailty and saw something that she preferred to consider a vision and by evening had forgotten.

### Exposing the Silver Fox
General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]], the Silver Fox, confessed to Minerva that he was serving a “second lady” whose goals are “light and noble.” Minerva responded that she wanted to decide what goals to consider bright, and promised to “try to forgive” him. Alone with Leyla and the captain of the azure guard, [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]], she explained that the general would save his life and rank because he was useful: her power was worth something, while other forces larger than her own balanced each other. Shatterhand, who had seen the three emperors, Telurian, Adrian, and Minerva, admired the new ruler more than the former.

Leila then showed a syringe with a thick dark liquid taken from the Znacharka's body. ([[znakharka|The Healer]]) This servant of the owner of the Rings appeared at the palace in April, and the Kairs "uncarefully killed" her, removed the talking Object from the corpse, and kept the body in the cold, in the cellars of the palace. ([[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]) Origin put forward the theory that the Objects speak to people of special blood, and conceived the experience: pour the blood of a caught shooter with the Cyre Rings. Leila offered to introduce a liquid to either a faithful Minerva man to get an exceptional warrior, or a person close to him to attract the suspicion of Origin and pit the northerners with someone. She did not dare to test it herself: if successful, Origin would consider her a servant of the Puppeteer, and Minerva himself a villain. Minerva almost fainted. She recalled the bloodletting and dungeon of [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]], who was searching for the "elixir of immortality", and guessed that Layla overheard her private conversation with Origin. The Empress banished the maid of honor: “You still want land!” Leaving, Leyla said that there is something in the world besides intrigues and conspiracies: it happens that a person really needs help.

Shatterhand did not like to tell the ruler what he did not want to hear, but on order said: Layla, perhaps, is right, and she should be forgiven. Minerva replied:
> “I’m too tired to forgive, that’s the point.”

Too many people have inflicted terrible evil on her and have gone unpunished: [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] disappeared, Almer's arch was inaccessible, the Shayland brothers were under the protection of Origin. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]; [[dom-shejland|House Shayland]]) "Silver Fox drank the last drops". Shatterhand took the syringe with him. He was distracted by Lieutenant Augustus Mace, whose company had once lost "the fortune of the Dynasty", with an oral message from Captain [[uitmor|Whitmore]]. Minerva had to secretly come to [[marengo|Marengo]]: Whitmore and Major Backfield were carrying there from Almera important information about the stolen Objects. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]])

### Backfield in Marengo
Three days later at Marengo, in the Palace of Silence, Backfield drank the Northwood hanty from the chief of the post, an original nobleman with a university degree nicknamed Birdman. In the winter, Backfield was thinking of running to [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] or the Volta. ([[folta|Folta]]) In the capital, he was waiting for the Kyrovsky sword, to the northerners and even to the Northwoods, he did not find an approach, and Gallard Almera dragged on time. Then he met Captain Whitmore, who was searching for stolen items of the Dynasty in Almera, and joined him. Within two months, they found the kidnappers' tracks, a couple of witnesses, a secret scheme on human skin, and information about [[absolyut|the Absolute]], a weapon collected from the Objects. During the interrogation, two vassals of Origin were present and were surprised on a par with everyone, so Backfield believed that the Items were not stolen by the Duke. The chief witness, a whisker with a funny nickname, begged not to give it to the mistress. The Crow of the Crown swore not to hand him over in exchange for testimony, Whitmore gave his word of honor to protect him, and Backfield only nodded, and the witness took a nod for the oath. ([[mark-voron|Mark]])

Backfield and Whitmore reported to Minerva. Whitmore only apologized for the shame of his company. Backfield gave the names of the witnesses, Inge Price, nicknamed Couple and Girls Baby G, and Minerva turned pale. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) Whitmore recalled that he and the Crown Raven had sworn not to extradite Inge, but Backfield objected that he had not sworn himself. Witnesses were brought from the station hotel. Minerva stared silently at Inji for five minutes, then said, "Down him." The girl that Whitmore wanted to attach, she also sent to the next cell. Backfield asked to reconcile him with Origin, dreaming of the rank of colonel. Minerva recalled that he killed several dear vassals of the Duke, including his beloved cousin, but agreed to silence the word.

Beckfield's main "gift" was reserved for Origin. These were three letters that came in January and February: The Birdman, who owed him, answered them, as the Major had commanded, and sealed them with his coat of arms. On his way out of the dovehouse, Backfield met Shatterhand, recently promoted from lieutenant to captain, and noticed a corner of the letter in his pocket. The captain threw it to him, "Lucia's playmate was sleeping with hoops. And with Shavans. And their slaves.” In a narrow corridor, Backfield's blade is stuck in plaster. Shatterhand punched him in the face and nailed him with a sword to the floor, and then, moving a blade in the wound, forced him to hand over the letters. Looking at the letter, he said: “Perhaps my question has been removed. On behalf of the Crown, I thank you for the gift.” Backfield survived thanks to a good doctor of the Palace of Silence. What was in the letters is not revealed in the interlude.

### Baby G and Inji
Baby G was orphaned early: her father disappeared, her mother died from the sea. Her aunt and uncle lost their home in a fire, and then they were killed by a “terrible man named Led”. Then she had “Uncle Inge”. G dreamed of becoming a pirate, and “pirates don’t cry for nothing.” From the cell opposite, Inji taught her to smash a wooden bucket, flatten an iron hoop and pick up a bolt outside the door with it. The attempt was interrupted by Minerva, an azure guard and an executioner in a leather apron. The executioner on the order only depicted torture: in a whisper he told the girl to shout “Don’t!”, so that Inzhi could hear. Minerva explained to Inji that she had once listened to the cries of an innocent girl who was suffering with her. She asked me to call her Mira, as before, and reminded me what he taught her: to cope with a hangover, to fight boredom, to charm men, to kill people. “Now learn to forgive.” Inji twice threw himself at the guard, and both times he was thrown to the floor, and the guard offered to cut his tendons.

Inji told his life. He killed three, then joined the guild and killed five more. Only one murder was proved in court, and instead of a loop, he received a galley. From the galley he fled, killing two overseers, and then in [[uejmar|Waymar]] hired the "good Count Shayland". Mira, he hinted, was not the first to be kidnapped by the count. According to him, only the Foremothers could judge him. Here came Shatterhand with Backfield's letters, which he said had a direct bearing on the conversation about forgiveness. After reading them, Minerva spoke warmer. She offered Inge a choice. The first is to return to the gallery, trusting the honest judge who once convicted him, and then Ji will be immediately released. The second is an injection from a syringe with "a mixture of blood, corpse poison and witch potion." Inji could die, survive or survive and gain special strength. Minerva called it “the court of Ulyana Sad”, which Inzhi considered fair: if he dies, it means that the Mother took him, and if she survives, Minerva will forgive him, return his friendship and give him a job. One condition: no more killing, at least not without her orders. Inji asked for two minutes and a date with G.

### Six days later
Minerva hasn't had a drink in six days. Wigam voluntarily brought her an Orkad kuado of seven years of aging, without water and impurities, and advised not to wash down the wine with chocolate. Minerva, who sat over the registers of Sacred Objects, asked him to teach her how to understand wines. According to her, she only decided to limit herself: “I am far from Wilhelm the Great.” In Marengo, she had to judge, decide, forgive or not forgive, and she realized that the one who undertakes to judge should himself become at least a little better. Cuado she said to bring when he will be proud of himself, and in the meantime to serve coffee.

Happy Wigam collided in the hallway with a pregnant girl in a philistine dress, about the third month. She was led by a guard captain, who called her "the personal guest of Lady Talmir". Through a crack in the service corridor, Wigam overheard the conversation. The girl was Leila’s daughter. She spoke to her mother with "you" and bitterness and got slapped. Leila gave her the address of a "lady" who would find her a husband from a good family ready to turn a blind eye to her position, and five thousand ephes in bills and appropriations. In addition, she presented her daughter with a letter signed by the mistress about the compensation of her lazy possession, two villages and eight hundred acres on the bank of the Hanai, and a gift in the name of her daughter, so that at least the granddaughter was born a noblewoman. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) Leyla admitted that for this she committed a “very disgusting act” and quarreled with the empress, and the papers were received only by the mercy of the mistress. The daughter threw in response that the mother did the same: “I would not be in the world, if you were so holy!” Leila replied that she did not intend to forgive her, and hopes to see her again in five years, when the time comes to give her granddaughter to boarding. Wigam was shocked that the only punishment for his daughter was unforgiveness.

## Significance in the Series
The interlude links [[yanmejskaya-okhota|the Yanmei Hunt]] with the [[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]]. She explains how Znaharka's blood (the [[pervokrov|firstblood]]) was at court, why Silver Fox, despite his double fidelity, kept the post, and how Inge Price got back to Minerva. The body in Bertie Crane's chest, by all indications, belonged to the Znaharka: Leyla herself says that her corpse was kept in the cellars of the palace, and in a "disgusting act" she confesses to her daughter.

In Humans and Gods, Inge, who survived the injection ("Promators didn't take you to the Star"), unsuccessfully tries to heal the cut with the Hand of the Infirmary, and Minerva wonders why the primrose didn't work. ([[ruka-znakharki|Healer's Hand]]) She later gives Inji an imperial pardon, under the Crown Raven, who promised him freedom, and a guarantee of adoption of Baby Ji. The girl is already studying in a good boarding house, paid for all four courses. Backfield is arrested and accused of, among other things, deceiving the Empress. The theme of forgiveness returns in the main cycle.
