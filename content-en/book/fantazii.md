Fantasies is a collection of individual stories written by the author after [[ten-velikogo-dreva|The Shadow of the Great Tree]]. The action of most of them takes place in 1776–1777, in the world after the battle for the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]] and the fall of the Puppeteer, on the eve of the election of the new ruler. Stories do not continue each other: each has its own heroes, genre and intonation, from political comedy to detective and mysticism. The general background connects them only weakly. This is the upcoming election, the interim reign of [[minerva|Minerva]] of the First Winter, Adrian as mayor of Faunterra and the split of the church left without a head. The seventh fantasy, "Ad-ri-an!" gives the solution to this line: elections in the autumn of 1776 are disrupted, Adrian dies, and power is shared by Minerva and Erwin. The last stories of the collection show her reign: the reduction of the court and the army, school reform and disputes about how to write about the war in textbooks.

## Foreword by the author and status of the collection
In the preface, the author admits that he usually considers the prefaces "an unnecessary and useless part of the book", but here he is forced to deviate from the rule. The main plot of Polari, according to him, is completed: it ended with the “Shadow of the Great Tree” on the words “The choice is yours.” The author considers such a finale to be accurate and successful. It reveals the main, in his opinion, the issues of the universe, and in the shadows left small things, for example, whether the punishment or reward individuals and “who of the characters married”. The world built by the Foremothers remains just regardless of such particular cases.

However, readers have asked many questions about further developments, and some of them have "excited the imagination" of the author. He considers his fantasies a sufficient reason to get to work and recalls that the novel “[[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]]” arose “from pure flight of imagination”, without a commercial purpose. The status of the book is stated directly:
> “This is not a continuation of the story you know Polari, and not even a sequel novel with its own plot.”
This is a collection of fantasies about how the fate of some people, as well as some political events of the following years. Therefore, the stories should be read as possible future options, and not as a canonical continuation.

In the full edition, the author sums up: he let the imagination “free to soar”, not limited to the plot or the framework of genres, and received a collection of twelve works. Among them are four detectives, one mystical story, two love “with elements of eroticism”, one story about the interaction of politics with science, three everyday dramas and the story “Ad-ri-an!”. Only this story, which is closest in genre to the main cycle, “shines light on the future of the entire empire”, and the rest of the fantasies are devoted to the fate of people, not states. Therefore, fans of great geopolitics and epic events, the author of the collection does not recommend, but, according to him, he will please those who are interested in the lives and heart affairs of characters, and "not only the main characters, but also the most charismatic among the secondary ones."

In the original version of the preface, written when the collection was published on the story, the number of stories was not determined in advance, but, "out of respect for the holy numbers", they will be no less than eight and no more than sixteen. At the time of the preface, "life drama and political game against the background of science" were written. A classic detective, a light mystic and a “severe northern thriller” were conceived. The author did not know the release dates, but promised to release on the story for the great Polarian holidays: Spring Dawn, Sofiyny days, Abundance, Descendant and the birthday of the emperor. Imagination, he says, flies freely along the timeline, so the near future may be next to events “five years later.” The author admits as an adventure that he offers to buy a book that has just begun. In favor of such a decision, he cites the atmosphere of lively discussion that developed while the “Shadow of the Great Tree” was coming out in chapters. The title and cover are chosen out of love for [[pramater-agata|Agatha the Radiant]], but the book will not be dedicated to one of her descendants: the author’s imagination does not want to be limited to “any framework, including generic”.

### Fantasy number one: "The Device"
*March-April 1776, [[faunterra|Faunterra]].* By a peace agreement, [[adrian|Adrian]] recognized himself as the mayor of Faunterra. His personal secretary wears a white mask, concealing injuries his family says are “protecting the honor and freedom of a great man,” and stutters from excitement. He turned the town hall into a palace. Once he confessed his love to Minerva, but she missed the confession by her ears; he did not accept the healing from her hands and now considers her a usurper. The secretary is not named, but it is, without a doubt, [[itan|Ethan]]. Adrian reads an article in the University Journal of Science about the recording apparatus of Professor Nicholas Ollie and calls the inventor to himself. The device consists of a cylinder, a diamond needle and a trumpet. He records Adrian's voice, but on high notes he breaks into a shrill squeal. Ultra-thin wire for the device was ordered from jewelers, and the coils were manually wound by the assistant professor, student Alice Cavendish. It took a month to assemble a single copy.

Adrian thinks to appeal to the common people with his voice, bypassing hostile lords and preachers. To do this, he needs a hundred devices by September. The election he intends to propose to postpone to the fall and submit it as a "gesture of nobility" to the North, where Northwood is in turmoil and the Origins in mourning. Colonel Croydon Backfield and Major Hand Dodge report on the moods in the lands and are ordered to guard the laboratory. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]]) Soon, a blue notebook of forty-eight pages with drawings disappears from the professor's table. Backfield's men search the lab and find Ollie's "Voice of the Crown" page with a portrait of Minerva. They suspect that the scientist is secretly serving the mistress.

[[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], the viceroy of the Crown Lands, who is nicknamed the Unexpected Lady by the metropolitans, and her father [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] want the device not to be ready too soon. Magda reminds that she controls the budget of the Ministry of Science. Maurice gives the professor a “donation” of about half a thousand ephes and promises to buy the invention for ten thousand, only without haste. Alice, who has spent the last agate, willingly takes the money. Then the apparatus is shown to the highest ranks of the church: the supreme mothers of Orlando and [[mat-alisiya|Alicia]], Bishop [[episkop-amessin|Amessin]], the Second of the Five and King [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]]. Both branches of the church lost their heads, and their treasury is not available, so they can buy the device only with the money of the king. Francis-Ilianus pays, explaining that this help to the church will allow him to participate in the elections. He, an ordinary monk, claims the mantle of the priory, and in the morning he intends to announce his nomination to the lords: at least one of the two elections he "will definitely win."

The professor accuses Alice of stealing a notebook: she really gave the article to the Herald so that he would not go to Minerva. Alice runs away in tears. Later, Ollie finds a notebook in the box, tickets to the mystery of "Carrog and Liola" and a note from the bearded gatekeeper Eugene. ([[liola-i-karrog|Liola and Karrog]]) He admits that he took a notebook, hoping to figure out the schemes himself. His "mistress" does not mind that the car went to Adrian, and in the Conto bank, the professor expects her to "hello monetary properties." ([[konto|Konto]]) By all accounts, Eugene is Inge Price: he has a "very funny" nickname, which "not for academia", the adopted daughter in the boarding house, he "moved around the world", and Backfield his face seemed familiar. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) The following account mentions that Minerva's "old assassin" left for Faunterra in the winter. Portrait of Minerva professor, avid theater, kept not out of sympathy: on the back was a review of the play. The story ends with the unsociable Ollie breaking into all audiences in a row in search of Alice.

## Significance in the Series
"Fantasies" does not change the finale of the main plot: the open choice, which ends with "[[ten-velikogo-dreva|The Shadow of the Great Tree]]", remains unanswered. However, the stories show the world after the war. Minerva reigns from the First Winter as a temporary empress, Adrian became the mayor of Faunterra, Magda became the viceroy of the Lands of the Crown and the mother of his son, and elections of the lord and priest are being prepared. In "Ad-ri-an!" this line gets a denouement. Adrian, caught in a robbery and disrupting the election, is killed during the assault on the House, by all indications at the hands of the people of the House of Almer, and his body disappears. Francis-Ilianus becomes the principality, and the power according to his plan is shared by Empress Minerva and the military ruler Erwin, who is engaged to Nexia Flame. The reader learns the possible fates of the heroes, including those whom the main cycle considered dead or left in the dark: Alanis lives under a false name (although in "Ad-ri-an!" her servants call her deceased), Sir Humphrey and Sophie avenge the House of Almer, Mark became a private lawyer, Ethan and Inji serve each his own side. Later stories look further: Minerva cuts spending, abolishes the Ministry of the yard and dissolves the shelves, Alanis becomes the Minister of Education, and "Brilliant Calculation" shows the battle for the First Winter through the eyes of General Davey - as a chain of failed plans, which was retroactively given up for a brilliant idea. The stories also answer minor mysteries, such as the nature of Anxiety. All this, according to the author's reservation, is only possible options for the future.
