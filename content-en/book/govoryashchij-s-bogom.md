The second of three interludes between the novels "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]" and "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]". This is a tale of a simple sailor who tells his "native" story of winter crossing the mountains. His story is interspersed with short "prayers" of the peasant Stanley and his wife Frida, addressed to a certain "god" - in fact, this is a cunning game of [[mark-voron|Mark]]-Crow and [[gvenda|Gwenda]]. The name refers to the trophy Object, through which the heroes suddenly got the opportunity to talk to the enemy.

## Narrative form
The text consists of two alternating votes. The main story is conducted by the sailor of the schooner "The Seal", whose comrades are called the [[potomok|Descendant]]. He turns to the listener ("native", "cute") and every now and then swears that "never lies", although he can "lie a little ... for expressiveness". His chapters are interspersed with the "prayers" of a simple-minded shepherd Stanley, who, together with his wife Frida, bows to "God" and asks him for health, children, and money. Only by the middle does the reader learn that Stanley is [[mark-voron|Mark the Raven]], and "God" is the one who listens through the trophy Item. The first “prayer” opens the interlude and is repeated almost verbatim later, in a scene at the bridge.

## Plot
### The Collapse of the Seal
In December 1774, the Seal returned from a campaign for [[reka|the River]] among floating ice floes. Off the coast of the seamen waited for trouble: the owner of the nearest port of [[belomorye|Belomorye]] was the Count Fleming, who changed the house of the [[oridzhin-zemlya|Origin]] and already tried to destroy the expedition on the way. ([[fleming|Benedict Fleming]]) The Seal itself belonged to Fleming's flotilla: Captain [[dzheff-bamber|Jeff Bamber]] was his vassal. The attacker was killed by traitors, and without him, no one knew the safe fairway to the local bays. When three ships appeared on the horizon under the flags of Fleming, the Seal rushed into the nearest bay and ran aground. The crew abandoned the ship, on boats reached the shore and went to the snowy hills. Before the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], it was about one hundred and fifty miles across the hills and mountains, kneeling in the snow.

The narrator lists the squad. It's got two [[kajry|kairs]]. He forgets the name of one, and calls him Mine, as the Gray John-John, the Friend of the Descendant, called him. The second is [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]], a "living legend", who walked with the young Duke to the Beyond: according to rumors, there first Jemis wanted to kill the Duke, then the Duke, and then they became loyal friends. Then there is Captain Jeff Bamber, the boatmaster of the Biven, who brags around the world, a half dozen sailors, among them the merry Larry. Two more half-crazy prisoners: a young man who considers himself a goat, and frightened, "like a gopher", [[gvenda|Gwenda]]. There was a huge gray sheepdog under Jemis. And finally, a sly rascal [[mark-voron|Mark]] nicknamed The Crow. The Cairos took the chase with a false trail, but realized that a hundred Fleming soldiers, who arrived on three ships, would soon begin a raid. There were only three swords and a couple of crossbows for the whole squad.

### The mute bracelet
Mark suggested using a "trophy toy". In the fort beyond the River Jemis got the [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Object]]: a bracelet that looks like a wedding, smooth as glass, warm as living skin, colors "amber mixture with milk". Jemis suspected that Mark knew how to handle [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]] and hid it. Mark reasoned differently: the watchman at the fort grabbed the bracelet, although the kair sword was a second away from it, which means that the Item works in a couple of seconds, and since there are no buttons or protrusions on it, it does not rise and does not press. Jemis put on a bracelet, aimed his hand in the forest and shouted “Bay!”, “Death!”, “Zalp!”, “Shoot!”, “Fire!”, “Kill!!!” The subject was silent. Later, Mark tried everything: both hands, words aloud and mentally, prayers, kisses, his own blood, a holy spiral, even a sight at Jemis. Nothing came of it, and he offered to simply sell the bracelet, which is also silent “thousand forty is”. Jemis refused and told him to think until the very first winter.

### Cold
On the fourth day, the sailor Hess froze to death. On the eve of the kairah forbidden to build a fire for fear of smoke, and when the bossman tried to light it, Moi knocked off his hat with a sword. The sailors hated the Kairs, and at the same time John-John, who held the side of the master. John-John asked Me to go forward: they walked faster in the snow and in three days could reach the first outpost of the mountain guards of the Origins. My only wish was to take with me the Duke’s personal prisoner, Mark and Kozlik, who would “please the Duke.” “The lives of sailors are not our concern,” he said. Mark thwarted this plan: fell into the snow, cried out about the sick “leg” and loudly lamented that Lord Desmond would have to answer to his son for the lost prisoner. Jemis punched him on the jaw "so as not to murmur", but agreed: "You can't quit the team. The Duke wouldn’t do that.” Mine, John-John and Goat went ahead, and Jemis stayed with the sailors and gave the bracelet to Mark: "Live me this thing." Mark himself, whom Jemis allowed to choose, also remained.

### The Bridge Over the Gods
On the third day after the departure of My Gwenda, she first heard the chase, and soon two riders in black cloaks appeared on the trail - reconnaissance. Jemis showed the Valley of the Boy of the Gods looking like a boat and coming down to the south with a suspension bridge four miles away. Mark's got five hours to go, and they'll catch up in two. Jemis took two sailors with crossbows to delay the chase, and ordered the captain to bring the team to the bridge in three hours and set it on fire with him. Captain Bamber is alive. He yelled at the crew, as on the deck of the Seal, and the sailors "ploughed" four miles of virgin land. The bridge was made of two ropes with boards and two more ropes for the hands, with holes and a foot of snow. Descendant and Larry swept snow from him, the rest were carrying firewood, a polished orge.

Jemis is back on a trophy horse. One crossbowman died in the woods, the other, Buddy, struck down Fleming's rider's bolt. Jemis' horse refused to step on the bridge, and the count's patch swung at the keel with an axe, but the dog rushed at the rider. The fire started too slowly. Jemis chased the dog - "Sagittarius, away!" - and one stood across a narrow bridge against the Latvians, who walked one by one, covering themselves with shields, right through the flames. Gwenda squealed in desperation and demanded a bracelet from Mark, and he put it in her hand. She held it up to her lips, whispered something, and the Object shone with moonlight:
> "Contact".

Two or three seconds later, Mark dragged Gwenda by the trees, banged on his knees and shouted "like a fool": "Who is this?! Who says?... Oh, God! Is that you?! The ropes burned, the bridge collapsed with the county warriors, and Jemis, hanging on an icy rope, the sailors from the last forces dragged up. On the other side of the gorges were hit by crossbows.

### Mark's idea
While hiding in the woods, Mark explained his guess. Having noticed the enemy, the sentry first of all raises the alarm, and the sentry in the fort was not going to shoot, but to signal the owner. So, the bracelet is not a weapon, but a way of communication, “like a mail pigeon or a wave” (this is the [[golos-boga|Voice of God]]). The voice in it, according to Mark, belonged to the one who built the fort, obtained the Feathers and pitted the duke with the emperor. Having tried with Jemis options for the answer - from "Give up, son of a bitch!" to "You can't cope with Agatha the Radiant!" - Mark revealed his. He portrayed a dark peasant, confident that God was speaking to him. The enemy will decide that the Object has got to the fool, and will want to take it. Mark will be day after day to feed him nonsense, and then "accidentally" tell him where he lives, and the messengers will ambush the kairs. Jemis acknowledged the plan, but warned: “There is no one smarter in the world than the grandchildren of Agatha the Radiant!”

### News on bail
The next day the detachment met the mountain guard, sent by Mine and John-John. They came to the outpost without any new losses, only with frostbite (the Descendant froze his finger). At the outpost, the sailors were warmed up, and the captain of the outpost reported the news: the war is over. The young Duke [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] took the capital a month ago, and when the Crown troops encircled him, Colonel General [[stetkhem|Statham]] and Kair [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] broke through the ring. [[minerva|Minerva]] Stagforth signed the peace treaty on behalf of the Empire. Emperor [[adrian|Adrian]] died: his train derailed and collapsed into the river, "the Agatha the Radiant itself" collapsed the bridge "under the wheels of a tyrant" (see Crash on the Back). ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]]) Mark's white. He replied to the descendant only “In vain...”, and then added: “Now it is a matter of honor for me” and ordered to bring Gwenda.

### Stanley's Prayers
From that evening, Gwenda was revived by the bracelet at every stop on the way to the First Winter, and Mark spoke to “God”. "God" never answered, but the bracelet glowed - so he listened. In Stanley's "prayers" there was a whole legend. Stanley thanked for the cure of the cold and complained about his wife's sick tooth. He told how, after going to the farm for a sheep healer Gordon to a shabby black sheep Marcy, he found a dying warrior with a bracelet in the grove. He asked for children, and when Marcy did not recover, he decided that the “god” fulfilled the second request and they “will soon have a baby.” Then he asked for money in case of war between the Count and the Duke. Finally, Stanley reported that he had been visited by the duke's cayres, the "holy dozen", found the body of that warrior and gave the shepherd three gold ones. He asked for advice on whether to give the bracelet to them, and admitted that he did not understand the first words of God - whether "on the rope" or "on the harness." The descendant, looking at this, decided to pray sincerely only to the waves from the side of the ship: after all, a person with a Sacred Object is heard by a “scumbag and a murderer”.

### In the duchess's chambers
A week later, the squad arrived in the First Winter. Lord Desmond did not take anyone sick. ([[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]]) Mark wanted witnesses, and Jemis explained to the garrison commander that Mark was a "rare talker and liar." Three went with them: Gwenda, Captain and Descendant. They were received by Lady Sophia Jessica, Erwin’s mother and Desmond’s wife, who kept them “as if she had silver instead of blood in her veins.” ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]) A descendant noticed a heavy black curtain in the room in a place where there could not be a window. The door was open: behind it lay Desmond, a sick [[kamennaya-khvor|stone disease]], and listened, and the incense drowned out the corpse's smell.

Mark spoke in detail about the expedition, about Fleming's betrayal and about his conclusion that the lord is innocent. Sophia replied that she and her husband suspected such a thing: Adrian insulted the nobility, “but before the gods he is clean,” and “the House Origin does not bother itself with slander.” When asked about the award, Mark said that he did not want to return to the prison of the First Winter. Sophia replied that it was their duty, not their reward. Then Mark asked a detachment of kairs to ambush the owner of the Rings. On the eve of the "god" first called him, and Mark was sure that he ordered to leave the bracelet in some church under the altar. Sophia asked Gwenda to show her how she spoke to the Object. Gwenda whispered, “Help me...” and the bracelet replied, “I hear you, mortal!” But as soon as Mark began his “God, hello!”, the Duchess took the bracelet and called herself: “Sophia Jessica Augusta, Lady Origin.” She said the interviewee owned the Rings and staged a civil war, and offered a deal: a bracelet in exchange for her husband's healing, "the word of Lady Origin." The voice replied:
> "Go to Faunterra. Bring the bracelet and the husband. I’ll tell you what to do next.”

In the ensuing silence, Mark slammed his hand across his forehead.

### Epilogue
A column of hundreds of kairs took in the covered sleighs of Desmond and Sofia to the capital. They were accompanied by Jemis and Sagittarius and Mark. Sailors rode in the tail and, as Mark taught, played dice on stories: the Origins were going to start a fleet on the East Sea, Captain Bamber was promised a schooner, the sailors - a profitable service. The raven was not given a title, nor money, and freedom came to him “with a soul”: he was simply told to go to the capital. His only reward was that he was forgiven, as he called the Duchess of Origin a fool at all.

## Significance in the Series
The interlude connects the finale of the second novel with the third: she explains how the [[golos-boga|Voice of God]] fell into the hands of the Origins and why Lady Sophia and her sick husband went to [[faunterra|Faunterra]], where the mysterious “master of the Rings” promised healing. ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]) In "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]" he is called the Puppeteer, and a messenger comes to Faunterra to the Origins with the words "The Puppeteer sent Znacharka - the old lord to heal." Here is Mark’s conclusion about the innocence of [[adrian|Adrian]], which will be the starting point of the Crow’s investigation in the third novel. Gwenda in the capital lived in the palace as a servant of Jemis's queer, and it was with her that Erwin began to check who was still able to revive the Objects.
