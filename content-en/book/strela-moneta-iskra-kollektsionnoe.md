"Arrow, coin, spark (collection edition)" - a special edition of the novel "Arrow, coin, spark", dedicated to the release of its printed version. ([[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]]) After each chapter, there are comments by Lady [[karen|Karen]]. The author writes them on behalf of the heroine, as if the novel had already become a well-known book about recent events in Polari. Karen reads it after the end of the whole cycle: she is free, lives with [[menson|Manson]] and personally knows many heroes. Therefore, the notes are full of hints of later events and reveal the plot of the following books.

Karen comments on the novel as a strict and mocking critic. She catches the "author" on mistakes in armor, etiquette, titles and laws, explains how everything was "really", asks participants of events and does not hide his sympathies. In between, she talks about her life: about trips with her husband, about his outbursts of jealousy and anger, about a valerian who drinks over especially terrible chapters. Remarks are not always reliable. Karen is biased, and some of her "amendments" themselves turn out to be jokes of the author. Therefore, information from them is given here with a reservation “according to Karen”. How Karen sees the unnamed author and the novel itself as a book of his world is told in the articles "[[avtor-v-mire|The Author (in the world of Polari)]]" and "Arrow, coin, spark (a book in the world of Polari)". ([[strela-moneta-iskra-v-mire|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Book within Polari)]])

## Preface by Lady Karen
Karen begins with the fact that the book can be called both ordinary and unique. The fair and the Northern Outbreak are described in nine historical works and more than a dozen novels. The book is made unique by the hype that the metropolitan nobility raised around it: the novel was praised as artistic, but “more precisely, many historical”. Out of the spirit of contradiction, Karen did not open it for six years.

Then she was addressed by “my lord N”, the owner of a large printing house in the [[serdtse-sveta|Heart of Light]]. He was preparing an expanded edition of the novel and wanted to supplement it with her comments. In his opinion, Karen is familiar with many actors, versed in court orders, worked a lot with books and, most importantly, was born in the Heart of Light, like himself. Karen recalled that that year could not be an eyewitness: she was in a hospital on the island of Faraday Riley. ([[ostrov-faradeya-rajli|Faraday-Riley Island]]) Still, she agreed, but warned that she would not soften the expressions. Already in the second chapter, this reservation, according to her, turned out to be “very far-sighted.” She worked on the notes for five months.

## Who else is involved
- **[[menson|Manson]]**, Karen's husband. By name, he is almost not named: Karen calls him “those who are destined for me”, “my diamond”, “guardian of my peace of mind”. He laughs at the mistakes of the author, argues with his wife and twice writes notes himself. After the difficult scenes, he reads the chapters in advance to decide if it is possible to show them to his wife.
- **[[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]].** Karen asks him about the affairs of the First Winter: whether Erwin could have dedicated [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]] to kairs and who was at the meeting at the bedside of the dying Duke.
- **William Davey.** ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) The general reads the chapter about himself as a comedy and is even proud to be depicted as a rude simpleton.

### Chapter 7. “Coin”

The reason is a gray pestilence, and Karen writes a humorous essay on medicine, based on the experience of close acquaintance “with one medical institution.” ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) The tailor always knows how to sew. The main trouble of the chapter, according to Karen, is that the author does not understand the northern tactics and does not know who the Greeks are. Tommy is not a grey, but a resident or a vestige. Gray is a servant and a student of kair, and Erwin is not a kair and can not have gray. Gray and not a vassal: the vassal is a nobleman who has sworn an oath to the elder, and a person of any class can become a Graham if he is taken by some cairn. More precisely, according to her, a comparison with the craft: kair - master, gray - apprentice with a very harsh master. There is no division “kair is a rider, gray is an infantryman”. Both can be horse and foot, depending on whether there is money for the horse. A two-color coat can also be earned by a bad rider. The Kairs receive a good salary, and the cavalry is more honorable than the infantry, so many eventually move to horse units.

The Kairs and the Greys are trained to fight both in formation and alone.

## Afterword

After completing the "great work", Karen admits to conflicting feelings. Her favorite absurdities of the author are Yanmei as a patroness of flowers, [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]] on a caravel and Minerva, who gives the general lessons in military affairs. At first, she did not understand what the author was inclined to and when the story would begin. Descriptions of court life amused naivety, but awakened nostalgia, and combat scenes, though not too reliable, were alive. She was most touched by the feelings of the heroes: the suffering of people she knew was shown so sincerely that indifference was lost. Knowing what awaits them in the near future, she experiences fear and sadness.

Karen showed notes to several well-known participants in the events. They wanted to write their own: everyone thought that she had missed something, and everyone had information that could turn the perception of events. Therefore, the next edition may be published with notes of other persons. Or the publisher will be horrified by the thickness of her work and forever abandon this venture, which, according to her, from the very beginning was ridiculous, “like Yangmei, the patroness of flowers.”

## Appendix: about the Julian law

Karen found two pages on the floor after one of the disputes with her husband and attached to the notes, leaving the publisher to decide where to print them. The occasion was Erwin's conversation with the Duke of Litland about the fords through Holliwell (see Julian Law). ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]; [[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]) According to Karen, the author attributed to the Duke a vague concept of the law.

**What is Julian Law?** This is not one law or one set, but a “complex of codes” of laws. By the end of the sixteenth century after the Descent, when [[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]] began her reign, the Empire was living with a contradiction:
- The brilliant Dynasty claimed supremacy throughout the Sublunar world and demanded that all lands comply with the imperial code, which dates back to the code of Foremothers Yangmei and Yumin. ([[pramater-yanmej|Yanmei the Merciful]])
- The commandment “Let the other be” justified local customs contrary to the code, and the Crown lacked administrative and military strength. Northwood and Reis, Darkwater and Origin lived by age-old orders, and about three-quarters of the mainland's inhabitants were criminals in terms of imperial law.

The main achievement of Juliana Karen considers not the victory in the Second Horse War and not the liberation of the center from the Shavan oppression, but legislative reform. ([[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]]) Juliana legalized local customs. Every land is entitled to its own laws, and man is subject to the code of the land where he is. Julian's code incorporated all the laws of the land that existed at that time.

**Local features:**
- **[[oridzhin-zemlya|Origin]]** is a special class of [[kajry|kairs]]. The consecrated kire, even not original and without laziness, enjoys all the rights and honors of a nobleman. In other lands, the title is inextricably linked to the land.
- **[[nortvud-zemlya|Northwood]]** - the forests belong to all the people. Peasants have the right to hunt, collect and chop wood, paying a reasonable tax. In the rest of the empire, the forest is the property of the feudal lord, who can prohibit any fishing.
- **[[shimmeri|Shimmery]]** - polygamy is enshrined in the law. Alters and althes are available throughout the empire, but only the legal spouse and her children have rights. ([[alter|Alter and Altessa]]) In Shimmery, the heir to the title and condition can be recognized as a child of any altessa.
- **[[almera-zemlya|Almera]]** - poll tax replaced by income tax. This revived trade and crafts, and by the time of the Northern Flash, the Lands of Crown and [[nadezhda|Hope]] had begun to do likewise. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]])
- The steppe lands of [[rejs|Reis]] and Holliwell - the spoil is considered to be the extraction of any battle, including raid and raid. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) The rest of the land is recognized as a trophy only taken in a legitimate war.

Legal war is a personal innovation of Juliana. It is impossible to prohibit wars, and the ruler sought to make them as less ruinous as possible. War is legal if:
1. it is clearly and publicly announced;
2. He who began it accurately named the conditions on which he was ready to end it, that is, he said what he was fighting for;
3. these conditions leave the enemy the right to exist;
4. It is conducted only on the lands of the parties and their allies;
5. Prohibited tools are not used, especially [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]], as well as witchcraft and poisons (except for the kingdom of [[darkvoter|Darkwater]], where they are allowed);
6. mass extermination of civilians is permissible only if the enemy himself involved them in hostilities;
7. The inviolability of ambassadors shall be respected in negotiations;
8. The prisoners and the wounded are shown mercy.

The results of a legitimate war are recognized as legitimate: trophies go to the winner and his soldiers, the captured lands join his possessions. A commander who has violated these principles is equated with a criminal, his trophies are equated with a stolen one, and after the war he can be tried by an imperial tribunal.

**Of the Shepherd's Meadows.** The meadows along Holliwell are indeed common pastures and do not belong to any lord, but this is only one of dozens of articles in one of the hundreds of chapters of one of the eighteen volumes of the codex. If the two dukes had said "Julian law" in conversation, they would have had in mind the principles of legitimate war, not Luga.
