The third interlude between the second and third novels, written on behalf of [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] - a man who is sympathetic with the strong and ruthless with the weak. His life rule is “from above or below”: everyone should be approached either with a bow or with a fist. The chapter shows the war-torn [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]] and the plans of the defeated House of [[dom-labelin|Labelin]], and at the same time reveals who the merchant Horam from the interlude "[[nebesnyj-korabl-i-devushka|The Skyship and the Girl]]" was.

## Plot
### "Pig and Goose"
In January 1775, while [[faunterra|Faunterra]] was preparing for the coronation of the new mistress of [[minerva|Minerva]], Buckley sat in the Pig and Goose Inn on Meat-eater Square and played cards every evening of his partner-grocer, nicknamed the Sixth. ([[koronatsiya-minervy|Minerva's Coronation]]) The stakes fell from the deer to the silver half. ([[dengi|Money]]) The more the Sixth lost, the more affectionately he called him Buckley: “boyfriend”, “friend”, “friend”, “brother”. The sixth hated coronations, though he did not see any.

A man came into the inn, whom they noticed at the station in the morning, and immediately tried to escape. Buckley and Six jumped into his briquette. Buckley explained the trap to the prisoner. After the escape of Major Backfield, the top of [[protektsiya|the protection]] broke up, its informants were no longer fed, and the station "whisperer" pecked at ten gold for information about the passenger. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]]) He did not share the money with the station manager, so he worked directly for the head of the patronage. Under the fists of the Sixth Man, he agreed to sell the ledger. After trading from one hundred and eighty to one hundred and forty ephesas (twenty forwards), he brought six volumes to the station church two hours later, one for each month from July. Three other informants, he said, escaped with Backfield. In the books were recorded notable visitors with notes: "Visible nobleman", "Bringing a dog of unknown breed", "Leaving the station, distributed clothes to the vagabonds." As soon as the seller left, Buckley sent the Sixth "to benefit." Ten minutes later he returned with all one hundred and forty Hilt.

### Abbot Ferrain
In the books, Buckley searched for the names of "two useful and one small sage." The useful men were not so small as to be powerless, nor so conspicuous as to fall under the supervision of the northerners; they left the capital when it became frightful there, and returned to the coronation. The first was Abbot Ferrain, a minister of St. Wilhelm of the Proto-Paternal [[tserkov|Church]], a man from Almera. The doorkeeper who blocked the entrance, the Sixth broke his finger, and Buckley ordered to convey: "He who believes in St. Sophia, came to confess."

With the abbot, Buckley took a bottom-up approach: fell to his knee, kissed the ring, and allegorically laid out the case. Saint Sophia the Great, whom he allegedly worshipped all his life, “turned away” from him from November and ordered to turn to Agatha the Radiant, “central” and not northern. Her illness will not heal “even three million gold” and “four thousand riders.” Buckley suggested "talking" - a circle of the right people who would hold hands and say the right words - but the abbot rejected it as well. When Buckley asked about the "spirits of the steppes", the abbot replied that not to use a means of salvation would be a heresy, but warned: releasing the spirits into the wild, they are difficult to drive back. Then he promised: winter will change in the spring, people will be filled with faith, “the holy word will become stronger”, and then one prayer will suffice, and central Agatha will enclose Sophia in her sister’s arms. Upon leaving, Buckley spat three times and called the abbot a "smug ass", like all saints: "only knights are worse".

### Ira Medea
The second “useful” is the Shimmery Ira Medea, about forty, six years old as a withdrawn from trade, cousin of Ira-Merkur from the Shimmery Council of Five, who knows eye catching. With her, Buckley chose the approach “from above”: he called her “beautiful”, chatted with tea and assured that he had walked three hundred miles through the snow to talk. Ira-Medea took off her gold wire bracelet, planted with more than a dozen eyes, and told about the classes of spark stones:
- "lily" - the smallest, for daggers and ceremonial swords, only deafening;
- "Rose" is cubic, for swords and swords; above the heart kills. They are also equipped with a compact female self-shooter, a porcelain tube that hides in the sleeve;
- "chrysanthemum" - for long copies of spark infantry; discharge stops the horse's heart and melts the mail. Such eyes at [[bitva-pri-piksi|Pixie]] killed six thousand rebels;
- "Cypress" - a section of half an inch, melts the buckles of the cuirass and is discharged even when applied to iron or skin. Therefore, they are armed only every tenth, the most experienced infantryman.

She advised to arm the “holy dozen”, seventeen people. Buckley shrugged off: seventeen people are "not a number," and even a company is "very few." Ira-Medea said she can’t sell that many eyes, and he should know why. Then Buckley without words began to rake fruit from her dish: grapes, peach, orange, second, fourth, finally the whole dish. In the eyes of the southern woman, greed broke out - she counted fruits. She promised to "talk to the fruit supplier" and demanded guarantees, and Buckley showed a bank bill: "Big class money is the most effective means against enemy manpower." As he was leaving, he took the bracelet with his eyes and his own arrows.

### Death of the Sixth
At Merchant's Downhill, in the midst of a festive fireworks display over the Table Citadel, Buckley noticed the surveillance. In the spark workshop, he for five gold persuaded the owner, a former soldier, to charge himself with a cypress. The stone was not placed in the barrel, and the arrow stuck out of it for three inches. The sixth beat up a spy in the alley. Buckley never knew who sent him, let him go and shot him in the back. Cypress burned a huge burn between the shoulder blades. The eye cost two hundred ephes, and Buckley ordered the Sixth to cut it out of the body.

In the evening, the Sixth refused to give the stone: it was bought with the master's money, and the Sixth will give it to the owner. When Buckley called him a "stupid mortician", the Sixth lifted him by the throat and put a stone just taken from the dead on his tongue; Buckley humbly apologized. Then the Sixth put the "cypress" on the horse and won: Buckley had two kings, the Sixth had a king and an ace. Having taken two hundred ephes, the Sixth announced that he was leaving: Buckley calls him "brother", and holds him by the cattle. Buckley humiliatedly asked to drink the "world" for the last time, took out the "that bottle" from the luggage, he only pretended to drink, and the Sixth died of poison.
> Top or bottom, my friend. Your place is below and mine is above.”

### Council in Grace
In Grace, on the coast of the East Sea, stood two galleons with three million gold ephesas, taken from [[labelin|Labelin]], under the protection of dozens of warships. The city was almost all that remained of Buckley's master, Grand Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]], lord of a third of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. In the palace of the marquis Gracend, where the Duke had a week to postpone the meeting in anticipation of Buckley, he was met by six: the marquis Windley, who lost his lands; Baron Hugo Darryl, the "iron fist" of the Duke, who brought the knights to Grace; the marquis and the marquis Gracend, the masters of Grace, according to Buckley, "who gave birth to a round fool"; their daughter. ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]; [[valeri-grejsend|Valerie Graysend]]; [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]])

Darryl, according to Buckley's sarcastic assessment, glorified in three battles (in one he drew the sides of the Northwood bears, of the other two he "extremely successfully escaped"), offered to attack: in [[dojl|Doyle]], [[uindli|Windley]], [[soltaun|Soltown]] and Elfort there were two companies of northerners, in Labelin there was a battalion, the duke had four thousand riders, and the rails were destroyed. ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]]) Windley suggested joining up with the Earl of Fleming, who with five hundred [[kajry|kairs]] rebelled against [[ervin-oridzhin|Origin]] and held the coast of the Ice Sea, and together go to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]. ([[fleming|Benedict Fleming]]; [[more-ldov|Sea of Ice]]) Gracend - negotiate with the horde of [[moran|Steppe Fire]] against the [[litlend|Litland]] fortress of [[melorandzh|Melorange]], and then transfer the horde to Faunterra. Magda sarcastically ridiculed every plan. The Duke said that he was ready to buy anyone, “even the whole damn North”, but he would not fight with Origin anymore. The attempt to bribe him had already cost him a hundred thousand gold and five agents: General [[stetkhem|Statham]] took the money and remained loyal to the suzerain.

Buckley reported. [[almera-zemlya|Almera]] will not help either money or people, but [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] strengthens the power, will be ready to act by spring and, judging by the answer of the abbot, is already negotiating with the [[shavany|Shavans]]. [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] is looking for new buyers. Under the treaty with the Crown, which was in force until the death of [[adrian|Adrian]], the kingdom could sell to the side no more than ten percent of the eyes, but the imperial army is defeated, and the eyes of Origin are not needed: he will have enough thousands of captured copies taken at Labelin. The Council of Five is ready to break the contract if you buy weapons for four regiments, and in warehouses there are at least fifty thousand eyes - for fifteen regiments. Buckley found out about this in the "fruit language". Darryl, Windley and Graysend left to prepare the march, confident that the Labelins would arm their eyes.

### A World Without Knights
Left with his daughter and Buckley, the Duke revealed the real plan. You can not arm yourself with eyes: kairs will open a hunt for them. It is necessary to buy up all the stock and sell it cheaply to Westerners, Almera monks, bandits, pirates, capital garbage - to anyone who does not like the Northerners. Magda shot from a gift of self-armor in knightly armor against the wall: the buckles melted, the breastplate fell. Buckley managed to replace "cypress" with "chrysanthemum". The Duke was ready to lose more than a million, but the cairns will “obsolete”, and with them all the chivalry. Magda picked up: the knights did not save them from the northerners, “money and those who have them” should rule the world. Buckley said, “We will build a world without knights. We will make progress as the late Lord Adrian wished. We will end feudalism!” — but under the eyes of his father and daughter immediately recovered: “You, not us.” When asked why he would help, Buckley replied that he wanted a lot of money, and Magda rubbed his hair: “You serve well. You'll have a bone."

### Harmon's Trail
The Duke then asked about [[kharmon|Harmon Paul]], who had replaced the Light Sphere with a fake. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) Magda believed that [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] was behind the merchant, and offered to whisper to the Northern Princess, [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]], from where her husband takes the money: either he will show his wife the Sphere, or he will quarrel with Origin. The Duke was sure of something else. Harmon went missing for a week before the deal and then lied about being kidnapped and tortured. This week, he replaced the Sphere, which means he conspired with someone else, otherwise why would he run.

Harmon's name wasn't in the station book. But it was marked by a traveler who three times, passing through Faunterra, distributed clothes to the poor and bought himself a new one, and lastly took a ticket to [[marengo|Marengo]]. Buckley went there and found in the port a captain whose ship, the "Glorious Horam", had chartered to Laham. ([[lajem|Laem]]) There the merchant remembered the whole city: he bought at auction the cheapest and most evil slave, and then gave her a heavenly ship. So it turned out that Horam from "[[nebesnyj-korabl-i-devushka|The Skyship and the Girl]]" is Harmon. Father and daughter laughed that the ball will not raise any of them.

The Duke decided that Magda would sail to Shimmery with two million gold, a regiment of Darryl and Buckley, and he himself would go to Faunterra to portray a "beaten puppy" and intrigue against Origin. Buckley's first task is to find Harmon and bring the Sphere back. Upon learning that the Sixth "wanted to leave with everything he knew," the Duke laughed, "you'll take the Seventh."

### On board the "Great"
At the stern of the flagship “Great” Buckley tried to flatter Magda, but she cut him off: she knows what she is, and does not tolerate “beautiful lady” and “your lordship”. The light will be her older brother. Then Buckley offered something that would please her. He knows how to save a million of the two that will be lost on eye resale. He didn’t tell the duke, otherwise he would have given only one million. Magda realized that they will return in six months, and in six months with a million of gold you can do a lot. Buckley only asked for a "minor share of the profits." He thought to himself that he meant by a share all one hundred percent: two million in holds and all the eyes they would buy.

## Characters
- [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] is Labelin's agent, a storyteller-focalizer. Short, with small, close-set eyes. He does not like weapons, knights and former soldiers.
- The sixth is Buckley's rumble partner, poisoned by him.
- [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] is the Grand Duke of the Southern Way, fat and funny.
- [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]] is his daughter, twenty-five years old, unmarried, intelligent and sarcastic; the Duke's heir is her elder brother.
- Baron Hugo Darryl, Marquess of Windley, Marquess and Marquess of Greysend (parents of Valerie Greysend) are members of the council in Grace. ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]; [[valeri-grejsend|Valerie Graysend]])
- Abbot Ferrain is an Almeran priest of the Church of the Forefathers, a minister of St. Wilhelm.
- Ira Medea is a Shimmer merchant in the capital, cousin of Ira-Mercura of the Council of Five.
- The Station Whistleblower sells the book and dies at the hands of the Sixth.
- [[kharmon|Harmon Paula]] is the wanted kidnapper of the Sphere.

## Significance in the Series
The interlude introduces [[moger-bakli|Buckley]] and [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], who will become important actors in the third and fourth novels. The idea of "peace without knights" explains where the rebel [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] in the "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]" sparked the shots: Buckley himself admitted to [[minerva|Minerva]] that he gave the men a "holy dozen" of shots and promised to bring more. In "Janmei Hunt", Erwin caught Maurice, and he admitted that he gave the Snowdrops "only a dozen eyes"; ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]) Buckley, who betrayed Magda and tried to rob her, was stabbed by the Duke himself after being tortured by [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]. The Duke's joke about the "Seventh" also came true: on the "Great" Buckley recruited himself new thugs nicknamed Seven, Eight and Nine. Horam's identification with Harmon links the southern interlude line to the main story, and Buckley's hunt for the Sphere enters the Harmon line ("Coin") in the Yanmei Hunt. ([[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]])

## In "Fantasies"
In Fantasia, a possible future after the Battle of the First Winter, Magda became Adrian's wife and the governor (viceroy) of the Crown Lands, which the metropolitans nicknamed the Unexpected Lady. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]]) After the winter events in the First Winter, she was pregnant and dreamed of a crown prince, and later gave birth to a son, Adrian. Maurice with her settled in the Cathedral Citadel, the lower floors of which were occupied by the knights of Baron Hugo Darryl; ([[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]]) Darryl guarded Magda and the Citadel. Windley gave it to the Origins under the peace treaty. “Round fool” Valerie Gracend, whose seigneur is named Magda, became Lady Gracend-Smile, the wife of a retired general, and gave birth to twins. Buckley is mentioned there as the deceased: Jemis told that Hyder Leed tortured him to death.

## Trivia
- Buckley's life rule is "from above or below": he approaches the doorkeeper and the woman "from above", the abbot and the duke "from below". The interlude ends with his thought: “You most despise those before whom you crawl on your knees.”
- The owner’s saying, which echoes Buckley’s, “Desire is a valuable thing. “He who wants much is useful.” Hence the title of the chapter, and his command to the Sixth: "Be of use."
- Magda gives the exact figures of the treasury of Origin and the mistress: two million three hundred thousand expenses with one and a half million revenues.
- The “holy dozen” in Polari are seventeen people: so Ira-Medea advises to start armament.
