"Confident" is a side-interlude. The author's introduction calls it a story unrelated to the Puppeteer, the Northern Flash and the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]: it is a story about love, a little about intrigues within the Pramatery [[tserkov|Church]] and a little about the past of one of the main cycle characters. By all indications, this character is the abbess, the future archimper of Ellin (see "Value for the cycle").

## Plot
### Law Miriam
The Abbess of the small monastery of Sophia the Wonderworker (sixteen nuns, twenty-two novices) anxiously awaited the arrival of [[darius-khajtauer|Darius Hightower]], emissary of the church court and "eye of the archimatry". She only reported to the Chapter about the incident with the Object and was afraid that the emissary was going to destroy her, but she hoped to impress him: her monastery brought the church more than five hundred Hilt a year, and with his patronage it was possible to get a large monastery in [[labelin|Labelin]], and then the diocese. In the rain, Rose Wingram, the daughter of a wine baron, came to her, generous with donations. She asked the abbess to become her confidante for "Miriam's right," or the right of the first night. It is the right of a girl without the consent of her parents to choose the man to whom she will give the first night. The Confident (father, mother, confessor or respected priest) swears to keep what has been said as a secret of confession, and records it in the church book. Marriage obligations do not arise from this, and the child born does not inherit any titles or property, but must be loved.

Rose was chosen by the poor seminarian [[devid-atterton|David Atterton]]. He sat in the basement of the monastery under an honest word: the abbess accused him of stealing the [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Object]] of the monastery, because the two sisters saw how he lifted it to the wall, as if he wanted to throw it outside. Rose explained that David only took the Item for a while because of popular belief. If you look through the Object at the Star and call the name of your beloved, Sophia will connect the hearts, and there is a well near the wall, over which there are no branches. Father had already found Rose a rich suitor, and the engagement was about to take place. Abbess called David a thief and heretic, said that he would be hanged when the emissary arrived, and put the girl out the door.

### Alchemist and Seminarian
Rose went for comfort to the old alchemist Brimsworth, a shaggy freak and a friend of her father. He consoled her with alchemical comparisons: love is only a fleeting reaction, and the “ignitioner” can be replaced. In between, he talked about unusual forms of flame: Ratla powder flashes from the water, and the Shimmer mucus ignites in the air. Rose was angry, but in his words she had an idea, and she volunteered in the morning to help the master to disassemble the blockages behind the cabinets.

David’s friend, third-year seminary student Colin Baker, told how it was. In the summer, the seminarians worked in the monastery and painted fences. Lisbeth's sister was unwell, she left them a key in the temple castle and went to bed. David carried the Object to the well to look at the Star, and Colin stood guard at the dormitory and had to meow for a friend to throw the Object into the well. The cat of the abbess distracted him, and he missed the sisters Maya and Lisbeth returning from the pantry (Lisbeth, according to Colin's guess, drank sweet wine there). Colin meowed too late, David rolled over the bucket in fright, and he was caught with the Object in his hands. The seminarians sent a petition in defense of David to the Archbishop of Labelin, the Archimedes and the Priory and were going to meet the emissary on the road with all the course. Rose took an oath from Colin to remain silent and admitted that she had bought a thieves’ set from the “master of special crafts”: she needed Miriam’s right to enter David and give him the keys. Colin convinced her that escaping would forever make a friend a criminal: David must be acquitted.

### Emissary
Hightower came in a black carriage with two swordsmen at spark swords. It was a thin ascetic in black with a beard wedge, requiring the conversion of "my lord". He ridiculed the decorations of the abbess, rejected wine and a better room (he stayed with a friend), received the blessing reluctantly and nitpicked at everything. The statue of Helen should stand in the temple, not in the gazebo. The rose trade is a benefit from suffering ("Spicy thorns, isn't it?") and a violation of the third commandment. Dormitory looks south through the windows, and according to the canon should be to the east. On the way, he was detained by a crowd of seminarians, and he got tired “on behalf of David”. About the thief, he said one word: “hang.” Abbess suddenly objected. The subject is not on the list of miracle-working, but faith in it strengthens marriages. It allows you to touch it only those couples who will sacrifice at least two ephes, and half goes to the church treasury. David did not have a penny, but loved the daughter of a noble man, and she would not allow him for money. Hightower admitted that the chapter was going to send another, but he himself took this mission: in the city live his friend and his bride, and he wants to get engaged in Sofia days. He asked for an abbess.

### Bride
Rose turned out to be the bride: Hightower was a longtime friend of Baron Wingram, watched her grow up, and responded by agreeing to her father's letter. He examined her without any interest and asked only about what affects pregnancy and childbirth: about wine, about Susan’s wishes, about horse riding, about food. Rose honestly said that she did not love him, and he replied that he did not love her either: for passions there are altessa. Then he explained his position. The Church of the Pramatery is ruled by a Chapter of seventeen High Priests of all orders, and a man cannot enter it. Below the chapter stands the ecclesiastical court, which can execute or pardon a priest of any rank except the higher mothers, and the emissary is the spearhead of this "sword". A year later, on the following days, the new composition of the court was to be approved, and some of the mothers trusted the Agat Abbess of [[aleridan|Aleridan]] in the place of the emissary. Therefore, the archimandrite told him to marry: a married man “is more understandable than a single man.” He considered love a risk, because through his beloved wife, enemies could control it, and Rose’s protest only convinced him of her unselfishness.

### Sofiina Days
Three days before the holiday turned into a nightmare: twelve weddings and twenty-three engagements, three altars, a thousand guests, a lack of flour, rounders, which last year was not enough and eight boxes. The chest with the object was lashed, and the castle was flooded with something sticky, and it was not open. Hightower silently followed the abbess, found thirteen flaws, but praised her diligence: "Work hard to the best of your ability." He donated eight ephemera to Rose's wedding to be allowed to touch the Object and the gods tied her to it. He suspected Rose was in love, and wanted to know if she had used Miriam's right. The abbess was bound by the secrecy of the confession and invited him to demand information as a church judge, in writing. The emissary hinted that he needed a "faithful and intelligent woman" in the capital - an assistant, which means a way to the capital. Then he wrote in the book of confessions: “I demand information about confidentiality. Darius Hightower," and at her insistence spelled Rose's name. The request lay on the back of the sheet where Rose's rejected request was recorded. Abbess answered the truth: "Rose Wingram is clean. Miriam's right was not exercised by her." She advised him to acquit David before the wedding so that Rose would see mercy in him, not just rigor, and he agreed.

### Night delivery
Rose came to the deputy’s workshop, chose a beautiful “church” castle with a spiral, but never bought anything and secretly took away one of the keys to it. She then met Lisbeth's sister, who was following the new chest lock, and showed her the way to the same workshop. Through Lizbeth, Rose reported that the night after the evening song, her father would send eight barrels of wine and a "little gift." At night, Rose, Colin and seminarian Hayes brought barrels under the guise of movers. But instead of Lisbeth, they were met by Sister Lucille, the elder sister who knew the warehouses: the barrels were loaded into the library, and Lucille herself went with Rose to the temple to pray. Lucille called Hightower a "black man" and a korshun and invited Rose to escape her fiancé by taking the tonsure of a novice. Once she herself fled here in Sofia days from the monster groom, and the abbess protected her. Mother goes to the capital with the emissary, said Lucille, and the monastery will remain on it. While Lucille was catching an escaped cat on the choruses, Rose keyed open the chest with the Item. What she did there wasn't immediately said. Later, she recalled how she risked with a candle, because open fire was dangerous.

### Court
On June 23, on the eve of Sophia’s days and wedding, the abbess was in a great mood. She was already going to [[faunterra|Faunterra]], thought to take the cat with her and promised him that "in five years" his mistress would enter the chapter. She let David out of the basement. Hightower brought Rose, who he said wanted to see the trial. Two dozen seminarians crowded at the gate, citing Julian's open court law, but the abbess declared the monastery a feudal possession, and herself his seigneur, and let in one Colin, who had a written challenge. ([[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]) At night, after her father’s persuasion, Rose decided: let David become a priest, and she will go into the novice and stay next to her parents. Lucille gave the abbess her application for admission to the convent. The abbess allegedly kindly told Rose that "David wanted to see you" and locked her in an empty basement. Hightower, after reading the petition, ordered to keep the girl there until the wedding. Abbey warned that in the city there is a monastery of Ulyana, from where the novice will not be taken legally.

Lisbeth and Maya testified at the trial, and Colin at the pulpit dragged time and shut up. Rose slender tools from the thief's set spread the door boards, threw off the bolt and broke into the temple. The monastery is a feudal subject, she cried to the abbess, and, having locked up the baronial daughter, she took the captive. Then Colin showed that David carried the Item to the well because it caught fire. False testimony is punished by five years of hard labor, but Colin stood on his own and offered to open the chest. The abbess opened it, and the Subject flashed before everyone's eyes. The sisters shouted, “[[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]]!” The emissary's swordsmen ran a burning chest to the well. Colin noticed that David did exactly the same thing as them. David refused to lie at the altar. He said he wanted to look through the Object at the Star and call the name "Lady Rose Wingram". Hightower understood everything, found out from the abbess that David was not allowed to touch the Subject and there was no fee from him, and sentenced him to hang "at the first sound of the evening song."

### Blackmail
After the trial, the emissary demanded that the abbess explain the burning Item, calling it a charlatan trick, and promised to hang all the perpetrators next to the seminarian. The abbess recalled that he was going to justify David, and the young man did as he demanded honor, although the story with the burning Item could save him. Hightower replied that it was cowardice: David's lies, swordsmen would have skinned him to pieces, and the gallows compared to that is happiness. In addition, he referred to the first commandment “Take your place in the world with dignity”: a poor son took a nap on the Baron’s daughter. Then the abbess, saying that she had never sacrificed faith for profit and that to serve Sophia was to serve love, went on the offensive. She demanded David's acquittal and canceled the wedding and showed a book of confidentiality, where Rose's request was backed by his own written demand. If Hightower executes the young man, the captain will decide that the emissary forced her to violate the secret of the confession, learned the name of the opponent and dealt with him. And when Rose gives birth, the whole chapter will suspect that this is the child of the executed. She locked the book in a box: she was the vassal of the chapter, not his. The object, she said, caught fire because the gods were disgusted with his work.

Hightower promised to acquit the "small puppy" and cancel the wedding, but warned:
> “Without marriage and execution, I will not be afraid of a book. I'll burn you."

The story ended with the abbess petting Murchik's cat and promising him that he would still be "the fattest cat in the capital."

## Characters
- Abbess of the monastery of Sophia the Wonderworker - the name is not named; by all indications, the future Arch-mother of Ellin. Not yet old, and attractive; nineteen years in service. Ambitious, calculating, but faithful to the service of Sophia; she rules weddings. She has a gray cat Murchik.
- [[darius-khajtauer|Darius Hightower]] is the emissary of the Pramatery Church Court, an ascetic and careerist.

- Colin Baker is his friend, a third-year seminarian. Hayes is another seminarian.
- Sisters Lucille (elder sister, in charge of warehouses), Lizbeth (barrel with wart on the chin, loves wine), Maya, Violetta, Bertha and Martha.
- Brimsworth is an alchemist, a friend of the Baron.
- Baron Wingrem is Rose's father, a winemaker.

## Significance in the Series
The interlude reveals the structure of the Pramatery Church: a chapter of seventeen supreme mothers, a church court and its emissary, the "law of Miriam".

By all indications, the abbess is a young Ellina, the archimper of the main cycle, although her name is not named in the story. In [[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]], a survivor of Ellin's mind calls the cat Murchik, takes Erwin for him, searches for the "went cat" and mumbles about the rain at the wedding. It also says that Ellina came out of the Sofia order and all the youth of the wedding rules. At the wedding in Waymar, she gave a brilliant wedding speech and said that she returned to her best years. The abbess's dream of entering the chapter thus came true in excess, and Hightower's threat apparently did not. Colonel Hightower from Adrian's entourage in "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]" is the same name of the emissary; no connection has been established between them.

In "Fantasies", a possible future of the cycle, fallen into childhood, the archimper Ellin favored Origin: stroked him on the head and called Murchik. On the second of February, two months after the Descent, on a cold night when the maid had flooded her chambers too much, she died in her sleep, hugging the pillow, with the words "Well, hello, kitty...". After her funeral, the chapter is mired in squabbles about choosing a new archimatrix.
