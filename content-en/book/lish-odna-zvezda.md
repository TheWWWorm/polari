The young Duke of Origin is convinced that the emperor Adrian secretly owns the deadly Sacred Objects and exterminated people in the Beyond. Erwin announces an ultimatum to the Lord, and this is the plot of the second novel. The Northern War draws everyone: the head of the secret guard is sent to kill the rebel, the bereaved swordsman saves the burned heiress of the burned castle, the court jester sees the world in colors and smells, and the Minerva imprisoned in the monastery turns out to be a bargaining chip for several parties at once.

## Name
The name refers to [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]]’s saying, “There is only one Star.” The star in the world Polari is also called the afterlife. This phrase sounds in the novel more than once. On the night of [[evergard|Evergard]]'s death, "only one Star" shines over the burning castle. After hearing that Alanis was burned alive, Erwin remembers that she was talking about herself, and grimly notices that "The Star is burned." On the night of the capture of the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]], the living Alanis, embracing both the Origins, shouts: "We are the heart of the Empire! We are only one star!

## Structure
The novel is divided into two volumes. The first ends with the battle of Labelin, the second leads from the capture of Labelin to the coronation of Minerva. ([[labelin|Labelin]]) The chapters bear the code names of the narrators:
- "The Feather" - [[mark-voron|Mark]], Crown Raven (8 chapters);
- The Sword by [[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]] (12 chapters)
- Arrow by [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] (12 chapters)
- "Kolpak" - joke [[menson|Manson]] (5 chapters);
- "Iskra" - [[minerva|Minerva]] (11 chapters).

[[kharmon|Harmon]] is a storyteller. The chapters of the Arrows in the campaign along the Southern Way are interspersed with excerpts from [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]]'s book Questions of the Commander.

## In "Fantasies"
The novel's storylines are echoed in [[fantazii|Fantasies]], where the characters appear under other names.
- The story of Joaquin, who took the burned Alanis out of the burning Evergard, and lost two fingers under Arrowback, is by all indications reinterpreted in the story "A Composition on a Theme". The students write an essay about the lady and the knight who saved her. Mrs. Gale's teacher, a "gargoyle" in black gloves, with a cane, is by all accounts a survivor of [[alanis-almera|Alanis]]. In the secretly written sequel, the author of which, apparently, was herself, the lady lacks two fingers, teeth, nails and pieces of skin. The knight also does not have two fingers, he "admired his kindness" and "did almost nothing", and his friends tortured the lady. The last line is "Lady killed a knight."
- Eugene's gatekeeper from the story "The Device", moustached, with the nickname "not for learned circles", is by all accounts Inge Price, Minerva's former jailer in Waymar. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]])
- There, the burgomaster Adrian serves as a stuttering secretary in a white mask, mutilated, protecting the "great man", - according to all signs [[itan|Ethan]]. Mark, Crown Raven, by all accounts became a detective Mac from the stories "Ruth Case" and "Deadly Evidence".

## Significance in the Series
This is the central war novel of the cycle. The conflict stated in the finale of [[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]], goes all the way here: from the ultimatum to the fall of Adrian. Three new storytellers appear in the novel: Mark (The Feather), Joaquin (The Sword), and Manson (The Bell). Harmon falls out of the book: only Joaquin's memories, the mention of Labelin's purchase of the Light Sphere, and an account from Vittor's office remind him of it.

The main result is that Minerva, a former “exchange figure” of conspiracies, becomes the empress, and Erwin turns from a rebel into her powerful and dangerous ally. This sets the balance of power in "[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]". At the same time, the novel reverses the conclusions of the first book. Mark's investigation, the vanished fort, Gwenda with her "twilight men", the Ulyana Dust, the Citadel's empty treasury, and Mira's admission that Adrian did not own the Rings, point to an unknown puppeteer who pits the Great Houses. This mystery becomes central to the following books.
