## Source
The events in Lacey are known mainly from the interlude of The [[dusha-zapada|Soul of the West]]. This is [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]'s thought letter to [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria Northwood]]'s missing friend. The narrator herself admits that she is not interested in politics and does not read well by face, so much in history remains her guess. The contents of the letter received by her father, she does not name.

## Background
In the summer of 1774, the Empire changed rapidly. The Crown was born to the Northwoods, Shayland to the Origins, and the Bear Fang and the First Winter were pulled by rails. At the first dinner Count Damir Reis himself said that the North will soon become many times stronger. The Litlands replied that the South should also be strengthened. The Count came to negotiate friendship with a long-time blood enemy. Rebecca's father, Lord Douglas, compared his visit to a wolf leader who came to the psarna and wags his tail.

Rebecca's father, Lord Douglas Litland, was Litland's representative in the House. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) He liked to ask for advice, and his main advisor was a second representative, Lord Brandon Coss. On the advice of Coss, Litlanda received guests not in the capital house, but in the country estate of Lacey. It lies ten miles from [[faunterra|Faunterra]]: a spacious house, twenty acres of fields, a grove with an ice stream and stables for thirty horses. My father did not know why it was better, but he did not argue.

## Arrival of Ambassadors
There were six ambassadors and they came on horseback. The Litlands also met them on horseback: Lord Douglas, Coss, Rebecca and the seven knights. Rebecca noticed that the meeting in the saddles is the custom of nomads, and the owners seemed to adjust to the guests. Of the guests, she remembered two: the Count Damir himself, a man with a body “like iron” and rotten through one teeth, and the thin blue-eyed warrior Moran of the Steppe Fire, the Count’s right hand. ([[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]]) Knight Gage later explained to her that Reis was ruled by two "iron hands": the right one by Count Damir, the left one by Shavan Moran. Some even said that Moran could kill the count and rule himself, but while Damir is alive, he is loyal to him.

## Moran and Rebecca
On the first evening, Moran grabbed Rebecca's hand and summoned her at dawn to the stables. In the morning, they raced through the meadow, grove and river to the apple garden. Rebecca picked up both apples, but Moran said that they managed to level, offered to divide them and cut a ripe apple on the fly. Then, pushing her to do something “special”, he put a spoiled apple on his top. Rebecca fired, and Moran caught an arrow on the fly. He said that she had “a free soul, as if you were from the West.”

Rebecca soon began to suspect that Moran was taking care of her in order to get to her father through her. Still, she kissed him and rode with him through the meadows until dawn. For this, her father locked her in a security room. Sitting locked up, she compiled a list of twenty-four monasteries of Ulyana Sad, where Gloria could be.

## Letter
On the day of the arrival of the ambassadors from the capital jumped courier with a letter (this was noticed by the shavana). The next day, after the morning race, Rebecca burst into the office, where his father and Coss were conferring before the dinner, and saw his father cover one paper with another. Under the palm of his hand was seen the edges of the map, under which, apparently, the text was going. Through the door, she heard her father say, “It changes everything. “We have to tell the Duke.” Coss replied, "But there's something to talk about with the savages." Since that day, the Litlens have held out with ambassadors from a position of strength: “Do you want to say, gentlemen of the Shavans? Tell me, we are listening.”

## Negotiations
Coss called his tactics a "wait-and-see strategy." For several days the Litlens fed and watered the guests, spoke friendly speeches, but did not promise anything. Finally, the ambassadors made a concession. They proposed to lay through the Shepherds of the Meadow and Flight to the west coast a road under the constant protection of count's troops, and Litland could put his own forts on it. Under it, the Lithuanian merchants would trade with the North bypassing the Crown and the South Road for a negligible fee. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) Coss found the offer tempting, but added, "that's different, better." The Litlands sent pigeons to Duke Willas in Bassifor and waited for an answer.

Rebecca asked if peace with a blood enemy was not in itself a value. Coss replied that after decades of raids, Litland wanted a refund and to show what he was now setting the rules. Rebecca asked her father for half of his men to look for Gloria, but was refused.

When she was released, Count Damir and Moran recalled her to the side and asked about the letter. Damir said the Litlands are serving their right hand and the left hand is holding the blade. Rebecca honestly replied that she knew nothing. Before sunset, two pigeons flew to Lacey with the Duke's answer. At night, his father sent eight pigeons, two to each of the four fortresses on the fords of the sacred Holliwell River. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) Rebecca realized that Litland was preparing for war.

## Dinner
The next evening, Koss began his health "for our future, illuminated by the warmth of friendship." Count Damir banged his fist on the table and demanded a direct answer. Coss then announced that the offer was not generous enough and the House of Litland wished to take possession of half of the Shepherd's Meadows. Rebecca wrote that it would be better if he poured a hot goulash on the guest's head: since the time of [[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]] Luga was considered a common possession, and this was "as holy a truth as that the gods created the world."

When the count began to rise, Moran shouted:
> "Rebecca, what was in the letter?"

At that moment, she understood everything: what the northerners bought her uncle, why Coss put forward obviously offensive conditions and why there are a dozen knights at the table. Moran read it on her face. Rebecca shouted "Run!"

Under the countertop at the place of Coss was attached a raised crossbow on a rotary axis, which can be imperceptibly aimed at the stomach of any guest. Count Damir snatched the dagger and immediately fell on his face on a tablecloth with a bolt in his stomach. The Shavans were confused for a second, and the knights of Litland were already standing with naked blades. In the battle of seven Shavans and a dozen knights for a few breaths fifteen people died killed or wounded. There was one Moran on his feet. He walked across the table to Rebecca, knocked over the knight Gage, and jumped out the window. Coss took aim at him, but Rebecca hit a crossbow from the bottom, and the bolt went to the sky. Then she hit Kossa's knee with her shoe.

The number of Shavans at Rebecca diverges: upon arrival there are six, and in the fight she has seven.

## Aftermath
### Immediately after the ambush
Gage died that evening, Count Damir - two days later: the doctors could not do anything about the wound in the stomach. The pursuit of Moran lasted for several days, his father sent out pigeons with orders to intercept him, but the fugitive left. It was said that Moran killed five Litlandes in the fight. Rebecca admitted that without the shot, Coss Litlanda would have lost the fight and her father might have died. Coss himself went down with a cracked kneecap. The father stopped talking to his daughter, believing that she thwarted the plan. The Duke of Willas ordered to accept the "other proposal" from the letter.

Rebecca unraveled Coss's plan: to make notoriously insane demands, to wait for the savages to grab their daggers, to kill them at the table and leave Reis without both leaders. Then the county would be plunged into turmoil for several years, and the Origin Marines might finish off the remains. She ended her story with the words that her beloved father acted “as a complete bastard.”

### War in the West
Moran took power in Reis and vowed revenge. Soon, two of his ambassadors came to the court of [[adrian|Adrian]], who told the rulers of the West to come in person. They accused the emperor of breaking Julian's law and giving common land to "his minions the Litlands." According to them, Count Damir was "sinisterly killed by the Litlands with the emperor's permission. He was shot dead at the dinner table.” The ambassadors stated that the Litlands had embezzled the crossings through Holliwell, and the Duke had a letter signed by the emperor. Adrian replied that he knew nothing about it. Then one of the ambassadors stabbed the ruler of the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]], [[graf-rantigar|Count Rantigar]], with a spur, who had just assured the Crown of his loyalty. Adrian summoned the killer to fight with swords, cut his wrist and finished off the Eternal Hilt, and let the two surviving Westerners go with the news to Moran: to break the West, he does not need the Rings.

Soon the Chavans of Reis captured the crossings through Holliwell and invaded [[litlend|Litland]]. Moran defeated the Litlanders three times, burned down seven cities, and approached [[melorandzh|Melorange]]. In December 1774, Adrian and Prince Hector defeated his horde at the walls of the city. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) Later, one of the Shavans reminded Moran that from the ambush of the Litlands, he returned alone, and Rebecca saved his life.

### The Secret of Fake Letters
Adrian later admitted that [[mark-voron|Mark]] had predicted him exactly such a provocation. The Litlands want fords across Holliwell. Someone in the name of the emperor gives them fords with a false lazy letter, the Litland take them without hesitation, and the Westerners rush into battle, believing that the emperor covers the capture. Adrian himself then suspected Origin, but later began to attribute the fake letter and the conflict with the West to a third force. Mark, telling his colleagues the course of events, said that the letter of complaint “was fabricated by someone”, and Adrian did not even see it in his eyes. Later, speaking with Grandfather, he took her to the moves of the Master of the Rings: he intervened in the party "with a fake lazy letter that threw the Lithuanians with Shavans." Rebecca does not name the content of the letter received in Lacey, but the picture converges: there was a map and a "other proposal" for which the Litlands rejected the world.
