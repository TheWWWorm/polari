## Source
About the tournament is known from the story of the merchant [[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]. At the end of May 1774, while driving around the marble quarry in the hilly part of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], he told his guard Joaquin Yves Hanna from boredom that he had seen a knightly tournament in [[aleridan|Aleridan]] last summer. ([[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]]) Joaquin listened so enthusiastically that he almost dropped the reins.

## Tournament device
The tournament was organized with a real scope, "like all the plans of the Duke of Almer." He was notified in the winter. The champion was given a huge prize of six thousand ephesas, and the second place, as well as the winners among crossbowmen and horse archers, was given three thousand. The main draw was the Damon the Great Arena, one of the largest in the Empire: its stands would have accommodated the entire population of a small town like [[smolden|Smolden]], and yet on the days of the tournament they were beaten to death.

Battles followed the northern rules: if after three broken copies, both opponents remained in the saddle, they were in a hurry and continued the fight on swords. Many spectators, and Harmon among them, preferred foot fights to horse-drawn error: the outcome of a horse attack was decided in a single moment, and sword fight lasted sometimes up to five minutes and showed the skill of the fighters.

## First day
On the first day on the list came less noble warriors - poor and young knights. Their armor and support were modest, but the crowd greeted them with a special excitement: the victories of the lords were taken for granted, and the valour of the poor warriors was admired. The public’s favorites are two.

Sir Karl the Handsome of the Duke of Almer's Guard fought with bold enthusiasm, on the verge of recklessness. He kept the spear raised to the sky and lowered it only before the collision, so that the enemy could not guess where the blow would go. Charles hurried the four knights from the first blow and only once brought the matter to the swords. After each victory, he took off his helmet and drove a circle around the arena, and his thick red hair caused delight to the audience.

Ser Lauren of [[nadezhda|Hope]], a middle-sized bald warrior in a gray coat, oiled mail and with a brown shield, did not look like a noticeable figure. ([[loren|Sir Loren]]) But in the fight, he seemed to be carved from a granite block: not a single stroke of the spear did not make him shake. On swords he did not resort to fints and rounds, but methodically pressed the enemy, knocked him down and put a blade to the observation gap of his helmet. Carl and Lauren were the winners of the day.

## Second day
On the second day, the originals left the arena. Powerful horses were painted under colorful hooves, gilded helmets depicted tiger snouts, bears, wild boars and eagles, wrists and shields were covered with family coats of arms. Of the thirteen Great Houses, eleven sent knights to the tournament; there were no only Shimmers and Origins. Each knight traveled with a bannerman, under fanfare, and heralds twice proclaimed his name and title.

The winner of the day was Ser Adamar of the Yanmei family, the cousin of the Lord [[adrian|Adrian]]. On his helmet sparkled three golden drops - a symbol of the Merciful Mother, and on the shield crossed the imperial pen and sword. Tall and stately, he beat with a spear exactly in the center of the enemy's shield - such a hit is considered the most honorable and the least dangerous. Before him could not resist neither a hefty baron from the West, nor a gray Lithuanian warlord, nor the captain of the Cauzur Guard, who twice won in previous tournaments. ([[lazurnaya-gvardiya|Azure Guard]])

He was not inferior to the Fang Knight of Northwood, so nicknamed for the black helmet in the form of an open bear mouth with four silver teeth. Like Adamar, he defeated five opponents and became the second winner of the day. The crowd expected them to meet on the third day.

## Third day
According to the rules, on the last day, the original winners fought with the winners from the unknown knights, and then the final followed. Sir Adamar lived up to expectations, overturning Karl the Handsome with the first blow. Sir Lauren and the Fang Knight broke two spears without success. The Northwooder, weaker on swords, rushed into the third attack with a furious speed; from his blow, Loren's shield shattered into chips, but he miraculously stayed in the saddle, and his own spear knocked the enemy out of the saddle under the weight of the patch.

The Duke of Almer stretched the wait "by all the rules of the theatrical scene", giving most of the day to competitions of crossbowmen and horse archers, and the final took place only before sunset. Sir Adamar drove up to the balcony of the nobles and dedicated the battle to the Duke's daughter; Sir Lauren went silently to the position. Three horse-drawn skirmishes did not knock Lauren out of the saddle, though Adamar struck harder and more accurately, and the stands shouted, "Persevering Lauren!" Loren took speed on his swords, and heavy lats fettered Adamar. After knocking off his helmet, Lauren lowered his sword and allowed the enemy to lift it. Adamar soon overthrew Lauren and also generously retreated.

> "Get up, sire Lauren. I pay you back your debt.”

The fight lasted another ten minutes. Finally, the tired Adamar, making a fall, lost his balance; Lauren hit him in the shoulder with a shield, knocked him down, and put a blade to his pick-up.

## Devotion to Alanis
After winning, Sir Lauren made a circle of honor and stopped in front of the balcony of the nobility, dedicating his victory to the "beautiful Lady Alanis". Sharp-tongued [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] with a cunning smile noticed that she had not heard such words from him before the fight. Lauren, unabashed, replied that the true knight served the lady by deeds accomplished, not promises. The young lady smiled and threw him a flower, and the duke invited the champion to his table for a gala dinner.

## Value
Joaquin was delighted that the tournament was won by a "low-born warrior". Harmon clarified that Lauren was a knight, but not a lord, and agreed that "the nobility of the heart is more important than the nobility of the blood", as in the Song of Teresa. Then the merchant said that he saw Alanis from twenty yards and that she was “much better than the icon” of Agatha. According to him, it was dedicated to the fights of the count and ducal sons, military commanders and guards captains, and poor sir Lauren, it is true, fell asleep at the ducal table among this brotherhood. Joaquin kept in his pocket a four-fold page from The [[golos-korony|Voice of the Crown]] and asked about Alanis with greedy interest.

## Fate of participants
- Sir Adamar Suzanne Elizabeth of Yangmei, second heir to the throne, died on the hunt in May 1774; Mira initially suspected him of attempting to kill himself.
- The fanged Knight is Craig Northwood, "an Empire-wide knight" who "won tournaments more than once"; he later fought on the side of Origin (see Craig Northwood). ([[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]])
- About Aiden as the organizer of tournaments was remembered later: he regularly held tournaments and maneuvers so that his knights did not relax.

## Contradictions
In Harmon's account, the Fang Knight is called "the first son of the Earl of Northwood", as elsewhere in the cycle: Craig is the eldest of the three sons of the Earl of Elias and the heir to the county. In the early media there was a “second son”.
