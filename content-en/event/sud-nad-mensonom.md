## Background
Emperor [[adrian]] was believed to have died in December 1774 in a train crash on the Back; fishermen claimed to have seen a jester hit him in the back with a spark dagger. ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]]) In May 1775, a Lady in Darkness delegation arriving at [[marengo|Marengo]] handed the living Manson to the Crown; Lord Emmon Darklin recounted how the jester was found and arrested. ([[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]]) On the train on the way to the capital, Erwin offered a joke deal: doctors recognize him as insane, according to the commandments of Pramatery Yumin, he will be spared responsibility, and in return he will reveal the truth about the death of the lord. Manson suspected a catch - "My brother did this" - and, recalling Erwin's words about the "battle of charisma against calculation", threw away the overlaid beard: "No, wolves. I will go to court.” If anyone believes that he killed Adrian by his own will, "let him try to prove it." At the opening of the House on 10 May, [[minerva|Minerva]] called Manson's act vassal treason and murder and suggested that he be tried by law; the lords raised blue flags "without much inspiration", as most did not even know the jester was alive. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) The trial was decided to be held in the House building; on the train, someone planted a note to Erwin "There should be no court".

## Proceedings
**The situation.** Residents of the capital demanded publicity - and received it. The nobles filled the audience balconies of the meeting room, the burghers were not allowed to the Chamber closer than a hundred yards, but the heralds notified them about each event, and the "Voice of the Crown" and the "waves" stations carried the news further. The charge read: “Manson Louise Violetta of the lineage of Yanmei the Merciful is accused of murdering Adrian Ingrid Elizabeth… at dawn on the twenty-second of December last year near Beck Castle.”

**First meeting (17 May).** The eight-judge trial was presided over by [[albert-kantor|Albert Cantor]], and was conducted by first secretary Dorian Amber. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) The plaintiff was the Crown, and the accuser was the new head of protection, [[mark-voron|Mark]], appointed by Origin. Manson was accused of murdering the lord, vassal treason and fleeing justice. He turned down a defender, but King Shimmery [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] volunteered as an adviser. The jester clarifies; the court examines his "moral appearance", and the footman Vimas and Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] show that Manson once kissed the footman. The court also examined the cartoons of the jester, where each courtier received a nickname: the judge considered the humor of the accused “gross and uncultured”. The main witnesses were four Almeran fishermen from Kosiy Yar - Jacob, Smith, Larsen and Beard, for whom Baron Bonegan vouched (the baron himself, listing them, forgot Larsen); when identifying by portraits, which was arranged by Francis-Ilian, they did not recognize the portrait of Adrian himself and chose the portrait of the unnamed nobleman in the uniform; questions about the joker of their coat.

**Second meeting (21 May).** Kair [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]], a doctor and a pathologist of the protection testified to the body found. Mark demanded to repeat the identification in natural light - remove the curtains, put out the spark lamps and put portraits at the window - and this time the fishermen coped. Mark then told each of them, while the others turned away, to poke a wooden mannequin in the back in the guard uniform as had been struck; all four marks lay within three inches. So the prosecution proved that the fishermen really saw the stabbing, and then identified the Eternal Hilt. Francis-Ilian put forward a version of the “second killer” and demanded that Manson be acquitted for lack of corpus delicti; Amber confirmed that Manson only took the vassal oath to Thelurian (May 5, 1744) and the military oath to the naval officer (April 8, 1746), which became invalid in 1755 along with the admiral way; Lord Douglas Litland recalled that after the coronation of Adrian Aiden Almer, he did not allow a jester to swear an oath ("This scumbag should not swear an oath!"), but Mark the rouge. When the defense did not have a list of witnesses and the judge was going to leave for sentencing, Minerva intervened, threatened to dissolve the panel and gave the defense a day to prepare.

**Third meeting (25 May).** Minister of Railways Liam Schelier claimed that the bridge collapsed due to frozen water at the junctions; ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]]) Francis-Ilianus revealed contradictions in his version, but on Mark's objection, the court found the issue of the bridge irrelevant. The defense then summoned survivors of the crash of Sir [[sanders|Sanders]] and the aide-de-camp Forlemey, who had spoken about Manson's strange behavior the day before; a joke with tears embraced Forlemey, who had served with him for sixteen years. ([[forlemej|Forlemay]]) Francis-Ilian summoned Erwin, who admitted that in December [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] had offered him a plan to destroy Adrian, whom he considered dishonest. Mark expressed suspicion that the fishermen's testimony was prepared by the people of Earl of Arrowback and Baron Bonegan; according to the [[golos-korony|Voice of the Crown]], Erwin's testimony indicated the involvement of the people of Arrowback and Alanis Almer in the collapse of the bridge. Minerva ordered the arrest of Baron Bonegan and the fishermen. The newspaper reported that Manson's blow is proven and the joke awaits sentencing.

**Fourth meeting (29 May).** [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] announced that Manson was acting under the rule of Ulyanina Dust, which the Puppeteer had experienced on him at a summer ball when the jester "thrown on a poor footman". ([[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) Erwin admitted that he had ordered the bishop's letter to be intercepted and learned from him that Dust, "having the shape of an apple", was at [[gallard-almera|Gallard]]'s Priory; after which the jester began to commit frightening acts, Manson received it in the Priory's Palace. The Lords demanded, “Let’s joke!” Then Manson, insulting the lords with "stupid seats", blurted out: "I am guilty! Yeah, damn it, guilty! I betrayed Adrian!" He showed the hall a biting forearm and admitted that he took Dust “both at the ball in the summer and in the winter in the palace of the saint,” and was guilty of not finding the willpower to take the blow away and then cowardly disappeared. Then he pulled up his cap, called the tambourines, and with a terrible smile announced:
> “Adrian is alive!”

The chamber was frozen, and the jester laughed, calling it "the best joke" - his and the lord's for a couple. When questioned by the judge, he explained that the spark discharge only reduced Adrian's leg, and he took the help of the jester "from despair"; holding on to the wreckage, they sailed downstream, put the body of Captain Grace instead of the lord, putting Hilt on him, and Adrian himself disappeared. He praised the joke and a certain doctor who “did not fail: I understood how to play”; who exactly he meant, from his words it is unclear.

## Aftermath
The revelation shook the House. Erwin demanded an immediate vote: Adrian was stripped of all rights to the throne (Minerva voted against, stating that the lord was Adrian), but the election of the new lord fell through when Erwin interrupted the news of trouble with Sister Iona. Later, on the proposal of Francis-Ilianus, the vote was postponed until the victory over the Puppeteer, and Minerva became a temporary ruler. Manson was found innocent and released by a court decision; Minerva later took him as a court jester. Minerva believed that this “hopeless trial” was a triumphant return to politics for Francis-Ilian.

Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collector’s edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark says that for the sake of one remark, she spent four hours in the court archive. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) There she found a decree that acquitted Manson in three ways, and in addition the imperial pardon. The joker's wife, [[karen|Karen]], also found out about the trial. In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], she recounts that she thought Manson was dead for twenty years "until she read an article in The Voice of the Crown" saying he was being tried for murder.
