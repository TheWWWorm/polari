## Background
The great House [[dom-oridzhin|Origin]] was the noblest and one of the poorest in the North. Count Wittor Shayland was, according to [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], "not high-born and damn rich." ([[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]) His grandfather was a merchant, his father a banker, he himself did not leave the banking business. The two houses date back to at least August 1756: then, after the victory over the sunsets, the northerners, led by Desmond, were camped at Waymar, and the Shaylands honored them (flashback "The Price of Fear").

The marriage allowed the Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]] to receive money, and the Shaylands to be related to the first house of the North. Erwin considered the union humiliating and believed that the Great House Origin "lost another battle with poverty and pays tribute to the winner." In a conversation with his father, he stressed that the groom is a “banker Shayland” from the ninth kind of Pramati. Desmond replied coldly that he had allowed the marriage and that said it all.

## Redemption
The amount of redemption in the books is called differently.
- In March 1774, Desmond informed Erwin that the first cost of expanding the Origins' tomb under the Cathedral of Agatha the Radiant would be about thirty thousand Hilt. Erwin immediately realized that the money was taken from the ransom.
- The merchant [[kharmon|Harmon]] decided in May 1774 that the Count had to borrow thirty thousand Hilt, and that he was selling the Light Sphere to pay off his debt. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) According to Harmon, the ransom “exceeded the cost of the Sacred Subject.”
- Treasurer [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] in August 1774 named Erwin exact terms. Shayland paid twenty thousand gold coins at once and pledged to pay ten thousand a year for the next ten years, a total of one hundred and twenty thousand ephes. Erwin called it a monstrous sum: a rare emperor paid such a ransom to the bride’s family.
- Vittor himself told Iona in the spring of 1775 that Desmond had asked for "an unworldly ransom of a hundred thousand ephesas," and so he had to sell the Sphere.

According to Robert's report, the first twenty thousand diverged as follows:
- Two thousand and two hundred Hilt went to the device of the wedding;
- And Iona took a thousand and a half with her to Shayland, to give to the poor, and to the hospitals.
- One thousand two hundred Duchess [[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia]] spent on the theater in Lilliday;
- six hundred received a bishop for frescoes for the Rock Chapel;
- The remaining fourteen and a half thousand and twelve thousand from the treasury Desmond paid the workshops a deposit for the underground tomb.

## Training
On March 22, 1774, Erwin returned from the capital to the First Winter, when a week remained before the wedding. The castle was preparing for the holiday: peasant carts with cheeses, sausages, pickles, barrels of wine and orja entered the courtyard. The maids hung ribbons and flowers on the balconies and galleries, trial jokes exploded in the sky. Duchess Sofia turned a small refectory, where servants usually ate, into a music salon: hung tapestries and rehearsed with not very well-coordinated musicians. We were going to put the wedding party in the yard. The splendor of the preparations unpleasantly struck Erwin: to celebrate so violently, in his opinion, meant to flaunt his own shame.

Iona was waiting for five hundred guests, and maybe a thousand. She joked that the first week will only say hello, and the second only to say goodbye. Her brother taught her to greet guests coldly: “You are the Princess of the North. All these guests are not worthy of you.” And the groom called "the leader of those who are not worthy of you."

## Feelings of the Bride
In the greenhouse of the castle, Iona spoke frankly to Erwin. She admitted that she was happy to leave the First Winter, although she had never been to Shayland. There, she thought, would be a forest and a large river with ships, and people would laugh, cry, and stomp their feet with resentment rather than grab their swords.
> “I don’t know if I love the county. I'm happy, but I hardly love him. He’s a funny creature I can get used to.”

## Guests
Iona was waiting for the wedding of five hundred guests, and maybe the whole North. In the First Winter came the old Count [[elias-nortvud|Elias Northwood]] with his sons. Countess [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil]] refused to go and called the bride a "malyaholic princess", which the Origins "finally married". Instead of a wedding, she took [[minerva|Minerva]] to the capital. Her daughter [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]] wasn't at the wedding either. Later, Mira realized: otherwise, Vittor and Iona would have recognized the switch when they met her at a summer ball under the name Gloria. Elias brought to the wedding the news of the attempt on Minerva Stagforth, and Erwin immediately appreciated its political importance.

Sybil, refusing to go, told Minerva that the sister-in-law was waiting "three weeks for a wakeful drink and a baby princess in addition."

Among the guests were mechanic Luis Maria, later a member of the exploit, and, according to Mira, both Shayland's brothers, including [[martin-shejland|Martin]]. ([[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]]; [[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]])

## Celebrations
Iona appeared to the common people only on the day of the engagement. She drove in an open carriage from the castle to the cathedral and back, and thousands of townspeople crowded along the streets early in the morning to see the Northern Princess in a wedding dress. She did not have time for the townspeople: she received five hundred noble guests. To ensure that the mob did not feel deprived, the Duke exhibited two Sacred Subjects in the Cathedral of Agatha the Radiant. ([[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]]) Erwin later said that the First Winter did everything to blind guests and hide their misery.

## After the wedding
### The first months
As early as May 9, 1774 (Harmon's head is dated May 8–9), the couple lived in [[uejmar|Waymar]], where Harmon caught them at lunch. Vittor adored his wife: it was visible from every glance thrown at her by stealth, by how carefully he touched her elbow. Iona treated her husband with bitter chocolate, and he frowned. The Count kept the deal with the Sphere secret, first of all, from the “beloved noble wife”: it was considered a disgrace to sell part of the family estate. To his men, Harmon explained that Lady Iona needed the money for the hospital.

On the same day, May 9, the Countess Sybil at the emperor’s audience explained why she came with the report, and not the Minerva suzerain: Count Vittor is allegedly in the First Winter at wedding celebrations. This is at odds with Harmon's testimony that the count of the house in Waymar, therefore, seems to have been just a pretext.

In the summer of 1774, at a ball in Faunterra, Emperor [[adrian|Adrian]] personally congratulated the Shaylands on their wedding.

Barber Harry Hogue recalled that the first month after arriving, “such sweetness reigned” between the spouses that everyone was amazed. ([[garri-khog|Harry Hogg]]) Then Iona, he said, was getting colder, “like she pretended at first and then she got tired of playing.”

### Doubts
In the fall of 1774, Minerva, who was visiting Waymar, noticed that Iona, who had married in March, was still wearing narrow dresses and drinking wine. Mira remembered about the drug that girls drink in order not to get pregnant, and suspected that marriage is a “fancy”: manipulation on the one hand, on the other or on both. Perhaps, she reasoned, Iona is Vittor's insurance against Erwin's victory, and perhaps it is Iona who controls her husband. In the spring of 1775, Harry Hogue also said that “more than a year has passed, but there is no child as there is no one.”

Mira suspected something else. The stone sickness struck Desmond shortly after the wedding, which was attended by both of Shayland's brothers. She wondered if Shayland was involved. The text does not confirm this suspicion.

### Disputes and reconciliation
After the Origin rebellion, Vittor maneuvered between Erwin and the emperor. The couple quarreled when Iona locked Martin in the lower circle of the dungeon and released Minerva without her husband's knowledge. After learning of the defeat at Pixie, the count began to prepare detachments to help Adrian, and Iona in the heat asked if he had ordered Martin to kill. When Vittor came out of the bedroom, he said, “I swear by my mother Vivian.” In the morning news of Erwin's victories came, and her husband met her with hugs and laughter, calling her "Agatha the Radiant". Iona refused to secretly test him with Martin's elixir. The count seemed to her a soft, easy man, "for that I loved him." Her closeness with her husband was pleasant, but it always seemed strange. Soon Iona went to his brother in the capital. In the winter, Vittor sent her a seven-page letter about the life of Waymar, and Iona wept over him: her husband seemed to her the best person in the world. In the spring of 1775, Iona returned to Waymar. When she found out about the sold Sphere, she reproached her husband, and then he referred to the exorbitant ransom. Trying to be a good wife, she became submissive, and he humiliated her alone and called her “ice” and “northern stuff”.

### Gap
In May 1775, Iona realized that her husband was the Puppeteer, sending letters to Ulyanina Dust, and staged a [[perevorot-v-uejmare|coup in Waymar]]. ([[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) After the defeat, she remained a prisoner of her husband for almost six months. She was kept in a cage with the sign "She betrayed her husband", and [[laura-farvej|Laura Farway]] accused her of breaking her vow. When Iona was told by Ulyanina Dust to rewrite the fake confession, the count told her that she was "no longer worthy" of his love, but he did not harbor hatred for her and maybe someday he would forgive. Victor himself said:
> “Unfortunately, my darling broke the bonds of oath when she betrayed me. I'll let her see the dead bodies of her relatives, and then I'll let her go to Star. And as a widower, I can think about remarriage.”

When Joaquin suggested that Iona bewitched Vittor, Martin picked up: “Long ago, still at the wedding!”

At the end of 1775, Vittor was frozen in [[chrevo|the Womb]]. Iona decided that his fate should be decided by the Supreme Court. The day after the battle, Vittor told Adrian about his ex-wife.

## In "Fantasies"
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], a possible future cycle, Erwin refers to her sister as Countess Shayland and the empress's prime vassal. Adrian speaks about the powerlessness in Shayland: the only heiress of the title for personal reasons does not enter into law. A womb with a count awaits a church trial in the Holy See. ([[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]]) In the summer of 1776, Iona appeared to a man in silver cloth: “Hello, sweetheart. “Did you miss me?” She shot him with a crossbow. It turned out that it was not Vittor, but the spirit of Dalia Raven, who frightened the inhabitants of the First Winter. ([[dalliya-rejven|Dallia Raven]]) In the divination, Iona called the main desire: “I want to be loved.”

## Value
The wedding opens the cycle. The money raised by Vittor for the Light Sphere launched the storyline of Harmon and Joaquin (see Sale of the Light Sphere), and the marriage itself connected the house of the Puppeteer with the Origins. ([[lzhe-sfera]]) According to Vittor, it was the ransom that forced him to sell the Sphere. In May 1775, Iona in a letter to her brother described this story as a sacrifice of her husband for love. Erwin was at first amiable ("a story worthy of a poem"), but his inner voice, the Althessa Anxiety, immediately drew attention to the Sphere itself. Later, after the exposure of the Puppeteer, both Adrian and Harmon wondered for what purpose the Count actually sold the Item.

## Trivia
- Erwin promised himself that his “wedding gift” to Iona would be to rid the First Winter of poverty.
- Magda Labelin admitted that the marriage upset her: she considered herself smarter, richer and more noble than Iona and did not understand why she married the ruler of the land, receives a huge ransom “and even spins her nose.”
- Erwin called the underground tomb, which took the lion's share of the ransom, a "huge grave", and later grimly joked that it would soon be useful to him himself if he did not find money for the army.
- Explaining to Harmon why he was selling the Sphere, Vittor embarrassedly referred to Lady Iona: "We're doing something for love. Sometimes it costs a lot.”
