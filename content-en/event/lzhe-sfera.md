## Background

In April 1774, a merchant named Harmon Paulo Roger was waiting for a challenge to [[uejmar|Waymar]]. ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]; [[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]) There Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] showed him the Light Sphere of the seventeenth Gift of the Gods. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) The Count explained that selling the Item is not a sin: what is given, that is what you own. But to buy a shrine means to try to buy the favor of the gods for a coin, so buyers keep such transactions secret. He instructed to sell the Sphere not cheaper than thirty thousand ephesas for a tenth of the proceeds and gave two months for this. According to the Earl, the Duke of Labelin had bought another Object from a Western Lord a year earlier for thirty-six thousand, and the eighty-seven Objects in the possession of the hated Origins, against his sixty, did not give the Duke peace.

When asked why he was selling The Item, the Earl hinted at his love for [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]], and Harmon concluded that the money was needed for the bride's ransom. The Count named four possible buyers, among them the Marquis Hedwig of Faunterra, who had purchased Items for the Crown, Baron Darryl, and the Salttown shipbuilder Hobart-Synthal. ([[gobart-sintal|Gobart-Sintal]]) He gave a letter of trust to the merchant, in which the goods themselves were not mentioned. To his men, Harmon lied as if he was selling county land, and promised each of them three golds, and Louise and Joaquin more.

## Developments

**Soltown.** Harmon decided to start with Hobart-Synthal: the merchant is still not as terrible as the baron, about whom the fame of a cruel man went. The Soltown negotiant himself guessed that he was offered the Sacred Object, and reached thirty-two thousand, but Harmon was never able to part with the Object. He later admitted to himself that the Sphere gave him the feeling that he was "higher, better, smarter than other people."

**Baron Darryl.** In early June, Harmon requested forty-one thousand from Baron Hugo Darryl, vassal to the Duke Maurice [[labelin|Labelin]]. ([[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]]) He explained that the new product is five thousand more expensive than the previous one, because it is “capable of some wonderful interaction with the owner”, and refused to give the name of his lord. All he said was that he was one of the thirteen great land-lords. The sphere Harmon prudently left in the hotel. The Baron promised to send a letter when the Duke could inspect the goods.

**Kidnapping.** In mid-June, while Harmon was waiting for a letter, unknown people stole him from the hotel at night, tortured and took the Sphere, and the merchant himself was thrown into a stone bag under the ruins of a fortress ten miles north of the city. During the interrogation, Harmon posted everything: "I'm not a knight, I'm not a warrior, I can't stand the pain." [[polli|Polly]] tracked down the kidnappers. [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], Snip, and Doxet freed the host, and Harmon found the Sphere in a chest in the captain's quarters of the barracks. ([[snajp|Snipe]]; [[doksett|Doxett]]) According to the jailer, she was first taken somewhere and only returned the day before. Later, looking at it in the light, Harmon noticed two tiny transparent sticks: the inner ring was held on an axis. It was a fake.

**Deal.** Harmon understood that if he told the truth, he would be left without the Sphere, without money and with the wrath of the Duke around his neck. So he decided to sell a copy. On 27 June, the duke, accompanied by the baron and the secretary, received him; for this meeting, the duke had twice travelled three hundred miles by train from the capital, having had to return empty-handed the first time. The secretary read a description from the registry: a smaller ring is held inside in a larger "divine way". The Duke noticed the axle rods. Baron Darryl countered that the description of the ring should hang in the air, like a light in the Object "[[vechnoe-techenie|Eternal Flow]]" in the Cathedral of Agatha the Radiant in the First Winter. The Duke shrugged off: there are seventy Objects in his possession, and "no one hangs anywhere." Confident that the Objects were indestructible, he hurled the Sphere across the room against the wall. It bounced off the whole thing, and the fake was found to be genuine. The turning of the rings fascinated the lords. For a week's wait and for the goods were first offered to the "shipman from Soltown", the Duke dropped five thousand. The Baron threatened to send a solonina from Harmon and a letter about the deal was not to the Earl of Shayland, but to Iona or her brother: the Labelins, it turns out, already knew whose product it was. The originals, he said, know a "very reliable way" to end the marriage. After that, Harmon "crushed" agreed to thirty-six thousand, secretly barely holding back the delight.

At the bank, the baron wrote a promissory note for 36,000 ephemera in the name of Harmon: neither Shayland nor Labelin wanted their names listed in bank books. The bill was sealed with the coat of arms of the Labelins, and the records were included in the current and archival books. Harmon transferred 32,400 to the Earl of Wittor through the Shayland Bank, 3,400 left on the bill in his name, and two hundred received a coin. In farewell, the baron said that the merchant disgusted him, “like your master.”

**Return of the original.** Doxet and Wihrenok saw a bald, shaggy man take suma out of the fortress. Polly noticed an overgrown country, which could leave the riders. Turning on him, Harmon reached the farm, and the innkeeper by signs identified in the "debtor" brother Ludwig from the Maximian monastery, which is two miles to the west. Louise, Doxett and Polly went there in exploration under the guise of pilgrims. Polly was the first to say that the villains are the monks themselves: on their faces "tension and hidden anxiety". Harmon wrote a letter on behalf of the Duke to the abbot Augustus Eliza Alexander, accusing the brethren of sacrilege and demanding the return of the original Object. Polly cut a copy of the dolphin seal on the bill. At night Joaquin, calling himself "Sir Desmond Iona Alanis of the Agatha family", handed over the letter in the purchased camellias of the Labelin guard. The abbot gave himself up: he was indignant without asking what the goods were about, although he was not named in the letter. Under threat of a search, the abbot promised to deliver the Item to where it is indicated. Harmon chose the barracks in the same ruined fortress.

On July 4, 1774, Brother Ludwig brought the Sphere. As soon as Harmon’s fingers touched the rings, he felt the “blessed warmth” and realized that the Object was genuine. Ludwig asked to return the “second product”, that is, a fake, and demanded a receipt from the “knight”. Joaquin clumsily deduced: "in the service of evo the Lordship of Labylin." The monks insisted on escorting the detachment to the palace. On the way, one of the brothers recognized Joaquin's horse, seen at the hotel on the night of the abduction. Harmon shouted "We have been uncovered!" and let the horse run up, and the monks chased after them. In the pursuit and ensuing quarrel, Doxet, Snipe, and Polly died. The vortex lost its fingers and then died from infection of the wound, Joaquin was seriously injured. ([[vikhor|Whirlwind]]) Harmon fled with the Object south.

## Aftermath

- A few days later, the Maximian monastery was burned by raiders. According to David's father, only three maids survived, a doctor, brother Andrew and brother Ludwig, who was lying in a hospital in Labelin with a broken bone. ([[otets-david|Father David]]) Ludwig showed David the letter of the “Duke,” and David recognized Harmon’s handwriting. The Duke of Labelin promised five hundred hilt for the head of each criminal. The entire Southern Way was “crazy”: knightly detachments combed through steppes and forests, peasants gathered militias. The raiders were never found. They were a group of northerners nicknamed Ice, who came from Shayland across the lake and followed the footsteps of Harmon, lagging behind for a day or two. He was seen on Smoky Distance, in Soltown and in Labelin.
- David explained to Harmon the plan of the monks: if the merchant disappeared with the Sphere, Count Vittor would have started to look for him, so the kidnappers expected in advance that his servants would save him and he would sell a copy. He was starved to death for the Duke to believe in kidnapping and torture. The monks themselves, he said, tried not to kill.
- Later, Labelin recognized the deception: the inner ring of the fake rotated on the axis and stopped after a few minutes. He decided that Harmon had conspired with the monks, and in January 1775, on the report of [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]], found out that the thief under the name "Horam" was hiding in [[shimmeri|Shimmery]]. The fake was kept in Grace. According to Labelin, it was collected from several small items that are not included in the registers. Baron Darryl considered himself personally deceived and pursued Harmon until the end of the cycle.
- Rumors of a purchase have leaked. In the autumn of 1774, the merchants of Soltown told [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] that Labelin had paid an "unknown merchant" for the Light Sphere forty thousand. Ervina was surprised that the duke-sophian was humiliated before the purchase, and even so that outsiders learned about it. Putting this together with David’s silence in the burned monastery and Iona’s letter about the monks-abductors, he realized who was involved in the theft.
- David recognized that the order to which the Maximian monks belonged (the [[sadovniki|Gardeners]]) was able to forge Objects. Erwin suggested that there were two fakes: one Ice took the Puppeteer, and therefore [[absolyut|the Absolute]] did not work.
- In June 1775, [[adrian|Adrian]], dealing with Harmon, noticed that the count had deliberately made a deal so that she insulted Labelin. He sent an aboriginal merchant who traded, instead of sending a noble vassal with a gift. Adrian suggested that Shayland was counting on Labelin's rejection or wanted the Sphere to go to the monks. The sphere, in his opinion, was not a weapon, but “something like a visual tube.” Adrian admitted that he has been fighting over the mystery for more than a month. Later that night, it seemed to Harmon that he understood the count's plan, but the lord no longer wanted to talk about it.
- Brother Ludwig survived and later appeared among the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] as [[salem|Salem]]'s assistant. In the spring of 1775, [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] recognized him as the monk who brought Harmon the Sphere.
- In the summer of 1775 Maurice Labelin decided to leave the Light Sphere to Vladyka as a wedding gift, judging that the emperor's hands cleansed any thing, and pardoned Harmon.
- Finally, [[navi|Navi]] revealed the idea: to sell the Sphere through an “inconspicuous merchant with poor security” offered to Count [[paul|Paul]]. He hoped that the Item would go hand in hand and sooner or later be with Navi, and then Paul will destroy it with Navi.
- [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] found out about the deal from Joaquin. Vittor explained that he had sold the Sphere for a ransom: [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] demanded a hundred thousand ephemera for his daughter.
