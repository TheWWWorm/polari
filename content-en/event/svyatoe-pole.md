## Background
The confrontation on the Holy Field is a failed "battle" between the northern army and the insurgent Snowdrops: there was no battle, because Bishop Minerva intervened at the last moment. In the spring of 1775, the peasant army of the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]], which had risen in the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], moved through the [[zemli-korony|Crownlands]] to the capital with a request for fair taxes. General Gore's squad tried to disperse them, but several rebels shot the parliamentarians with sparked self-fire, which they secretly brought the Labelin agent [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]].

At the military council, Lord Chancellor [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] made [[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]] interim commander-in-chief of the spark army. The place of battle with almost eighty thousand rebels chose the Holy Field - a lowland between two hilly ridges, from which the peasants could not run away with a fan. Northwood bears were placed at the front, behind the echelons stood the kairs, and the headquarters where Erwin was going to observe the "triumph" was smashed at the lowest point of the field. [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] implored his brother not to carry out the massacre. In secret, Erwin counted on another thing: he considered the spark weapons of the peasants to be the bait of Kuklovod and hoped to lure him to the field and capture his brigade led by [[paul|Paul]]. ([[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]])

### Military Council
At the council, General Gore asked for reinforcements to complete the suppression of the rebellion, and the Northwood brothers rushed to deal with the rebels themselves. Gore warned that the Snowdrops had a spark and needed to be careful with them, but Erwin sent his hull to rest. The officers were arguing about tactics. Craig Northwood demanded a frontal attack by a heavy cavalry. [[stetkhem|Statham]] suggested first striking from the rear, and when the peasants mix, hitting the front. Count Lilliday advised that archers should be led up the hills on their flanks and that rebels should be shot until they surrendered. To Iona's dismay, Erwin supported Craig, saying it was better to "quickly and triumphantly end the riot". He promised to find a place for the headquarters, from which “our triumph in all its glory” will be seen, and to stock up on an orge for the celebration. Northwood, he objected to only one thing: alone Tom did not organize the persecution, so the Kairs will follow.

### Erwin's plan
Alone, Erwin revealed to Iona his real plan. He himself admitted that the upcoming battle was "a little idiocy", but hoped that the Puppeteer would not suspect a trick: three months Erwin deliberately taught him to consider himself a fool. According to his calculations, the Northwood bears will run into the spark spears of the peasants and roll back, the cavalry of the First Winter will crash into their ranks, and the army will plunge into chaos. At this moment, the brigade with the Rings will hit the rear, on the defenseless headquarters in the very lowlands, to kill Erwin and Robert, and then move to Faunterra to finish off the rest of the Origins. But in the staff tent, she would find only convicts dressed in the front armor of the northerners. Erwin's best battalion, sheltered in the hills, would cover it with a thousand arrows, and the survivors would be taken alive to extort the real name of the Puppeteer. Only Iona and a couple of Erwin's closest helpers knew about the true plan.

## The Buckley Deal
The snowdrops were armed by Labelin agent [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]]. He showed up to their chiefs in tents, gave a "holy dozen" of sparked self-shooters, and promised another thousand self-shooters and a thousand copies if in two weeks they came to the Holy Field thirty miles north of Faunterra. According to him, there “the gods showed a miracle, sending people their first gift.”

The day before, Moger Buckley appeared to [[minerva|Minerva]] with a letter of recommendation from [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]. He admitted that he had armed Podsnezhniki according to the plan of [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]]: a sudden spark volley was to destroy thousands of kairs and prove that "not the sword, but the coin will rule the world" - after which the North would cut out all the peasants. Magda offered another way: the weapons will not be delivered, the peasants will run away, “only” ten or twenty thousand will die, and the Labelins will demand the displacement of Origin in the Chamber as an executioner of the innocent. To prevent the weapons from arriving, Buckley asked for ten ephemera per head, that is, “less than a million coins,” to save eighty thousand lives. Minerva told him to write him a deposit and promised the rest if the events went as he described. The text diverges in the amount of the deposit: under Buckley, Minerva dictates a bill for eighty thousand ephes, and Leila Talmir later says that she wrote out “only forty thousand.” After consulting with [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]], Minerva saw that the Labelins had calculated everything in advance. If Origin kills the peasants, he will become a "blood despot worse than Adrian," and if the peasants beat the kire, he will be disgraced. In addition, a punitive campaign in the Lands of the Crown demanded the permission of the Empress, which she did not give. Fraileen advised to warn the Lord Chancellor by letter, but Minerva decided that Erwin's words alone would not stop, and went herself.

## On the field
At dawn, an imperial escort carriage under Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] raced along the entire northern army already built for the attack. It was the first time I had seen an army of this size. West Statham, who asked about the purpose of her arrival, she replied that she appeared to prevent his sister-in-law from making a mistake. The Kairs did not dare to stop it, and through the Northwood Bears, the Guards broke through by force. Behind the strip of moats stood a solid wall of peasant penny-makers, mail among which was rare, and behind them stretched a shaft of vans and carts strewn with arrows. When Minerva came out of the carriage at the distance of the arrow, the peasants recognized the lady and greeted her with cries of “Glory to Minerva!”. Forward came the leaders of the rebellion led by Salem of Summerswith; he himself took the blame for the deaths of the parliamentarians and showed Minerva depreciated bills for requisitioned food. ([[salem|Salem of Summersweet]])

To speak on behalf of the citizens of the Crown volunteered dentist [[zub|Tooth]] from Lowville. He reduced everything to “honest taxes” and forgiveness of “poor people who trembled with fear.” Minerva noticed that in the retinue of the leaders of the peasants only a couple of people, and the rest are craftsmen from the cities. Salem, interrupting the Tooth, spoke of justice. The sparklers, who were going to beat the northerners, raked out the clean shut. The Marquis of Grace drove them away by the army, "as if we were blind." Unfair Salem called and that their blacksmith killed Baron Summerswith, and shots at the parliamentarians: with the general killed "boy of the originals", which Salem was especially sorry. The Snowdrops themselves were hoping for Buckley's promised spark, and this, according to Salem, is "already bad":
> “There is no need for sparks. We wanted peace.”

Together with Minerva, the new Minister of Taxes, Frank Morlin-May, and the Marquis of Graysend, the largest Lord of the South Road at court, arrived. Morlin-May announced a tax reform: Bourne is put on trial, a fixed annual tax is introduced in the Crownlands, paid through banks, and tax collectors are abolished. ([[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]]) Minerva promised triple compensation for food taken away and amnesty to all participants of the campaign, and the Marquis of Gracend publicly agreed. Only those who had shot themselves were punished. Minerva demanded to know whose hands held the weapon, and mentally begged Salem not to take the blame. A cavalry soldier, a former sergeant, and two philistines rushed to flee, and they were captured by the guards. The snowdrops themselves gave out the Tooth, "which was always for the war," and unanimously confirmed that Salem did not shoot. Fifth, the brewer Tramp, was not on the field, and Minerva announced that he would be caught and hanged. She believed that four “quite enough for a cautionary execution.” The murderer of Baron Summerswith, as demanded by the Marquis of Graceland, was awaiting trial, and Minerva promised to visit Summerswith next spring. The snowdrops threw spears and went home. Count Lilliday and Craig Northwood were ordered to withdraw. Craig, appointed the day before by the Crown general, was advised to disappear from her eyes "until it became the shortest service in history", and Lilliday was told:
> “The Crown did it on its own.”

## Aftermath
Erwin's trap for Kuklovod failed, and the peasants, deprived of spark weapons, never gave battle. Once the empress forgave the peasants, it became impossible to throw an army at them. The Cairos did not engage, the rear headquarters did not remain a defenseless bait, and Paul left the trap. Erwin sent mounted patrols in every direction but found no trace of the brigade where an army twenty thousand strong had just passed. Erwin's vassals condemned the act of "Mimi": according to the laws of the North, the peasant deserved severe punishment even for disrespecting the warrior. Close associates demanded to throw the "scared doll", although only two assistants knew about the main fault of Minerva, the trap broken. Mira herself slept all the way from St. Paul's, the first time since the news from Melorange was so strong. Minerva sent the chancellor a snowdrop with a ribbon: “Take your place in the world with dignity. You are not the Lord, my lord. Come to terms." A wounded Erwin summoned her close, and then decided to play the role of a villain: threatened to expel her people and put guards from a “special company” on the guard. According to rumors, the "special company" with Agathan ribbons in the loops was the revenge of Erwin Minerva for the fact that she was ahead of him on the Holy Field, and Erwin himself did not dispute this. Soon Minerva secretly fled to [[marengo|Marengo]].

- Morlin-May defined the new tax as “at the tithe level of average income.” The reform concerned only the Lands of the Crown: the taxes of the Southern Way remained under the jurisdiction of the Great Houses of Labelin and Origin.
