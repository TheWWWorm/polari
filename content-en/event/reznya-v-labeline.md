## Name
In the cycle, this victory is most often called the "Night Labelin": "the heroes of the Night Labelin", "the winner of the Night Labelin". It is also called the “victory of the Labelin”. The name “Labelin massacre” is descriptive: the text itself says that in one part of the city the soldiers slept peacefully, “while in another the massacre raged.”

## Design
The trap was set by [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]. Leaving the battlefield at [[bitva-pri-piksi|Pixie]], he left Colonel General Statham with a sealed package of orders. Already in the besieged Faunterra, Erwin described [[alanis-almera|Alanis]]' plan. The rest of the army had to flee and, pursued by Alexis, fled to Labelin. There, the kayras will descend into the catacombs and exchange clothes with the city's garbage, thieves and beggars. “Army of the homeless” in helmets and red and black raincoats will go north, and the petty-bourgeois will shout to sparks: “The Northerners are gone! Run away!" The soldiers of the Crown will celebrate the victory and drink, and their spears will remain unloaded, because Erwin did not leave a spark in the city. At night, the cayres will come out of the ground.

Robert Origin later called this part of the plan a "masquerade" and told his cousin, "The masquerade is successful."

## Developments
After the victory at Pixie, Silver Fox entered the Labelin to replenish supplies, charge the eyes of the copies and continue the chase. The city had no walls, kairs "many saw" running north, and Alexis allowed the regiments to spend the night in it rather than freezing in a damp field. Officers were accommodated in the homes of wealthy philistines, soldiers - in hospitals, barracks of the city guard, hotels and cathedrals. Imperial regiments were scattered throughout the city.

In the middle of the night, five battalions of northerners emerged from the catacombs, killed sentries and took control of the streets. Then they methodically destroyed the imperial parts one by one: in alleys, on the doorsteps of houses, in barracks and in hotel beds. Most of the soldiers died in their nightgowns, barely getting out of bed. If any unit managed to run out to the square and build, it was destroyed after a few minutes. The couriers were intercepted, so orders were not given, and some parts slept while others were cut out. Only two companies, who spent the night in the cathedral of Gloria the Intercessor, fought back, locked themselves in the temple and struck the alarm. Only then did the survivors rise to the alarm.

The army was already cut and unmanageable. Those who could, fled the city, others gave up. In the first hours, the northerners did not take prisoners, but by dawn, confident of victory, they showed mercy: eight and a half thousand Alexis soldiers were captured.

## Outcomes
From an army of fifty thousand shields and twelve thousand spark copies, Alexis brought out of the city only the remains of several regiments. Retreating to the south, he destroyed bridges across the Hanai and railroads, and this delayed the northerners for three weeks. Thousands of Northerners captured at Pixie were freed, among them the younger Northwood, Donald. The eldest, [[krejg-nortvud|Craig]], Alexis managed to take with him. For two days the northerners brought order to the city, and then moved south, to Faunterra, to help their duke: according to Robert, "with 12 baht. + Northwoods".

Robert Origin boldly reported the victory to the emperor in a letter:
> "In our hands, former lord, are eight and a half thousand of your quails."
He suggested that [[adrian|Adrian]] put on a hat and come bowing to Erwin in the palace, where “recently, it seems, you yourself lived.” For the Crown, it was the worst defeat since the First Horse War, for the Origins - the greatest triumph in their history.

The news reached the besieged in [[faunterra|Faunterra]] on pigeon tapes. [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] read them first, secretly. Robert wrote, "We lost 5 baht. at Pixie, but returned the hundredth at Labelin." Statham - that in two battles the army lost a third of the troops, but the enemy is defeated and you can come to the capital only on foot, in about twenty days.

## Discrepancies in the text
The number of surviving imperial regiments in the sources is different. According to Adrian's court, Lis led out of Labelin "incomplete four regiments". Statham's report and Erwin's words refer to "incomplete three" of the twelve.

## Aftermath
- The remnants of Alexis' army could not prevent the northerners from marching on the capital. By the end of December, there were twelve thousand kairs and eight thousand Northwood “bears” on the left bank of the Hanai.
- The Leed Wolves of [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]] prided themselves on "exceptional valor in the nighttime Labelin" and in pursuit of the Silver Fox and stacked three hundred trophy spark copies at Erwin's feet.
- Both of Northwood's brothers were captured at Pixie, so in Labelin their regiments were commanded by the Snow Count Lilliday; according to Erwin, it was to him that the "bears" were owed "unprecedented efficiency in that battle".
- [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] had nine thousand dead in the night Labelin.
- Half of the trophies of “Night Labelin” Erwin gave [[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]], although, according to him, he did not deserve a quarter.
- The Northerners, who received a spark discharge at Pixie and therefore did not participate in the victory in the night Labelin, were left without fame: two of them, for example, got the most boring watch at the beggar's gate in Faunterra.
- Becoming Empress, [[minerva|Minerva]] pointedly asked Alexis if he had not lost the “night Labelin” on purpose on the orders of Kukovod. The general, putting a spark dagger to his own heart, swore that he was fighting at the limit of his strength and was simply tricked. Minerva believed him.
- In 1775, when Adrian approached Faunterra, Minerva hoped that Alexis, "broken in the night Labelin", would advise him to enter the city in the dark, so as not to run into an ambush. And [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]], listing strong opponents, was the first to call General Statham, "the winner at the night Labelin."

## In "Fantasies"
In Fantasia, which the author calls the possible fates of the heroes, Robert Origin justified himself before the ghost of Damon, who died in a besieged palace: the campaign on Faunterra was delayed due to the difficulties of the winter offensive and the fatigue of the soldiers after the “double battle in Labelin”.
