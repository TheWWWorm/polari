The **Mill War**, or War of the Mills, was a campaign by Earl Rantigar of the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]] to the disputed lands of the Mills in 1773, a year before the start of the cycle. ([[graf-rantigar|Count Rantigar]]; [[melnitsy|The Mills]]) It is told mainly by [[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]], who fought as a mercenary in the Count's army.

## The course of war
According to the stories that [[kharmon|Harmon]] heard, Rantigar "had the dexterity and general talent" to gather a strong army of twelve thousand swords from diverse Western knights, militiamen and mercenaries. With him he crossed the Gulkiy ford and invaded the disputed Milllands. The Melnik barons did not unite immediately, and Rantigar took four castles one by one, and then won a difficult battle in the fields. However, the Melniks defended themselves desperately and stubbornly: they rebuffed from behind the fortress walls and flew from the fields with a light cavalry. Rantigar's army "came, but melted".

Joaquin arrived at the war from the South Road, when several battles had already been given, and spent only two months in the army. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) According to him, then “the worst began.” The millers avoided fighting, retreated and exhausted the enemy with constant crossings, burned crops, ambushed and destroyed foragers. The count's army starved and lost days in search of food, and the Millers, meanwhile, were gathering strength.

Both sides asked for help from the First Winter. The Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] helped the Melniks and sent them kairs. When the Melniks finally met Rantigar in the middle of the field, his men were exhausted, and the enemy had a superiority of a thousand horsemen and cairns. The Count weighed the odds and chose to negotiate. The troops of the day stood against each other, and then Rantigar's men laid down their arms and surrendered. The original knights of Melnik were left in captivity for ransom, and ordinary soldiers, like Joaquin, were released.

## Aftermath
The war cost Rantigar dearly. At the 1774 ball, Becca showed him to [[minerva|Mira]] as "the same Count Rantigar" who had started a great war in the West and "lost with a deafening crash". ([[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) He lost more than a thousand warriors and “dark money”, and the final battle was not at all. But he was known as the “main villain of the West” and was in the center of attention of ladies. [[menson|Manson]], in [[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]], noted that Rantigar violated the laws of the Empire and encroached on the possessions granted by the Dynasty to his vassals.

After the war, mercenaries poured into [[almera-zemlya|Almera]], released by Rantigar: "Whom Rantigar dispersed, everyone poured here, as if honey smeared." The war was fought by wage-seekers from all over the Empire. Joaquin met [[berk|Burke]] on a schooner from Belomorye to Reis, stuffed with mercenaries hoping to make money on the Mill War.

## Joaquin and His Stories
Joaquin loved to embellish his service. He first told Harmon that the count personally instructed him to ferry a hundred desperate horsemen at night: they crossed the river in the same leather armor and at dawn struck the Melniks in the rear, overturning them in the water. Siru Ben Womak, he recalled that they fought together under the banners of Rantigar "on the Mill ford and at the Three Eves." ([[ben-vomak|Benjamin Womack]]) Joaquin later admitted to [[polli|Polly]] that he had never been in a battle and had never spoken to the count, but had invented it because the truth was “shame.” Harmon's opinion was short: if we discard the noble words, everything Rantigar did would be called massacre and robbery.

In "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]" Joaquin was proudly recommended by a veteran of the Mill War, including [[alanis-almera|Alanis]], and in "Yanmei Hunt" he said that he "stood even under the banners of the sunset Count Rantigar." ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]) There, the archer Merrit recalled how at the Mill War he shot down mail pigeons, “so that the barons would not ask for help.” Joaquin also heard of General Orissa, Rantigar's chief general, who "then fought for Rantigar." ([[oris|General Oris]])

## Remarks by Karen
In the collectible edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark, Lady [[karen|Karen]] calls the description of the Mill War "the only dubious moment" of the first chapter: it caused her to smile, and her husband to have a bout of laughter. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) She corrects the author in three points. The basis of Rantigar's army was not knights, but [[shavany|Shavans]]. No talent was needed to collect the troops: the Shavans love robbery, and the settled Melniks are their original enemies since the Iron Sun Empire, so four words were enough: “Brothers, let’s go rob the Melniks!” and clarification: “Only quickly, until the lord intervened.” Finally, Rantigar's first general was General Horis-desh, "a well-known man in the West", who plays a "significant, albeit hidden, role" in history.

## Contradictions
- Harmon heard that the First Winter had sent "its own battalion" to the Millers, and Joaquin in another conversation spoke of two battalions of kire.
- Rantigar's commander is named in the novels by General Oris, in the notes by Karen - by Horis-desh, and elsewhere by General Horis.
- Harmon heard that Rantigar had assembled an army of Western knights, militiamen, and mercenaries, while Karen claims that these were mostly Shavans.
