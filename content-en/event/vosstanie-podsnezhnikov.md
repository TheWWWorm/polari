## Causes
In the autumn of 1774, during the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], the peasants of the Southern Way were ravaged from all sides. The Duke of Labelin drove fifty thousand men into the militia near Labelin and took away grain carts from them. ([[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]]; [[labelin|Labelin]]) The Imperial foragers took the crop, leaving the Crown in return for a bill, and the retreating after the defeat, the spark army raked out the remains. The village of Summerswith, which stood above the thresholds of Hanoi, was threatened with hunger, and there was nothing to sow in the spring. ([[khanaj|Khanai]])

About thirty peasants, led by [[salem|Salem]], went to their baron to ask for help. The old greedy baron told them to leave. Only two guards remained in the castle, the rest went to war. They killed the chief and his son, but the peasants overcame them, and the blacksmith Blake killed the baron. In order not to wait for the revenge of the baronial sons, the villagers took horses and supplies from the castle and moved to the Duke "for justice". The neighboring villages sent a couple dozen men, and three hundred people came on the way.

## Developments
### Southern Way
At Pixie, a former brewer nicknamed the Tramp joined the campaign, in fact [[brat-lyudvig|brother Ludwig]], who once kidnapped the Bright Sphere. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) He showed the peasants along Hanoi a chain of frozen spark bodies. Hundreds of chain mails, heifers and pairs of boots, daggers and even a few sparkly eyes were removed from them. As the column grew to seven hundred men, it was intercepted by the baron's sons with fifty riders. The knights got stuck in carts deployed across the road and were interrupted by braids, chains and axes. Half of them lay in the snow, including all three of the baron's sons. The peasants lost fifty killed and nearly a hundred wounded. The carts were unrolled by retired Sergeant [[ruka-dodzh|Hand Dodge]], and Salem instructed him to teach people how to build.

The town of Bakers was met by “murderers from Summerswith” locked gates and road signs with threats. Then the Tramp threatened to stay under the walls and received a month's supply of food for a thousand people. Salem forbade doing this again, but agreed that people should be taught. Travelers walked slowly, sometimes five miles a day. Winter is the only free time of the peasant, and new people came to the column every day. Salem divided them into hundreds, appointed centurions, and set up food records (see [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] for details).

By the end of January, after Adrian’s death and [[minerva|Minerva]]’s peace treaty and coronation, Salem had led eight thousand people to the port of Grays. Colonel Sir Walter Day of the Marquess of Graysend, with four hundred soldiers, demanded that the baron's assassins be released. Salem, bowing low, replied that he would give them only to the one who has the right to judge. He got permission to buy food from the townspeople for the trophies sold, overpaying twice or three times. Learning that the Duke of Labelin had left for the capital, the peasants, on the advice of the Tramp, decided to go to [[faunterra|Faunterra]] - to the Dukes and to the mistress herself.

### Lawville and the "honest tax"
Under Lowville, the first city of the Crown Lands, [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] ("Three-Fingered", or "Deserter") and his squire Veselchak joined the army. ([[veselchak|Jolly]]) Joaquin made the condition that he would not fight and would not go to the palace. In Lawville itself, the Snowdrop scouts saw Minister Dreyfus Bourne's tax collectors demanding "three tithes" ahead and raking everything valuable out of their homes. ([[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]]) There was no written order from the collectors. The tramp demanded to be shown, a fight broke out, and the townspeople, led by a dental doctor Tooth, tore up the pickers and shooters. The next day, Zub brought eight hundred people: merchants and seven hundred volunteers with carts, crossbows and a hiking forge. Salem reproached him that the lesser evil had been answered by the greater.

Three days before Niara, Zub convinced Salem that “justice” was a vague word, and people would follow a clear goal: a firm and feasible tax. He confessed to Salem that he had called people in Lowville not for justice, but against taxes. A hundred and twenty centurions supported him with one hundred and eight votes. Soon four thousand men came out of Niar, and over the army sounded: “Glory to the Lord — and an honest tax!”

### Battle at the Gate
On the way to Izluchina, the road was blocked by an ancient outpost in the hollow between the forest and the ravine, with a company of crossbowmen behind a log barricade. The tooth with two thousand citizens and Dodge with the young men went forward with homemade siege "appliances". The road was dug with pits and littered with logs. Joaquin guessed that there was an ambush in the forest: the hired loggers left without leaving any smoke. He led thirteen hundred around and at dusk, under the cover of fog, dispersed the archers. He then struck the rear of the enemy infantry, following the cavalry, which attacked the regiment of the Tooth. When the peasants scattered in pursuit, Veselchak gathered them screaming as if he saw Gloria the Intercessor. Constructed in six ranks, Joaquin's detachment repulsed a new attack by a light cavalry. The enemy lost fifty killed and two hundred wounded.

The next morning, he surrendered to the tramp. The prisoners said they were sent by Dreyfus Bourne. The gunmen were his tax guards, infantry, and horsemen — two brigades he had hired. Salem let everyone go, taking only money for food from them, and ordered him to tell the capital that he wanted to talk to the mistress about honest taxes. The tooth, wounded in the arm, became the hero of the townspeople, and Dodge nicknamed him "the general". At the end of February, Salem found a clearing of snowdrops and ordered the flowers to be tied to spears. This is how the trip got its name.

### Spark from the Labelins
After the war, the army exceeded thirty thousand copies. More than half of them were citizens, and behind the column stretched a wandering market. [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] came to the camp with a guard nicknamed Seven. He spoke of unnamed "masters" sympathizing with the honest tax, and gave Salem a box with sixteen sparked self-shooters. On the Holy Field, he promised to give a thousand more self-shooters and a thousand spark copies. Buckley assured that the real ruler was Lord Chancellor Origin, who feasted on taxes, and offered to oppose him. Salem, remembering the mercy of the Duke at Labelin, refused to raise weapons at him, but he nevertheless accepted the gift. The box was immediately taken under the protection of Tooth and Dodge. Joaquin realized that they wanted to be pitted against the cairns, but did not figure out how to prevent this. Interviews in the “tail” confirmed that the court lives in luxury and everything is run by the Lord Chancellor.

Hampton met the Snowdrops with bread and honey. The elders promised five thousand men and presented three thousand Hilt. Salem tried to give up the money, but Zub accepted them, and the chest was placed under the protection of the young men. The army grew to forty thousand, two-thirds of whom were citizens.

### General Gore
In March, under Hampton, the path was blocked by General Joseph Gore with two thousand spark copies and two thousand riflemen and a light cavalry. Through the standard-bearer and day-bearer, he conveyed the "merciful permission" to disperse and demanded the extradition of the baron's murderers. Salem replied that they would only go home when they found justice. When the general turned the horse around, Zub and Dodge opened fire with their own arrows. They killed three scarlet guardsmen and a young standard-bearer, and the last guard closed the general himself. Gore retreated without taking the fight. Salem ordered the shooters to surrender, but Dodge's men arrested him. Joaquin, who stood up for the chief, was hit with a sparked arrow by Charlie Bull, and then he was deafened. In the tent, three young men tried to finish him off, but Veselchak fought off them with a dagger and took the wounded away.

Salem was locked in a van with the treasury, declaring that he was "not himself." During the night, Joaquin with Jolly, Tramp, Salmon and Billy outwitted the guard and freed him. Salem, however, refused to flee the 40,000 men he was responsible for, and Joaquin withdrew from the army.

### Erwin's trap
In the capital, Gore reported that he had retreated because of a spark from the rebels. Lord Chancellor [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] ridiculed him and temporarily appointed [[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]] as commander-in-chief of the spark army. The Northwood "bears" were supposed to hit the Snowdrops first, and the Kairs of the First Winter to follow and catch runners. The place of battle was chosen as the Holy Field, a lowland between two strands, from which the peasants could not disperse. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) Secretly from the vassals, Erwin considered the spark in the hands of the peasants to be the handwriting of the Kuklavod. ([[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]) The battle was to be a trap for his brigade with the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]], and Erwin's headquarters deliberately placed in the very bottom, as a bait. Sister [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]], he promised that as soon as the brigade gets caught, the battle will be stopped, and the Snowdrops will receive a pardon and bread. They were only required to fight off the bear attack once.

## Holy Field
On the eve of the battle, Buckley appeared to Minerva with a letter from [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]. He admitted that he armed the Snowdrops according to the intention of the Duke of Maurice, and offered a deal: for ten ephemera "by the head" the weapon will not be delivered. Then the unarmed peasants will flee, and the Labelins will accuse Origin in the House of slaughtering the innocent. Minerva wrote him a deposit, and then on the advice of [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]] decided to break the trap herself.

At dawn, her carriage, under the protection of Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]], rushed through the Northerners and bears. Sherengi Podsnezhnikov met the mistress with shouts of “Glory to Minerve!” Salem showed her devalued bills of forage and took the blame for the deaths of the ambassadors. The tooth claimed that the shots were a "brutal accident". New Tax Minister Frank Morlin-May announced the reform: a fixed annual tax for the farms and workshops of the Crown Lands, payment through banks and the abolition of collectors. The reform should have come into force by winter. Minerva promised triple compensation for the seized food and impunity to all participants of the campaign, except for the shooters. The Marquis of Graysend, on behalf of the South Road, publicly agreed, stipulating only the trial of the Baron's murderer. When the lady asked who had shot himself, the Snowdrops gave Dodge, two townspeople from the retinue and the Tooth. The tramp was told to be caught and hanged, and about Salem everyone said: “No, Your Majesty! Salem is not." The army threw the spears and dispersed. Lilliday and Northwood Minerva were ordered to withdraw.

Erwin's trap slammed shut. The battle did not take place, and the Kukovod scouts found no reason to attack the headquarters. Soon Erwin received a snowdrop from the Empress, although these flowers had bloomed already two weeks ago. It read: “Take your place in the world with dignity. You are not the Lord, my lord. Come to terms." Erwin's vassals, according to the laws of the North, considered the massacre of the rebels a matter of honor, condemned the actions of the ruler.

## Aftermath
The news of the pardon quickly spread. Three days later in Waymar, it was “not news.” According to the report of the Marquis of Gracend, those who returned home were paid on a spruce per capita. However, between five and seven thousand former Snowdrops did not lay down their arms and were looted in the Southern Way. On June 1, 1775, Minerva released from the Throne Citadel of Tooth and Dodge and promised them clemency and glorification for each trained soldier if they brought these people to the imperial service in a month. ([[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]]) The leaders gathered a mercenary army, but the Duke of Labelin bought it, and about seven thousand former Snowdrops became [[adrian|Adrian]]'s soldiers. In the summer of 1775 they entered the [[labelin|Labelin]] as "liberators", and Salem tried in vain to make them aware. In August, when Adrian occupied [[faunterra|Faunterra]] without a fight, the “young men of Major Dodge” scattered in a festive crowd and beat those who shouted against the new ruler.

The uprising also became a political weapon. At a meeting of the House, the Earl of Lilliday, defending the conquests of Origin in the South Road, showed the letters of thanks of the travelers and opposed them to a hungry rebellion in the lands remaining under the rule of Labelin. During the interrogation, Buckley confirmed that Maurice Labelin had ordered him to give the "eyes" to the Snowdrops. Erwin threatened the duke that such sparks in the hands of men against knights "will not forgive any judge and no lord." By October 1775, the tax reform came into effect, and the Crown Lands workshops were paid to file through banks. Salem blamed himself for the fate of those who became mercenaries.

## In "Fantasies"
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], where the author describes the possible fate of the heroes, the uprising is remembered in the summer of 1776. The Holy Mother Alicia told Erwin that the Chapter Pramatery considered the rebellion scattered without a drop of blood to be the best solution for him and Minerva: Erwin led an army ready to suppress the rebellion, and Minerva showed mercy. The only mistake the capitulation made was that the leaders were not punished: now they command gangs of young burgomaster Adrian.

## Contradictions in the text
- **Number.** The Sword Chapter names forty thousand people after Hampton. Erwin at the council speaks of nearly eighty thousand, Buckley before Minerva speaks of a hundred thousand.
- **Buckley's deposit.** Minerva says to write a bill for eighty thousand ephes of “deposit”, and then thinks that she wrote out “only forty thousand”.
- **Number of Ambassadors killed.** General Gore and Erwin talk about the four parliamentarians who were killed. In a scene at Hampton's, it is said that four noblemen remained lying on the ground, but "one moved".
