## Background
In the spring of 1774, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] led an expedition for the River, and Captain [[teobart|Theobart]] commanded a detachment of kairs with him. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) The inexperienced young man was despised and called by the eyes "gentle." This contempt was expressed most loudly by C.J. Lilliday. ([[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]]) At the beginning of the campaign, he kicked his crippled grey and challenged Erwin's order to send him home. In the village of [[spot|Spot]] Jemis cut off the hand of a village guy who quarreled with his gray Hank the Sailor. Erwin, in response, forced him to pay the victim and apologize to the “black man”. Jemis also arranged small dirty things for the mechanic Luis: he put a dead badger in his bed, and thorns under the saddle of a donkey. ([[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]])

When Erwin found in his tent a deadly poisonous snake, the “widow tape”, he built a detachment and suspected Jemis at all. He vehemently denied the accusation: he, the son of Lilliday and the dedicated kair, would not fall to the assassination of his lord's son. Theobart vouched for all the kairs and advised Erwin to apologize, but he refused. The last drop was the passage through the swamp with “bad air”. Erwin went straight, listening to the young guide [[kid|Kid]], not the captain and Jemis advising the bypass. A fire broke out from a badly extinguished fire. The detachment escaped, but one gray drowned, and Erwin himself, rushing to put out the fire, swallowed smoke and lost consciousness.

## Conspiracy
At dusk, the Kid woke Erwin up, and he crawled up to the gap between the bales of provisions. Behind the bales of the lamp sat nine kire; there was not only the captain and Hayvis kire. Jemis convinced his comrades that "the baby is not a lord, but a child": he constantly puts himself and the detachment at risk, and they will have to answer to the Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]]. I didn't mean to kill Lord Jemis. He wanted to intimidate Erwin, take away his weapon and, together with Matthew's kire, take him "prisoner" in the First Winter, and return the weapon only before the First Winter. ([[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]) Then Erwin would have to choose: confess to his father in shame or lie that he came back himself. Matthew and Donnell were with him from the beginning, and Oswald gradually leaned towards them. Only Frederick objected that it was his business "against wool".

Erwin understood why his father had sent him on a campaign: it was a test. Returning under escort would mean losing the right to inherit. Jemis said, “That’s not the son. If his brother had been in his place, it would have been different.”

## Confrontation
Erwin went out into the circle of the world and turned not to Jemis, but to the hesitant. He listed his weaknesses: he is afraid of the cold, suffocates after a mile of running, and is a bad fencer. Then he reminded many of them of their glory and their kind. Frederick brought to the cathedral of Agatha an Object donated by the Earl of Shayland. Oswald was initiated twice. At Hilbert's wedding, Erwin and Iona were children. He reminded Martin of his uncle, Bishop of Belomorya, the best player in the strata, and that Martin's father had taught Erwin the game. He reminded Jemis that the Lilliday family had served the Origins for eleven generations and had been related to them four times, and demanded that what he said in the dark be said to his face.

Jemis demanded abdication of command. Erwin then asked him his full name and title, and then whether Lord Origin had been made a prisoner of his own vassal. He drew his sword, put his hands behind his back and stood before the best swordsman of the detachment with his chest open:
> Make your choice, Kyle. If you want to kill me, now is the time.”
Jemis did not dare to the worst shame for a nobleman, the murder of a suzerain. Donnell and Matthew retreated, two kairs stood next to the lord, and Frederick said, "Jemis, no, it's not going to happen." Jemis threw the bandage with the sword and knelt down, asking only to take care of his dog Sagittarius.

## Sentence
Erwin laughed: the tension was relieved with laughter, and later he was told that it sounded terrible. He did not execute Jemis. In the name of the Great House, he deprived him of the title of kair and all the rights associated with it. Jemis had to leave the detachment and explain to the Duke himself why he lost his cloak, or hide in his father's castle. He was allowed to take only a knife and a hunting bow, and the gray Hank the Sailor was also expelled. Oswald and Frederica Erwin thanked, Matthew and Donnell did not complain.

## Aftermath
The disgraced Jemis confessed later that he secretly followed the squad to get revenge, since no one in his family had ever lost a cape before. However, the detachment was interrupted by people with the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]], and Jemis, finding the wounded Erwin, left him. He then vowed to serve Erwin. On 30 August 1774, Erwin returned his red and black cloak on a slope above the First Winter. In a report to his father and vassals, Erwin about the “stupid conspiracy of Jemis” was silent. Since then, the former rebel has become one of the Duke's most loyal and forthright associates (see [[dzhemis-lillidej]]).

The mystery still spread among the Greeks: expelled along with Jemis Hank Seaman told about the riot, and in the fall of 1774, in the fleet of Fleming, Gray John-John in revenge told [[mark-voron|Mark]] that Kair Jemis in the Soft Fields "wanted to raise a rebellion and kill the Duke", but he "crushed" him with "one force of will". Mark thought that Jemis would not live long if other Kairs found out that he tried to kill Erwin, and Jemis's silence about the Beyond was explained by shame for the "bad mutiny". Jemis himself, seeing the guineas of Hedin and Tuff mocking the prisoner Gwenda, said, "Six months ago I was almost in your place, Hedin. And I had the Duke of Origen on me.” Later, Erwin, defending Minerva's maid of honor Leila Talmir before Iona, reminded his sister that Jemis "was deprived of a cloak for rebellion against the suzerain," and recalling the former dangers, noted that even when Jemis rebelled, Kair Frederick remained loyal to the Lord.
