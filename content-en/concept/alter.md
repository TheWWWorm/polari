**Alter** is a permanent, socially recognized lover of a noble lady with a living husband. **Altessa** is the corresponding title of the beloved noble man. Words sound in the cycle from the first chapters to "Fantasies". The custom divides the lands of Polari: in the south it is almost obligatory, in the North it seems to be a fiction of novels.

## Custom
Holy Scripture allows that a lady may be in connection with several men. Foremother Sophia said: “A woman’s life is too valuable to be entrusted to the care of a man alone.” However, over seventeen centuries, the North has forgotten about many men: male jealousy was stronger than spiritual covenants, and the northern lords used to consider wives their property. Therefore, for the Northern women of the Worlds, the words “alter” and “altesse” sounded like a fiction from the capital’s novels. ([[minerva|Minerva]]) The northerners themselves, according to the southerners of the "The Skyship and the Girl", reproachfully say "lover" where the Shimmer would say "althessa". Myra’s friend Becca Litland explained the difference this way: “Alternatives are started only by married ladies. ([[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) Single girls have lovers... although the essence is the same.”

## Capital
### Clarence and Sybil Northwood
In the capital of the World, she learned that Lord [[lord-klarens|Clarence]], the imperial stabler, was the alter of Countess [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]]: "Everyone knows, milady." According to the knight Vanden, the old count [[elias-nortvud|Elias Northwood]] did not mind: he had long been cold to women, and the countess is full of strength, "so it is calmer for him, and she is pleased." Mira once overheard a date between the countess and an alter. Clarence warned Sybil against dangerous acquaintances - with Henry Farway, Catherine Catherine, General Davey and the disgraced Gallard. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) He called her a “faithful dog” and assured her that he did not watch, but looked after her so that she “did not harm herself.” Soon a Northwood baron summoned Clarence to a duel and stabbed him. He believed that this connection, "albeit seemingly approved by the scriptures", still stains the name of Count Elias. Sybil hasn't shown Mir for two days. Later, Mira discovered that Gloria was the daughter of Sybil and the prior of Gallard Almer, not the Earl of Northwood. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]])

### Court and Dynasty
Alters and althes occupy their place in the court hierarchy. As Mark reflected, in the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]], under the advisors, ministers and guards are "wives, children, brothers and sisters, alters and althes of all those named above." The emperors did not deny themselves violas. Lord [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] remembered Lord [[mejnir|Maynir]], “who had fifty violasses, killed a hundred wild boars, and made no law.” In early 1775 [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], who became the mayor of Faunterra, shared a bed with Erwin, although the marriage did not end. In the theater, Erwin sat in a box “with his althessa” - Alanis. Minerva herself, angry over Erwin's kiss, reminded herself that "the Empress is allowed favorites and alters" and the love affair does not oblige her to marry.

## South
### Shimmery
In the south, the custom is even freer. According to the Polari Land Guide, [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] has a kind of female cult, and a truly successful man “can and should” afford at least one althessa. Altesses are different "profile": for conversations about high, for going out, for walking, for love pleasure, even to help his wife with children. In Shimmery, there is an altess market. Half of it is veiled slavery, the accumulation of captives captured by the Shavans in the steppe wars, half pimping. The price depends on the origin, nature and color of the skin (the whitest are valued the most), there are even auctions. But the girl bought is taken care of, to be bought by a nice man is considered prestigious, and the best choose who to belong to.

In "The Skyship and the Girl", Laem's pimp Onorico (Rico) and his wife Vanessa-Lilit explain to the northern Horama why altess are needed: for pleasant conversations if the wife is deprived of her mind; for the beauty of the house; for going out into the light when the wife is on a trip; to help the wife-"mother of five"; finally, for diversity. According to Rico, a cheap Western woman can be bought for a dozen ephes, a pretty one for a hundred, and for three hundred - such that "you will not be happy for a century." There is even a Lisa-Margo viola school in Laem that buys out capable girls and teaches them. The most famous example is Catherine Catherine, Adrian’s adviser on secular intrigues. She was born in Halliwell, does not come from any of the Pramaterie and was sold to the South, where she became an altess of a Shimmerian merchant, then a mayor, and then a prince. In Laem, her story is retold as a fairy tale: "one Western prisoner" reached the master himself.

Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collector’s edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark writes that polygamy is legal in Shimmery according to Julian’s vault. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]; [[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]) Alters and altess are available throughout the empire, but only the legal wife and her children have rights, and in Shimmery the heir to the title and fortune can be recognized as the child of any altess. In another note, Karen, however, says the opposite: the title and status are inherited by the children of the legitimate spouse.

The Shimmerian nobles have many violasses, and Prince Hector even stole the best viola from the Third of the Five. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) In "A Doll on the Throne," Erwin spent hours teasing Hector "and a pack of violas." Shavan Neymir, making his way through Shimmery under the guise of a southerner, gave his companion Char for bought in Laem "altesta for love pleasure": such a couple was not suspicious of anyone. ([[nejmir|Neimir]]; [[chara|Chara]]) In the "Fantasy of the Fifth" (Laem, August 1776), the market was "sudden", because the chavanas defeated by the northerners did not fight among themselves for almost a year and did not sell captives. ## Law Miriam
With the custom of alters is the right of Miriam, or the right of the first night. According to Foremother Miriam, the first night of the girl belongs only to her, and “the first flower will be plucked by the man whom the girl chooses with her heart.” Parents’ wishes are not required. To exercise this right, the girl chooses a confidant - a father, mother or a respected priest, and later the clergy can certify whether she has exercised this right. The plot of the story "Confident" is built on it. ([[konfidentka|The Confidante]])

In "Shadow of the Great Tree" Minerva in a sleepless night recalls that she never applied the law of Miriam. In Fantasia Six (August 1776), she applies it, taking Erwin's favorites. Iona reproaches her: since Miriam's right is applied, "there must be a confidante", and closer to Iona, Minerva has no woman. ([[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]])

## Alters and altesses in the fates of heroes
- [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia Flame]] was Erwin's lover before the cycle, and in The Shadow of the Great Tree, Minerva mentally calls her "the Lord Chancellor's former altess". She herself refused to "be, as before, a disenfranchised althess" and wanted marriage. Even in the steppe campaign with her there was “neither a servant woman ... nor even an alter to pass the night.”
- At the end of the “Shadow of the Great Tree”, the brother and sister of Origina, teasing over Minerva, married her a young [[navi|Navi]]. “It’s a big responsibility to be an alter of the Empress,” said Iona, and Erwin promised to demand a report from the young man after each date.
- In the third fantasy of the world, guessing with spirits, asked whether it is possible that the groom was not a husband, but a “secret alter”.
- In the "Fantasy of the Sixth" Minerva, secretly coming together with Erwin, rejoices that the court has not yet imprinted them with the stamps "favorite", "altesse", "fiancé and bride". When Iona suddenly had fun, Erwin exclaimed, “You’ve got an alter!” Iona replied with laughter that "tragic widows are strangers to love pleasures."

## Anxiety - Erwin's "Althessa"
Since the fall of 1774, when [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] began a mutiny, his anxiety has acquired in his imagination the appearance of a “tender althessa”. For the first time she comes on the last night before the performance of the troops from the First Winter. She goes to bed with him, hugs him, whispers doubts and ridicule ("Ah, what nobility!.. I'm proud of you, honey." Erwin argues with her, chases her, but consults her more often. The alarm deals with his lapses in fencing ("you're too kind"), advises to make Minerva an ally, and conducts an internal dialogue with him through all subsequent books. In The Voice of Winter, Erwin "put a name on her": she has been a capitalized alarm ever since. In the story "Let's catch him" from "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]", this althessa is identified with the ghost of a girl Dalia Raven, who fell under the ice and died in the castle of the First Winter, and Erwin presents her to Minerva with the words: "The Cairos gave you my althessa." ([[dalliya-rejven|Dallia Raven]])
