**Stone sickness** is a disease that Polari considers almost more terrible than death. People call it not the sea, but the curse of the gods. The funds from it "in the sublunar world there is no", although the rumor attributed the healing of a sick stone disease child miracle of the Holy Subject in Farway.

## Current
Stone sickness does not bring a quick death, but slowly, day after day, turns the skin into stone. The skin loses flexibility, hardens and seals the person “in the sarcophagus”. If the patient manages to bend his arm or leg, the skin on the bend crunches and breaks like wood bark. Cracks do not heal, but bleed too weakly for a person to die from blood loss. In the end, the muscles lack strength, and the patient turns into a fixed “stone image”. Cracks on the chest allow him to breathe, cracks on the cheekbones - slightly open his mouth and squeeze out a few words. The eyelids can neither blink nor close, so the eyes of the patient are moistened, and for sleep they are closed with a bandage.

In the North, this disease is feared more than death. Erwin, upon learning of her, thought that if there was anything worse than death in dishonor and death in poverty, it was a stone sickness. The most terrible seas kill in a week or a month, and it does not bring a quick death: a person lies sealed in his own skin for months.

## Desmond Origin's disease
The Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] fell ill shortly after [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]]'s daughter's wedding to [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] and [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]]'s departure for the exploit in the spring of 1774. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) When Erwin returned on 30 August 1774, the Castelians explained to him that the Duke had fallen ill shortly after his departure and the disease had "already reached full development". His father was an immovable “granite statue”. His face was the color of a cobblestone pavement, the skin to the touch did not differ from the brick, and when he lifted his head with great effort, in the cracks on the neck protruded the mother-in-law. He spoke in whispers, ragged words, and the Duchess [[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia]] translated them, fed him liquid porridge from a spoon and wet his eyes with a sponge. Desmond could read only with the help of a device like a well crane, which held an open book over him (the pages were leafed by a servant girl), and look out the window through an inclined mirror above the bed. To think, he rolled his eyes, and his wife covered them with a bandage. However, the illness did not damage his mind. Because of her, Desmond transferred the title of duke to Erwin on the same day.

At the end of the summer of 1774, after the abdication, the disease continued to develop. Accepting Adrian's envoys, Erwin warned Emperor [[itan|Ethan]]'s secretary that "the great Lord Desmond had fallen ill with a stone disease" and he would be able to see it tomorrow. His father's skin became rough and gray, like a cobblestone pavement, and he himself did not want to be seen, and lay in the shade behind the pointed lanterns. In “A Doll on the Throne,” [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] came to her father and felt a thick fog of death in his bedroom. Desmond’s skin cracked and bled, but his jaw was temporarily mobile and he could speak almost clearly. [[minerva|Mira]] in December 1774 noted a suspicious coincidence: Desmond fell ill shortly after the wedding, which was both Shayland brothers, and Erwin himself. Immediately she objected to herself that it was impossible to poison a person with stone sickness: it was “not poison, but the curse of the gods.”

For the sake of healing her husband, the Duchess of Sofia agreed to a deal with the mysterious Puppeteer: he promised to cure Desmond in exchange for a talking object extracted by the keir of [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]] for the River. The puppeteer sent his servant Znaharka to the palace with a healing object - the Hand of Znaharka. Erwin and Desmond suspected that Znacharka was sent to kill them, and ordered the kairam to capture her alive. Iona believed the healer and tried to take her to her father; the plan fell through, Znacharka resisted and died in battle, and her body, along with the Object, went to [[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]]. Meanwhile, Iona gave [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]]'s potion to his father, and Desmond got better. Iona did not know for sure what helped - witchcraft or potion. Erwin later publicly called his father's illness "poisoning for stone sickness", one of the atrocities of the Puppeteer. In The Shadows of the Great Tree, this is confirmed by the thoughts of [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] himself: before the battle for the First Winter, he decides to execute Old Desmond, "as long as the stone sickness did not cope with the case." In Vittor's mind, Desmond is named one of those before whom the count once stood on his knees, dying of fear (along with Tellurian, Aiden Almera, and Adrian).

Recovery was slow. In the spring of 1775, Desmond, already able to speak clearly, chew and rotate the wheels of the wheelchair himself, was still pale, constantly freezing, curled up in plaids and demanded a hot fireplace even in warm weather. Previously, the disease caused him despair, now anger, and Erwin considered this a good sign. However, he refused to go to the House, not wanting to appear to the high lords as “such a stuffed animal”. In the summer of 1775 he again stood on his feet and commanded the troops. Enemies explained his caution under Waymar, including illness: stone disease “put Desmond on the threshold”, and feeling the proximity of death, a person appreciates life more. Even in the First Winter, he had to rely on General [[stetkhem|Statham]]'s hand to straighten himself out: "the remains of the stone sickness still haven't left him." He himself died on the night after the Battle of the First Winter at the hands of his eldest son, [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard]]. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]])
