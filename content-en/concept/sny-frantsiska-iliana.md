The **dreams of Francis-Ilian** are the prophetic dreams of King [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] Francis-Ilian, thanks to which the whole south of Polaris, "from Laem to the Heart of the Light", knew him as a prophet and a dreamer. ([[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]]) The first and most terrible dream, about the death of the world, turned his life around. The king handed over the affairs to his son, Hector, spent eight years in the monastery over the works of the Foremothers and Forefathers, sent vassals to collect information about the secret order, for the sake of solving the dream went on a journey to the north, joined the order of [[sadovniki|gardeners]] and became an ally of Lady in Darkness. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]; [[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]]) Then, about once a year, he had new dreams — “not as dazzling as the first, but still prophetic.” People everywhere grabbed his sleeves and asked him what he had dreamed.

The following are listed in the order in which they dreamt of the Prophet. The dates are taken from his account to Lord Emmon Darklin in Gates of Trust and from Shadow of the Great Tree. ([[vrata-doveriya|The Gates of Trust]]; [[ten-velikogo-dreva|The Shadow of the Great Tree]])

## The First Dream: The Death of the World

**When and where.** Eight or nine years before the events of “The Gate of Trust” (the narrator calls both “nine years ago” and “eight years”), on the eve of the Days of Abundance. The king visited the Maximian monastery on the edge of the Boneless Proval (in the "Yanmei Hunt" he is called the Abode-u-Bezdna) and spent the night in the guest room. ([[bezdonnaya-propast|Bottomless Abyss]]; [[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]) That same evening, the Abbot, the Second of the Five Earls of [[kuindar|Quindar]], told him of the secret order and offered to join it.

**What he saw.** “The goddess of dreams,” as it says in Gates of Trust, showed him this picture. The sublunar world bends in the middle and picks up the edges, becoming like a funnel for oil, and under the neck of the funnel is a bottomless Fail. People, buildings and animals slide into the abyss, plants fall there with lumps of earth, exhausted birds fall next. All that remains is the [[vselenskaya-spiral|Universal Spiral]], smooth as glass, but it also cracks and falls into the abyss. There is nothing left, not even the disembodied eyes that watched this dream.

**As he understood it.** The king immediately decided that the dream was associated with the order and served as a divine clue. But the hint allowed two opposite meanings. Either the order will save the world, and then the king should give him all his strength, or the order is the source of death, and then his duty is to raise troops and wipe the order off the face of the earth. For eight years, he sought an interpretation in the writings of the Forebears and found none: the Forebears “believed that the world would stand forever.” He did not dare to tell the dream, because he would have sounded "the worst of heresies." Lord Emmon, who asked about the signs for Darkwater, only hinted at a sign concerning all lands, "vague, chaotic and very disturbing." Apparently, this was the first dream: the prophet hoped that Emmon would ask more about him, but he did not ask.

**To whom did he dream?** In February 1775, in the "moth chamber" of the Nan Clair Palace, he told the first dream of the Lady in Darkness, at her "three gates" of trust, to [[menson|Manson]] and Adjutant [[gerion|Gerion]]. The Queen replied that the order knew about the danger: she echoed the faith of the Folticians, as if in three hundred years Polaris would split into pieces. The vassals of the queen, having learned at the same hour that Manson had been forced to raise his hand to the lord by Ulyanina Dust, decided that the priory "possessed the Secret", and shouted that "the world falls into the abyss" and "the prophet's dream portends the destruction of the Tree". ([[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) On the way to the capital, the king joined the order and learned what kind of Tree the brothers are raising and what kind of split threatens the world in three hundred years if the Tree does not hold its roots.

**That's right.** The response to sleep has changed. At the end of The Shadow of the Great Tree, the prophet, along with [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], [[minerva|Minerva]], Iona, and [[adrian|Adrian]], listened to [[navi|Navi]]. ([[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]]) He explained that if the order rises to [[zvezda|the Star]] and changes the signal of the false lighthouse, the world of the “gods” will absorb Polaris. For the first time, the king questioned the order:
> “The writers showed me in a dream the death of the world and told me to prevent it.”

He believed that the Second Tree was a cure, and now he was asking: were the Foremothers warned against it, or was it the crash Nathaniel described? The choice between the death of Navi and the triumph of the Order of Navi left Minerva, and this is the main cycle ends.

In "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]" (the author calls them possible fates of heroes), the prophet no longer doubts. He tells Iona that the dream "nine years ago" was the answer to the question "What have we done?": they prevented the destruction of the world, and without the actions of "you and me and a few more people" the world would have been destroyed. What kind of actions, he does not specify: he speaks allegorically, because he does not know how dedicated [[obri|Aubrey]] is to the secret. It can be understood that this is a decision made after Navi’s story (see Navi’s article), but this is not directly stated in the text. ([[navi|Navi]])

## Dreams of War and Peace in the South

**When and to whom.** The winter of 1774/75, the prophet's journey from the Abode-u-Bezna to the north. In the Shimmer Mountains, he answered questions, recounting in his memory “the seven last dreams”, that is, everything except the first. To the merchants of Surlion he revealed only that part of the prophecies which concerned themselves.

**What he predicted.** To the Highlanders he said that the misfortunes that befell the North would never affect the “happy South”. To the Litlanders in the Surlion — that [[adrian|Adrian]] would defeat the Steppes and liberate Melorange.

**That's right.** The second prophecy came true almost immediately. When the prophet sailed to Leongard, the sea gate of [[nadezhda|Hope]], he was greeted with the message: “Prophecy has come true,” Adrian broke the [[moran|Steppe Fire]]. Pilgrims believed that the prophet knew in advance about the Northern Outbreak, and about the rebellion of the Steppe Fire, and about the death of the Lord. ([[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern Uprising]]) The first prophecy, apparently, did not materialize: in May 1775, the Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] declared war on Shimmery in the House (the “Yanmean Hunt”), and in the summer the king told Erwin about [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]’s invasion of Shimmery with stolen eyes (the “Humans and Gods”). According to him, this invasion reconciled him with his son.

## Ships without sails and wind

**When and to whom.** He had a dream before the Descent of 1772. The prophet told his guild of negotiants in Surlion, and then, in the Gates of Trust, to Lord Emmon Darklin.

**What he saw.** Ships that sail the sea themselves, without wind and oars, guided only by the will of the captain and the divine power.

**That's right.** The merchants considered the dream a good sign and wanted to give the prophet the best ship of the Surlion shipyards. He asked to give the ship to the order of Maximian and made only one voyage on it - to Leongard. There is no direct sleep in the cycle. The closest to it is the model of a ship with a spark engine, which the Earl of Northwood showed to Iona in the "Yanmei Hunt" ("North Bird - 6"): such a ship could go "in the most unfavorable wind" and without sails, but there were no full-weight samples. Vessels going against the wind without oars, predicted and Mother Agatha in his "Fantasies". In the Yangmei Hunt, the chapter where Erwin reads them says that this prediction “has not yet come true.”

## The girl rescued from the clutches of the monster

**When and to whom.** Spring Dawn of 1773 ("last year"). The Prophet told Duke [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]] a dream in the [[serdtse-sveta|Heart of Light]] and then Lord Emmon.

**What he saw.** A young girl rescued from the clutches of a terrible monster.

**That's right.** Farway was in disarray: Vladyka Adrian held his beloved grandchildren hostage. The next day after the story of the prophet came the news of the death of Adrian, the war ended, Farway's grandchildren survived. The happy Duke gave the prophet the book “Divine Mechanics”. Gerion later interpreted the dream "twowise": the saved girl is Minerva, who ascended the throne, and Farway's granddaughter, who survived on the imperial train. The prophet wanted to reveal the “truth” to Farway, that is, apparently, the first dream, but he did not dare to overshadow his joy. Adrian, as it turned out later, did not die.

## Yangmei and Agatha on the same bed

**When and to whom.** Sofiina days, summer of 1774. The prophet's dream told the townspeople Leongard and Lord Emmon, and in Fantasies - Erwin and Minerva.

**What he saw.** Naked Foremothers Yanmei Merciful and [[pramater-agata|Agatha the Radiant]], who fondled each other like mistresses. ([[pramater-yanmej|Yanmei the Merciful]])

**That's right.** In Leonhard, the dream was understood so that the North would reconcile with the Crown and the war would end in a dynastic marriage. Gerion, in Gates of Trust, assured that the predicted peace between Agatha and Yangmei was "already here." In Fantasia, in the right nave of the Cathedral of Wilhelm, after the death of Adrian, the prophet discovered Erwin and Minerva a different meaning. The dream showed him their connection, “even before you met.” Minerva said that they broke up forever. The Prophet replied that for politics it does not matter: two are “one force”. This dream became the basis of his plan: Minerva is an empress with secular and financial power, Erwin is a military ruler, a “shogun” according to the book of the Forefather Haruko “Empire of the Rising”.

## The Ninth Dream

**When and to whom.** Autumn 1775, sea voyage to [[soltaun|Soltown]]. The prophet told a dream at a dinner in Soltown, under Adrian, [[karen|Karen]], Manson, [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]], Lady in Darkness, the Second of the Five and Hector ("The Shadow of the Great Tree", "Witness - 5"). The guests said, “Ninth?” Karen was surprised that the light kept track of his dreams. The king replied that people consider all his dreams to be things, and he himself is “absolutely sure only of the first.” Karen asked about the first dream, but Hector asked his father not to talk about it at the table. The Prophet promised to tell her later, in private; this conversation is not shown in the books.

The story of the dream was, according to Karen's observation, a political move. Adrian was going to say something, but the prophet ahead of him, attracted everyone to the game of interpretation and became the master of the feast. He himself admitted that he did not understand all the images, and asked the guests to help.

### Crucified man with a burning finger and a “wooden structure”

**What he saw.** A terrible man crucified on a cross; his finger burned like a candle.

**As he understood it.** It was the only image that the king, he said, understood firmly. The burning finger is Wilhelm's Ring, that is, an indication of Paul, and if a person is crucified, then Paul will be able to catch. ([[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]; [[paul|Paul]]) The guests agreed.

**That's right.** Paul was not caught: he died on December 2, 1775 under the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]. But in "Fantasies" the prophet tells Iona that the last of his dreams reported even such a trifle as the "wooden structure" to which her Gun is attached. ([[orudie|The Weapon]]) The weapon of the Origins was called a prisoner of Shavan with the Perth of Wilhelm, who was screwed to a strong chair, "fortified by the bars", and his hand with the Perth was fixed in the lever system. [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] shot them, and on the Belomorye Road the Gun helped solve the battle for the First Winter. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]) Which of the images of the ninth dream the prophet meant, he does not specify. Apparently, this is a crucified man with a burning finger: both the fastening to the wooden frame and the fire of Perst converge in it. If so, the prophet himself, waiting for the capture of Paul, at lunch interpreted the dream incorrectly.

### Sea monster and naked woman

**What he saw.** A sea monster that hugs a naked woman with its tentacles.

**Interpretations.** Some guests decided that there would be a sea battle, others - that an unequal marriage. Manson clarified whether a woman is beautiful and naked, and announced that the dream is “not prophetic, but ordinary”: the southerners “have a dream forever.” Lady-in-the-Dark connected this image with the third: the monster is an indication of Minerva's ship, and the tentacles are a sign that Minerva is intoxicated with his power over the Object. There are no other interpretations and implementations in the books.

### Girl with pharmacy weights

**What he saw.** Dressed girl holding pharmacy scales.

**Interpretations.** The scales could indicate a merchant, jeweler, pharmacist or judge, but the guests judged that none of them fit. Lady in the Dark suggested reading the scale as a sign of the weight itself. She recalled how Minerva's ship "had lost mass and climbed into the sky": at the end of August 1775, Navi lifted it with the Yangmei Glove. ([[perchatka-mogushchestva|Glove of Might]]) According to her, the dream foreshadowed this moment and indicated that Minerva was driving the weight. Everyone accepted this interpretation. Note that the rise of the ship occurred before the king saw the ninth dream, so that the “prediction” in this interpretation refers to the past.

### Watchmaker with a severed hand

**What he saw.** A gray-haired master with a careful look and a chain of mechanical watches from his pocket; the king took him for a watchmaker. The hand of the master was cut off, but the blood did not flow, and he did not scream, as if the wound had healed long ago. The severed hand lay at his feet and seemed rotting at first, then charred.

**Interpretations.** Karen, asking about the details, suggested that the clock is a symbol of time: a person must succeed, otherwise he will become a cripple. Lady in Darkness called the guess "a very mark", and the prophet, Adrian and the Second of the Five exchanged meaningful views. The prophet explained to an embarrassed Karen that he had recently joined a secret society, and that it was “a bit time-obsessed” and very afraid of not having time to do something. It can be understood that the dream was associated with the terms granted to the order; according to Navi, the fuel of the false lighthouse will be enough for two thousand hundred years.

**That's right.** The interpreters at the table were wrong: the dream indicated a specific person. The gray-haired master is Inji Price, the son of a watchmaker and himself a watchmaker by trade, who by that time was at Minerva. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) On December 2, 1775, in the Battle of the First Winter, he was ordered to divert Paul's companions in the dungeons of the grotto of Kosuli ("The Shadow of the Great Tree", "To the Day Song"). ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]; [[grot-kosuli|Roe Deer Grotto]]) Inge hid behind the statue of Wilhelm and put out a fake hand because of it, on which something glittered. Shavanka [[chara|Chara]] took the shine for the Perst and shot: the hand was “burned to the bones”, and the “charred claw” hid behind the sculpture. Inzhi squirmed against the wall, pressing the "armspot" to his stomach. When Chara approached the "wounded", he stood out from under the cloak of the real, "third" hand, put a bolt from a spring self-shooter in her face, finished off the dag and took it from the Feather.

Hence the details of the dream: the hand is cut off, but the blood does not flow and the master does not scream seriously, because the hand is not his; it lies at his feet and is charred. What was meant by “rotten first” the books do not explain. Apparently, the dream also noted that Chara with her Perst was from the companions of Paul, the carrier of "rotten" (Chara called him Pus-ganta). This is one of the scenes by which the prophet could confirm to Iona that all his dreams had come true.

## Dreams that did not exist

While the king lived in the monastery, his daughter, Infanta Demeter, published a monthly journal, The Diary of a Dreamer: secular gossip in it alternated with the "fresh prophecies" of Francis-Ilian. ([[demetra-nellis-lajon|Demetra Nellis-Lyon]]) Most of these "dreams" Demeter wrote herself, and interpretations were written by her friend-philosopher. The magazine sold well, and the owner of the printing house grieved when the prophet left the monastery ("Fantasies", "Amicable").

## How the Prophet Treated His Dreams

- **I told everyone only a part of it.** People wanted to know what was waiting for them, and the prophet revealed to them only the part of the prophecies that concerned them. He “did not impose the truth on those who did not need it”: people rejoiced and did not ask further. Count Lighthart, of whom he had no dreams, said only what he was sure of: "The worst days of the House of Lighthart are behind us." ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]])
- **He kept his first dream a secret.** For eight years he had not told anyone his first dream, because it sounded like heresy, and he did not know which side the order was on. Letters about the order he, apparently, wrote and sealed himself. With him, he deliberately kept the “most near and greedy” vassal, Gerion, so that through him unimportant secrets would flow. So he found out that his secrets were bought by Lord Emmon and his own son. Finally, he chose the only listener, Lady in the Dark, “the wisest woman of the South,” and himself passed the “gate of trust” for her. The Queen in response called eight years of his cautious research “gates of trust” that she passed.
- **I built a policy on dreams.** Dreaming of Yangmei and Agatha was the "second bottom" of his plan in Fantasies. He allowed Adrian to use [[nikolas-olli|Nicholas Ollie]]'s device to concentrate the conflict of the common people with the nobility in one Faunterra and become manageable. At the same time, he checked how well the "future crowned couple" - Minerva and Erwin - can act. On his ninth night at a dinner in Soltown, he intercepted a conversation with Adrian.
- **Comforted by dreams.** He told Farway about the rescued girl. He explained to Iona, crushed by guilt after the First Winter, that all his dreams had come true and that together they had prevented the doom of the world. He advised Iona to confess to an impartial man and take a strict penance. Fantasia’s narrator calls him the hero who “saved the princess from the tower.”

## The Supreme Gods and the Question of Prophecy

The prophet himself speaks differently about who sends dreams. In the Gate of Trust, it is the “goddess of dreams” who “did not lie to the prophet.” In "Shadow of the Great Tree" - Foremothers, who ordered to prevent the death of the world. At a dinner in Soltown, he jokes that the gods once frightened him, “outraged by my laziness.” After Navi's story, the question became acute. Navi spoke about the world of the “gods” outside of Polaris, who do not recognize Polaris as a species and for whom Polaris would become a reserve or amusement park. If such are the “gods,” who showed the prophet the future?

The prophet answers Iona in Fantasies. Dreams are sent not by the gods of whom “much has been heard in the last days,” but by others, “higher and more authentic.” The dreams that have come true prove that such gods exist: “Who else could show me prophetic dreams?” The second piece of evidence is that someone told Erwin the exact time of the attack. It appears to be a reference to the voice of Pramatery heard by Erwin: the prophet remarks that Pramatery "talked to him too often to doubt her." The Prophet (pbuh) said the same thing to Erwin, and he was pleased.

Books leave room for other reading. The first dream of the prophet appeared on the very night that the abbot told him about the order. Many dreams came true, because the prophet himself then acted on them: for eight years he searched for a solution, joined the order, sought an alliance with the Lady-in-the-Dark, built on a dream about Yanmei and Agatha a division of power between Minerva and Erwin. Interpretations more than once adjusted to what had already happened: the “saved girl” was immediately Minerva and Farway’s granddaughter, the scales were the lifting of the ship, which occurred before sleep. The prophetic dreams of Francis-Ilian can be read as a true revelation, and as a partially fulfilled plan of a smart politician. The cycle does not give a definite answer. The prophet himself in Soltown admitted that he was confident only in the first dream, and in the “Fantasies” that everything came true.

## Differences

- **How many years ago.** In the Gate of Trust, the first dream was "nine years ago", and in the monastery the king spent eight years; it also says that both the invitation of the Second of the Five, and the first dream were "eight years ago". In Fantasies, the prophet speaks of a dream “nine years ago.”
- **Who sends dreams.** The "goddess of dreams", the Foremothers or the "higher" gods - see above.
- **The prophet's confidence.** "Absolutely sure only in the first" (Saltown) - and "All my dreams came true" (First Winter, "Fantasies").
- **The interpretation of the ninth dream.** At lunch, crucified with a burning finger meant the capture of Paul; in Fantasia, the last dream turned out to be a prediction of the "wooden construction" of the Gun.
