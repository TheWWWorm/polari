## Description

At the University of Faunterra operated the largest printing shop in the whole of Polari: his mechanic for the day did what the monk took a month to copy. (In early media outlets, the workshop is named the only one in Polari, and the secret of the machines is jealously kept by the Crown.) This allowed the Crown to issue The Voice of the Crown, a regular publication about recent events, rather than about the past or fictional: for example, in April, to write an essay on the March events, and in May to print and send to readers. It printed essays and illustrations, and published land grants: every decree of lazy possession was duplicated in the news book, so that the April issue, according to archivist Hammil, allowed you to learn all the latest information about land holdings. Read the "Voice" first of all the rich and nobles, as well as the feudal lords who received it by mailing. The mechanical font was simple and sparingly clean, without veneers and red letters.

## Before the war

### "Arrow, Coin, Spark"

[[minerva|Mira]] first saw the “Voice” in the library of the train on the way to the capital and found the mechanical font soulless, although she liked the drawings. Later, reflecting on the plans of the lord, she asked the maid to bring a book “with a name of two words” and found an article about the tax and rails in it. In the issues of that time, the Voice painted [[adrian|Adrian]]'s rail dream and placed portraits of his supporters - Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] and Duke Aiden Almer. ([[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]])

[[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], on the way to Shayland, slowly, confusing words, taking the book out of the case, read aloud "The Voice of the Crown" in the roundabout of the merchant [[kharmon|Harmon]]. The page with the description of the ball and the portrait of [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], torn from the book, became his cherished relic: he wore it folded four times in his chest pocket until the leaf burned in an abandoned barn. Even a year later, in Shadow of the Great Tree, Joaquin recalled how beautiful that portrait was.

### The Case of Counterfeiting

In the summer of 1774, [[mark-voron|Mark]] investigated the murder of the printer Jake of Godwin, who dialed the July "Voice", and the disappearance of the April letter from the archive. He suggested that the attacker produced a fake page of the Voice to give credibility to the fake letter, and asked Adrian to cancel all July grants and send a denial to all feudal lords who receive the Voice. Captain Backfield, by contrast, decided that the criminals had stolen the trial pages to undermine confidence in imperial news, and offered to take the printing house under guard. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]])

In the autumn the newspaper reported on the fall of [[evergard|Evergard]] and that the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]] spoke in the hand of Adrian; The Shaylands deliberately showed Mira this issue, and the main news was on the front pages. In Waymar, Inge Price did not intentionally bring Mira the "Voice" so as not to upset her with the news, and Mira herself taught Lindsay's servant to write in printed letters, "like in The Voice of the Crown." ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]; [[lindsi|Lindsey]])

## After the Northern War

### Daily newspaper

After the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], the “Voice” changed: instead of a hundred pages, it became only a few sheets folded like book turns, but it was published daily, not monthly, and fell tenfold. According to Mark, this was right: in alarming times, people crave news. The newspaper did not mention the war in a word, but praised the new empress - the "Bearing Peace" - wrote about the festivities at the court, the withdrawal of troops to the outskirts and the unhurried appointments of officials. She mentioned the rebellion of Count Fleming only then, according to Mark's solution, so as not to look like hurrah propaganda: there is a rebellion, but very far away. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] personally checked and approved the texts before printing; Mark admitted that House Origin had succeeded in propaganda no less than in military affairs. After the coronation, the newspaper called Minerva "the true embodiment of mercy and nobility."

According to the portraits in the Voice, Minerva was recognized in the face: once the hostess of the living yard "House with Ivy" (the future lady Leyla Talmir), seeing the guest, compared her face with the face from the pages of the newspaper. Later, Leila admitted that she knows about the events at the court “only what the Voice of the Crown writes.” [[ded|Grandfather]] taught his granddaughter to read daily. ([[vnuchok|Grandson]])

### Controlled by the Chancellor

In Gates of Trust, Secretary [[itan|Ethan]] referred to the Voice printing the results of Adrian's body examination; Erwin replied, “The Voice of the Crown is under my control! You too easily believed in the death of your beloved master. Later, the newspaper portrayed Erwin himself as an intriguer and power-hungry: Minerva, answering questions about the Duke, angrily referred to what the Voice writes about him.

### Chamber and Court

In the spring of 1775, the Voice printed records of the House proceedings and special issues of Manson's trial; several chapters of the Yanmei Hunt are opened by the newspaper's texts of 10, 12, 15, 26 and 28 May 1775. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]; [[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]]) The May 26 issue began with the fear that after yesterday's shocks, the number would come out boring, and reported that the testimony of the Lord Chancellor gave reason to suspect sabotage on the bridge over the Back. Erwin threw the paper out in anger because of the phrase about the “great hope” that a proposal would be accepted. [[navi|Navi]] considered the court "showy", although the minutes of the meetings were printed in each issue, and Leila Talmir noted that the newspaper does not write anything about the temporary status of the mistress.

## "Humans and Gods"

The Voice later shouted from the cover: “Horrible attack by heretics! Wilhelm's fingers sow death! The tomb of the Dynasty is robbed!” — and reported about the suspicions of the prince in the heresy and the holy war against him. General Davey lamented that the Voice was denying him a deserved victory, attributing the rescue to Meloranj Minerva. Karen Lighthart, hiding with [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]], gave the Countess to rewrite the issue to sell the original and leave a copy - but Sybil instead wrote a penitential confession, which Karen had to burn. ([[karen|Karen]]) Navi once slipped Karen "The Voice" to distract her from her own brooding, and on another occasion on a fresh release explained to the fugitives that the parish, against which the holy war is being waged, is not up to them now. Karen also learned from The Voice that her husband [[menson|Manson]] was alive: the newspaper wrote about his trial.

## "The Shadow of the Great Tree"

Navi admitted to Minerva that he followed her finances “on the Voices of the Crown”. More importantly, he said, Erwin helped him “when he made The Voice of the Crown mass and accessible to the common people”: copies of the newspaper reached the Faraday-Riley hospital, and from them Navi learned about the disappearance of the fort in the Beyond, realized that the villains created the deconstructor, and decided to act.

When the troops came out of the capital at night, the authors of the "Voice" with notebooks tied up behind the column, guessing, the mistress is evacuated or goes to war - the second "would raise sales to heaven." Minerva herself, among other things, wrote articles for The Voice. Minerva's speech, prepared with the help of Dorian Amber, "Voice" printed with a vivid description of the Yangmei Glove and a ticket dancing in the air; newspaper sales took off, and queues for ships and stagecoaches that took runners away from the capital declined. Minerva had a whole garnish of articles and speeches dedicated to her, and Amber led a series of stories about the life of the yard. "Voice" told about the attack of heretics on the tomb of the Dynasty, about the oath of Minerva to stay in Faunterra and about the flight of her heavenly ship: the whole turn was devoted to eyewitnesses, and the author of the article put all the flyer's mentions on the map and concluded that the ship most likely went in the direction of Smoky Distance. Talking to his daughter [[magda-labelin|Magda]], Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] clapped his palm on the "Voice of the Crown". When he retired, Mark suggested that Minerva call him, "giving an announcement in the Voice."

## In "Fantasies"

In "Fantasies" "Voice" is read in the province: the postmaster in Almera retells from him the news about the school reform of Minerva. The publisher notes that in six months because of the “ordinary” Voice of the Crown “, which turned out to be more interesting than any fiction, had to burn ten thousand unsold art books. At the same time, the illiterate mob “Voice” does not read, and in one of the stories the colonel reminds Vladyka that it is impossible to address her through the newspaper. In "The Device", Colonel Backfield, searching the office of Professor Nicholas Ollie, found a page of the old "Voice" with a portrait of Minerva at the opening of the theater season and considered it evidence: the professor, they say, serves as the mistress. In fact, Ollie kept the sheet for a great review of the play printed on the back.
