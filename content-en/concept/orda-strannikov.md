The **horde of Wanderers** is a posthumous army in the faith of the [[shavany|Shavans]], the steppe horsemen of the West. Shavans recognize the cult of the [[pramateri|Foremothers]], but closer to them are the spirits of nature and the “animals”. The Lord of evil for them is not the [[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]], but the Spirit of the Worm, and salvation from it promises only the Horde of Wanderers. The spirits that make up the Horde are called Wandering Spirits. They are called and sworn to help.

## Faith
According to the Polari Land Guide, the sacred property of all living things in the West is the ability to move. Everything that moves or gives energy to movement comes from the gods: people, animals, wind, clouds, waves, rain, sun. Evil is that which takes away mobility: death, powerlessness, rust, decay, disease. It is ruled by the Spirit of the Worm, "the lord of decay, decay and decay." The most revered of the first animals are the firstborn Ramsun and the first-born Aristod. The words “the first horse rides with him” sound like a blessing to the Shavans, along with the help of the “Spirits of the Wanderers”.

In the novels, faith is expressed as follows:
> “Shavans believe that every day and hour the Purulent Spirit of the Worm devours the world of people, decomposing and turning into dust all living things. Sooner or later he will eat everything in the world, except for the disembodied Horde of Wanderers, which will flee from him to another universe.

The Guide adds that only those who have lived a life worthy of Shavan will be saved: their souls will join the Horde after death and go to wander in search of another world. The steppe legends that [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] and [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] listened to as children from an old Shavan stablemate from Holliwell in the First Winter are full of the same images. Chavan Verilla searched the seven lands for a winged horse, soared into the sky and became "absolutely free as the Wanderers Spirits themselves." But such freedom stunned him, and he fell to the ground from the height of the clouds.

## How to get to the Horde
From the actions of Shavans in the novels, you can see what they need for death:
- **Dying on your feet.** In [[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]], Neymir and [[chara|Chara]] were captured by the Shimmerians and waited to be executed in the cellar. ([[nejmir|Neimir]]) Neymir did not ask for mercy, but begged to untie their legs: "We will die with our feet bound - we will not get into the horde of Wanderers. Our spirits will remain in prison forever!” Prince Hector knew this belief and therefore sentenced Chara to worse than death: to cut off her legs so that she would live long, crawl and ask for alms. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) Even after her death, her soul would not join the "happy Wandering Spirits."
- **Do not lose mobility.** Neymir and Chara crippled both legs of their leader [[moran|Moran]], who betrayed them for the sake of victory: now he will forge, "like a crawler", and after death "will crawl in pursuit of the Horde of Wanderers." Breaking a person’s legs, according to the “Guide”, is considered almost as grave a sin as murder.
- **Don't give a soul to a sorcerer.** Chara frightened the stranger by the fact that the Darkwater Witch “drinks the souls of the dead, looking them in the eyes.” Whose soul he will drink, she will never get into the horde of Wanderers.
- **Paying debts.** "The Steppe children remember their own debts and will certainly pay if they want to get into the Wanderers' Horde."

Strangers, according to the Shavans, do not fall into the Horde, but “on your Star”. The Embassy of the West poured at the feet of [[minerva|Minerva]] heap of legs of killed enemies. The chief explained that these people "were not Shavans" and got to the Star, and deprived of legs, will crawl there, "like worms."
