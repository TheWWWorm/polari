## The essence of the plan

The Adrian Reforms was a program of reforms that the emperor Adrian was preparing for 1774. ([[adrian|Adrian]]) It was based on two parts: the construction of rail roads throughout the empire and a general land tax, which had to pay for this construction. ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]])

[[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] put it this way. Traditionally, only the Lands of the Crown paid the Dynasty's permanent tax. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) The great Houses, rulers of counties and duchies, were indebted to the Crown for military service and other vassalage services, but not for filing. The tax was paid only by those who wanted to pay off the service, and there were few of them. Adrian, on the other hand, dreamed of connecting the entire empire with rails, and this required many times more gold than the Crown had. The only conceivable source Erwin called to file with the Great Houses: the vassal service for all lands should be abolished and replaced by a tax.

The [[golos-korony|Voice of the Crown]] recounted Adrian's speech in the House under the title The Lord's Secret Dream. The Emperor promised to connect all major cities with rails. He dreamed that the entire Polari “from the Belomorye to the Lion’s Gate” could be crossed in two weeks. According to him, then the end will come to the perpetrators, trade will put an end to hunger, on the construction sites there will be work for everyone, and the money that the land is spent on locks and lats will go to spark shops. Another note spoke of speed “in less than a month”, laying at least five hundred miles a year and the additional costs of more than a million ephesas. For this purpose, the Road Administration was established, and the collected tax was to come directly into its charge and be spent on roads and spark shops.

Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collectible edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark explains the scope of the design. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) According to her, during the reign of the five previous emperors, fewer rails were laid than Adrian outlined for one of his lives. The pace of reform was an eternal occasion for disputes with his father: Tellurian believed only in cautious changes and set aside 150 years to cover Polaris with rails and wires. Adrian wanted to keep up with his rule, thirty or forty years, and for this he needed huge power and bottomless incomes. Karen herself believes that evil made out of good intentions remains evil.

## Supporters and Opponents

The Great Houses opposed the tax. According to Erwin, the abolition of military service was a matter of honor, and to submit — by purse, and few people wanted to lose immediately “and the sword, and coins.” On the side of the emperor in the spring of 1774 there were only two Great Houses of thirteen. The law could not enter into force without the support of at least half of the House. Erwin estimated that, if confirmed, Origin would lose about seventy thousand ephes per year, and Northwood would lose about eighty-five.

Adrian himself believed that the severity of the tax and the complexity of the construction serve the lords only as a cover. Linked by rails to the capital, the feudal lords would be forced to rule with a constant eye on the Crown, and it was the loss of power that they were actually afraid of.

- **[[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]** supported the reforms: Almera was the focus of spark factories, and the growth of the rail network benefited her. ([[almera-zemlya|Duchy of Almera]])
- **[[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]**, the archpriest of the Forefather Branch, saw in rail trains, "talking wires", new laws and experiments with [[svyashchennye-predmety|Objects]] a threat to the foundations. Every Sunday he declared his attitude to the reforms from the department. In part, therefore, he hated the brother who sided with the emperor. He later told Adrian that it was not the changes themselves that were sinful, but “hurriedness” and that it was not the business of mortals to hasten the gods.
- The **[[dom-labelin|Southern Way]]** profited from the resale of goods to the North. A straight rail road to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]] and the Bear Fang would deprive him of these profits. ([[klyk-medvedya|Bear's Fang]])
- The Westerners, according to General Davey, would have "scattered" against the reforms anyway. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]])

## Voting and engagement

The House's sitting was scheduled for September 1774. Adrian hoped to get the missing votes through his own marriage: behind each of the three brides stood his own coalition of Great Houses. Erwin suggested a different path. His secret coalition — the three great lords-landlord and Archbishop of Faunterra (her name Erwin in the scene does not mention) promised to vote for reforms regardless of the choice of the bride. In return, the Litlands would receive crossings through Holliwell, the Northerners would receive the port of [[uejmar|Windley]], and Hope would receive money for a spark shop. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) According to Erwin, this money would be returned to the treasury, because the shop would be taxed under the reform law. The emperor learned that Ayden Almer had also proposed a counter-plan, but this plan included marriage to [[alanis-almera|Alanis]].

The reforms became the motive for conspiracies around the throne. Adrian considered the murder of his heirs in the summer of 1774 a warning: someone gave him time to abandon reforms, not bringing the matter to regicide. Mira, reasoning aloud with Becca, suggested that the Southern Way was scheduled for regicide at the end of August: after Valerie Gracend was named as the bride, but before the law was introduced to the House. Adrian himself said that the conspirators "will have to finish the job" - they have no other way to stop the reforms.

## Collapse

The vote never took place. After the death of Evergard in September 1774, Adrian sent a message to the Great Houses. ([[pozhar-evergarda|Fall of Evergard]]) In it, he called himself "the sole and sovereign ruler of Polari" and demanded a personal oath from the lords. Desmond Origin summed up: with the House over, Adrian became an absolute monarch. Lord Shayland's wife, speaking to the Northwoods, said that "the world we know is collapsing", and called in one row "the abolition of the House" and "universal mandatory filing". In history, there was already a similar case: under the First Dark-Eyed Dynasty, King Marion Despot tried to dissolve the House, and this ended in the death of the king and the change of the dynasty. "Lord Adrian repeated his mistake."

The old Earl Elias Northwood saw this not as Erwin's victory, but as a force of inertia. According to him, it was Adrian who wanted to turn the tide of history, and the world itself wanted to restore the old order. If Erwin did not raise the mutiny, the reforms would have stalled anyway, only for a different reason. Lords in the House in 1775 called Erwin's policies "moderate conservatism", pleasing to all "after the frenzied reforms of Adrian".

## Heritage at Minerva

Upon ascending to the throne, [[minerva|Minerva]] immediately told [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] that she would not stop Adrian's "spark reforms." In her personal plan of government, the fourteenth point was: "Restart Adrian's reforms." The rebel [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] demanded a solid, statutory tax. Erwin noted that this is one of the points of the “failed Adrianan reform”. For this reason, he hoped that the Empress would treat such a demand with sympathy.

Minerva's tax reform was designed by Frank Morlin-May, who was appointed as Minister of Taxes instead of Dreyfus Bourne. ([[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]]) Each farm and workshop of the Crown Lands was assigned a constant amount of annual tax at the level of a tithe of average income. It should be paid in the branches of banks opened in each city. Mira explained that the purpose of the reform was to get rid of the tax collectors, the source of “greed, cruelty and theft.” The reform concerned only the lands of the Crown. Morlin-May promised that it would come into force "only in winter", and by October 1775 it was already in effect. The taxes were paid at the offices of the Conto and Ferguson-Day banks, and from there the money went to the treasury accounts. ([[konto|Konto]]) Minerva had access to them even from the First Winter, so after Adrian returned, the money “flew to the North” right from under his nose. General Davey reproached her: Adrian had prepared a reform plan, and she only launched it and gave it glory.

In 2018, Roman Surzhikov wrote an article for the Polaripedia wiki cycle on Fandom about Adrian's reforms, set out as a view by Polari historians. - **Soil at Telurian.** The reforms continued the course of his father: Telurian pulled the rails from the lands of the Great Houses to the capital, gave the Houses loans for spark shops and built up a spark army. Houses gradually fell into dependence on Faunterra - through rail communication, debts for dams and because only the capital prepared people for spark technology. But Telurian followed the rule of "give today, collect tomorrow": his gifts brought the lords income now, and they were comforted by the fact that the old emperor was acting slowly. The only attempt to overthrow him was the Shutov conspiracy. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]])
- **The father-son dispute.** From the age of sixteen, Adrian accompanied his father in business and did not approve of only slowness in him. He compared the nobility to a boulder in the path of progress, which can only be moved by a sharp jerk. Tellurian answered, “You will tear the hamstrings with a sharp jerk,” and advised to take an example from the water, which has cut through the mountains for centuries.
- **Transfer of power.** In 1770, the seriously ill Tellurian handed over the actual rule to his son and died in January 1771, finally warning: "The people are a horse, but the nobility is a bridle." Before his death, he heard from three of his advisors - [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]], [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] and [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] - a promise to support the prince. By 1774, according to the novels, only two Great Houses remained on the side of the emperor.
- **Team.** Adrian raised talented people not of the nobility: [[katrin-katrin|Catherine Catherine]], Crown Raven, Minister of Science Isaac Flevin, Minister of Taxes Dreyfus Bourne and his deputy Frank Morlin-Mei. ([[mark-voron|Mark]]; [[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]])
- **Results of 1771–1773.** According to the article, four thousand miles of rails have been laid - as many as in the entire previous decade; three new dams have been drawn - on Canaan, [[torrej|Torrey]] and Lillo in [[litlend|Litland]], two of them have begun. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) There are more than thirty secular schools, four technical academies and two printing houses in the Crownlands, two new faculties at the University of Faunterra and a systematic study of [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]]; funds have been allocated for universities in Orwell and Hampton. Created the first devices "voice at a distance", later called "wave". In the novel, the same four thousand miles (and six spark shops) Adrian in 1774 announces as a plan for future construction, and not as made.
- **Reliance on common people.** Not hoping for higher knowledge, Adrian sought support from artisans and merchants: he showed the public "spark miracles" and festive riding on trains, received petitioners from the common people, simplified the filing of complaints and made the "[[golos-korony|Voice of the Crown]]" more accessible.
- **The Church is against.** [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] instructed parish priests to preach the goodness of conservatism, and the Protothean branch is closer to the common people than the Foremother. According to the article, it was this sermon that prepared the ground for the popular recognition of Adrian as a tyrant in the rebellion of Origin. ([[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern Uprising]]) Gallard was not against the changes themselves: the spark technique proved the greatness of the Foremothers. Advice to slow down, he gave, as the article estimates, good, but Adrian saw in him only an opponent.
- **Why we needed a tax.** By the end of 1773, the reforms were successful, but the treasury no longer covered the costs, although the Ministry of Finance worked as efficiently as ever. The tax reform deprived the lords of the right to arbitrarily impose taxes in their lands and, unlike rails and schools, required the law of the House, and of the thirty Lord Representatives, twenty-six were, by article, protégés of the Great Houses. In the novel, the composition of the Chamber is different: thirty-nine people, three from each land, except for the Lands of the Crown.
- **Sorry about the engagement.** [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] Adrian rejected, fearing the strengthening of Almera and not loving the arrogant lady; [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] did not consider at all, without betting on the conservative Origin. Rebecca and Valerie were discredited by court rumors provoked by [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]], and the emperor chose the neutral Minerva, which turned out to be [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]]. Success was only the marriage of Valerie with [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]], which gave an alliance with the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. Had Adrian followed his heart, the bride would have been the real Minerva, a staunch supporter of reform.
- **The main cause of the collapse.** In addition to haste, underestimation of the Great Houses and the actions of the Puppeteer, historians say the main thing: the engagement and the vote on the tax fell on one year. Together they spawned a bunch of conspiracies - Erwin's coalition, the murder of heirs by Aiden Almera, the replacement of Sybil's bride - and events got out of the Emperor's control.

- Bank Ferguson and Day boasted of having invested heavily in Adrian's reforms three times and that his money had "not one hundred miles of rails".

- Minerva herself in 1775 thought of Adrian: he “made excellent reforms, took care of people”, but allowed a mass rebellion and angered the feudal lords. There, she called him a great ruler, “perhaps the best since Juliana.”

## In "Fantasies"

"[[fantazii|Fantasies]]" the author calls possible options for the future. In them, on tax reform, “which was conceived by Adrian, and then supported by Minerva,” each working resident of Polaris paid to the treasury from sixteen to thirty-two percent of income depending on the type of occupation. The Crown spent the money at its discretion. In the main cycle, the Morlin-Mei rate is called differently - a tenth of average income.

Mother Alicia explained to Erwin in the summer of 1776 that the taxes had "reached threatening heights" back under Thelurian because the spark constructions required a lot of money. The situation was tense with Adrian. The reform of Minerva brought a relief: payment through banks eliminated the arbitrariness of collectors, but the rate itself remained the same.

After peace in the First Winter, the Lands of the Crown were collected by the Viceroy [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]. Half she sent Minerva to the First Winter, the other at her own disposal. Adrian as mayor of Faunterra received only fees from the workshops and guilds of the city. Not having the right to raise taxes and not receiving loans from Labelin, before the election he ordered the printing of paper money. Alicia warned that the banknotes will depreciate, prices will rise and the future emperor, whoever he is, will have to raise taxes.
