The legend of the Getta (about Getta, Hanosha and Ollay) is a Shavan parable about two horsemen brothers and a wonderful calf who gave them his power. ([[shavany|Shavans]]) In Yangmei Hunt, she is told by [[kharmon|Harmon]] Nisa: according to her, the legend shows "two chavans' dispositions". ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]; [[niza|Niza]]) Then she is remembered more than once by Nisa, Harmon, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] and [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]: “Gett” becomes a name for a man whom an alien force turns into a monster.

## Legend
During the time of the Swordsman Empire, a Shavan family from Reiss stood on the parking lot near its border. The swordsmen stole the cattle and took the people into slavery. Only the brothers who were on the hunt that day remained free: the stern warrior Gett and the younger, Hanosh, were "milder and kinder." In a ruined parking lot, they found a newborn calf. Gett had already drawn the sword, but the calf asked in a human voice: “Save me, brothers, I want to live very much.” The brothers realized that the Spirit of the Steppe had touched him.

The brothers followed in the footsteps of the Swordbearers, and the calf held them back. Hanosh rode to his neighbors for milk at night, and followed the trail during the day until he fell from fatigue. Gett wanted to throw the calf: a person “chooses a goal — must go to it.” With each trouble, the calf helped, “as much as I could,” and each time apologized for helping so little. Burdyuk began to fill himself with milk, the brothers who carried him on their shoulders received great power, and in the hungry dry steppe, eagle eyes and accuracy. The brothers called the calf Ollay, which in the old dialect of Stepi means “miraculous”.

The brothers overtook a detachment of forty swords and beat half of the bows. In hand-to-hand Hanosha did not have enough skill, and Gett cut for two. Then he came up with a cunning idea: he deliberately came under attack and shouted: “Oly, see how difficult it is for us?” The calf gave him the swiftness of a jaguar, and Gett dispersed the enemies. Rod was released. Gett called everyone to go further and crush the empire, but Hanosh and the other men of the family refused. Then Gett left alone, and at night he returned and stole Olly, although he asked to be released to his mother. Gett demanded strength "so that no one could defeat me", and did not believe that the calf gives everything that it can.

In the cursed land of the Swordbearers, they were surrounded by a pack of huge steppe wolves. When Gett was a hair's breadth from death, Ollay said, "It's very difficult for you. Take everything,” and gave him all the power without a trace. Gett grew up three times and got a bull's head, and the dying calf threw, "Then why do you need?" Within a year, the bull-headed giant gathered a horde of freed slaves and free riders, defeated several armies and burned a dozen cities. Shavans were afraid of him: those who asked where the power came from, Gett raised on the horns.

At Fairis, under the Mother Mills, he was met by the last formation of the Swordsmen. Their best warrior, Caderon, asked only one thing before he died: to say where such power came from. Gett decided to boast that the empire was crushed by the power stolen from the half-dead calf, but could not utter a word because of the hiccups. Enemies laughed, and while everyone laughed, Caderon killed him. The Horde left without a fight.

Hanosh was secretly following his brother. He picked up the abandoned Olly, went out and took his family back to the Steppe. There he married and raised four children. Ollay lost his magic and voice, but survived and left behind offspring. Hanosh was afraid that one of the descendants of Ollay would again gain strength and give birth to a new monster - "But this did not happen."

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
### The Story of Niza
At the end of March 1775 in Melison, Harmon does not know why he should live on: he no longer delights in money, and considers it meaningless to sell it for the service of the Second of the Five Nizas. She asks: if he wants it, why does he not take it, and if he wants fame, why does he not perform a feat? Then, “as if gaining strength,” tells the legend. For the silent Nisa, such a long story is already a sign of sympathy.

She explains this way: foreigners, hearing the word "shavan", represent Guetta - those who grab everything they reach, and trade people. But the Steppe also gives birth to Hanosha. Nisa's father was Hanosh, and she hopes to become one. She calls Harmon neither Gett nor Hanosh, but Ollay: he has many hidden wonders - a heavenly ship, money, a good heart - but he does not know how to dispose of them, and without care he is helpless. An offended Harmon takes her to the basement and shows the Light Sphere. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) Niza asks how many he killed for her. Four... Maybe five,” and how much good he did with it. Stopping Sphere's ring with her palm, she says, "I was wrong: you're more Gett than Ollay," and leaves. At night, Harmon barks: showed the Sphere, confessed to the murders — and, of course, became “worse than Guetta from that fairy tale.”

## In "Humans and Gods"
### Children's Memory of Erwin
Erwin first heard the legend as a child with Iona. ([[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]]) In the First Winter, a half-crazy old Shavan from Halliwell served as a stableman, and he told the children steppe legends. Among them were stories about Verilla-Wind and the beautiful Aliled, but this one was remembered more vividly than others by Erwin. The kind and strong Hanosh became an “unattainable ideal” for him: Erwin dreamed of being kind, but was weak, and the kindness of the weak, as he thought, was only cowardice. Gett seemed to be the embodiment of pure evil, and most of all the boy was frightened that the Shavans went after the monster "with great joy." Erwin recalls this on June 1, 1775, before negotiating with [[ganta-groza|Ganta Thunderstorm]].

### The New Gett
Persuading Rebecca Litland to marry Moran of the Steppe Fire, Erwin recounts a legend to her. ([[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]]) In his retelling, Guetta was stopped by "the virtue of one warrior and the magic of a calf." The conclusion he makes is: “Moran Steppe Fire is Gett.” According to his calculations, the Puppeteer will send to Moran his man - [[paul|Paul]] with the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]] - to turn the leader into a monster, and then the "most terrible war in recent centuries" will begin. Rebecca refuses first. Later, in a letter to Erwin, she still goes to the Steppe: “However, the new Gett is about to be born in the Steppe. I can, I have to stop it.”

Niza remembers Ollay again when [[magda-labelin|Magda]]'s men let her go to Harmon: she was told that he could not cope with the assignment, and she replied that "the holy calf Ollay is all capable."

## Meaning
- **Two habits of Shavans.** Niza tells the legend as a response to an alien view of the Steppe: Shavan is not only Gett, but Hanosh as well.
- **Another's power.** Guetta's power is taken from the defenseless and therefore "mysterious": he cannot even name its source. Harmon with the Stolen Sphere, Moran with the Fingers of the Puppeteer, Adrian with Objects try on this role. *A generalization is a reading, not a direct statement of the book.*
- **Take or stop.** Guetta's motto "You can take more - take it!" is contrasted with Hanosha, who took his family home in time. To Harmon, who has "no desires," Nisa tells the legend right after questions about what he wants.
