## Description

Few saw the Lord's medallion nearby; one of them was the jester [[menson|Manson]]. The object is a glass rod with steel rings at both ends, "as if a tiny transparent axis in the hubs." The decoration is elegant and simple, not to the emperor's face. But if you bring the axis to the eyes, behind the glass there is a figure of a woman - not flat, as in ordinary medallions, but voluminous and living, "as if not a picture, but a real, living person visible in the telescope." By turning the rod, you can bring a woman closer or farther away, to the point that her face will fill the entire field of view.

Manson didn't know this lady. It was not the late Lady [[imperatritsa-ingrid|Ingrid]], not one of Adrian's sisters who died in early childhood, and not the court lady of those who sought the role of favorite. She could have been considered a Pramaterica - the features were too perfect for mortal - but she did not resemble any of the Pramaterica. Manson realized that the woman was long dead, and never asked the lord about her.

In the spring of 1775, when Erwin and Minerva were wondering what "Object X" could be stored separately from the rest, Minerva remembered that Adrian was wearing a "fool with the image of the goddess", which she once glimpsed. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] explained that the pendant is a small Item and the Puppeteer would not look for it on the registers: it has only one supernatural property, a “wonderfully bright image”, and small Items are not entered in the registers. According to David's father, Adrian began wearing the Item around his neck, imitating his mother wearing the Sun Drop. ([[otets-david|Father David]]; [[kaplya-solntsa|Drop of the Sun]])

## History

### In the life of the Lord
In "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]", in a difficult moment for him, Adrian took the medallion out of his sinuses and, without looking, rubbed it in his palm. It seemed to Manson as if something had flowed into the hands of the Lord, which caused a “black fog” over his head to gather drops, and in the air it smelled of stormy freshness. Hearing a knock on the door, Adrian dropped the locket under his shirt.

### Beck Train Disaster
During the crash at Beck, Adrian, after getting out with Manson ashore, decided that he should be considered dead. ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]]) According to the story of the jester at the trial, the lord exchanged clothes with the drowned captain Grace and hooked the Eternal Hilt and the pendant onto his body. The agent who reported to Minerva about the discovery of the body, cited as evidence that he found the Eternal Hilt and a “medallion with a divine woman”: “Only the lord could wear them.” Minerva asked when she will receive the “Eternal Hilt and the pendant” Items that had been on the corpse for a long time were cleaned for two days and then handed over to her.

### Gift "From the Spark"
Leaving Faunterra before the return of Adrian, Minerva took the regalia of power, but left the Object, spiritually close to the lord: hung a pendant on a chain on the corner of the gilded frame of his coronation portrait and tied with a ribbon with the inscription "From the Spark". In early September 1775, after Adrian's triumphant entry into Faunterra, he was found by [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]. Unwilling to praise her rival in her husband's eyes, she tore and threw away the ribbon, and Adrian was told that the pendant "was lying in Mimi's bedroom under the bed." Adrian brought the object to his eyes and met the goddess with a warm look, “like a long-time friend or half-sister,” and his wife said “Thank you, dear” in a completely different tone than before.

### Before the battle and after
In the carriage of Objects, waiting for Lady in Darkness, Adrian sat on the transparent cover of [[chrevo|the Womb]], rubbed the pendant and looked into it with a dreamy look. Magda asked to see, but he hid the pendant in his fist: “Nothing special... Just a goddess.” After the imprisonment of the Puppeteer in the Wreath, when Francis-Ilianus mentioned that only unsuccessful defrosting was dangerous, Adrian “for some reason” touched the pendant and shook his head, as if rolling away sorrow.

## Who is depicted in the pendant

The woman is not named in the text. In the last chapter of the cycle, [[navi|Navi]], telling about the death of his starship, extended his hand to Adrian, and he immediately understood what was needed: he took off his neck and gave the pendant. Navi looked at the vision for a long time, and tears came out in his eyes, after which he said: “Lylanna died on impact. The worm has preserved her body.” From this scene it follows that in the medallion, apparently, Lylanna is depicted - a companion of Navi and [[paul|Paul]], whose body Adrian in his youth, in August 1756, saw in a transparent coffin in the bed of the Seventeenth Gift. Where Adrian has this Item, the text does not directly say.
