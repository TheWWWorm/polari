The author in this article is not a real writer, but a character of a literary game. The real author of the cycle “Polars” is [[roman-surzhikov|Roman Surzhikov]] (his own statements are collected in the article “The Author’s Word”). ([[slovo-avtora|The Author's Word]]) In the collection edition of the first novel Surzhikov writes notes on behalf of Lady [[karen|Karen]]. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) In this game, [[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]] is a book written and published in Polari himself about the events of 1774. Its author is an unnamed contemporary of the heroes, and Karen reads his novel as a critic and familiar to many actors.

All that is said below is Karen's words. She is biased, often pricked, and some of her "amendments" themselves turn out to be Surzhikov's jokes. Therefore, the information is given with the reservation “according to Karen”. For the book itself as an object of peace, see Arrow, Coin, Spark (a book in the world of Polari). ([[strela-moneta-iskra-v-mire|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Book within Polari)]])

## Who He Is
Karen does not give the name of the author and apparently does not know him. She calls him “our author”, “our kind author”, “literator”, and in hearts she calls him “slippery” and “pisaka”. All that is known about him is her guesses and random information.
- **Origin.** The chapters about [[kharmon|Harmon]] do his best. Karen's guess: maybe the author is low-born? Then it is clear why the common people are alive, and where it is about the Lords, "so many slaps."
- **Age.** Sir [[ben-vomak|Womack]], scolding the author, called him an "old devil".
- **Dating.** The opening scene of one of the chapters betrays, according to Karen, the author’s close acquaintance with doctors and their habits.
- **Views.** Karen accuses him twice of having a “patriarchal worldview.” He settled the monastery in the women's boarding house of Elena-u-Lake, where the monastery is not. ([[pansion-eleny-u-ozera|Elena-by-the-Lake Boarding School]]) He forgot Duke Jonas Lighthart's daughter, [[menson|Manson]]'s wife, that is, Karen herself: "Of course women don't count."
- **Courtyard.** Karen is not sure if the author has been to the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]]. Some descriptions are accurate, in others slaps, as if memory fails him at unexpected moments.
- **Army.** He did not see a sincere infantryman, according to Karen. Manson also doubts whether the author knows anything about the imperial army.
- **Multiplication.** Having met in the book a street writer of messages Velereky Jerome, Karen suggests that this is the "second name" of the author.
- **Fertility.** By the time of remarks, he is already writing the sixth book, and it is no thinner than the previous ones.

## Mistakes and “blunders”
Catching the author on the mistakes is the main thing Karen does. Even in the foreword, she promised to point out the stupidity “with all directness.” By the end of the book, according to her, there are fewer obvious blunders, and the more joy she brings to everyone found. She calls three favorites: [[pramater-yanmej|Yanmei]], the patroness of flowers, [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]] on a caravel, and [[minerva|Minerva]], who teaches the general military affairs.

### Military affairs, weapons and armor
- **"Sword".** At the time of the Northern Flash, the long blades were very different, and the collective “sword” almost did not sound. The author, according to Karen, "threw everything into one pile." Therefore, she can not say what kind of blade [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] had, although she assumes a leadling.
- **"The Ring".** In the era of the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]], mail was still worn, but not now. Karen suspects that the author calls any patchwork protection of the hull, like a sword - any long blade.
- **Spark spears.** For a long time, Karen endured the "spear" of spark infantry, but eventually fell off. The "double tip" is actually a self-shooter: two darts with spark eyes shoot a spring. Iskravik hits from five to ten yards into the horse, and with a spear he kills the survivors.
- **Mark's sword.** Once the author “brilliant knowledge”, but not to the place. [[mark-voron|Mark]] is the son of a shoemaker, he did not learn fencing, and protection agents are taught dagger and hand-to-hand combat.
- **Northern tactics.** The author doesn’t know who Greg is. Gray is a servant and student of [[kajry|kair]], not a vestige or vassal. He does not understand why the kair after initiation suddenly transplanted to a horse. The northerners are swordsmen, although in every northern company there are arrows.
- **The Imperial Army.** A note to one chapter is written by Manson. The company, he said, is divided into dozens, and the recruit would first be asked who his sergeant is. Manson's conclusion: "either Paul is an ass or an author" knows nothing about the army.
- **Caravella.** Richard, according to Karen, did not sail on a caravel, which the fleets wrote off two centuries ago, but on a schooner.
- **Chavistas and prisoners.** Erwin's idea that no army captures women is considered a mistake by the author, not the hero. [[shavany|Shavans]] willingly take, and Erwin's grandmother herself visited the steppes in captivity.

### Etiquette, titles and court orders
- **The Countess.** This is strictly called the wife of the ruling count. The author twice calls the Countess [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia]] and at the same time does not mention her father, [[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]].
- **Primitives.** Only five older births are named. ([[pervorodnye|Firstborn]]) Shayland's not one of them.
- **Distance from the Lord.** During the solemn greetings, only the guards and the secretary stand next to the emperor. The rest are held over four yards.
- **Flemings.** The author does not distinguish between the daughters of the Earl of [[fleming|Fleming]], and Karen calls him a "straw".
- **Stereotypes.** The author's serenades are sung for the lady, although in fact - for the boyfriends of the gentleman. His knights perform feats for the sake of the lady's heart, and Karen only laughs.
- **The capital letters.** “Empire”, “Great House”, “University” the author writes with a capital letter, then with a small one, and Karen “brings clarity”.

### Laws and the Church
- **Dissolution of marriage.** The church, according to Karen, dissolves the marriage only in two cases: if the bride was pregnant on the day of the engagement or did not give birth to her husband in four years.
- **Mantle of the Priory.** Vladyka can't legally take [[gallard-almera|Gallard]]'s robe. He can only put pressure on the [[tserkov|Church]] until its cathedral itself deposes the parish.
- **Church in the House.** ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) The claim that the Church does not have a direct voice in the House is called nonsense by Karen.
- **Julian Law.** ([[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]) It all starts with a reproach for the name: Juliana the Great was a woman, so the law of Julian. In the appendix, Karen writes that the author attributed to the Duke of Litland a vague notion of the law. This is not a single law or even a set, but a “complex of vaults”.
- **Section of the Gift.** According to custom, the [[semnadtsatyj-dar|Gift]] Items are divided into three parts. The Earl of Shayland was to receive forty-seven, not thirty-nine. Maybe he gave eight to someone, “or our author just calculated.”

### Geography and Capital
- From Stagforth to [[uejmar|Waymar]] about three hundred miles: in three days there is no way. ([[stagfort|Stagfort]])
- Farway's people built a railroad not to [[litlend|Litland]], but to [[aleridan|Aleridan]]. On the way to Litland, the water does not freeze in winter, as the author himself writes about. (In the 2026 edition of the media, this is corrected: the road leads to Aleridan.)
- The main feature of the Palace Bridge over Hanai is that it is movable. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) The author writes about handrails in the form of crossed swords.

### History and Faith
- **Agathans** rule the North not for six hundred years, but for a thousand hundred: since the time when the capital was [[lid|Leed]].
- **The Miriam dynasty.** The “rebels” who put an end to it are the soldiers of the founder of Olgard, the first emperor of the current dynasty. They should be known as knights.
- **Lightharts.** ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]]) The Latin with the cross is the coat of arms of the Farway, not Hope. ([[dom-farvej|House Farway]]) Here, "formally, the author is right," but Karen is outraged at the way he has crumpled up Hope's story. The son of Duke Jonas Edgar, he "didn't notice."
- **Alfred Almera.** Brother [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] the author “written off” ahead of time, and then still remembered about it.
- **Fathers.** The author’s ignorance about Maximian angers Karen the most: “Where did this writer study?” Yangmei is the patroness of flowers. ([[pramater-yanmej|Yanmei the Merciful]]) He calls the collection of the Forebears "Acts", although everyone says "Proverbs".
- The Light Sphere came only with the [[semnadtsatyj-dar|Seventeenth Gift]], and the Forebears did not see it. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]])
- **Dates.** [[minerva|Minerva]] was born on August 24, and this is now known to both the author and "any educated Polish citizen."

### Misunderstood people
- [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]] in the book looks like a "dumb and evil despot". Karen believes that the real Duke at least hinted to his son about the true purpose of the exploit. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]])
- General Davey is similar in the book to a Northwood man, and was born in the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]]. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]])
- The scene where Minerva sets Aidan on Labelin, Karen declares "pure fiction" and "cheap move": the author, in her opinion, wanted to throw a false suspicion on Labelin. ([[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]; [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]])
- The letter of the Duke to the rector of the Maximians, according to her, is also clearly made up, “and very embarrassing.”
- The author described the meeting at Desmond's bedside without [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]], although he was there (see below).

## What he knows surprisingly accurately
Karen's praises are rare, but there are.
- The first chapter on [[smolden|Smolden]] is generally reliable. Karen is even sorry that it stands first: after nine chapters of "chush and heresy," it would be a rest.
- The expedition Karen checked with the memoirs of the two participants and, “about the miracle”, found it transmitted more or less correctly, even the moods of the characters. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]])
- The portrait of Countess Sybil Karen, familiar with her, is ready to confirm “every word”. ([[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]])
- Erwin's feelings and thoughts, according to Karen's niece, are conveyed as if he were writing himself.
- [[ajden-almera|Aiden]]’s words in one of the chapters are very similar to the original Duke: in them, according to Karen, the whole “color of the Almeran nobility”.
- The policy of the Crown, pitting the great lords, is shown with a "thin understanding".
- Rumors about the adventures of [[ben-vomak|Womack]] Karen is inclined to believe the truth: so violently the knight reacted to the questions.
- About [[dejmon-oridzhin|Damon]], the author “did not err against the truth”: that day, he commanded a company in the gorge of the Creature of Heaven. ([[stvorki-neba|Gates of Heaven]])
- About the Shepherd's Meadows on Holliwell the author writes correctly. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]])

The most mysterious chapter is a conversation between [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil]], [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] and Minerva in front of the monastery. Many readers consider it a fiction, but Karen is "forced to stand up for the author" and believes it was. It’s amazing how the author found out. The participants would not tell the writer anything. Karen suggests that he went to Northwood to [[vanden|Vanden]], whom the Countess sent to the North, because he heard too much. It turns out that for the sake of one conversation, the writer made a two-month journey.

### That he is "dark on purpose"
Sometimes Karen admits that the author is not silent because of ignorance. About the chapter with Father David, she writes that Father David lies in many ways, and the author “inserts a fog”, but this time on purpose. ([[otets-david|Father David]]) Secrets are likely to be revealed in other books, and Karen does not want to spoil the reading. She also explains the strange marriage, approved by Desmond: the author “did not want to reveal to us the full picture.” About the role of Violet Fleming Karen is silent, because the author may still tell about it himself: “I will not take away his bread.”

Not all of his fictions are condemned by Karen. Finding in the archive a genuine letter from Captain Corvis, not at all like a book, she remembers that the novel is artistic. The author is not obliged to accurately cite the documents and could have invented the letter, and Corvis himself. She found no trace of [[polli|Polly]] in [[labelin|Labelin]], [[niar|Niar]], or Izluchin. ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]) Perhaps the author invented it, but Karen wants to believe that the heroine had a living prototype.

## Manner of letter
- **Slow start.** Karen only finds the conclusion of the coin line in the fifth chapter, almost a hundred pages later, and mocks the "discovery in the theory of literature." She promises to write a novel where the tie will be on the last page.
- **Cheap receptions.** Information for the reader, the author puts into the mouth of the heroes. False suspects are introduced, then surprised by the turn.
- **Poetry.** In the former edition, [[polli|Polly]]'s verses were interrupted by prose. According to Karen, someone explained to the author that a bun with rotten raisins was worse than a bun without raisins, and the poems were removed.
- **Cliché.** Myra’s lover, she says, is “the absolute cliché of a girl in love.”
- **Horrors in a ladies' novel.** Manson, writing for his wife over the terrible chapters, scolds the author: the book is “clear for ladies”, why is it such horror? “I’d rather write something funny.”
- **Tragic comedy.** The neighborhood of the funny and scary Karen, on the contrary, considers the strongest side: it touches the soul.
- **Inaccuracy.** The author, she says, “rejects an aversion to accuracy.” But it is often impossible to praise him: men and writers, over-praised, “begin to wonder.”

In the afterword, Karen sums up. The court life of the author is naive, but awakens nostalgia. The battle scenes are not very reliable, but alive. Most of all - the feelings of the characters: the suffering of people she knows are shown so sincerely that Karen has ceased to be indifferent to the text.

## The attitude of heroes to the book
Karen repeatedly wondered why the prototypes of the heroes did not call the author to a duel and did not send assassins to him. Minerva in the book often looks like a fool in love, Erwin is a walking misunderstanding, and all [[dom-almera|Almera]] is a scoundrel. She began to question the participants and was surprised to learn that almost everyone was happy with their portraits and even flattered.
- **[[minerva|Minerva]]**, according to Karen, is widely known for her appreciation of the book. Karen doesn't understand that.
- General Davey prides himself on being portrayed as a rude simpleton: saying what you think is his privilege. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) Reading the chapter aloud, he found the book a comedy and regretted only Polly.
- Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] called the book “empty”: the author, according to him, only pleases the [[dom-oridzhin|Origins]]. And yet he sent the author a hundred Hilt for how he described his daughter [[magda-labelin|Magda]] in the "Yanmei Hunt".
- [[ben-vomak|Womack]] cheese is the only exception. When he heard the question about the scene with him, he groped and cursed the "cursed scribe".
- **[[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]]** is not offended that the author forgot him at a meeting at Desmond's bedside: he is pleased with his portrait in the second novel.
- Captain [[uitmor|Whitmore]] mistakenly read the wrong chapter and praised it. He refused to read about the “trader” Harmon.
- **[[menson|Manson]]** laughs at the author's mistakes and argues with his wife. Karen even advises readers not to discuss the book with their husbands.

## Other books by the author
According to the notes, it is clear that Arrow, Coin, Spark is only the first novel by the author about those events.
- "Only One Star" is the second novel. He is pleased with Robert Origin (actually - "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only one star]]").
- The Yanmei Hunt is the book where [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]] appears. It is spoken of by the Duke of Maurice (actually, "Yanmei Hunt"). ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]])
- **The sixth book.** Karen has heard that the author is already writing the sixth book, which is not inferior in thickness to the previous one, and refuses to comment on it in advance.

Karen herself in the “other books” sees a place where the secrets of the first will be revealed. In addition to the author's novels, about the same events, according to her, nine historical works and more than a dozen novels were written.
