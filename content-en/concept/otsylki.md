References, or paschalks, in Polari are winks to the reader: a phrase, name, or joke, behind which an earth book, film, or winged expression is guessed. There aren't many. The world is closed, and the hero has no place to know the earthly books, so the Easter eggs hide in three places:
- In the speeches of those who know more than they should: [[navi|Navi]] is an alien from the world of the “dark”;
- in jokes of characters with sharp tongue, first of all [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]];
- in "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]", where the author allows himself more game.

The descriptions of the Objects themselves (the Voice of God is like a telephone, the Absolute is like a teleporter) are not included here: it is a device of the world, not an Easter egg. Below are the Easter eggs from the books, in order of release. Separately, it is noted that the author confirmed and that is only a coincidence. Interpretations without the label "author's word" are wiki observations.

## What the author confirmed
- **Erwin's name.** According to the author, "the very name Erwin" refers "to two famous characters: Erwin Rommel and Erwin Schrodinger", but he did not sketch Rommel's biography. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]]’s military and political career, according to the author, is more similar to Gaius Julius Caesar, although this was “not intended” (see Word of the author). ([[slovo-avtora|The Author's Word]])
- **Agatha's dilemma.** The author explained that Agatha's dilemma is more Heisenberg's uncertainty principle than Schrodinger's cat. Read more in the section about “Humans and Gods”.
- **Oh, God damn it.** The author conceived an interlude where the devil comes to the village "in a purely Gogol style". It was never written, and the plot gave the parable of Grandfather - see the section on "A Doll on the Throne".

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"
- **Archimedes.** In the second chapter, Erwin goes home with the news: “Let me talk to my father — and I will turn the world upside down.” This is a reworking of Archimedes’ words: “Give me a foothold and I will turn the Earth upside down.” The point of support here is the father, Duke [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]].
- **Icewind.** In the nineteenth chapter, the northern name is first heard: "Dustin Gloria of Icewind, second cousin of the Duke of Origin." Later, Baron [[kurt-ajsvind|Kurt Icewind]] appears. The name literally repeats Icewind Dale (Icewind Valley) from the Forgotten Kingdoms - the edges of Salvatore's books and the computer game of the same name. By itself, the northern "ice" name sounds natural, so it is rather a neighborhood with Morrowind that makes it an Easter egg (see "Humans and Gods").

## "A Doll on the Throne"
**Damn it, Gogol.** In the chapter "Pero - 8" [[ded|Grandfather]] tells [[minerva|Minerva]] a parable: "One kid lived on the outskirts of the Empire. One day he went on a long and dangerous journey, and the devil got behind him. The devil is called Hen, he "could manage luck", and when offended - "clicked his fingers - the guy immediately choked on an apple." The village devil, who is found with a simple guy and is harmful, is a Gogol image, and the author confirmed that he kept Gogol in his head (see Proverbs of Deda). ([[pritchi-deda|Grandfather's Parables]])

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
**Griboyedov, "Caret to me, coach!"** In the chapter "Coin - 7" someone depicts an insulted Chatsky: ""Caret me, now!" "Gitan shouted and coughed for some reason." The narrator winks at the reader once more: here “often there were lovers of shouting: “Caret, now!”

## In "Humans and Gods"
- **Morrowind.** In the chapter "Star-1" the ship with Paul goes through the Hanai delta "on the sleeve leading to Morrowind - the last town before falling into the sea." This is the title of the third part of The Elder Scrolls series (Morrowind 2002). This is the only name in the cycle that coincides with the well-known game letter in the letter; the town itself does not appear in the plot: Paul tells to fold into a "fast sleeve" that "goes by Morrowind straight into the sea."
- **The uncertainty principle.** In the chapter “Witness 3,” Navi explains why God cannot simply tell the future: “In our underworld, this phenomenon is called the uncertainty of the name of one ancient god, and the name there is such that the tongue will break. And you have Agatha's dilemma... It's simple. The mere fact of reporting changes the course of events.” The “ancient god” with a difficult name is Heisenberg, the author confirmed. For [[pramater-agata|Agatha]], this means that her gift of seeing the future is bound by the same law.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
- **"The Death Note".** The most obvious cycle reference. In the chapter "Witness - 3" [[adrian|Adrian]] invites his wife to guess what kind of fruit the Great Tree has. Magda first calls the statue “the statue of bliss”: “You put it on the square, and people meekly obey, never rebel.” Then - the "paper of death": "Write on a sheet the name of the enemy - he dies. You have to give the date and time.” These are the rules of the manga book and the anime Death Note: the name of the victim, and, if desired, the time and circumstances of death. Adrian replies, "Milady, nothing like that."
- **Dialectics.** In the same scene, Magda tries again. She recalls two visitors: "One is a frail snot, the second is a brutal killer. It means... oh! Unity and the struggle of opposites?! This is the first law of Hegel’s dialectics in Engels’s exposition, familiar to everyone who handed over philosophy.
- **Strugatsky, "Picnic on the roadside."** The story ends with the words “Happiness for all, free, and let no one leave offended!” Erwin recounts the purpose of the Order in Arrow 8: "Every resident of Polaris will receive his talking Object. There will be great abundance. Happiness is for everyone, and no one is offended.” Minerva in "Iskra - 1" grumbles: "Progress ... One great happiness for all. Let each one of you think.” “Picnic” the author called the most iconic book for himself Strugatsky.
- **Maxwell's Demon.** In Spark 2, Navi describes the [[vechnyj-efes|Eternal Hilt]]: "A three-sided transparent Object, similar to a dagger. Entropy mirror. Demon of the second kind. The symbol of a brilliant dynasty.” The “demon” and the “entropy” together are Maxwell’s demon, a mental experience with a being that breaks the second principle of thermodynamics. The "second kind" refers to the eternal engine of the second kind. The name “Eternal Hilt” also sounds like a “perpetual engine”.
- **A parody of scientific jargon.** In Iskra 8 Navi says, "Sublimated time quanta with the inverse sign, antimatter of the second kind." Minerva responds in the same way: “I know a lot of them myself. Listen: exaltation, infantilism, passionary ..." “Passionary” is a term of Lev Gumilyov, a joke for the Russian reader.
- **The Trojan War and Sodom.** Navi, looking at Polari, says directly: “You have a real Middle Ages here, exactly in fantasy! And you want to be a god, to be executed and to have mercy, to make destinies - easier than easy! Take a few Items and make a Trojan War, even Sodom and Gomorrah. The alien can call earthly names, so here the references sound open. "Being a god" among savages is the theme of Strugatsky's "Hard to be a god".

## In "Fantasies"
- **“You can’t just take it and...”** In "Fantasy of the Fifth: Amicable", Prince Hector warns [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]] about her sister: "You can not just take and bring her to the tender soul of the groom - from sudden happiness can happen injuries." ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) This is a meme from The Lord of the Rings: Boromir says that you can not just enter Mordor. Throughout the cycle, the turn occurs only here.
- **"The North Remembers".** In Fantasy Six: Closer and Further, Erwin tells the ambassador, "The North remembers you as marauders, robbers, and femicide." This is the Starks motto from The North Remembers. Martina is one of my favorite writers.
- **Detective.** The latest fantasies are written as a classic detective: a locked room, evidence, a train with a passenger. In "Fantasy Nine: Passenger", detectives find things "in the name of John Doe" and conclude: "So John Doe is a dead man." John Doe is the conditional name of an unidentified body in American law and film, and the murder on the train is a greeting to Agatha Christie.

## Coincidences or references?
Here is collected what looks like a reference, but there is no direct evidence.
- **Forty-two.** Henry Horton’s nickname is reminiscent of “the answer to the main question of life, the universe and all that” from Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy. ([[sorok-dva|Forty-Two]]) But in the book, the nickname has its own explanation: at the tournament, he “earned forty-two points.” The number occurs in the cycle in other places, for example in Navi: "With a probability of forty-two percent." Nearby, in the "Shadow of the Great Tree", Minerva commands: "Don't panic! Everything is going according to plan!” "No Panic" is also the motto of Adams' guide.
- **Gloves of Power and Physical.** Minerva's glove works very similar to the Physics Gun from Garry's Mod: an invisible "beam" grabs an object by hand, holds it in the air at weight, carries it and hurls it. In The Shadow of the Great Tree, Minerva "accurately aimed a ray and snatched a spear from it", and Inge Price "carefully lifted ... four rays". In Fantasias, Iona "forgot to untangle the rays from the harpsichord. At the stroke of the hand, the instrument took off like a core of stone" - exactly as in Garry's Mod they launch objects with a fisgan. But telekinesis in rays is a long-standing image of fiction, and there are no direct hints in the text, so this is most likely a coincidence.
- **Navi.** This is the name of the fairy-tip in Zelda and the people in Avatar. Navi himself calls himself the “god of navigation,” and this explanation is simpler.
- **“The objects are not on fire!”** In "Confident" sounds a phrase that resembles "Manuscripts do not burn" Bulgakov. It's probably a coincidence.
- **“Those we forgive.”** The name of the interlude reminds the prayer “Our Father”: “as we forgive our debtors.”
- **Alice Cavendish.** Professor Ollie's assistant in Fantasia is named physicist Henry Cavendish. There is nothing in the text to indicate this.

## Observations
- Paschalok to mass culture in “Polar” is not enough: the search for hundreds of key phrases from movies, TV shows and memes gave only “Notebook of death”, “You can not just take” and “the North remembers”.
- Easter eggs almost never break the peace: Magda, Gitan and Erwin say what they could say themselves, and the reader learns an earthly quote in this. Right earthly names - Troy, Sodom - is called only by the alien Navi.
- Magda's jokes about the Tree are built as a collection of miracle objects from fiction and philosophy: the statue of submission, the notebook of death, dialectics. Adrian rejects them all: the fruit of the Tree is not a thing, but a discovery.
