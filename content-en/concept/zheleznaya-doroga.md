The **railway** is the main technical miracle of the Polari of the 18th century. There are trains on the railways that pull a spark tractor. He takes the current with metal "horns" from a wire stretched from post to post along the way. There are no steam locomotives in Polari: even the smoke above the cars is steam from spark water heaters.

## Train
[[minerva|Mira]] first saw the train in 1774 at the station in [[labelin|Labelin]]. The bronze tractor, the size of which was not inferior to the "fortress tower, put side by side", stood on eighteen wheels, each "was higher than Mira". He had two huge glass "eyes"-flashlight, and in the front - a triangular ram to sweep the barriers. It was followed by seven cars of lacquered mahogany, each named after one of the Pramaterie: Mira rode in the car "Sophia the Great", in the "Elena the Seer" was a library. ([[pramateri|Foremothers]]) The wheels are covered with Litland rubber, and the smaller wheels from the inside rest against the rail so that the composition does not jump off the path. On the roof there is a veranda with benches and copper water tanks. The train goes at a speed of fast lynx, about seven to eight miles per hour, making stops twice a day, and travels three hundred miles from Labelin to Faunterra in about two days. There are trains faster: in the summer of 1774, the Duke of Farway drove the "superfast train" three hundred and forty miles to the Heart of Light in less than a day, and from Faunterra to Arden (sixty miles) the train goes four hours. There are third, second, first and "higher" classes in trains. A third-class ticket costs two or three Elenas, and thirty or forty people are stuffed into the car; the place of the upper class from Labelin to the capital cost Harmon nine ephes. Between Labelin and Marengo there is a "buyer's" train with cheap seats and a lot of freight cars; the capital it passes at four o'clock in the morning.

The Empress had her own train. Minerva's personal train in 1775 consisted of seven cars: in the first and last there was an azure guard, in the second - a horse, in the third - servants, in the fourth - important guests, in the fifth there was a two-storey refectory with a tea salon in the attic. In the penultimate car, the august rooms, there was a bedroom, an office, a small library, a game room with a table for execution and a tea room.

Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collectible edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark tells how horses are driven. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) At first, they were put along the train, eight in the car, and tickets were prohibitively expensive. Then the wagon was widened and turned horses across, and they began to fit sixteen. The horses were nervous. Then the horse cars were made closed, and in front of the muzzles put a wall with a large picture of the city and flat silhouettes of people. Silhouettes sway from the train, and the horses calm down: they think that they are standing, and people are passing by. In case some horse doesn't believe it, a horse with a bag of apples and sugar enters the car.

### Tractor
The face of the tractor is decorated with a directional sign. In [[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]], [[kharmon|Harmon]] and [[polli|Polly]] see a train with a bronze eagle with wings spread out on the nose of the tractor, and the train that was going to Hope is decorated with a mirror sun; a gold pen and a sword on the face mean that the train is going to Faunterra. Harmon, who has seen the Bronze Monsters many times, did not understand the power that moves them: “Hundreds of horses would have to be harnessed” to move such a weight. In A Doll on the Throne, Erwin shows Mira a drawing in his notebook: a square tractor, whose windows and lanterns folded in his face, and a path-cleaning knife resembled a beard.

In an interlude of The [[dusha-zapada|Soul of the West]], Becca Litland recalls believing as a child that a dozen horses were sitting inside the tractor and pushing their hooves away from their sleepers. ([[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) Father paid the train driver Gloria, he started the car idle and brought the girl inside. There, among the "tubes, cylinders, coils, wires, shafts, wheels," she did not understand anything and ran away: "Inside the tractor there was chaos, much more complex than the rest of the world I knew."

According to the “[[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]]”, the crew of the spark tractor is six people: a senior machinist, mechanics and others. There are also small station tractors: in the "Yanmei Hunt", Erwin rushed to Arden on an urgent train from such a tractor and two cars. ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]) The tractor buzzes before departure and on the way to the station, roars on the move and, according to Karen, whistles. In Fantasia, Erwin calls it the limit of current technology: “Now the limit of our technology is a spark tractor.”

### Wagons and amenities
Passenger trains are luxurious: lacquered painted sides, gilded veneers, carved ornaments, sculptures on the roofs, stained glass windows, balconies on which passengers stand in transit. A white haze rises above the cars - Harmon explains to Polly that it is not smoke, but steam: thanks to the spark in the train, there is always hot water for tea drinking and ablution. In winter, the cars heat up; in the broken composition on the Bake, coals flew from the furnaces, and the train caught fire right in the water.

In "A Doll on the Throne", [[mark-voron|Mark]] travels first class from Aleridan: lackeys in red caps of imperial rail roads with sheets and in vestiges with brass buttons put their luggage, bring first-class passengers a "welcome jug of wine" as a gift from the road, take orders in a compartment or in a refectory car. The lower luggage compartment and horse wagon are closed on the way, but on the roofs of the cars there are communicating balconies, along which you can walk to the “moving stables” and visit your horse. Before departure, the footmen pass through the corridor with a bell, the station heralds wish a happy journey, the cars creak with springs and knock with cast-iron couplings. In the best imperial trains, passengers are given warm soft slippers. In the "Fantasies" coupe of the first class is equipped with an armchair, a reading lamp, a book shelf, a glove box, a spark heating pad and a "soft light mode" without glare in the window.

From the train you can send a message "wave": Minerva in 1775 sent a wave to the capital from his personal train.

## How to build the road
The mechanic Luis Maria, who marked the way through the Crystal Mountains in 1774, explained the main limitation to kairam: the loaded composition cannot go steeply uphill, the tractor will not have enough power. ([[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]]) Hence the rule of five coppers: if you put one end of a foot of wooden ruler on the ground, and the other on a pile of five copper coins, you get the greatest inclination that the train is able to defeat. Through the depressions and gorges you need to build bridges, small hollows to fill with stone, steep cliffs to bypass serpentine, sometimes giving a hook in an extra dozen miles. If you can not get around the mountain, it is cut through a tunnel - few of the soldiers have seen this.

The path goes along a stone embankment, along it there are pillars with a wire, and on deserted areas there are “rare turrets with spark machinery”. Spark lines are given by dams and castles: a plot of fifty miles at Beck is fed by a spark from the Earl of [[erroubek|Arrowback]]'s castle. Without a wire, the train gets up, so the saboteurs cut off the wires or harness the substation. The copper wires of the road, and previously they were guarded by the spark itself: “the black man fears her like the Dark Ido.” In the winter of 1774/75, when the bridges collapsed and the spark disappeared from several lines, the poor dragged dozens of miles of wires in a month.

## Road work
**Offices.** The Lands of the Crown are run by the E.I.V. Ministry of Rail Roads, the same as the Office of the Imperial Rail Roads; its coat of arms, tractor silhouette, route scheme and passenger name are printed on the ticket — "the whole printed page". The Minister of Railways is responsible for the rails and bridges of the Crown Lands and for the branches of Faunterra - Aleridan and Faunterra - Heart of Light. In Almera and Nadezhda there are their own "E.I.V. rail roads" of the duchies, Nadezhda has its own rail management. The train can be ordered: in the “A Doll on the Throne” it turns out that the composition on which Erwin broke into the capital, was hired in advance from the management of “some rich man” under a false name.

**Schedule.** Trains run according to schedule, and locals know it by heart: fishermen at the bridge across the Back on the hum and the sun distinguish "Blackmore", "Aleridan", "Flissky" and "Orwell" trains. In the Janmei Hunt, the Aleridan Express arrives at the capital station at 11:50am on a "giant brass station clock"; by his arrival, cab drivers line up, and the station constable checks to see if their jackets are clean and if there are bags under the horse tails.

**Staff.** The train is served by a machinist with a senior machinist, mechanics, lackeys and stewards (in "Fantasies" - "crew" with "on-board mechanics"), and the main - the head of the train; in "Fantasies" the train is "commanded by a master", who keeps an on-board log of unscheduled stops. The small Fisher Hut station costs six employees: a supervisor, a spark mechanic, a walker, a constable, a janitor, and a cashier. On a deserted stretch of the desert in Hope, at Fort Big Luck, six months live two "duty path keepers": food and water are dumped from the passing composition, and their business is to check the trains with the schedule. If the composition is late for an hour, the elder sends a pigeon to the Heart of Light, and the younger jumps to find out the cause.

**The alarm system.** After the attacks on trains in the winter of 1774/75, “smart men from the Ministry of Science” came up with a small device, “like a greatly simplified wave.” It takes a spark from the wires and sends a three-digit train code through them every minute. If the signal is gone, then the train broke down or was attacked, and the schedule shows which mile to look for it. The Ministry of Railways introduces the system slowly, as money grows, and Henry Farway had already put it on the three main roads of Hope by the summer of 1775. In the “Shadow of the Great Tree” this “train tracking device” is named among the largest inventions.

**Loads.** Transportation by train is more expensive than by sea: in the tasker, which Minerva decides in the "A Doll on the Throne", the transportation of porcelain from Farway to Labelin costs agate for ten pounds by train and an agate for thirty pounds by ship. But the train goes four days, and the ship - three weeks, and the merchant turns the money six times more often. On the "commercial" train (in "Humans and Gods") - ten freight cars.

## Stations and lines
- Faunterra - Labelin (Southern Way). The last station in front of Labelin is Fairwell on the River Mile, twelve miles from Pixie.
- Faunterra - [[aleridan|Aleridan]] - Julian Bridge on the Holliwell River: "in three days you can get from the capital to the west bank of Holliwell." ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) On the way to Almera, trains cross the Snake River (thirty miles west of Orwell, on the Almera border) and the Back River at Arrowback Castle; Fisher Hut station lies fifteen miles from the castle. While the bridge over Back was destroyed, trains from Aleridan went to the capital bypassing Orwell, and at Mark's in "A Doll on the Throne" - through Marengo, in four days.
- Branches on [[marengo|Marengo]], Orwell, Heart of Light in [[nadezhda|Hope]], Julianum and other cities Crown Lands and Centers. Labelin is connected by rails to the Crownlands, Almera, Hope and Litland; from Labelin to the Heart of Light six hundred miles, the train runs them in five days (on carts - a month and a half). Orwell lies halfway to Marengo; his station has a spare way behind the warehouses.
- Holliwell rail road was built under the emperor Telurian, who, according to Minerva, thanks to the unconditional obedience of the vassals, carried out both it and the third spark shop on Canaan.
- In Hope: South Branch from Mason Bay to Heart of Light (almost a hundred miles across the clay desert, without a single station), Leongard Road - Heart of Light, Marengo Coast Line - Leongard.
- In 1775, Almera and Hope agreed to make a second way on the Heart of Light line, Aleridan.
- In [[vrata-doveriya|The Gates of Trust]], Lord Emmon admits, "The great rail road has not yet reached the realm of shadow, but we are trying to follow the progress." Lady-in-the-Dark put a yellow wagon on the rails near her castle in [[darkvoter|Darkwater]], which in two minutes transports guests on the overpass to the castle balcony, - to teach them to know about sparking techniques. In Faeries, on "Fantasies", trains do not run.

**Stations.** In Aleridan, the capital train - "huge and luxurious, with balconies, sculptures and stained glass windows" - departs from the individual platform; the ministers shout "Pzhaaalte on the wagon!", the priest offers a blessing on the road, the merchant - pies. There are four aprons at the capital station. Stations in the wilderness are modest: a two-story hut serves Fisher Hut, fish, seeds and beer are traded on the platform.

### City Train
In [[aleridan|Aleridan]], a city train has been running for a long time - small spark wagons on narrow rails with round flashing eyes. In Mir's "[[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]" in December 1774, he rides on such a wagon: inside it smells of wood and varnish, along the walls of leather sofas, the conductor takes two agates for passage and explains how to return. In the "Janmei Hunt", [[alanis-almera|Alanis]], who became the mayor of Faunterra, turns wagons in the capital. Against them were the guilds of cab drivers, road builders, and spark mechanics, and Alanis sent a council of elders by express to Aleridan to drive through the city there in a wagon, and back on a sled. To keep the sparks, the streets began to turn off for an hour a day.

## Adrian's Reform
Emperor [[adrian|Adrian]] dreamed of “connecting all the great cities of the empire” with rails, “from the First Winter to the Lion’s Gate”, so that the journey took less than a month. He believed that then the disunity of land and domestication would disappear, trade would grow and the poor would have a job. To do this, the Crown proposed a universal filing that would replace feudal service. The great Houses saw the rails as a threat: roads would tie the land to the capital and put an end to the absolute power of the lords. In 1774, only two of the thirteen Great Houses, Shayland and Almer, supported the reform. The struggle over tax, rails and the choice of the imperial bride led to a chain of conspiracies.

According to early media reports, the plan was announced at the House's April 1774 meeting: four thousand miles of rails and six new spark shops (in the 2026 edition, these figures are cut along with the Colin Felicia scene). According to the "[[golos-korony|Voice of the Crown]]", it was supposed to build "at least five hundred miles of rail track per year", which required "more than a million ephesas" in excess of previous expenses. For this purpose, the Crown established the Roads Authority. The general land tax was to come directly under his control (in the early editions - a tenth of the income from each feud, market and workshop). Adrian was in a hurry, realizing that he had been given thirty years:
> “It is easier to build a thousand miles of track than to convince a single lord.”

Some of the roads were built on borrowed money: the bank "Ferguson and Day" invested in Adrian's reforms three times, and "not one hundred miles of rails" were laid on his money (see Money). ([[dengi|Money]]) [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] in his exploit also charted the route for the future road through the Crystal Mountains. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) See also [[reformy-adriana|Adrian's Reforms]].

## Ministry of Roads
Rails, spark shops and "waves" are in charge of the Minister of Railways, that is, "responsible for the introduction of scientific inventions." In 1775, this position was held by Liam Shelier, appointed by Adrian. ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]]) In the winter, he went with an inspection of the roads and bridges destroyed by war. Minerva in anger “sentenced” him to perform the duties of the Minister, as in hard labor: “I need good rail roads, “waves”, spark shops.” Schelier reported that six rail bridges had been destroyed (three in the Southern Way, two in the Crown, one in Almera), and the damage, along with the stolen wires, amounted to about eighty thousand ephes. He soon reported that the money for repairs had been exhausted, the work had been frozen, and no line had ever entered service. At the trial of Manson, Schelier claimed that "bridges sometimes collapse", but [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] recalled that the last time the bridge itself fell twenty-two years ago: then the Arden Bridge failed, and Vladyka Telurian declared mourning, "for the fall of the bridge is a tragedy for the Yangmei Dynasty." Later, in "[[ten-velikogo-dreva|The Shadow of the Great Tree]]", the Minister of Railways reported to Minerva that the road to the Heart of Light was working properly, and met with an inspection in Litland.

## Comparison with the real world
*Watching wiki, not the author's words.* The Polari Railroad combines two eras. In general, it is similar to the early railways of the 1830s and 1850s in England and Europe: the first lines diverge from the capital to the largest cities, construction is controversial and requires huge loans, passengers are divided into classes, horses and crews are carried on open and closed platforms, and trains run on a schedule with a station clock. Similar to the slope: the early locomotives also did not take steep climbs, so the roads led around the hills, through embankments, viaducts and tunnels. The same early appeared and sabotage: the break of wires and the burning of bridges.

But the pull in Polari is not steam, but electric, with a wire over the path - in the real world, this appeared only in the 1880s-1890s (the first electric trams and Siemens lines, Spray). Sparky carriages of Aleridan and Faunterra are a direct analogue of the horse, which was replaced by an electric tram, along with a dispute between cab drivers and a lack of power stations. The signal device that transmits the train code over the wires resembles a railway telegraph and a late lock. Trains bearing coats of arms, with a name, pennant, and dining car, are closer to the luxurious named trains of the late nineteenth century, such as the Orient Express. There are no steam locomotives, coal smoke and steam engines in the cycle: the smoke above Surzhikov’s car is a vapor of water heaters.

**What the author says.** There were no direct comparisons with real railways in the books, the “Guide” and the notes.
