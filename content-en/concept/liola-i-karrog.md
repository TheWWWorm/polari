"Liola and Karrog" - an ancient legend of Polari, which from the era of the mysteries put on stage: the goddess of meadows Liola asks the mortal warrior Reomur to defeat her neighbor, the fiery god of the volcano Karrog. In [[kukla-na-trone|A Doll on the Throne]], a new, free-form production of the mystery is given at a closed premiere at the Grand Crown Theatre of Faunterra. The action on the stage runs parallel to [[minerva|Minerva]]'s conversation with Iona, and the evening ends with the public humiliation of the empress. ([[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]])

## Legend
Carrog eruptions burned the meadows of Liola year after year, and God answered all prayers that this was his nature. One day, a crying warrior, Reomur, was driving through her possessions: his bride was dying of “pale disease”. The goddess promised to heal the girl if he defeated the god of the volcano, and gave him a blue flower about eight petals. Seven days of infusion healed the bride, and the eighth petal Reomur hid under a ring of mail "for good luck."

The first challenge ended in burns: the volcano simply responded with a flame. A month later, Reomur cheated: he waited until the volcano breaks out of strength after the eruption, put on a wet woolen doublet and ice patches and made his way into the crater through a small vent. Karrog whipped him with one sneeze, and when the warrior pretended to give up, he heated his sword. Unarmed Reomur pressed the last petal against the forehead of the god, and Karrog fell asleep. As he left, the warrior said the famous words: “Life conquers, not the sword.”

The legend continues. Years later, Reomur got bored at the hearth and, when the sons grew up, again went to look for battles: no one can resist his nature. And the meadows of Lyola began to wither: it turned out that the volcano warmed the ground, and the ash fertilized it. Liola did not remove the petal, so that everything did not become as before, but burned her flower braid and ashes awakened Carrog with the words: “In life there is death, and in death there is life.” Since then, both have remained the same, but have learned to understand each other and give in when necessary.

By tradition, the three heroes represented the three forces of being: Liola - life, Karrog - destruction, Reomur - human will. The audience came to the mystery in the colors of their favorite hero: red and black (Carrog), green and yellow (Liola) or white with silver (Reomur).

## "A Doll on the Throne"
### Premiere
The Prime Minister was given the day when the Chancellor announced the laying of the Cathedral of [[pramater-agata|Agatha the Radiant]]. Mira went to the theater with Lady Sophia, who on the way told her the history of the theater and the subtleties of theatrical etiquette. ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]) The lodge opposite was occupied by [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] and [[alanis-almera|Alanis]].

The production was free with the legend, and Lady Sophia enthusiastically called it a “fresh interpretation”. Carrog became a rough walking baron who came to Liola to drink and beat the dishes. Liola turned into a pale, fragile noblewoman reading flowers of poetry, and Lady Sophia immediately compared her to Iona and Erwin as a child. Reomur was held by a daring charming mercenary. The healing scene was modernized: a horde of healers and barbers rushed to the bride’s house with a song on the motif of the military march, where the chorus was “Money, go!”. The infusion on the seven petals of Lady Sophia connected with Pramary [[pramater-syuzen|Susan]], whose number is seven.

In the intermission, the audience noticed that the colors of the heroes now meant three varieties of nobility: the pretentious nobility, the rude barony and the beggars, but arrogant “free swords”. The Northwood brothers came in Carrog flowers and laughed at the resemblance. ([[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]]) The Duke of Labelin and his son looked ridiculous in white with silver, and Erwin and Alanis dressed in salad, like Liola. ([[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]]) Mira accidentally wore the colors of all three characters: white and green were her favorites, and black she wore in mourning.

### Conversation with Iona
In the second act of Peace, she was left in bed alone with Iona and decided to start "when Reomur goes to battle." Then the lines of the two women are interspersed with the stage of the fight. Mira demands an answer as to why [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]] lives in a comfortable tower. Iona hides behind the debt of his wife and sister, and Mira calls this debt the shield with which she closed herself from responsibility. While Carrog smashes Reomur's shield, helmet, breastplate, and sword, the argument reaches hints of Erwin. Mira says that for Erwin, she is "the same mistress as that actress on stage - the god of the volcano." At the moment when Reomur presses the petal to the forehead of Karrog, Iona says: “I yield.” She adds that Minerva is "very eager" to be an enemy of the Origins, and leaves the lodge. The world remains “tired and desolate.”

### How to understand the play
In the end, the actor added to the legendary phrase one word: “It seems that the sword does not win, but life...”. The audience argued about two deviations from the legend. According to the canon, a mortal cannot compete with God and wins only with cunning and the Petal of Life, and on the stage the fight looked almost equal: knighthood was delighted, the clergy were outraged. The dubious "seems" to have sounded as if Reomur was already foreseeing Liola suffering without her eternal adversary. Mira sees this as a “very agate” meaning: when you see all the consequences, the real feat is not an action, but a decision.

Like Reomur's victory, Mira's victory over Iona is ambiguous: the argument is won, but Iona leaves with words of enmity. *This is a reading, not a direct statement of the text.* A similar lesson - about the opponents who need each other - can be seen in Minerva's relationship with the Origins, but the book does not draw such a parallel.

### Scandal in intermission
In the second intermission, the drunken Minister of Railways Liam Shelier told Mira in the whole yard that the railroads were ruined, [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] was appointed treasurer without her knowledge, and [[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]] bought the post of Minister of Taxes. ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]]) He finally shouted that [[adrian|Adrian]]'s body had been found. Mira left without looking at the third act.

Erwin later apologized in a letter for the "unexpected deliberateness of the incident in the theater", but in fact did not abandon what he said. In a private conversation, he assured that Schelier acted “on his own initiative”, and wrote the letter in such a way as to maintain the reputation of a cunning person: such a scene could not happen “without my participation”. Iona reproached her brother that Minerva "didn't deserve such humiliation." Mira herself then told Shelier that his "theatrical antics" finally pulled her "out of slumber" and forced her to act.
