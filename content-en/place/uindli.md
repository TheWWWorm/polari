## Description

Windley is a port city on the north coast of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], in the foothills of the [[kristalnye-gory|Crystal Mountains]]. According to the Polari Land Guide, it is one of the largest cities in the Duchy along with [[labelin|Labelin]], [[dojl|Doyle]], and [[soltaun|Soltown]]. The Southern Route merchant fleet, the largest in the Empire, was divided into three naval guilds, and the first of these was based at Windley. The second was in the bays of [[soltaun|Soltown]] and Fishtown. The town had five anchorages and a baronial castle. The military fleet did not stand here, only “unsettling trade galleons”. In 1775 it was called “the most profitable port of the Southern Way”.

The Marquis of Windley, a vassal of the Duke of Labelin, rules the port; this is what he is called already in Arrow, Coin, Spark, when Mira recollects the sea guilds of the Southern Way (in early editions of the novel he is called a baron).

## The Origins and Southern Way Dispute

According to the Guide, in 735, the Lightbearers captured the entire north of the South Road, "including the strategic port of Windley" for a four-month campaign. In Arrow, Coin, Spark, Erwin reminded [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] that the port "was originally given to the Northerners". Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collector's edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark corrects both: the port was not "given" to the Northerners - they themselves founded it under the Light-bearers, in the IX century. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) In the fifteenth century, three hundred years ago, the Crown seized Windley for the debts of the then Duke of First Winter and gave it to the people of the Southern Way. In fact, according to her, the Crown was afraid of the strengthening of the North and therefore did not return the port, despite all the merits of the northerners. The countess objected that it was three hundred years ago, and Erwin's grandfather and great-grandfather had repeatedly gone to war on the South Road to get Windley back, and each time the Crown opposed them. According to Erwin, all the ports on the East Sea belonged to the Southern Way, and his trade monopoly was for the North "a trade choke on the neck."

In the fall of 1765, the Origins again fought for Windley. During this "campaign in Windley" Lord [[valter-oridzhin|Walter Origin]], father of [[robert-oridzhin|Robert]] and [[dejmon-oridzhin|Damon]], went missing. He was considered dead in battle until Robert learned that Walter had been kidnapped and tortured by monks subordinate to the Queen of [[darkvoter|Darkwater]].

### Port in Erwin's plans

In the spring of 1774, while assembling a coalition against Adrian's betrothal, Erwin told Sybil, "By controlling Windley, having our fleet on the East Sea, we will get much more than is required to pay taxes." For his support, he promised to give her personally "a baronial castle, an adjacent area and two of the five anchorages" so that Northwood merchants could settle there. In an interview with Adrian, Lady Catherine Catherine and Sir William Erwin, when asked by the emperor what House Origin wanted, he replied that he "would be happy to receive the port of Windley with all the ships of the First Naval Guild of the Southern Way." Northwood House wanted to take possession of one of the harbor bays. Later, during the interrogation, Sybil said that Erwin only wanted to take Windley and give her one of the harbors in exchange for military assistance.

In August 1774, while discussing a plan for war with his father, Erwin took up the idea of capturing Windley with the fleet and transferring the army to Adrian's rear. Desmond replied "no", and Erwin agreed: Windley is only "reckless shopping galleons", they will intercept at the Jolly Islands or Labelin himself will burn the ships.

## Battle of Windley

In September 1774, after the victory on the Wise River, Erwin did not go to the open Labelin. One of the satellites objected: “Windley is a big port, the dream of our grandfathers, it’s clear... The road is open!” Erwin, with his cousins and the Snow Count Lilliday, moved east. On the natural path, the path was blocked by phalanxes of penny-miners and a hill with archers, only about five hundred knights and ten thousand infantry. The flanks rested in a swamp and a lake. The North's infantry was two days behind, and beyond the hill, Windley's merchants had already loaded gold and families onto ships to sail south.

Erwin drank a few drops of medicine and led two and a half thousand riders in a frontal attack in front of the troops, shoulder to shoulder with Damon. The road before the phalanx was torn down, archers from the hill beat both on kairam and on their own. The second phalanx, seeing the death of the first, ran. Erwin stopped the massacre and shouted: “On your knees, South! Save your life!” The travelers began to fall to their knees. Then Erwin told Hobart-Synthal that the Marquis' army had surrendered so quickly, and the horse was so zealous that he entered the city before the merchant ships had time to depart. ([[gobart-sintal|Gobart-Sintal]])

The battle became a legend of the Southern Way. Labelin's soldiers recounted how a rebel "with three thousand horsemen scattered ten thousand troops at Windley", and dug moats "to prevent a second Windley". Merchants-brothers reported that the Duke started in the city “the smartest and most profitable order”. Erwin, like the Wise River, released the captive commoners. At a council meeting in Labelin, it was feared that by entering Windley, the mutineer had received the ships, and "the north of the South Road becomes part of the Duchy of Origin". [[magda-labelin|Magda]] later rebuked Baron Darryl by saying that his knights "surrendered to Origin at Windley". ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]) Lady Sophia proudly recalled Windley among the triumphs of her son.

## Under Northern rule

Marquess Windley, "the lord who lost the land", with a handful of men and ships fled to the Duke of Labelin. In January 1775, he sat on his council and, together with the Marquis of Greysend, was ordered to guard with his flotilla the cargo that was being carried to Shimmery (see [[poleznye-lyudi]]). According to Darryl's calculations, the North only had two companies at Windley.

In the summer of 1775, as [[adrian|Adrian]]'s army marched north, the Kairs left all of the South Road except Windley. Adrian did not turn to the port, where “to this day there were two battalions.” Later, Wittor Shayland found out from his spies that one battalion of kairs remained in Windley, cut off from the First Winter. ([[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]) [[magda-labelin|Magda]] at one time figured out whether to tie Adrian and bring a "doll" to Windley to give kairam in exchange, for example, for Labelin. Prince Hector and King [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] told Minerva that Shimmery would not storm Windley or any other city occupied by northerners. Magda admitted, "Windley stayed with the Kairs, but Main is with us." Under the terms of the peace of 1775, the Labelins returned the Origins to Main and retained the entire Southern Way, "less Windley". Erwin said, "This port is ours."

The port became the gateway of the North to the East Sea. In the autumn of 1775, [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]]'s flotilla, with its yard and treasury, sailed from Faunterra across the East Sea to Windley, and [[kharmon|Harmon]] sailed on one of the ships. In early November 1775, Lady Sophia's squadron returned to Windley from the Volta. ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]) The city was full of quirks. Sofia distributed bills to sailors and only on the shore learned about the resurrection of Adrian and the blockade of the First Winter. Here, at Windley, Dorian Amber fled from Faunterra: he was captured by the Kairs as Adrian's spy and thrown into the basement until Lord Robert arrived. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) In 1775, the schooner Attadora was sent here with Lady Dorothy and [[karen|Karen]], who were to transfer to Windley for a crew to Northwood.

## In "Fantasies"

In "Fantasies", which the author calls possible future options, after the Battle of the First Winter, [[magda-labelin|Magda]] in negotiations gave to the Virgins Windley and Main. Her father [[moris-labelin|Maurice]] vouched for the deal, lifted the blockade on Windley and handed the Northerners "one of the most profitable cities in the duchy". In 1776, when [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] was treating patients at the Mercy Clinic in the First Winter, fast stagecoaches were sent from the clinic to the port of Windley every half hour.
