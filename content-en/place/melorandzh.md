## Description

Melorange is the ancient capital of [[litlend|Litland]], an "ancient and glorious" city on the edge of the sacred jungle, which the locals call rope forests. According to the Polari Lands Guide, Melorange is named first among the three largest cities in Litland; the other two are Rainforest and Colmin. The population is about two hundred thousand people.

### Three Cities in One
Naimir, who first got to Melorange, saw in him “three cities one in another.” ([[nejmir|Neimir]]) The first is the ducal castle, the strongest and tallest, but tiny. Around it, behind the stone wall, there is a second city - the houses of nobles, merchants, priests and successful masters. The third, the outer city is a labyrinth of shacks of the poor; it is protected only by an earthen ramp and a wooden palisade, but it is so large that it will not be possible to circle it on horseback in less than an hour.

### Face and manners
Houses in Melorange are built of white sandstone; flower beds are broken on the roofs, palm alleys and magnolia gardens stretch across the city. There are three permanent theaters in the city, and street actors give performances in a dozen squares. Life-loving Lithuanians are famous for their health, so there were only three hospitals in the capital, pushed to the outskirts. Becca Litland said that there are so many stray dogs in Melorange that it is enough to go outside and open your palm - someone will lick it. Neymir, posing as a Shimmer merchant, joked that for the “children of the sun” all the north of Melorange was already north.

### Surroundings and roads
Nearby is Bassifor, the ancestral castle of the Litlands. Nearest to the capital of the harbor - Maple, Colmin and Rain Forest; Maple lies just forty miles from the city. From Melorang, four roads diverge: to the south, to the steppe; to the east, to the jungle; to the west, to the Shepherd's Meadows, sacred to the Shavans; and to the northwest, along the shore of the Extreme Sea, to the Duchy of [[nadezhda|Hope]].

## History in the Series

### Autumn of 1774 (One Star Only)
Moran's Shavans of Steppe Fire crossed Holliwell, devastated the grassland of Litland, and approached Melorange. ([[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]]; [[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) [[adrian|Adrian]] with the army and the Shimmer army of Prince Hector went to the rescue for more than a month and stood at the pirate bay for a week, waiting for the Shimmerians. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) On the eighth day of the journey through Litland, the army reached the edge of the rope forests; from there to Melorange remained thirty miles along the course of the Orange River, which had dried up in time immemorial. To the left of the channel there were jungles, to the right there were meadows. Here, on the outskirts of the city, the horde attacked and fell into a trap: a quarter of the Shavans were killed, another quarter were wounded, and Adrian and Hector cut off the horde from the camp, depriving it of supplies, trophies and slaves.

The victory was won on December 2, the day of the descent of the Mothers. On December 3, Melorange greeted the army with a holiday: the facades were hung with paper lanterns, tin stars and balloons made of blown glass. The city, which was preparing for a siege, stayed there less than two weeks, and the Duke of Willas ordered to open warehouses and set tables for soldiers right in the squares. To the emperor and generals, the Duke presented bowls of walnut creda with honey wine from the cellars of Mollis the Lucius, and then arranged a three-day feast in Bassifor. The wounded, who were not accommodated by hospitals, were sheltered by the townspeople.

Going north, Adrian left Hector's Shimmerian army in Melorange - as if to help the Litlands against the remnants of the horde, but in fact to get rid of the risk of treason in the campaign. Here the Imperial Spark Regiment of General William Davey remained: he was entrusted with the wounded and the supervision of the loyalty of the Lithuanians. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) The prophet Francis-Ilianus, wandering, bypassed the "boiling pot" of Melorange; he predicted to the Litlanders that Adrian would defeat the steppes and liberate the city. ([[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]])

### Winter of 1775 (A Doll on the Throne)
Moran kept the horde in Litland and planned to deprive the Shimmerians of their ships. He sent to Meloranj under the guise of a Shimmerian merchant and his altess Neymir and Charu. ([[chara|Chara]]) When they arrived, the gate stood wide, and on the spears of the guards hung black ribbons: the city bore mourning for the deceased Adrian. Hector, warned by a letter from a gauntlet from the horde, grabbed the scouts. Chara later said that Moran himself sent them to Melorange and gave them to "creepers": under torture, they discovered false plans, and so he deceived the enemy. Hector learned that other scouts were with them, which means that Moran most likely knows about the arrival of the Shimmer ships in the Rain Forest on January 20; he ordered the prisoners to be executed, but, according to Chara's later story, they were saved by a "sorcerer". The prince decided to meet the horde on the approaches to the Rain Forest, led the army out of Melorange and forced the march eastward. Shavans, who did not know his plan, decided that the spirits of the steppes confused the prince of reason. The horde, standing in the jungle, struck him in the flank and in the back; the Shimmerians fled to the ships, throwing a convoy and weapons. Horde camped three miles from the capital.

The siege began. According to the report, which [[minerva|Minerva]] received, the city was held for the fourth month: it had two knight battalions, a couple of thousand shooters and a spark regiment of Davey, and under the walls - not less than forty thousand riders. The Shavans built a dozen stonemasons, which beat stones day and night and burning tar, and began to let out of the city alive those who carried twenty pounds of provisions. Before the Litlands stopped this, the city lost thousands of poods of supplies; there was a threat of famine over Melorange.

In March, Becky's father Litland offered to give it to Moran: he asked the world for a ransom of one hundred ephesas for the rider and for the bride. ([[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) Becca fled to the quarter where Davey's regiment stood - the only place in the city not subject to the Litlands - and lived at the infirmary, caring for the wounded. A week later, the Shavans broke into the city, but the militiamen filled a hole in the wall, and Davy's sparklers killed the trapped steppes. The second assault was also repelled. Here, Becky's account diverges from Minerva's earlier report, which said that the city was not being stormed by the Shavans. Erwin later confessed to Iona that he deliberately fed Minerva little information, so she learned about the siege of Melorange last.

### Spring 1775
Minerva agreed with Moran, and he lifted the siege and took the horde for Halliwell. The Voice of the Crown credited Melorange's release to her. She herself called the negotiations her triumph, and the saved city - "a load that fell from the soul." The horde was unhappy with the lifting of the siege, but Steppe Fire kept her in obedience.

### Summer of 1775 (Humans and Gods)
Lady Sofia Origin spent several days with the judge on the way to Volta: she walked around the city, watched theaters, and the judge looked at the open court session at the town hall. ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]; [[folta|Folta]]) This is where Sophia met General Davey. He was offended: the "Voice of the Crown" gave the victory to Minerva, although the city, according to him, was held by his regiment and the city militia. Davey also reported on the disarray in Melorange after the war: the horde, he believed, had gone nearby and could return in two weeks. Sophia brought him Minerva's order to withdraw Meloranj's defenses and act on his own and Erwin's letter offering to transfer the regiment to the North, to Main; the general considered it absurd. Nevertheless, the regiment left Meloranj: Minerva later found him in the Main Valley, transferred him to the First Winter, and in the Battle of the First Winter, Davey's sparklers fought on the side of the Origins.

### After the war
Later, in The Shadow of the Great Tree, Davey expressed this resentment to Minerva herself: Steppe Fire accepted her conditions only because it broke its teeth against the walls of Melorange. Minerva replied that she knew almost nothing about the siege, because the courtiers kept her in the dark.
