## Geography
According to the Polari Land Guide, the Southern Way lies on the east coast of the mainland between Crown Land in the south and the Origin and Northwood in the north. In the east it has numerous seaports, in the west it has access to the [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]]. The north of the duchy is hilly, the south is flat. There are many rivers, the largest of them is the Hanai with tributaries. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) The climate is moderately cold and very rainy: the sea and the Smoky Dahl give evaporation, and the Crystal Mountains trap clouds. Due to the rainy spring and warm summer, the land is very fertile.

The northern border runs along the Twin Rivers. ([[bliznyashki|The Twins]]) On a narrow wedge of land between them stands a border fortress, and for the bridge over the Lower Twin take a considerable duty. To the northerner Mir, the way land seemed strange: the fields were too wide, cows and dogs were fat, peasant huts were large, although untidy, and even the sky was “too clean”. ([[minerva|Minerva]]) In the north, near the cold sea, it is always tightened by a haze, and here it shines with azure.

## Cities
- **[[labelin|Labelin]]** is the capital, according to the “Guide” the second most populous city in the world (325 thousand inhabitants). It is connected by rails to Faunterra and has no city walls.
- **Doyle** is the third largest city in the duchy and a powerful castle on the way from Northwood to the south. The lower city lies at the foot, Upper Doyle with a castle stands on a hill, where it is impossible to roll siege towers. The city belongs to the Marquis of Jeremiah Doyle. According to the Guide, in the 8th century, the city of Doyle was the capital of the then rulers of the Way. During the era of the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]] at the Battle of Doyle, the Origen sword The Voice of Winter defeated the great gant Hadinai, and the Steppe's movement to the north was interrupted.
- **Windley** is a port on the East Sea in the foothills of the Crystal Mountains, according to the "Guide" one of the largest cities of the Way. The originals considered it their own (see [[uindli|Windley]]).
- **[[soltaun|Soltown]]** is a rich seaport, a free city under the rule of merchant elders.
- Grace is a port city on the East Sea, the capital of the Marquess of Graysend, where the Labelin fleet stands. Moger Buckley is remembered for his grave-grey houses and inhospitable windows under an evil damp wind from the sea. From the coast of Grace there is a white dry wine "Dance of the Dolphin". The town of Winslow is nearby.
- The White Stone is one of three large cities Erwin took in the fall of 1774.
- Stonekip is a port where ships from the Almeran Fliss went.
- Larsie, Bakers, Thunderbolt and the Wise River are mentioned in the chronicle of the Northern War.

## Economy
According to the "Guide", agriculture is very developed in the Southern Way: grain and vegetable crops, cattle breeding. The second main source of income is trade. The path holds near-monopoly control of the trade routes from Shimmery and Crown to Northwood and Origin and ranks fourth in GDP in the Empire. The merchant fleet of the Duchy is about 1200 ships, the largest in the Empire.

The novel describes the same picture from the perspective of the northerners. The only trade route from Crown Lands to Northwood and Origin passes through the South Road. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) Road margins give local merchants and feudal lords much more than the sale of their own grain, copper and marble, and over the past century have brought them fabulous wealth. The Southern Way pays the emperor a lot of money. [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] called him "a noose on the neck of the Northmen." According to Erwin, the monopoly rests on the fact that all ports on the East Sea belong to the Southern Way. The duchy has its own navy and guilds.

The merchants of the Way are united in a guild. The merchant [[kharmon|Harmon]] wore her silver bracelet, but rarely wore it: the travel merchants are "famous for stinginess", and in other lands they are reluctant to do business with them.

## People and Customs
The Guide describes the Southern Way as a feudal society heavily influenced by merchants. Financial success is respected here, and architecture, clothing, and art emphasize luxury. The people are economic, hardworking, "not without cunning." The glory of stupid and greedy travelers, according to the “Guide”, is the fruit of northern propaganda: the Way is inferior to the North with valor, but surpasses it with enterprise and ingenuity, and therefore, apparently, the northerners do not like it. Putev's cuisine is considered by many to be the most delicious in the Empire, and the Traveling Women are the most caring mothers. Both titles are contested by Shimmery.

The land so often passed from one kind to another that the peasants brought up the philosophical saying: “Dogs fight, cows graze” — fights of the feudal lords do not concern them. The patroness of the Path is Sophia the Great. Folk folklore contrasts the good mother-Sophia with the evil Agatha, although the origins of the feud with the North, according to the "Traveller", are purely material: the Travel Lords burrowed into the silver mines of Main and [[lid|Leed]], and the northern ones dreamed of going to the sea.

The novel mentions the travel tradition: when the defenders of the city die, at sunset they beat the bell - a blow for each fallen. Marquess Doyle deceived the natives by ordering them to ring the bell more times than the soldiers died. The northerners called the travelers "torgashami" and "bloods".

## Ruling House
The Duchy is ruled by the House of Labelin, whose coat of arms is a dolphin. ([[dom-labelin|House Labelin]]) The house's flags depict a dolphin with sheaves of wheat, and the Lady [[magda-labelin|Magda]]'s battle flag carried one black fanged dolphin in a blue field. During the years of the cycle, the Duke is [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]], nicknamed the Fat Dolphin. The largest vassals are the Marquises Doyle and Gracend. From the Greysends comes Valerie Greysend, one of the brides on Adrian's watch in 1774. ([[valeri-grejsend|Valerie Graysend]])

## History
### Before the cycle
According to the "Guide", the Southern Way was mapped by the Forebears in the first year after the Descent. They passed this land in the spring, in a flood of rivers, and, without stopping, they rafted along Canaan to where they founded Faunterra. Since the II century, the fertile land began to be inhabited by farmers, and in the IV century it entered the Empire. In 709 the Duke of Doyle took the silver mines of Main from the Light-bearers, and in 735 the order responded and in four months captured the entire north of the Way with the port of Windley. The capital, Doyle, resisted. The merchants especially flourished in the XIII-XIV centuries, after the opening of sea routes to the south - to [[litlend|Litland]], [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] and [[darkvoter|Darkwater]].

The Dukes of Origin and the Earls of Northwood went to war on the Way's "bloodsuckers", especially for the port of Windley, but each time they were forced to leave when the Crown stood up for the merchants.

### Northern War
[[adrian|Adrian]]'s rail reforms threatened the Way's revenues: a road to the North would have deprived him of transit duties. According to Mira, in the spring of 1774 the news of the reforms alarmed the Duke of Labelin "worse than the gray sea". Maurice did not support the reforms.

In the autumn of 1774, in the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] invaded the Southern Way. He divided the army into three parts: seven [[stetkhem|Statham]] battalions went to Doyle, others - along the central Trade Route and to the coast. Erwin himself won the Three Copies, at Windley, and on the Wise River. Under Doyle, Statham took the Lower City in three hours after a month of siege, but the Marquis of Jeremiah and the townspeople locked themselves in Upper Doyle and repulsed the assault. Erwin forced Upper Doyle to surrender without an assault: he threatened to kill fifteen hundred prisoners and did execute twenty-five unarmed men. The same day, he confessed to his father David: “Today I killed twenty-five men. unarmed and innocent." In seven weeks, the North advanced a hundred miles, took twenty castles and twelve cities, including Windley, Doyle and the White Stone. In early November, they occupied the Labelin without a fight (see the Labelin massacre). ([[reznya-v-labeline|Night in Labelin]]) Fifteen thousand Path infantrymen defected to Erwin's side, and they were hastily trained in the ranks. The Duke had only a third of the land with the center in Grace, which Maurice made his new capital.

The impoverished peasants of the Path formed the core of the uprising of the Snowdrops. ([[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrop Rebellion]]) [[salem|Salem]]'s troops reached Grace, where the merchants bought trophy weapons from them, and the Marquis put out an army to drive them away. In the House, Maurice unsuccessfully demanded the return of the seized lands. His daughter [[magda-labelin|Magda]] led a fleet of Paths to Shimmery, hoping to obtain money and mercenaries to reclaim or redeem the land.

### 1775 year
In July 1775, Adrian's army returned the Labelin, and the northern garrison left without a fight. Adrian, who became Magda's husband, signed a map that showed the County of Main, with six silver mines, leaving the Southern Way. The Labelin troops entered Main. Maurice admitted that his daughter's marriage paid off: Adrian returned the Southern Way to them, and the Duke himself received "a quarter of Faunterra and Main in addition." Windley, meanwhile, remained in the hands of the Kairs; in the fall, after five months of sailing to the Metok Islands, Duchess Sophia of Orange landed there. ([[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Jessica Augusta]]) After the Battle of the First Winter, Magda offered peace: to return the Virgins to Main and leave Windley to them. It was not a loss in her calculation: “I wanted to return our land — I returned it.”

## In "Fantasies"
In Fantasia, which the author calls possible future options, under the peace treaty Magda gave to the Origins and Windley, and Main. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]]) Maurice lifted the blockade on Windley and handed the Northerners "one of the most profitable cities in the Duchy." In return, the Dynasty in the person of Minerva officially recognized their debts to him, Maurice received under the personal supervision of the Wreath with a frozen Puppet Master, and Magda became the Viceroy of the Crown Lands. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) The Duke left his sons to restore order in the Southern Way and went with his daughter to Faunterra (see House of Labelin). ([[dom-labelin|House Labelin]])

There are also two detective stories about the executioner Walter Jane John and the lawyer McFride Crowe, nicknamed Mac. By all indications, Mac is a former Crown Raven [[mark-voron|Mark]]: he lived in Faunterra for many years, was a defendant and underwent torture interrogations, wears a prominent scar on his hand and "served in the same department." In the Rutin Affair (late June 1776), they travel by river barge to Finborough. It is a quiet free city where the mayor has the right to preside over the court. The city lives by fishing, river transportation and weaving, and its spark machine on eight water wheels is owned by the Devo brothers-barons. ([[barony-devo|The Barons Devo]]) Mac reveals that Baron Reginald Deveaux killed Bernard's older brother and hid the body in a sewage pipe, and the sentenced dog Kusaka is innocent. In Deathly Hallows (June 1777), the action takes place in Winslow, thirty miles from Grace. Within a year, Count [[kuindar|Quindar]] had been there, refusing to consider the wine here as wine, and Lady Valerie Gracend gave birth to twins. ([[valeri-grejsend|Valerie Graysend]]) Her husband, retired General [[aleksis-smajl|Smile]], was invited to read military history at the University of Faunterra. Mac proves that the priest of the port church, Father Farnsworth, was poisoned by his coachman Pelmon and stabbed himself to death to incriminate the murderer.
