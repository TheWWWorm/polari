## Geography
Mills lie on the west bank of the mainland, at the Ocean of the Abyss, north of [[rejs|Reis]]. In the east, the land is bordered by Holliwell County, in the north by the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]]. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) According to the "Guide to the lands of Polari", in the depths of the mainland is a plain, and near the coast - the hills; the climate is windy and quite dry, the seasons abruptly change each other: snowy winters, dry and hot summers.

When [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]’s detachment came here from the Great Steppe in 1775 (at the time of harvest), the change caught the eye immediately: instead of kibitki and yurt, clay huts under straw roofs, canvases of arable fields, peasants who did not run away at the sight of kairs, but came out with greetings and goods. Among the fields, the ruins of ancient walls, towers and forts of “alien and long forgotten” outlines darkened; next to each ruin, a farmhouse was huddled, and peasants took stone from the ruins for houses, stored supplies in ancient cellars and placed churches and barns on the old foundations. Even before [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] recalled these edges as follows: "on each hillock on the mill", and between them all the gold from wheat.

## Cities
The heart of Mills are three seaside cities that once competed as city-states: Faeries, Minnis, and Offer. According to the "Travel Guide", only they survived in turmoil after the fall of the Iron Sun Empire, held the iron mines and, having access to the Ocean of the Abyss, became sailors. The cumulative line of fortifications around Fairis and Minnis, remaining from the Swordsmen, stretches for forty-six miles and has one hundred and thirty towers; the Offer has an "Offer Throat" - an artificial bay half a square mile away.

### Faiss
Fairis is an ancient city on the shore of the Ocean of the Abyss, the former capital of the Swordsmen. Half of its streets have no names, three-quarters are crooked and so looped that they are “tied into knots”; the same houses are white sandstone, dark shutters, flat roofs with a catchment. Squire told the Commandant that even under the Swordbearers of the Shavans, having broken into Fairis, they were confused in the slums and left with nothing. The ancient district, built by the Swordsmen, has preserved the “weapons” names: Narrow and Wide Blade Streets, Sekir’s Blade descent, Kopeyn Avenue, Shield Square. On the main square are the arch of the Brave Captains in the form of a sea wave, along which stone sailors with maps and visual tubes rise to the sky, and the palace of the Baron-Burgomaster Fairis; the city guard wears yellow. The city is famous for ships, sailors and ceramics: glazed dishes with gulls, fish, anchors, mermaids and drawings of Mother millers. In Fairis, large ships are built - they are recognized by the stern of a special form - and the Fairis Maritime Academy, according to the "Traveller", is one of the best in the Empire.

On the promontory at the bay stands the Origen outpost of the Sunset Star, a granite fortress connected to the shore by an arched bridge. It exists on the port tax from ships going to Origin without receiving a "no agate" from the dukes; from time to time, Treasurer Robert Origin sent messengers to seize half of the income, but for all of Erwin's reign there were no inspectors, and the income remained at the disposal of the outpost. In 1775 it was commanded by Lieutenant Claude Berengar ("Commandant"), its assistant and captain was Gray Winston Flyth ("Squire"). Northerners considered such service at the end of the world almost a punishment.

### Mother Mills
The name of the land is associated with three giant Mother Mills built by the Swordbearers: Jenna, Arina and Milan. Each is a masterpiece in its own style.
- Jenna is a massive angular tower above one hundred feet with double-fortresses, similar to a rock torn from the Crystal Mountains; her head is crowned by a crown of stone teeth, where watchmen stand. Jenna serves as the county's border patrol, and anyone can climb up for Gloria. She still grinds the grain and "groans" with the creak of the old mechanism; it is necessary to run it after stopping with the help of cargo on chains, so in the windy season the Mother is not slowed down, and in the quiet week before Abundance she is disassembled and cleaned by the master from Minnis.
- Arina is tall and slender, "like a poplar", with roots-counterforces, green blades, and balcony-nests; according to Schram's guide, she is even larger than Jenna, but does not work. The Guide clarifies that Arina has been faulty since the beginning of the 17th century.
- Milan - in the style of a temple or palace: a multi-tiered colonnade instead of walls, a spire with a star, silver blades and rusting iron roofing.
According to the "Guide", each Mother is above one hundred feet, and the blades reach about one hundred and fifty. Hyder Leed suggested that Milan was visible from the top of Jenna, and Arina from her, and signals could be sent between them; the head of the patrol called it a military secret, but promised to signal the arrival of the detachment in Faerys and send out the news, adding that the Snow Count was highly respected in their land, and Nexia herself was "a true noblewoman of an ancient and glorious kind." He asked Leed if it was true that the Lillidays had decided to be related to the Flames, and called it great news.

### Rusty Giants Cemetery
The darkest legacy of the Swordsmen is the Rust Giants Cemetery. Erwin's detachment saw one of them on a plateau cut off by the merger of two rivers: tens of thousands of sculptures of warriors - on foot and horseback, in human height and height from a three-story house. In each there is iron: ordinary fighters have at least iron blades, the glorious ones have armor on a granite body, and the lords and heroes are entirely cast of iron, and in six centuries rust has turned them into brown rot. According to the Guide, the Swordbearers buried the deceased in the ground and placed an iron statue over him the more noble the deceased was; the statues of the rulers reach fifty feet. Father David told the legend: among the giants there are three most ancient, created not by people, but by gods, who came to teach people to process iron and believe in the “sun of metal”; when they froze, the Swordbearers built their empire in their glory. No one has yet found these three; it is said that the first giants do not rust. Scar called the legend Shavan nonsense, and Nexia Flame and [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] asked the Duke for a search.

## Economy
According to the Guide, mills export iron, fish, and seafood, grain, and flour, with almost all income-producing mines, forgeries, numerous mills, and fishing ports concentrated in a narrow strip along the coast, and the mainland occupied by poor agriculture; eight of the ten largest lords of the land own land on the shore. Through Fairis, Minnis and Offert, flour, iron and skins go to Sunset Coast, Reis and Shimmery. Mill ships also entered Nan Clair, where they hired pilots to pass through the Tops of the Dark Kings.

The land connection to the Empire is provided by the road from Julian to the bridge over Holliwell: after Rei-Roy and Corden, the Barons of Mills, "descendants of the swordsmen", made their route from the bridge to Fais. At the fork of these paths stands the town of Trezubec, where rhei, hall and descendants of the Swordbearers live intermingled.

## People and Customs
According to the "Guide", the culture of Mills has evolved from several eras. From the nomadic ages there were simple songs about fields and horsemen and the Birbin - an instrument like a harmonica, which no one outside of the Mills can listen to without shuddering (a similar "springy iron" in the "Shadow of the Great Tree" tormented the warrior from the detachment of the [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia]]). The era of slavery and confusion left an extreme distrust: for the miller, "theirs" are only the inhabitants of his village or city, and the Mills are called the only land without a common national feeling. The era of navigation is a love of knowledge: there are many schools and libraries, knowledge of history and geography is mandatory for a literate person, and Mills are considered the most enlightened land of the West.

In the Milllands, the old language of the Iron Sun Empire is preserved - in the "Yanmei Hunt" they pray on it, and it does not look like any living speech. Swordsmen adored iron and made everything out of it - money, rings, bracelets, paintings, icons; Erwin believed that iron became a symbol of luxury for them, because it was the iron weapon that brought them the West. The descendants of the Swordbearers are outwardly different from the Shavans: Erwin noted in the sentinels dark skin and a cut of the steppe eyes, but hawkish noses and heavy chins. Border crossings here wear the same helmets with visors and uniform jackets with coats of arms of the Offer.

## Ruling House
According to the Guide, the nominal ruler of Melnitsa is Baron Faiss, the largest feudal lord of the land (18 Objects), and in fact the council of barons and burgomasters of ten or twenty people rules the land. The Faeries family is considered the Great House and is represented in the House; it is the only baron among the great lords. In the novels of the ruler, Fairis is called the baron-burgomistre. On the 1775 House list, however, Mills is represented by "Counts Faeries and Minnis", and the land itself is often called a county, so that the title diverges in different places.

## History
### Empire of the Iron Sun
According to the "Guide", these lands from the VI-VII centuries inhabited the Shavans that came from the south, from Reis. In the 9th century, one of the tribes found rich deposits of iron ore, settled down to hold the mines, and called themselves the Swordbearers: each man had an iron sword. The free shanties did not want to work in the mines, and the Swordsmen began to enslave their neighbors en masse; the slaves needed food, land and fortifications - and therefore new slaves. By the eleventh century, the tribe numbered about 80 thousand free people and 700 thousand slaves and had begun calling itself the Empire of the Iron Sun; by the mid-twelfth century, it had absorbed half of Reis and most of Hollywell, stretching from the Ocean of the Abyss to Smoky Distance. The swordsmen sought to "overwhelm" the distant Faunterra without having a clear idea of it; hence the Western saying "to jump over the Iron Sun" is to set a huge and unclear goal.

In the second half of the XII century, the uprising of slaves for six months boldly Swordsmen. Some of the freed went north and founded Silens - so the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]] began. In the novel, this story also sounds from the mouth of a Waymar mercenary: Silens was founded by slaves who rebelled against the Swordbearers “under the Iron Sun”. The Shavan legend in the Yanmei Hunt tells how the steppe hero Gett with a horde approached Fairis, the "brilliant capital of the empire", and the Swordsmen gave the last battle under the blades of the Mother Mills. ([[legenda-o-gette|The Legend of Gett]]) There is also a legend about how the leader of the steppes set a fire on a rock among the reefs, the Swordsmen took it as a lighthouse, and their fleet, sent around Rei-Roy, crashed. Baron Abelheim's Tears of the Night wine, which was treated to guests in A Doll on the Throne, is produced by a dynasty of winemakers dating back to the lost Iron Sun Empire; its vineyards grow on the far western island of Valcridi.

### Seafarers
After the fall of the empire in the steppe began to turmoil, and of all the cities survived only Fais, Minnis and Offer; the townspeople were afraid of the steppe, "like fire", and became sailors. According to the Guide, in the 14th century, believing in the sphericity of the world, the sailors of Fairis and Minnis tried six times to sail west to Faunterra. The last, step-wise expedition left Fairis on eighteen ships; the caravel "Star of the Sunset" left farthest away, opening the island of Hach three thousand miles from the shore, but it turned back too. Mill mariners have discovered more than a hundred islands, the archipelagos of the Sin and Volta republics and a safe way south, to Shimmery, bypassing the Tops of the Dark Kings. ([[folta|Folta]]) The Origen outpost in Fairis bears the same name, the Sunset Star.

### Name
The origin of the name is explained in different ways. According to the "Guide", the nickname was given to the Shavans of Reis, for whom mills on the hills of the Iron Sun were outlandish, and the expression "seeing mills" in the West means falling into slavery. In The Shadow of the Great Tree it is said otherwise: Lord Evrian the Expander of Borders persuaded the warring barons Fairis, Minnis and Offert to recognize the patronage of Faunterra and, shocked by the greatness of Jenna, called the new county Mills; the barons did not object, because the common name saved them from the dispute, which city to write in the documents first. Later, in the Second and Third Horse Wars, "Fayris' lecherous barons" beat the imperial troops in the back, and the Voice of Winter sounded against them. Sources also differ on the capital of the Swordsmen: The Guide calls it Theorate, burned by insurgent slaves, and in the novels the former capital of the Iron Sun is named Faerys.

### The War for Mills
In the summer of 1773, the Earl of Rantigar of the Sunset Coast gathered an army of twelve thousand swords from Western knights, militiamen and mercenaries, crossed the Gulkii ford and invaded the "disputed Mill Lands". ([[graf-rantigar|Count Rantigar]]) The Melnik barons did not unite at once; Rantigar took four castles and won the battle in the fields. Then the Melniks went to exhausting defense: they snapped from behind the fortress walls, flew from the fields with a light cavalry, avoided battles, burned crops, ambushed foragers while the count's army was starving. Joaquin recalled the battles on the Mill Ford and at the Three Eves (there also fought the Womack cheese) and how the detachment, on the advice of the commander, crossed the river in one skin and at dawn hit the Millers in the rear, overturning them in the water. Both sides asked for help from the First Winter, and the Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]] sent a battalion of kairs to the Melniks, after which Rantigar was defeated. Mercenaries from all over the Empire flocked to this war: Joaquin met [[berk|Burke]] on a schooner from the Belomorye to Reis, full of income seekers, and after the war, the thugs dissolved by Rantigar poured into Almera. In this war, the young Joaquin Yves Hanna served as a mercenary for the count; ([[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]]) [[menson|Manson]] later noted that Rantigar thereby violated the laws of the Empire by encroaching on the possessions granted by the Dynasty. General Orys also fought for Rantigar.
