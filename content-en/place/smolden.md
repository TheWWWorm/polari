## Description

Smallen is a town in the east of the Duchy of [[almera-zemlya|Almera]], among the red clay hills at the "artistic Snake River". Instead of a fortress wall, the city is surrounded by an earthen rampart with a palisade on the floor, "rather for order than for protection." The gate tower is cut down from the logs, "kind, wide, squat, like a prick sitting on his ass." On market days, the gates are open wide, and the guards peacefully sharpen the forests in the shade: visitors are welcome in Smolensk. According to [[kharmon|Harmon]], the city had six squares, several dozen streets and about five thousand residents. From the gate square, the bridge rises to the central hill. On the outskirts there are adobe and wooden houses with courtyards, and closer to the cathedral houses stone, in two floors, with signs-jugs and pretzels. From the cellar she carried "the sour spirit of the vile wine here." On market Sundays on the Market Square traded and staged fun fights. Harmon bought glass, bronze and utensils here, which in Almera were “sincerely conducted in excess.”

The snake serves as the border of the duchy. On the bridge over it stands the Almera Bridge Guard. Mark-Crow's squad paid her two purses and heard in response, "Welcome to the grand duchy of Almer. Be law-abiding.”

The main pride of the city is the cathedral, “huge, powerful and ugly.” Harmon has long observed that the smaller the city, the larger the cathedral: towns that have neither antiquity nor wealth, do not spare money for one of their objects of pride. There was a whole block under his tower. The copper disc with the Holy Spiral above the portal was the size of a windmill, and the singing pipes at the top shone like diamonds, so that it seemed as if "the wind was powerful and solemnly howling over Smolden." ([[vselenskaya-spiral|Universal Spiral]]) Joaquin recalled it as a "healthy cathedral" that the townspeople built so that it barely fit in the square. [[mark-voron|Mark]]-Voron a year later noted that the cathedral is huge and unfinished.

## Ownership

The town, a dozen villages, and part of the Snake River were owned by the Smolden Baron. In the neighborhood lay the baronies of Hogarth and Bonigan, and to the north lay the lordship of Baron Fliss. Above them all stood the Earl of [[erroubek|Arrowback]], who owned the entire northeast of Almera, so that Smallden and the surrounding area was subordinate to this county. Two years before the events, the former Smolensk baron died of the blue sea. ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) People said that in the coffin he lay terrible, with blue lips and a purple face, “as if in blueberry juice.”

According to the “Guide to the Lands of Polari”, in the fifth century the “Duchy of Almera” was a tiny piece of land, the size of a single present-day Barony of Smolden. Only then did generations of dukes methodically “move” its western border.

## History in the Series

- **21 March 1774.** The first chapter of the coin cycle. The day before, Harmon's guard Doxet got drunk, and the merchant came to the bazaar unguarded. On the Market Square, at the platform of the Lyceum, he saw a young fighter, who repeatedly won with a wooden sword, sticky from the molasses. In the evening, in the hotel food supply, two city guards in defiance advised Harmon whether to take the “Pretty Boy” into custody. Harmon hired him. So in the story entered [[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]], who then repeatedly talked about the “troubled fight in Smallden”, including [[polli|Polly]] in the tavern of Izluchina. Later, Harmon, explaining to Joaquin how many bears walk in one town and how many baronies are placed in one county, began his reasoning with Smallden.
- **1774.** In the early media, Harmon recalled the governess Maria from the baronial castle near Smolden, the best of his three women; in the 2026 edition, these memories are cut out. Dreaming of wealth, the merchant imagined that a good house in the central square “somewhere, say, in Smolensk” is worth three hundred ephes. During interrogation in captivity, Harmon argued that Joaquin, hired in Smallden, could not know about the Object: then the merchant himself did not know that the Count would instruct him to sell the shrine.
- **Autumn 1774.** After the story with Alanis, sneaking east into his native Southern Way, Joaquin rode around Smallden on a stolen horse, "seen in the spring," and cried, remembering Polly's death.
- **Early March 1775.** [[mark-voron|Mark the Raven]] was following the trail of a gang of Sacred Objects kidnappers. Following the tracks on the ashes, he determined that the bandits had come through the forest from the direction of Yalestock and had gone towards Smallden. Crossing the Snake on the bridge, Mark spent the night in Smolden, "a nice town with a bustling fair." Since Smallden is subordinate to Arrowback, tax books should have been sought at the Earl's office, and Crow's road led to Back Castle. Later, Mark admitted the opposite: Almera could be a hoax, and from Smallden, the bandits turned into the Southern Way, where a good road leads.
- **Early June 1775.** The saboteurs, the people of the parish or Shayland, burned a spark substation on the border of Almera, and the trains stood up. Count Lilliday reported in waves: "The spark has disappeared due to sabotage in Smallden. The cars are standing.” Only the first trains arrived in Almera. [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]'s train just moved Snake: the captain led the cavalry to [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], and sent the infantry to the substation. Horton's infantry, on the next train, had to march from Snake Day Three, and Erwin had to wait for reinforcements from [[bek-most|Beck]].
- **August 1775.** The horde of Shavans of Juhan Reis, armed with the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]], bypassed the defenders of Beck, crossing downstream, and moved to Smallden, from where the road to the Land of the Crown opened. ([[yukhan-rejs|Johan Reis]]) [[minerva|Minerva]] ordered Silver Fox and Northern General Horton to meet her on the shores of Snake. ([[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]]) In part, she hoped to stop the horde at the border itself, in part keeping the Fox away from the capital, fearing its betrayal. The horde freely took Smallden, Caseworth and several other cities. The Shavans were hastily gutted at home and rushed on to Orwell.
- **End of September 1775.** A report came to the capital that the celestial sailboat had passed three hundred yards over Smolden. The commander of the city guard cursed [[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]] and ordered a volley of fire arrows, but they did not reach.

## Trivia

- The Khodzhis mill-scratch from the village of Three Poplars was known "all over the district, from Valient to Smallden".
- In telling Joaquin about the Alleridan tournament, Harmon described Damon the Great's arena: its stands would easily fit the entire population of a small town "like Smallden."
