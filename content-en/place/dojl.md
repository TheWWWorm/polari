## Description

According to the Polari Land Guide, Doyle is one of the largest cities in the South Road along with [[labelin|Labelin]], [[uindli|Windley]], and [[soltaun|Soltown]]. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) On Erwin's military map, three large cities formed a triangle: Windley to the east, Doyle to the west, and White Stone to the south. Colonel-General [[stetkhem|Statham]] went to Doyle, "not reaching the coast of Smoky Distance", and [[magda-labelin|Magda]] called him together with Labelin the two largest cities of the duchy. In 1775, Doyle was "the last major city on the way to the North".

The city was divided into two parts. Lower Doyle was inhabited by philistines, the rich had stone houses there. Above it stood Upper Doyle, a massive fortress on a two-hundred-foot hill with walls four steps thick. Behind them were the palace of the Marquis, the cathedral, the quarters of the nobility, barracks, arsenals and barns. Erwin considered it more of a "city within a city" than a castle. It was impossible to bring siege towers to him. The main cathedral, the temple of Gloria the Intercessor, had the shape of a regular cross, and its dome was painted with scenes from the life of Pramatery. According to the southern tradition, when the defenders die, at sunset the bell is struck for each fallen.

## History

According to the Guide, in 709 the Duke of Doyle, then ruler of the South Road, attacked the Lightbearers along with the Order of Wilhelm and captured the Main silver mines. Later, in the era of the Order of Light, the Royal Army did not venture into Doyle, which was guarded by a single company of Lightbearers. In the First Horse War, Duke Odar Origin at the Battle of Doyle defeated the Great Ganta Hadinai with the Sword of the Winter and thereby stopped the advance of the Steppe to the north. ([[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]]; [[glas-zimy-mech|Voice of Winter (Sword)]])

## Marquess Jeremiah Doyle

In 1774, the city was ruled by the Marquis Jeremiah Doyle, who was barely eighteen. Erwin knew him: in the spring, on the way from Faunterra to the First Winter, he drank a jug of wine with the Marquis and played a part in the strata, and the young people switched to “you”. ([[stratemy|Stratems]]) Wealth and power spoiled the young man early. He loved luxury and precious outfits, held from five to eight violas. His only adviser was Bishop Mesmery.

## The Siege of 1774

### Statham Under the Walls

Entering the Southern Way in September 1774 (see Northern Outbreak), Erwin divided the army. ([[myatezh-oridzhina]]) Seven battalions of Colonel General Statham marched southwest to take Doyle. Statham immediately threatened to burn the city to the ground if he did not give up, and the frightened philistines locked themselves in. Against six thousand northerners stood seven hundred soldiers of the garrison and fifteen thousand citizens on the walls. Two assaults were repulsed, the North lost almost two hundred keels. Then they cut down the pine grove, built a hundred siege machines and began to shell the walls. A month later, under a blanket of rain, the stones broke a hole, and Lower Doyle fell in three hours. Half of the burghers managed to take refuge in Upper Doyle, the rest died or were captured.

Statham vowed to take the fortress "with iron, fire and famine" until winter, but Erwin was afraid that it would lay half the hull. Before the arrival of the Duke, Statham once again went on an attack of "turtles" with a ram. Defenders threw a bell on the head "turtle". Ninety-six kairs and one hundred and two grays were killed. Erwin was furious: “I ordered Doyle, Colonel General! He didn’t say a word about the attack.”

### Negotiations in the Cathedral

On October 15–16, Erwin met with Jeremiah at Gloria Cathedral. He offered to withdraw the army and leave a garrison of two hundred men in the Lower City, release about one and a half thousand prisoners and allow the refugees to return home. The Marquis was to disband the garrison, sending half the battalion to help the Duke of Labelin, and Lower Doyle to get back when Erwin took the capital. The bishop urged to accept the conditions, but the Marquis demanded the unconditional release of the prisoners and to leave. He ridiculed the Northerners: the bell strikes, which they considered the loss of the garrison, were a fraud. [[dejmon-oridzhin|Damon]] hit the marquis and threatened to kill the prisoners, burn the city and throw the fortress dead. Jeremiah left with the words, "You dare not touch the captives."

### Execution of prisoners

The commanders demanded an assault, although Robert estimated the odds at "one in four", and Erwin saw the expected losses as a "slaughterhouse". Erwin chose something else. He ordered the construction of the prisoners so that they could be seen from the walls, separated the unmarried men of the first rank and ordered that their heads be cut off so that the stones would throw them into the castle of the Marquis. He announced to the wives and children of the married prisoners that their husbands would die tomorrow at noon, and sent them to Upper Doyle to beg the Marquis. To the question “for what?” he replied: “Because I want! That is my will.” The next morning, when the prisoners were brought back to the square, Doyle opened the gate. The garrison went south to the Labelin, and Erwin released 1,500 prisoners.

The soldiers believed that the Duke had taken the "inaccessible Doyle" in a day without a drop of northern blood. Erwin himself later gave the price of twenty-five lives and admitted before the court: “I did an act that I am ashamed of: in the city of Doyle, I ordered the killing of twenty-five unarmed prisoners.” Another formulation in the text speaks of “two dozen chopped bodies.” Iona and Erwin's unseen althessa repeatedly returned to Doyle as a measure of his conscience. The execution also changed others: later Baron Icewind under Fliss proposed to "repeat the Duke's antics in Doyle", and Mother Cordelia strongly opposed. ([[kurt-ajsvind|Kurt Icewind]])

### Tournament

After the victory, Erwin announced a knightly tournament in honor of the "great victory" and ordered to allocate ten thousand ephes. The army left the city for the fields, where the lists, stands and tents grew. The Northerners fed free of charge refugees and citizens who agreed to watch the fighting. The tournament was won by Henry Horton, a future kair of [[sorok-dva|Forty-Two]]. Later, Alanis, analyzing Erwin's plans, noticed that a stupid, at first glance, tournament under Doyle was needed to choose the best duels of the army, and that the spears issued to the kairs were a foot longer than usual. Under the walls of Doyle, Erwin met with [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] four times, and in the city itself, [[otets-david|Father David]], whom Erwin offered to accompany the army, received Alanis’s consent: “Stay. I’ll be glad,” and joined the North.

## After 1774

In the winter of 1775, in Doyle, as in Windley, Soltown and Elfort, the Northmen held only two companies. Baron Darryl proposed to the Duke of Labelin to knock them out of there. ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]) Magda blamed Darryl for the defeats at Doyle and Labelin and acknowledged that the two largest cities in the Duchy were taken by the northerner "with words backed by a couple of drops of blood."

The war ruined the townspeople themselves. Anna Greta with her husband and adopted daughter G, who lived in Doyle in a large house, were captured when the North broke into the city, and together with thousands of others were waiting for death in the square. They were released, but at home they found ashes: the house was burned not by the northerners, but by debtor neighbors. This family was later among the refugees in Inge Price's van. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) In 1775, in the campaign of [[adrian|Adrian]] to the North, the Marquis of Jeremiah held a feast in the castle in honor of the pregnant, as it was then thought, Empress Magda, allocated her the best bed and showed her how to sleep on her side. Here Magda realized that she was not expecting a child, but still sent her husband a rose with a note: “Dear, I feel: a son will be born. Let's call him after your father." Adrian answered with a chrysanthemum and a note: "You are not pregnant, alas, but I hope you will soon." On the balcony of Doyle's town hall, Adrian played dice with the joker [[menson|Manson]] and, between shots, questioned him about why Minerva had disobeyed the legitimate emperor and whether Karen's joker's wife was plotting anything.

## In "Fantasies"

In "Ad-ri-an!", one of the "Fantasies", which the author calls possible variants of the future, in October 1776 Minerva and Erwin with five hundred soldiers were driving from the First Winter to the capital through Main and Doyle. Erwin, remembering his victories, was the first to remember Doyle.
