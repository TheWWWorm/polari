The six-mile forest is a forest at the northeast end of the Duchy of [[almera-zemlya|Almera]]. It lay on the trade route of [[kharmon|Harmon Paula]]'s caravan. By name, the forest is named only in the first novel, in the chapters of “Coin”.

## Description
The forest was stretched by a narrow ribbon along the raw lowland. In length it reached a good thirty miles, and in width in the widest place it was only six, from which the name came. Crossing it across was considered a bad idea: streams swamped the lowland and turned the earth into scalding porridge. There were two roads through the forest. One spring became quite impassable. On the second, you could somehow drive if the load was light and there was no rain for several days. A trip around the forest cost a merchant's cart an extra day's journey.

Early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark at the edge of the forest featured the modest castle of Sir [[logan-katrina|Logan Katrina]], Harmon's regular buyer: two square towers connected by a gallery, a palisade yard behind a palisade, and two neighboring villages. In the 2026 edition, a visit to the knight is cut out; the castle itself is mentioned in "Only One Star".

## Confrontation with the Forest Lords
In the spring of 1774, shortly after Smallden, Harmon decided to cut the road. (In early editions, this was preceded by a week's journey to the northeast end of Almera and overnight stays at Sir Logan's castle.) For a week there was no rain, and he ordered the driver Snape to turn to the second forest road "Six-mile across", and he lay down to sleep in the van. ([[snajp|Snipe]])

After a couple of miles, the robbers blocked the way: a hoarse old man with a chain, two hairy axe-bearers in jackets made of boiled leather and an archer on a branch. The old man called himself "Lord Sedym", and the underlings - "Lords Burym and Oglobly" and "My Lord Trapper" and demanded a fare. The new guard of the caravan [[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]] jumped off the horse, dodged the arrow, repulsed the axe and cut the forearm of the axe with a short blow. Covering the wounded from the archer, he threw a dagger at the shooter. Snape grabbed the second axe carrier on the axes; Harmon broke the robber's crossbow shoulder because of the canopy, and Snape cut his side. Then the dealer put a crossbow on the old man. Clamped on the bridge, Sedoy recognized the dealer and threw the weapon. The doxet with Vivrenko was brought from the bushes by another, frightened boy; he was disarmed and released. ([[doksett|Doxett]])

It turned out that a year earlier, acquaintance with Grey cost Harmon a dozen agates. Now the merchant demanded for the release of the wounded thirty: the money, according to him, “to the money sticks like snow to snow.” Under the edge of Joaquin's sword, the old man gave out two sacks containing twenty-four agates and a handful of copper stars. Harmon called the gang not robbers, but "honest forest lords, workers of the chain and the axe," and let them go. Joaquin, who wanted to reproach the owner in the low, he gave half a dozen agates.

The battle showed Harmon that his new mercenary was really good at swordsmanship. Even more, the merchant surprised the dagger that Joaquin pulled out of the archer's thigh: a spark dagger with a silver-plated guard, a wenzel on the top, and two empty eye pits. Harmon identified it as the ceremonial weapon of a nobleman and warned that such a "trophy" is easy to please the shameful pillar, but Joaquin refused to sell the dagger.

## Later mentions
- While visiting [[otets-david|David]]'s father, Harmon recounted road rumors, among other things, "about robbers in the Six Mile Forest."
- In the autumn of 1774, Joaquin, who had fled from Almera on a stolen horse and on the way himself took food and clothes from the travelers, again passed by the castle of Sir Logan. The knight's sons asked him about the robber, who "walks around our district", and something gave him away. Joaquin left the chase through the thicket, and behind the forest he saw the boundary pillar, crossed the border and entered his native South Path.
