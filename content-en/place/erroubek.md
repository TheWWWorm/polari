## Description
### County
The Earl of Arrowback owns the entire northeast of Almera: the county can be driven from east to west in about a week. ([[almera-zemlya|Duchy of Almera]]) He is subject to the Barons Smallden, Hogarth and Bonegan, and further north to Baron Fliessa. ([[fliss|Fliss]]) The town of Smallden, with all its surroundings, is part of the county. ([[smolden|Smolden]]) Behind Beck, on the west bank, lies the town of Belleville. Once the count quarreled because of idleness with the abbot of the monastery of the Forefathers-hermits Marek and Simeon, who lived with income from the fair and brewery in the village of Road Pillar. The Count spent six thousand Hilt, paved a new road from Fliss to Aleridan, bypassing the monastic lands and opened his fair and brewery at it. The count's beer was more delicious than the monastery's, the Road Pillar was empty, and the monastery faded. At a station in the county, soldiers were offered "the best beer in Arrowback County."

### Castle
Arrowback Castle, also called Beck Castle, stands on a giant stone dam that blocked the Beck River. It stretches from shore to shore for a quarter of a mile, and its height, according to various descriptions, is from one hundred feet to fifty yards. Water passes through narrow tunnels, turns the wheels of spark machines and roars out of the holes at the base of the dam. There is also a lock gate that can miss the ship. Along the ridge, buildings are stretched - "houses and houses, towers and turrets, balconies and bridges", entangled with wires and shiny copper cars. On the banks there are massive square towers, from which the wires diverge. In fact, the castle is a fortified workshop.

The lower floors are filled with buzzing cars and mechanics in gray uniforms, and above the third floor, quiet and luxurious count rooms begin. The six windows of the Count's Lighthouse in a semicircular wall offer a view of the Back from such a height that the river seems "no wider than a path". The entrance to the dam is protected by rings of walls with towers and two bastions of a hundred shooters, and above them rises a central building of red Almer brick. The Northerners considered Arrowback the second-most fortified castle of Almera. A mile downstream, Beck crossed the Aleridan-Faunterra rail bridge.

### Count and his relatives
Count Arrowback is a short, vain, "whole pointed" man of fifty. He has prickly eyes, an arrow-shaped beard with a wedge, a mustache and a mouth similar to a razor scar. Joaquin, in appearance, considered him a Yelenist; Mark also found him "similar to many of the Yelenites." The count is quick-tempered, but calculating and suspicious, and the “good news” considers “probably a lie.” He was attended by the knights Bellem, Morrigan and Spike, and was commanded by his brother, Colonel Dolph, who was touchy and hot. The vassal of the earl was Baron Auguste Miranda Clare of the Catrina family, Baron Bonegan. The daughter of Count Rose in Fantasia is called "Foremother Agatha's pure-blood granddaughter"; she has no brothers, but has two sisters. The three of them will get “a colossal profit from a spark dam and a navigable river.”

## History in the Series
### Alanis letter and bridge collapse
In December 1774, [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] secretly sent a letter to Count Joaquin Yves Hanna from the besieged palace in Faunterra, contrary to [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]]'s prohibition. ([[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]]) The count was afraid of provocation by the "young Origin" or the priest. He tested the messenger by offering him poisoned wine. Joaquin refused and defeated the three knights of the earl in the fight, losing two fingers, and then grabbed the shaking earl by the beard with a brushless hand. Erwin, meanwhile, was angry that "Erouback doesn't write." Soon, a bridge collapsed at the castle, and the emperor [[adrian|Adrian]], as was then believed, died (see Crash on the Back). ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]]) For her help, Alanis promised the Earl of Blackmore County, and Gallard later agreed to give him a blackmore spark shop. Injured Joaquin was recovering at Beck Castle for two months. Prior to the trial, [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] suspected that Erwin and Alanis had a "reliable vassal" in eastern Almera with a powerful stronghold and control of the rail road.

### Investigation
In the winter of 1775, Erwin's men, [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]] and [[itan|Ethan]], interrogated the fishermen and the Earl's men who had first arrived at the bridge. In March, [[mark-voron|Mark]]-Voron, [[ded|Grandfather]] and [[vnuchok|Grandson]] came to the castle on the trail of the kidnappers of the Objects. Locals were afraid of "bands" for Beck. The count met the guests in the light, told the story of the bridge, “could not stand the frost”, and even dropped his head on his hands. He did not allow guests to visit the destroyed bridge under the pretext of danger. According to the count's books, Mark found a record of the burned inn "[[dzhek-bakler|Jack Buckler]]". All the way the guests were accompanied by patrols with two arrows on the coats of arms, and along the western wall of the castle there was a whole battalion. Raven concluded that the earl was "strongly wagging his tail in front of the duke" and was involved in something. Mark and Grandfather suspected that the count kept stolen items in the castle. Major Backfield, too, was convinced that "Count Arrowback is an Origin's dog" and the Objects he had. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]])

### Court
In May 1775, at the [[sud-nad-mensonom|trial of Manson]], Crow caught fishermen witnesses, for whom Baron Bonegan vouched. The Prosecutor stated that their testimony was falsified by order of Count Arrowback and Baron Bonegan. Bonegan was arrested.

### Alliance with Erwin
In June 1775, Erwin, with four hundred horsemen, approached Beck from the east. The dam was the only way to the west: another bridge, in the possession of the faithful parish of Countess Daynite, would have been burned by first order. The count went out to meet under his pennant. Erwin apologized for betraying the alliance with Alanis, and added that he would cross Beck anyway. The Count, formally a vassal of the Prince of Gallard, found himself between two fires. He gave Erwin the best rooms, provisions and a battalion and for the species wrote to the parish that he would "stand to death". On the occasion of the union, the Earl, at the request of Erwin, canceled so that Gallard would later learn about the crossing of the Northerners.

On June 7, the Count, by his own design, put forward a battalion to meet General [[veksler|Wexler]] under yellow flags, as if transferring an army to the arch. However, Wexler's knights attacked the column on the road from the dam. Erwin's kairas, which had settled in the forest in advance, struck them in the flank and defeated the Almerans. The Count lost half of the battalion and was "stunned". Dolph accused Erwin of reporting to Wexler himself and nearly fighting Captain [[gordon-syu|Gordon Sue]]. Erwin reconciled all, giving the brothers the herd of spare horses Wexler - from eight hundred to a thousand heads. The Count singled out about five hundred horsemen under the command of Dolph, and they fought for Erwin in Almera: near the town of Breakdown, the knights of Arrowback, along with the Xes and Cordelia's mother, beat the monks of Gallard. ([[mat-kordeliya|Mother Cordelia]]) Erwin left the wounded prisoners at Beck Castle. Almeran opponents called the count a “traitor” and said that Erwin “bought” him.

### Against the Horde
In the summer of 1775, Arrowback Castle was considered the best place to defend against the hordes of [[paul|Paul]] and [[yukhan-rejs|Johan Reis]]. Before him thought to build a strip of shafts. Here went the corps of General Horton and the remnants of the army of Count Lilliday - five battalions, and the count's battalion was to help them. The Horde bypassed the castle, crossing the Back. The Shavans complained that "Blackmore and Arrowback hid in their castles" and no trophies were won. The wave from the Wilhelmine Abbey went to the capital via Arrowback. In August 1775, according to a letter by Henry Farway Laure, the Count, together with Countess Daynite, opposed the authority of the House of Farway in Almera.

## In "Fantasies"
The author calls the stories “Fantasies” possible fates of the heroes after the end of the cycle.

In the summer of 1775, when the shawls came to Almera, the daughter of the Earl of Rose boarded a train with refugees at Beck station and spoke all the way to Faunterra with Somerset Lighthart, brother of [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia Flame]]. In the capital, she volunteered, and Somerset, “nothing to do,” too. At the end of the summer, Rose confessed her love for him, and he, deciding to “become a scoundrel”, made an offer. The Earl unexpectedly consented to the marriage, but Somerset's seigneur [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]] replied with a "resolute no". Farway explained that Earl of Arrowback is "the one of the Almeran lords who least wishes to submit to my authority." Somerset, on the other hand, believed that the Duke did not want an excessive strengthening of the Lightharts. ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]]) The Count also wanted to see the son-in-law of the Earl of Lighthart-Flame, not a fugitive. Later, Somerset fell in love with another and rudely broke up with Rose: "I do not love and never loved you." In the summer of 1776, Somerset's ruined father told him that marriage to Countess Arrowback "could save" the family, and he wanted to return to Rose.

In August 1776, Mother Alicia, from the capital Pramatery, proposed to Erwin to keep his battalions for the duration of the election in the County of Arrowback, from where it was easy to reach the capital when in need: the count already provided his lands as a springboard for the northerners. Under the treaty with the capitol, the cairas were to stand there, outside the Lands of the Crown. Alicia conveyed to Erwin the Count's warm gratitude for the fact that the Kairs had diverted the horde from his lands. She also handed over an envelope with two letters and a drawing: the count lamented that his beloved daughter Rose “fell victim to an insidious tempter”, and asked to write her a couple of words of sympathy. Erwin gave the letter of the count with the drawing to his cousin [[robert-oridzhin|Robert]] and called the proposal "very good." Robert promised to think and left the letter at home to read again.

### "Ad-ri-an!"
In the fall of 1776, Earl Arrowback, along with his daughter [[roza-erroubek|Rose]] and Count [[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]], came to Erwin's council at Faunterra. He suggested moving the election to another city, away from the screaming crowds, and then marrying Erwin to Minerva: "You are loved by the nobles, Minerva - by the common people." Erwin refused: he “sworn an oath not to marry her.” Count assured that the audience would vote for Erwin, “but it’s too little to win,” and grimly added: “someone, but Adrian will definitely not spare you.” There was a kire camp in Arrowback County. There Erwin sent encrypted orders, and from there, five days before the assault, three battalions of trains moved to the capital until Adrian disconnected the Faunterra-Errouback branch. After the death of Adrian, the knights of the earl, together with the Kairs and the people of Flame, entered Faunterra and brought order without a fight. The count "swallowed" his daughter Rose for [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]], and Francis-Ilianus agreed to marry them.

## Contradictions
- The height of the dam in different chapters is given as "hundred feet" and as "half a hundred yards".
- Joaquin by birth took the count for a Yelenist, and his daughter Rose in "Fantasies" is called "the pure-blood granddaughter of Foremother Agatha."
- Mark remembers that the count "wife, five children", while in "Fantasies" Rose has no brothers and only two sisters.
