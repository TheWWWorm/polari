## History

In the middle of the 17th century, the Marquis George Faraday of the Gloria family, a navigator of the Southern Discoveries era, married a young lady Miranda of the Vivian family at forty-two. Two years after the wedding, she began to hear voices and almost mutilated her own baby with a razor. Of the five doctors, only Master Riley honestly admitted that he knew nothing about insanity, and asked for money, space and time for research. He bought a tiny island in the Mason Sea for a pittance and set up a hospital in the old fort, where he brought in other mentally ill people besides Miranda. For nine years, Riley watched the sick and carefully tested the "procedures", assuring the Marquis of "marked improvements" until Faraday died of a heart attack.

Along with the death of the Marquis, the money also ran out. Then one of Riley’s assistants, whose name history has not preserved, found a new source of income: noble families are ready to pay to get away with a crazy relative and never see him again. For more than a hundred years, the hospital has turned into a semi-secret prison with a “very mild regime”, where hundreds of people died. Influential clients made sure that it was less mentioned in official papers. Doctor Galuers, eight years practicing in the "Island clinic Faraday", called her the oldest hospital of souls in the whole of Polaris.

## Organisation

### Order and “Procedures”

Every day in the hospital began and ended with procedures. The mornings depended on the method chosen by the doctor: the “path of harmony” prescribed by Dorothy Sly consisted of rewinding the wool, steam baths, combing other patients and choral repetition of the motto of the hospital - “We are tormented by a mental illness. We're on our way to recovery. We are in a place of care, where we are loved and accepted” — a hundred times in a row.

Evening procedures were assigned according to the severity of the "symptoms" manifested during the day, and were divided into three stages:
- "Awareness of success" - the patient listed his good deeds aloud, and the rest clapped his hands.
- "Think" - deprivation of any right (for example, drink salted porridge with water), many hours of standing tension or kneeling in a wooden pad, extra dirty work.
- "Strike on the disease" - hanging his head down above the abyss ("fear strike"), rotation on the carousel ("disorientation of the disease"), a steel hoop on the skull ("surrounding pressure"), a flashing lantern in the fixed eye ("striking light on the darkness of the disease"). Patients often lost consciousness.

The symptom was considered any violation of discipline, audacious response, the word "no" and any "strangeness" - even an innocent desire to wash your hands again. At the same time, each patient was said goodbye to a single symptom: Dorothy - unconsciousness, Karen - sleep in clothes and refusal to eat, Annette - the habit of introducing herself at each meeting. The culprits were hung in the basement. Sybil Northwood, according to her memoirs, was also “treated” with sparks, darkwater potions and sedatives.

### Work

The main tool of therapy was considered work. Patients sewed, embroidered, knitted, painted dishes; literate people copied books. Once a month, the product was taken away for sale by the sailboat. The scriptorium was headed by a hired master Gustav, not a doctor, but a craftsman, whose healing issues were of little concern: it was important for him that the writing shop worked perfectly. The best copyists got a place in a warm and bright common room, a comfortable table and even a snack at lunch. The most valuable of them was Navi, who gave out thirty-seven flawless pages a day; for this reason, Gustav and the doctors tolerated all his oddities.

### People

The hospital is run by Master Mullin, who served here for eight years by 1775; he is assisted by a physician named Finger. The senior guard was Coddy's nurse. Patients other than Dorothy, Karen, and Navi include Annette, Pammy, banging her head against a wall at full moon, Sarah, hearing the voices of the dead in her own stomach, and Wilhelm of Maple, confident that he is the Forefather Wilhelm. On the night when the schooner comes to the island, the nurses give the patients to the sailors for a coin. Here he practiced for eight years physician Galuers, a graduate of the University of Marengo, who later left after a dispute with Mullin; he had previously examined Martin Shayland at the request of Count Vittor, and in 1775 was questioned about this by Iona Origin.

A side gate leads from the fort, taken by rods, behind which lies a bay with a pier.

## Events

- **About 1755.** On the orders of Lord Telurian, Karen Lighthart, [[menson|Manson]]'s wife, was imprisoned here; she spent about twenty years on the island (Karen herself spoke of eighteen in 1775). ([[karen|Karen]]) Her name was Caitlin.
- **About 1765.** The young man [[navi|Navi]] himself sailed to the island, appeared to the master with the words "Look at me", was found possessed, said "Then treat" and paid a hundred ephes; this is confirmed by three witnesses and recorded in the archive of the hospital. Mullin diagnosed him with “acute asymmetry of the mind.” For ten years, Navi, according to witnesses, has not changed at all. Navi himself later explained that he was hiding here from the secret order, pretending to be crazy: his strangeness here went for madness.
- **December 1774.** Major Backfield brought [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] here under the name "Dorothy Sly", his cousin; the treatment deprived her of her memory. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]]) For almost six months, Backfield intercepted letters from the hospital so that no one would know where the Countess was hidden.
- **Spring 1775.** Looking for "numbers", Dorothy remembered her family and daughter; Navi calculated that the chances of escaping from the island were 0,15%.
- **May 1775.** When "Major Sly" came to pick up Dorothy, Navi counted the odds at 37,42% and on the night of 19 May brought her and Karen out of the fort, stopping Coddy's nurse with numbers alone. While the nurses rescued the sailor from the violent Pammy, the fugitives - Navi as a sailor, the women in the role of his girls - passed the gate, went to sea by boat and gave themselves to the flow, and not to row north, as the chase expected. They were picked up by the Shimmerian ship White Panther; a schooner with Backfield and a doctor named Finger came out in pursuit.
- **1775.** When Minerva ordered Navi checked, Mullin sent to the court his conclusion about his illness. Later, Karen openly told Adrian that she was in the clinic on compulsory treatment on the orders of Emperor Telurian and fled in the spring.
