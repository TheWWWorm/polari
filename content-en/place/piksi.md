**Pixie** is a town with a "ridiculous name" in the Duchy of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. It lies forty miles south of [[labelin|Labelin]], a couple of hours' drive east of the imperial rail road. ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]]) The town owes its glory not to itself, but to the battle that took place in its vicinity on the Day of Descent in 1774. ([[bitva-pri-piksi|Battle of Pixie]])

## History
The city of Pixie became just fifteen years before the events of the cycle, under the old emperor. Previously, there were two villages separated by a lake. The western one was called Kamyshy; as the eastern one was called, the narrator is mockingly silent. In both villages lived good potters, and for about a hundred years they fought an irreconcilable war for supremacy at the surrounding fairs. On their prosperity, this almost did not affect: the lion's share of the profit was still taken by the baron who owned both villages.

Everything changed “dynastic marriage”: the daughter of the elder Kamysh fell in love with the son of the elder of the neighboring village. At the wedding, the parents of the young people were upset, began to fraternize, and someone came up with the idea to unite in the city: the city has more rights, and less taxes. The Baron was against it. The villagers stubbornly petitioned the sheriff, the judge, the bishop, the count, the mayor of Labelin and, finally, the Duke of the South Road himself. Everyone refused, except the Duke. Then he rubbed his many chins and approved the petition:
> “Origin has sixty-seven cities, and I have sixty. Let it be one more.”

## Face of the city
The peasants paved with stone five acres of land under the central square and laid on it a church, town hall, shopping arcades, and then a public bath. The potters united in two guilds: one named Kamyshna in honor of their native village, the other - the First. The town was named Pixie, nicknamed the elder's daughter. The fortress wall and paved roads were left “for later” when the money accumulated. No one rebuilt the house in a city-like manner. The townspeople still lived in clay loaves under straw roofs and kept vegetable gardens in several acres, chicken coops, and some pigs.

The lake, the forest and the field, which had previously divided the villages, were in the middle of the city. Therefore, in Pixie Square, he came out the largest city in the Southern Way: he was only slightly inferior to Labelin, and if you count the house of Sue's curve on the back, then he surpassed him. The traveler, shaking along the earth road among puddles and geese, could doubt whether he got into the city. So that such doubts did not arise, the townspeople dug in at each entrance on the pole with the sign “Pixie City. Population 460". The tavern stood on the central square, on the south-western shore of the lake, which the narrator calls a “puddle”.

## In the Events of the Series
### Joaquin's recruitment
On November 2, 1774, at the height of the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], eight local boys were sitting in Pixie's Tavern, gathered on a long journey, and three coppers in caftans with the coats of arms of Labelin - blue dolphins and gold sheaves. The twelfth visitor was a starving traveler who had fled from Almera. The soldiers talked to him, gave him an ale and told him how “the duke of bats crossed the Twins” and took a dozen castles and half a dozen cities. The traveler named himself Joaquin Yves Hanna of the Sad Hill, a native of the Holmogorian bishopric in the Southern Way. Upon learning that he was not a knight, nor a squire, nor a vassal, the minesweepers announced that, on the orders of his Lordship Labelin, they were recruiting peasants for the militia, and took him along with the local boys. Caught [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] in this tavern Sergeant Dodge nicknamed [[ruka-dodzh|Hand]], who then became a mentor to the militia. According to [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], Dodge served as a troop sergeant in the infantry of the Southern Way, exposed to the curtain against the northerners, ran over to Origin, disappeared at the Battle of Pixie, and then clung to the [[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrops]]: on the road near Pixie, he met Salem's peasants from Summerswith and went with them to the capital. ([[salem|Salem of Summersweet]]) He received the rank of "people's major" from the Snowdrops, and in 1775 Maurice Labelin bought him a robber army for Adrian; Magda believed that "no major he was not."

### Battle of Pixie
In the late fall of 1774, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]'s army crossed the Mila River and camped a few miles from the town on the night of December 2. Joaquin, who had served under [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] by then, noted that the army had stopped for the night at the town, "where more than a month ago" he met Sergeant Dodge. General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]] sheltered a spark army in the woods less than a dozen miles away, putting only small detachments under the flags of the Southern Way on the edge, and the northerners' intelligence took them for local militia. At midnight, the general raised the regiments and moved them to Pixie. On the morning of the Day of Descent, the northerners were defeated (see [[bitva-pri-piksi|Battle of Pixie]]). Horse sparklers chased the runners, and thousands of northerners littered the space from Pixy to Mila with their bodies; the remnants of the army escaped through the bridges that held [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]]'s cairns. The "chrysanthemums" of the imperial soldiers, according to the eye dealer, killed six thousand rebels in this battle. Erwin wrote to his cousin that the North had lost five battalions to Pixie. Thousands of Northerners were captured, including brothers [[krejg-nortvud|Craig]] and Donald Northwood. Of Erwin's generals, only Count Main the Hammer died, "shaking his head" rushing into battle. [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]], reaching soul "to the place of Pixie in the fields between two rivers", felt the death of six thousand compatriots.

It was later revealed that, on the eve of the battle, Erwin had sent a detachment of X's led by Damon Origin to the nearest station, Fairwell on the banks of the Mila, twelve miles from Pixie. From there the train took the Duke to [[faunterra|Faunterra]]. [[mark-voron|Mark]] later reasoned that the train at Fairwell had been hired in advance: "The Master" had foreseen that the Silver Fox would fight at Pixie's, and perhaps the abduction of the Dynastic Items had been planned even before that battle. The victory at Pixie lulled General Alexis's vigilance, and a few days later he was ambushed in the night Labelin. ([[reznya-v-labeline|Night in Labelin]])

### Memory of the battle
The name of the town became a household name for a long time. Alanis reproached Erwin: "The Battle of Pixie is seven thousand dead on both sides." Kair [[sejmur-stil|Seymour Steel]] told Iona that Count Vittor "moved over upon learning of the defeat at Pixie." Erwin himself later admitted that at Pixie "sent thousands of warriors to the Star" but felt no fear or guilt at the time. In March 1775, as the [[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrops]] were marching from Hampton to the capital and one morning Joaquin was rummaging about the enemy's approach, it seemed to him that everything was "just like at Pixie": as if he had "fallen asleep in March, and woke up in December, the day of the rout." Baron Kurt [[kurt-ajsvind|Icewind]], nephew of the Earl of Main, was considered a "hero of the Battle of Pixie". Dodge's hand was presented as "a veteran of the Wise River, the Labelin, and the Pixie." Lady Magda Labelin, preparing to strike the First Winter in 1775, consoled herself with the thought: "We had already beaten the kairs at Pixie." According to Inge Price, [[minerva|Minerva]] and Iona had previously recalled on blood that the rebels would be surrounded and defeated at Pixie. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]])
