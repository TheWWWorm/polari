**The Twins** are two rivers, the Upper and Lower Twin, on the northern border of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. Behind the Upper Twin begins the land of [[oridzhin-zemlya|Origin]]. There is a trade route here.

## Geography
The rivers are flowing like sisters. Between them lies not an island, but a narrow, “only five hundred steps wide,” a patch of land. From the border station, both rivers are visible: in the north, the Upper Twin is "sparkled", in the south, the Lower Twin is "splashed". Both are covered with bridges. The bridge leading into the Southern Way guards a massive fort with flags, arrows on the walls and trebuchet levers. To the north, in the Origin, there is a bridge that no one guards: behind the Upper Twin, an open road goes to the hills, to the peasant huts and grazing sheep. The Origins do not guard the border - according to the narrator, there is no reason for any Lord of the Southern Way to risk disturbing the peace of the northern sheep. The chain of border forts of the Southern Way stretched along the Lower Twin.

In the Lower Twin River flows the Wise River. The town of Elfort in the north of the Southern Way stands twenty miles from its mouth. There is a courtyard between the Twins. A few hours' drive to the north, in the foothills of the [[kristalnye-gory|Crystal Mountains]], lies the boarding house of Elena-u-Lake. ([[pansion-eleny-u-ozera|Elena-by-the-Lake Boarding School]]) In September 1775, [[minerva|Minerva]], flying on a celestial schooner over the edge of the Northwood forests, saw two Twins “twisted ribbons” below. At the end of 1775, some ships climbed the Twin from the [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]]. So at least one of the rivers is navigable and connected to the lake.

## Owners
The land of the Upper Twins enters the Origin. The wealth of Count Main the Hammer, one of Erwin's largest vassals, "came from the silver mines at the Upper Twin". His great-grandfather was a simple mine foreman. When the then Baron of the Upper Twins betrayed the Duke and tried to destroy the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], a brigadier with a company of miners seized the rebel castle. The baron was executed, and the duke gave his possessions to the brigadier, and he became the only northern feudal lord of simple origin. In 1774, Main Hammer was called the "Lord of the Upper Twins". He was the first to support Erwin when he challenged the emperor, and according to Erwin, one of his generals died at the Battle of [[piksi|Pixie]], "shaking his head" rushing into battle. The Twin Barons, along with Lilliday, Main, and the Coast Barons, were the prime vassals of the Origins, bringing their own kairs to war.

## In the Events of the Series
### The first novel
In the spring of 1774, [[minerva|Mira]], who was traveling from Northwood to the capital, crossed the Twins along the Trade Route. The bridge over the Upper Twins passed freely, and on the Lower one they paid a “significant bridge duty”. In early media outlets, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] in Beyond jokingly referred to mechanic [[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]] as "the strangest man north of the Twins"; there is no such conversation in the 2026 edition.

### Northern War
In early September 1774, [[mark-voron|Mark]] and [[itan|Ethan]] drove through Elfort and spent the night in an inn between the Twins on their way to Elena's boarding house. In August 1774, at a military council, Erwin waited under the banner of "Lords of the Upper Twin". He warned that Adrian would use the Labelin rails to reach the Twins in two weeks and enter the Origin.

In September 1774, Erwin was ahead of the emperor. His army crossed the Twins and invaded the Southern Way. Border forts on the Lower Twin were found abandoned: empty towers and "useless trebuchets". In the Southern Way, it was said that "the Duke of Netopyres crossed the Twins and leaned against us." In October, the roads from Gemini to the south, to Labelin, were filled with refugees (see [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]]).

### 1775 year
At the end of June 1775, at the request of David’s father, having fantasized about what would become the lord, Erwin said that he would build a “spark workshop on Gemini” to raise ore mining and steel smelting. In October 1775, Minerva challenged the Minister of Railways, Liam Schelier, to build a "spark dam on the Twins". ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]])

In late November and early December 1775, during the campaign of [[adrian|Adrian]] on the First Winter, the Twins were in the rear of the imperial army. [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] heard that ships under the Almeran flags had risen over the Twin from Smoky Distance. Twenty miles from the town of Main, behind Adrian's regiments, he found a landing party: the Hope Regiment and five battalions of General Horton. Whittor said [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] had "almost six battalions here and five more on the Twin". [[karen|Karen]] Lighthart called for [[menson|Manson]]'s jester to run to the bridge over the Upper Twin, the day away from her brother's regiment camp. Manson remained, promising only to catch up with her stagecoach at the bridge. Adrian, having received a report "from the shore of the Upper Twin", realized that the Duke of Farway had provided the fleet to Horton and that the cairns were in his rear, and publicly accused Karen of knowing about it. ([[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]]) [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]] expected that Origin on Gemini has "no Rings, no spark".

## In "Fantasies"
In Fantasia, the author's stories about the possible future after the war, Minerva in the summer of 1776 took up a spark dam on Gemini. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]]) In the First Winter, Erwin dissuaded himself from playing the evening game because he was busy with the mistress of the case: they decided “whose barony to flood”, where to move the peasants and what to say to the baron. Erwin suggested starting the conversation by asking, “Baron, how do you feel about fishing?” In October 1776, a motorcade of the empress and duke, who was driving to Faunterra on a new road from the First Winter, passed Main and Doyle and crossed the Twins and the Wise River.
