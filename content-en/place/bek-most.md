## Description
The Beck is a fast river half the width of Hanoi. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) It is blocked by a giant dam with [[erroubek|Arrowback]] Castle, which in everyday life is also called Beck Castle. Below the dam, water roars out of the tunnels, and for hundreds of yards the river whitens with foam even in winter, when ice stands elsewhere. Above the dam, Beck spills so widely that it seems like a small sea: it flows slowly and smoothly, reflecting clouds. When the dam was built, the water flooded a wide valley, and in the lowlands between the western hills formed swampy bays overgrown with reeds. Therefore, the west bank near the castle is difficult to pass, and the army can approach the dam only along the road.

The Beck is an abundant river. Local fishermen will bring carp by the bridge, and dried carcases are considered the best snack. Downstream, Back flows into Holliwell, followed by Steppe. ([[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) In late June 1775, Alanis and her companions, who had reached Back from [[rejs|Reis]] County, rafted him on a fishing boat to Holliwell in less than two days. A tiny Flame River flows into Back: the ancient ancestor of the Flame Counts washed golden sand in it, bought a horse and armor and gave the family a name. ([[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]])

About a mile below the dam, Beck was crossing the rail road from Aleridan to [[faunterra|Faunterra]], with Beck station at the bridge. ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]]) The bridge stood for about forty years. The other bridge over the river stands to the south, in the possession of Countess Daynite, a faithful vassal of the parish. After the crash, the only way west was the Count's Dam.

## The bridge collapse
At dawn on December 22, 1774, when [[adrian|Adrian]]'s train was on the bridge, the bridge collapsed under a steam locomotive, and the entire structure fell into shallow water. From the furnaces that heated the wagons, coals poured out, and the debris engulfed the fire. The Minister of Railways Liam Shelier later testified at the trial that the supports were destroyed by water frozen in the seams. ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]]) This explanation he repeated from the words of the engineers of the Earl of Arrowback, and the Count himself told Mark the same. Francis-Ilianus countered that there was no ice on Beck that day. ([[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]]) The bridge appears to have been destroyed by [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], who passed it to Count Erroback via [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]]. For more information, see The Becca Crash. ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]])

### Investigation
By order of Erwin Hyder, Leed and [[itan|Ethan]] combed the banks of Beck thirty miles down from the castle with a hundred greys and a hundred agents. ([[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]) They picked up ninety-two bodies. Two hundred and sixteen bodies were identified among the wreckage, and there were three hundred and twenty people on the train. Witnesses to the crash were Almeran fishermen who “eaten the carp” at dawn. The Kairs questioned each of them three times, and the testimony did not differ. Erwin doubted that the fishermen in the interrogation were so simple witnesses, and later it turned out that they really prepared. Later, at the [[sud-nad-mensonom|trial of Manson]], the Raven caught the fishermen: they were shown a spark dagger and a drawing of Hilt in advance. Baron Bonegan, the vassal of the count who vouched for witnesses, was arrested.

In March 1775, the count did not allow the destroyed [[mark-voron|Mark]] Bridge. The raven saw him from the height of the count's light: at sunset, the bridge seemed to be a "black bone" over the "grave" of the imperial train.

After the crash, the message on this line was interrupted. The train with [[minerva|Minerva]], proclaimed empress, went to the capital bypassing, through Orwell, and the cars from Almera in the spring went through Marengo, and the journey to the capital took four days. Two winter attacks on trains — the theft of the dynasty's heritage and the "baking of the guards in Baek" — forced the Ministry of Railways to look for ways to protect trains in the race.

## Frontier of Defense
### Erwin Crossing
In June 1775, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]], with four hundred horsemen, approached Beck from the east. The dam was the only road to the west, and Earl Arrowback missed the northerners. On 7 June, the Kairs and the Count's battalion defeated General [[veksler|Wexler]]'s knights by the road from the dam. The Count lost half of the battalion, but received as a reward the herd of reserve horses of the enemy - from eight hundred to a thousand heads. The captive Almeran nobles Erwin released, leaving the wounded in Beck Castle.

### Against the Horde
In the summer of 1775, the river became the boundary between Almera and the Crownlands. Minerva and her council decided to meet the horde of [[paul|Paul]] at Arrowback Castle and along the banks of the Beck. Five battalions of General Horton's northern corps withdrew there, along with the remnants of Count Lilliday's army, and the banks above the castle were covered by the regiments of the Silver Fox. ([[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]]; [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]]) However, the horde of Juhan Reis moved back to [[smolden|Smolden]] and on to the capital. ([[yukhan-rejs|Johan Reis]]) Minerva ordered Lees and Horton to withdraw to the Snake River. Later, the Silver Fox abandoned its position on the Back and passed to Adrian, and Horton reported that with five battalions he remained on the banks of the river. Here, above the dam, Minerva lowered her flying ship to meet Horton.

In the autumn of 1775, five battalions of Horton on Beck worried the Labelins: Maurice thought they could strike the capital as soon as Adrian went north. Wittor Shayland later became angry that the Farwayes had apparently taken Horton's kires off Beck with them for the First Winter.
