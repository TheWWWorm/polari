## Geography
The Volta is a ridge of islands sixty miles west of the coast of [[darkvoter|Darkwater]]. The islands are separated from the ocean by a large quiet well: protected from currents and storms, it turned into the Tops of the Dark Kings - a stretch of sea that is so overgrown with algae that the water in it became black and green, and ships that stray from the fairway are forever stuck in the "sea jungle". Topi can be passed only by three reliable fairways; according to the Voltian Fanno Grok, all three know only eight pilots in the world. It is also difficult to get around Volta from the west: there is a strong ocean breeze blowing there, and treacherous reefs stretch along the west coast, about which more than a dozen ships crashed.

Seafarers also noted Folta as a point of reference: if you look at the Star from the Foltic Islands, it points strictly to the south, while from the rest of the points of Polaris you can see in the south-southwest. Far to the west of the Volta, on the maps shown by Mireille Nan-Claire, lies one of the Metok Islands, behind which the "waters of death" with sea monsters begin. ([[mirej-nen-kler|Mireille Nen-Clair]]) The range of calm waters in the west is narrow, so the Voltians often go for it.

## State and laws
According to the "Guide to the lands of Polari", the Volta for almost a millennium developed on its own, without Polari's influence, and therefore developed an original culture and religion. According to the “Guide”, it is “similar to ancient Athens”: it is, in fact, a conglomerate of several city-states that flourish at the expense of the fleet and trade. The Senate rules, which is elected by vote, but the vote is paid: for the right to vote, a citizen contributes an amount of about one ephes. Senate hearings and pre-election debates are open.

The Voltians themselves are proud of their independence from Faunterra and their form of government, which is called equanimity. They have no family nobility; their proverb says that reason is more important than blood, although in fact the position of a person depends on prosperity. One-man leadership on Folte is considered a terrible lack of freedom and avoids it everywhere, preferring advice or pairs of mutually balanced positions. Even on ships, according to the "Guide", the senior officer has the right to challenge the decision of the captain; sailors of the "Sea Arrow" told that there are two captains on any Foltia ship - a forman and a rehman, who are on duty in shifts, and important decisions are made only together, and until both agree, the ship can lie in drift. According to the Voltaic maritime law, both captains are needed on board in a difficult moment: so Captain Kolistad explained his return to the White Wolf. ([[likejn-i-kolistad|Likein and Colistad]])

The fleet of the republic goes under the flags of the admiralty of the Volta: on the black field there are two circles, a large white and a small blue. Lady [[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia]] considered this banner a “geometry problem.”

## People and Customs
In the ports of Polaris, the Voltians are considered eccentrics. Sailors wear round berets with a white ball in the middle - the Guardian team had them blue - and bright sleeveless; the islanders themselves are short and cheerful. Instead of an orge and a hanty, they drink droma - a tincture of droma, a hop fruit that grows only on Folta. Their games do not know any winners or losers: all the participants together try to do a common thing, for example, to spread the deck on suits, and together they rejoice or lose together. The Voltians love public debate: two debaters go out to the square and discuss philosophy or politics, and the crowd backs up one or the other; sailors can also argue on ships while officers listen. According to the Guide, the truth on Folt is what is proved in a dispute until another dispute refutes this, and professional debaters — philosophers and lawyers — belong to a privileged class along with navigators and captains. In addition, the Voltians like to ask for advice on any subject.

Botsman of the “Sea Arrow” nicknamed Bivén, who visited Folta three dozen times and was once nicknamed the Foltite, said that the islanders like to negotiate personally, face to face, and you can send your wife, sister or mother to negotiate only for a very good reason. Mireille Nan-Claire, who lived among the Foltians for ten years, admitted that she never learned to understand whether the Foltian lies, jokes or speaks seriously; in the island theater, where the Foltians portray themselves, she found more sincerity than in life. On Folta there are jewelers, one of whom Mireille ordered a commemorative decoration, and a special dark, “like the night”, wood: from it they built the schooner “Sea Arrow”. Island tobacco has a thick and pungent smell.

There are also bad stories about the Voltians. Sailors of the "Sea Arrow" claimed that one day in Fairis, after the sunset of the Foltic galleon, a sea broke out, from which even cats were sick, and the islanders quietly sailed away, and none fell ill.

### Monster Hunters
A special foltian craft is the hunt for sea monsters: black scum, kiln, mastolom, bottom ancient and the largest of them, gurken. Hunting is bought by the Voltaic merchants for a lot of money. According to Mireille, in the entire history of the Voltians only three times managed to catch the gurken, and the Polarians - never. The two hunting vessels that accompanied Mireille bore the names of mythical monster hunters; they were special-breed warships: the sides were reinforced with iron hoops, the masts were ringed with steel, rams were on the noses, and the deck ballasts were loaded with huge harpoons.

## Faith
The Voltaic faith is different from the imperial faith, “like the wind from the sea.” According to the "Guide", the islanders worship the underground gods and water deities, which are also revered in Mills and Halliwell, and the authority of the Pramatery is not recognized: since they did not look at the Volta, it is not necessary to listen to them. ([[melnitsy|The Mills]]; [[kholliwell|Hollywell]]) Captain Lickane, hearing that the Polarians did not know about the gurken, fell to his knees in front of the window and asked the sea gods to forgive these “dark” people. On Folt, anyone can come to the temple and make a vow in the face of the sea gods; the temple servants help to keep it. Such a vow gave Mireille: not to marry until she achieves one goal.

The main thing they know about the Foltic faith on the mainland is the prophecy about the end of Polaris. Admiral Fanno Groc explained to the prophet [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] that it would not be the universe that would perish, but only Polaris: the lords would build so many castles, and the knights would put on so much iron that the land would not survive, the mainland would split into many islands, and the Republic of Volta would flourish as before. The term, he said, is about three hundred years; until then, the Foltians have not tried to harm the empire. The Voltians themselves also hope to get to the Star, and the prophecy says:
> “Happy are those who will fall on the Star before the big split. Everyone else is unhappy.”
According to the Guide, after the split, the fast ships of the islanders will begin to sail between the fragments of the world, gradually teaching the Polarians a "reasonable foltic order", and until then, the Polarians should not be hated, but regretted, as uneducated children. Sailors of the "Sea Arrow" retold this doctrine in a different way: on the mainland there will be some disaster, a volcano or a flood, only those who flew to the Star will be able to escape; besides, the Foltians seem to believe that the world has the shape of a ball. In The Shadow of the Great Tree, the idea of the destruction of the world is called faith, which is shared only by the Foltians.

## History
According to the “Guide”, as early as the tenth century, when the Empire of Polari had not yet expanded beyond the Hollywell, the Foltans maintained trading posts on the western, northern, and southern coasts; through their fleet, the Swordbearers of Fairis and Minnis, the Shavans of the Steppe, Northwood pirates, and the lords of Origin exchanged goods. Long before the Polarian colonists, the Voltians bought tea, coffee, and even spark eyes from the southern Aborigines, which were worn as ornaments. At the same time, in its section on the Mills, the same “Guide” states that sailors from Fairis and Minnis “discovered” the archipelagos occupied by the republics of Sin and Folta during their fourteenth-century westward expeditions; these two claims are difficult to reconcile.

Gallard Almera believed that the commandments of the Foremothers are known to everyone in the world, "except the Foltians and the most dense Shavans." Fanno Groc claimed that the Foltians were the first to circumnavigate the Laem Cape in [[shimmeri|Shimmery]], long before Lord Lymon's crew from Marengo, and haul home tea, coffee beans, tailed birds, and monkeys: the Volta seemed to drink the best coffee in the world when Faunterra knew only barley broth. Foltic travelers were also at the bottomless failure - "in the sixth century, in the twelfth according to your account", that is, the chronology of the islanders diverges from the imperial.
