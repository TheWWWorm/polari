The Heart of the World is the capital of the Duchy of [[nadezhda|Hope]] in the center of the mainland. According to the "Guide to the lands of Polari", this is the strongest fortress of the Empire and the only city-hundred-thousander, fortified according to all the rules of the castle fortification.

## The fortress and its history

For five hundred years, the Heart of Light was the southwestern stronghold of civilization and was considered impregnable. In the sixteenth century, during the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]], the city held an army of nomads for a whole year. Darian the Wicked could not take him by storm and led a painful, costly siege, in a year of which a third of the besiegers died from disease and wounds. Aid from Faunterra never came, and the city fell; less than half of the inhabitants survived. The survivors did not talk about the horrors of the siege, and only one phrase entered the ballad: “Death devoured our bones.” This bone became a symbol of suicidal stamina, and the Dukes of Hope placed it on the coat of arms along with a shining cup - a sign of good luck.

From the same fortress, the duchy received a name. It was not until the sixteenth century, after the Horse Wars had ravaged both lands and restored them under a single power, that the Duchy of Hope appeared, as the saying related to the capital: “The Heart of Light stands, hope lives.”

In the novels, the fortress is described by the same words: "the majestic Heart of Light, surrounded by three belts of fortification, is considered the most protected of the cities of the Empire", and only once in five centuries it was taken by the army of Darian the Bad after a year's siege. When Minerva reproached [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] with indifference to the fate of the besieged Melorange, he objected that the townspeople themselves can go to the walls: the Heart of Light with its one hundred and twenty thousand inhabitants for a year stood against the enemy. Other strong castles compared to him: whoever saw "the impregnable hromadas of the Heart of Light, Evergard or the First Winter" was not impressed by the walls of Nan Clair. Shavan Ganta Royhar, building his steppe castle Rei-Roy, took as an example the Heart of Light - a fortress that accommodates the whole city.

## The Legend of the Dream Goddess

Neymir told [[chara|Chara]] a Shavan legend: the goddess of sleep, who lived in the Under Kingdom, flew into the underworld at night with a disembodied spirit and saw the souls of people. ([[nejmir|Neimir]]) She fell in love with a young shepherd with a beautiful soul, and when he died, her heart broke with grief; the accumulated love splashed with rays that made people wiser, animals fatter, and the earth more fertile. Around the house of the shepherd there was an oasis, and in its place now stands a city - the Heart of Light. According to Neymir, it is "the only city in the world where the foot of the goddess walked", and it contains a fifty-foot statue of the goddess of dreams made of pure silver.

## Lightharts and Farway

In the middle of the eighteenth century, the Heart of Light and the port of Leonhard were personally owned by Duke Jonas Lighthart of the family of Helena, who ruled all Hope. He received a quarter of all gold mined in the duchy - and gold there was mined as much as in all other lands together. When the Shutov plot collapsed, Jonas was in the Heart of Light, "where the hand of the lord did not reach", but, since his sons and daughter were in captivity, he himself went to Faunterra to die. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]])

The proud inhabitants of the capital, "hardened in many privations," would not recognize any authority other than Lighthart or one of his first vassals, the Counts of Flame and Farway. Emperor Tellurian chose the cunning Count Farway, who “honestly” brought him a purse with taxes, sleeping off a handful of himself. A month later, Farway received the Heart of Light and the ducal title and, contrary to expectations, lasted twenty years. The Lightharts retained only Leongard; with the loss of the Heart of Light, the trade route from east to west collapsed. ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]]) Jonas' youngest son, Edgar, who became Count of Leongard, dreamed with the help of kairs "to beat the Farway out of the Heart of Light" (see [[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]]).

## City and Rail Road

Through the Heart of Light came the main ways of the South. The railroad connected him to Faunterra: in "One Star Only", Adrian celebrated the day when "the ancient capital of Hope connected with Faunterra", and Henry Farway traveled three hundred and forty miles by ultrafast train in less than a day. ([[zheleznaya-doroga|Railway]]; [[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]]) The branches led to Aleridan, Farway, and Leongard, and nearly a hundred miles from the coast of Mason's Bay, the way went through the clayy brown desert, where signal posts monitored every delay of the train. In 1775, Almera and Hope announced the doubling of the road between Heart of Light and Aleridan. On these rails [[kharmon|Harmon]] in 1774 looped between the cities, covering the tracks, and even before the mechanic [[luis-mariya|Louis]] was robbed and beaten on the road between Farway and the Heart of Light.

## In the Events of the Series

- **The Return of Adrian (1774)** From Litland, never meeting the Shavans, [[adrian|Adrian]] led the regiments to the Heart of Light in a week's grueling march and found himself at the walls early in the morning. Henry Farway, who did not send troops against the Westerners, justified himself, but the emperor demanded to bring his grandchildren of the Agatha family - [[laura-farvej|Laura]] and [[dzheremi-farvej|Jeremy]] - and took them hostage. From here the army moved by train to Aleridan, and in the Heart of the World there were people who warned Gallard by the dove.
- **Prophet.** [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] in his pilgrimage came from Leongard to the Heart of the World, found the Duke of Farway in confusion because of his grandchildren and received from him a gift of the book of the Forefather Edward “Divine Mechanics”.
- **Laura.** Laura Farway, once in Evergard, compared the new place with the Heart of Light: there she freely argued with her brother about the Foremothers, and here, she suspected, free speech is a reliable way to get on the fire.
- **Strange maneuver Alanis (1775).** [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], as suggested by [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], on behalf of the Puppeteer boarded the train to the Heart of Light, but only reached Orwell and returned; [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] wondered who in the Heart of Light she was supposed to contact.
- **The Flight of the Brigade (1775).** [[paul|Paul]]'s brigade landed at Leongard, captured a train on the western branch to the Heart of Light, but two hours away from the city, a spark disappeared; after an attack by desert gunners, the detachment went into the sands, as far as possible from the Heart of Light, destroying everyone who could tell Farway about his path.
- **War with the horde.** In the autumn of 1775, the Minerva courtiers reasoned that the Heart of Light had strong walls, "but what are they against the Rings?"

## In "Fantasies"
In the story “Annoying trifles”, one of the “Fantasies”, which the author calls possible options for the future, the fate of the Heart of Light is decided in the summer of 1776. After capturing Almera, Henry Farway with his army moved to Aleridan, and the Heart of Light "stands empty." Farway vowed to return the city to Lighthartham if [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia Flame]] married Erwin Origin and brought him the support of the Northerners. ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]]) The Severians, appreciating the aid of Nexia and [[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]]'s regiment, promised Farway support. But Nexia quarreled with Minerva and in five months in the First Winter never got engaged to the Duke, so Farway postponed the deal. Without the “golden city”, Edgar was in danger of ruin. [[menson|Manson]], unaware of this, toasted Heart of Light, a "golden city, an impregnable oasis in the desert" that returns to its rightful owners, and then encouraged Edgar to take the empty city by force.

## Disagreement in text
- Neymir claimed that in the First Horse War the Heart of Light "stood a siege for a whole year", while according to the "Guide" and the text of the novel, the city fell after the annual siege. Neymir's words sound like a joke designed to tease Chara.
