## Description
Labelin is the capital of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]] and the residence of the Dukes of Labelin, the largest city of the Duchy. According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, it has about 325 thousand inhabitants and is the second most populous city in the world. The city stands on the rail line leading to [[faunterra|Faunterra]]: the capital is about three hundred miles and about two days by train. The rail link links Labelin to the Crownlands, Almera, Hope and Litland, and goods flow here by river. The trade route leads from here to the north, to Doyle, and forty miles south lies the town of [[piksi|Pixie]].

### Face of the city
Mira, who had previously seen only the narrow, walled Waymar and Bear Fang, was amazed. Labelin "grew up a good square mile" and knew no fortifications. The houses stood roller-coastered, separated by alleys: three or four floors, with marble columns at the entrances, bas-reliefs on the pediments, windows half human height and livery lackeys at the carved doors. On the wide streets drove crews for eight riders, and two oncoming carriages easily dispersed. The size and luxuries of the North seemed ugly.

Unlike Aleridan or the First Winter, where feudal lords look after the appearance of the streets, in Labelin everyone is built as they wish. Along the rails stretches the street of the rich with terrace houses, palaces on the arches above the ponds, huge frescoes, columns on the backs of lions and mosaic windows “like dragonfly eyes”. Harmon, walking around the market square, dreamed of buying a house near the station to see the trains from the window, but realized that the prices here are exorbitant: a good house costs more than a thousand ephes.

### Gorozhans
Labelins seemed to Mira strangely similar to each other: men are pussy and bearded, women wear braids below the belt. They dress loudly, in aloe and green with gold embroidery, and even drivers flaunt red liveries. My favorite thing about the city was food. Every fourth house was a tavern, and in the evenings the tables were taken directly to the streets, where bearded people feasted in bright kaftans until late at night.

### Buildings
Countess Sybil and Peace stayed for a week in the hotel "Star of the Empire" on Cathedral Square. This building of white stone occupied a whole block and was larger than the native castle of Mira. In the middle of it was a refectory hall for several hundred people, similar to the nave of the cathedral. In the city there is the Cathedral of Wisdom of the Forebears with the icon of Agatha the Radiant, which Joaquin considered the best of those seen (in early media Joaquin called here the Basilica of St. Sophia and the Cathedral of Wisdom of the Forefathers), and one of the Sacred Objects is stored on the altar of the central cathedral. There is also a library that Mira visited, a bank where bills are paid, a branch of the Shayland Bank and pigeon mail. Near the city itself lies the estate of Baron Darryl, “grown to the outskirts of the disproportionately huge Labelin”. ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]) Nearby stood the monastery of St. Maximian, burned in the summer of 1774, and outside the city - the country ducal castle.

## History
### Antiquity
According to the "Guide to the Lands of Polari", in the War of Despair, King Orsayn Myriametz, deceived by Heinrich Leed's false spies, decided to attack the "undefended" Labelin and lost thousands of swords. Under Labelin, Duke Odar Spesivius died in battle with the Shavans. ([[odar-spesivyj|Odar the Haughty]]) According to the book “The Secret Horrors of the First Winter”, which is read in “Fantasies”, the body of the deceased lord went to the Shavans, and made a bowl out of the skull.

### Sale Spheres
In June 1774, [[kharmon|Harmon]] brought the Light Sphere to the Labelin and, without risking carrying the shrine to the feudal citadel, hid it in a hotel on the outskirts. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) He sold the Sphere to Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] for 36 thousand hilts, then cashed the bill of exchange at the Labelin Bank (see [[lzhe-sfera]]). The treasury of the city at that time was managed by [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]].

### Northern War
In the autumn of 1774, during the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] approached the city. Maurice gathered about fifty thousand people, including thirty thousand peasant militia. The army rose across the Trade Route five miles from the city and for weeks the moat's snout. Erwin defeated him, losing, he said, two people. Labelin's cavalry fled, the infantry surrendered, and fifteen thousand journeymen went into service to the North for food and pay. The Duke fled by train with guards, vassals and family items, but threw pigeons. In early November, the North entered the Labelin without a fight. Erwin drove into the city in front armor, the philistines welcomed him from the windows. In addition, the night battle took place near Labelin, in which the spark army of the Silver Fox was defeated (see Night Labelin). ([[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]]; [[reznya-v-labeline]]) As Magda later recalled, it took sixteen battalions of kairs and two battles to capture the Labelin, one of which became "the bloodiest in half a century."

The city elders greeted Erwin as a "benevolent lord", and the guilds paid him taxes in advance in exchange for a discount. In the House of Snow, Count Lilliday presented letters of thanks to the philistine Labelin Origina "for mercy". Labelin became the stronghold of the Northerners: here Erwin received the Northwood army and received a warning letter from [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia Flame]]. The duke himself moved the court to Grace.

### Release
In July 1775, when there was talk of evacuation in the capital, [[konto|Konto]]'s First Credit Bank began to export its reserves to Labelin. At the same time, upon learning of the approach of [[adrian|Adrian]]'s army, the northern half-battalion left the city and locked itself in a country castle. The townspeople greeted Adrian's army as liberators, although the "liberators" went without uniforms and formation. Adrian then moved troops along the rails from Labelin to Hampton.

In October 1775, during the "liberation campaign" to the north, the garrison of the northerners left the castle. Magda noted the irony: the city, which the northerners took with such difficulty, the Labelins returned “without a single spark.” In the same campaign, Maurice sent his wife and sons to the Labelin, who came to the court of Adrian.

## In "Fantasies"
After peace in the First Winter, Windley and Main were taken over by the Origins, and Labelin remained behind the Labelin House. ([[dom-labelin|House Labelin]]) Duke Maurice moved to Faunterra, and his sons left to restore order in the Southern Way. [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]], responding to the ghost of Brother Damon, referred to the fatigue of the Northerners "after the double battle at Labelin." Detective Mack in 1777 called the “burnt monastery at the Labelin” among the affairs of the secret order.
