**Melison** is a "beautiful town of winemakers" in the [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] Mountains. He hides in a valley away from the White Tract, and a passing traveler will simply not notice him. Here, at the end of 1774, [[kharmon|Harmon]] settled under the name of “the famous Horam Paulina Roberta”. With him came [[niza|Niza]], Master [[gortenzij|Hortensius]] and Rico. ([[onoriko|Onoriko-Maysor]]) Almost all the chapters of the "Coin" of the first half of the "Yanmei Hunt" take place in this town and in the Harmon Manor. ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]])

## Place
From the north gate of Laham to Pentago leads the White Tract, paved with hexagonal sandstone slabs. ([[lajem|Laem]]) By the end of the second day of the journey in the van, and on horseback by the end of the first, the traveler reaches the Small Pass. There are a dozen hotels and taverns, a stable, a wine cellar, a hat, a shoemaker's workshop and a "comfortable brothel" "The wreath of forget-me-nots". A mile after the pass from Tract to the west, a narrow path departs. It is so littered with rocks and overgrown with grass that it is easy to mistake it for a fault between rocks. Two miles west, in a secluded valley, lies Melison. Harmon considered this to be the only access road to the town. Except for the mountains.

From the balcony of the estate, standing on a hill, the whole valley is visible. At the bottom of the town hall with a sundial, a church with a belfry and white houses under the tiles, each with a flower garden and two trees. On one side there is a foamy river, along it an olive grove and an alley for walking. On the other hand, there are colorful bazaar tents: they trade there once a week, but the tents are not removed. The mountain slopes are covered with patches of vineyards, on the farthest and steepest graze goats. Behind the olive grove is a grove. There is no winter in Shimmery: “as autumn has come, so it lasts until spring.”

## Residents
They say “with a funny mountain accent,” don’t wear hats, move their lips when they put numbers in their minds, and “never rush anywhere.” The Laemians consider them tight-minded and laugh at them in the bazaar, so the Melisonians do not like either the Laemians or the bazaars. Wine from local vineyards, "Monat Erlin", Harmon was once served in the Southern Way "with a certain pathos." Here it is sold in any zucchini a little more expensive than a simple ale.
- Master Cornelius is a burgomaster, wearing a burgomaster's scarf. A person is thorough and touchy, consults with the innkeeper about everything. He knows the history of the Council of Five and tells it to Harmon, and with pleasure. Together with his wife, he is a "conservative" and idolizes Origin.
- Father Elisius is a good-natured priest, he has a wife and three daughters. He lives by one principle: the gods are good and the world is just, “so wait and everything will be formed by itself.” To anyone who asks for advice, he asks questions about the twelve main commandments, starting with “Do you know your place in the world?” and then says that the gods will take care of a good person.
- The constable is the "faithful constable" of the burgomaster. I’ve never searched anyone before because the people didn’t hide anything. He hates rebellion in all its forms.
- Traffickers have at least three. The trader, with whom Cornelius consults, was the first to learn from the Laem cab driver that Horam was not Laemian at all. One of the innkeepers stands for an independent kingdom.
- Harmon's cook is a native of Melison, and she has a sister in the city.

## History
### Sale of the estate
Until recently, Melison was owned by Baron Monat-Erlin. All three years of [[adrian|Adrian]]’s reign, the autumn in Shimmery was sunny, and the grapes gained extra sweetness. The baron, on the other hand, “could not tolerate full women and sweet wines,” fell out of love with possession and put it up for sale. The town with vineyards was bought for the sake of income by the Count Auguste-Riemar, the Third of the Five. He did not take the old Monat-Erlin estate and never appeared in Melison. The baron sold the estate separately, for a modest four hundred ephes, to an anonymous buyer. The Laem mediator then wrote in the merchant name: “Glorious Horam Pauline Robert.”

### Horam and the townspeople (Autumn 1774)
At first, the townspeople met the strangers cautiously. Horam's servant was carrying strange goods from Laem: silk, ropes, a machine, books, a learned old man. Cornelius suspected that the visitor wanted to open a sewing shop without demand, and twice tried to start a conversation with him about this. The four most prominent city ladies demanded that the mayor do something. Cornelius told the constable to search another cart. The constable was outraged that winemakers were being brought to the city. Horam's assistant replied that it was an acid, and there was no law prohibiting its importation, and rolled away while the constable pondered. Harmon, as he later recalled, deliberately “made a fog” for fun and laughed, watching as the burgomaster “wastelessly seeks the reins.”

Cornelius lost sleep and went for advice to his father Elisius. Soon the secret was revealed. Laem driver said that Horam is not Laemian, and the townspeople took up the investigation. Cornelius and the innkeeper went to the Small Pass, Elisius' father "for the common good" visited Laem. It turned out that Horam came from the north, and the Laemians laughed at him: “And since the Laemians laughed at him, then he is a good man.” The Burgomaster asked for forgiveness, and Horam called him, the innkeeper and Elisius' father, with his wives for a conciliatory dinner. The guests decided that the owner was building a new large ball. And why he needed an acid cart, no one remembered.

### Winter 1774–1775
One morning, Nisa disappeared, and Harmon decided that she had been kidnapped by enemies. He sent a cook to the mayor to find out if there were any strangers in the city. In the evening, Nizu was brought in a briquette by Elisius' father. She walked through the city and the grove, went to the church and asked the altar of the Spirit of the Steppe. The priest prayed for her to Saint Sophia and invited her to dinner. Since then, Nisa went to dinner with her father Eliza.

News of Adrian's death and the power of the northerners in the capital caused a heated debate in Melison: for a quiet town it was "an exceptional heat of passions bordering on chaos." The mayor called a secret conference: his wife, the constable, his father Elisius, and three innkeepers. After four hours of debate, the separatist innkeeper left, slamming the door. The rest were reconciled by the idea that the king-prophet [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] left the cell and will soon decide everything. In mid-February came the news of Hector's defeat by the Shavans. ([[gektor|Hector of Shimmery]]) After her, the townspeople began to look bad at Niza and advised Horama to sell her, "however she stabbed you."

### Ball, Marquis and Murder (March-April 1775)
On March 8, in the backyard of the estate, in front of a flock of urban boys, a hydrogen ball was tested. The wind cut off the cable and carried the ball with the Bottom in the basket. She found the valve and dropped safely, and the ball was found at the eastern end of the valley. Then the Marquis of Miral-Senelius, a vassal of the Second of the Five, arrived in Melison. ([[kuindar|Quindar]]) He offered Harmon a thousand ephesas for a celestial ship. About the Second of the Five, Harmon then questioned Cornelius at lunch. After that, he hid the ball, and Hortense sent to his family.

At the end of March, before dawn, a carriage arrived at the estate: [[moger-bakli|Buckley]] and Rodrigo brought Rico's related family. Rico was killed in the house, and Harmon was taken away. In the morning, the servants found the corpse and carried it around the city, that the shavanka of Niza stabbed Onoriko, Horama and Hortensia, ate their hearts and, “turning to the mountain cat”, rushed into the night. For two days, the townspeople did not open the shutter. Then the cab driver remembered that Hortensius went to his family, and others remembered a strange carriage that raced through the city without stopping. A group of citizens, led by the constable, decided to enter the estate. They buried Onoriko, cleaned the house, sent letters to the burgomaster, Lords and Sheriff Laham, and began to read four prayers a day for the health of Harmon and Niza. Several men even began to practice archery.

In April, Harmon and Nisa, after escaping the kidnappers, returned to Melison on foot. Father Elisius fed them, and Harmon asked for a confession. As he walked through the commandments, the priest said that “only a coward will call himself a complete scoundrel.” When asked about the first step towards the gods, the merchant suddenly decided to give the Light Sphere. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) From there he went to the burgomaster to borrow money secured by the estate. Cornelius again had Miral-Senelius as a guest.

### Flight to the Abyss (May 1775)
The ball was all the time lying on the roof of the estate: none of those accustomed to walking on the ground did not guess to look there. When he was removed, the marquis, burgomaster and father Elisius did not keep up the surprised outcry. In May, the "Heavenly Sphere" with Harmon, Niza, Hortense and Booth flew to the [[bezdonnaya-propast|Bottomless Abyss]]: "Melison Valley turned into a toy kingdom with beaded roofs, patches of vineyards, azure river ribbon." Later, [[khalintor|Halintor]] came for the Sphere. He did not find Horam at the estate, and the mayor of Melison told him where he had gone.

## In "Humans and Gods"
Serving as a cupbearer at Adrian's, Harmon looked down from the plateau at a town "like Melison" and dreamed of a home and a loving wife. Then he changed his mind: “Let’s say you settle down in Melison ... and they grow up and ask: “Father, why do we live in the ass of the world?”

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
In a letter to Lowes in the summer of 1775, Harmon recalls: “You reproached me in Melison: they say that I want nothing and have no life purpose.”
