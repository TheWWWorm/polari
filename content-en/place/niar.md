## Description

Niar is one of the great cities of the Crown Lands. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) It stands behind the river, a couple of days from the town of Izluchina on Canaan. ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]; [[khanaj|Khanai]]) According to [[polli|Polly]] Janet Harvey, riding horses in Niar were held only by nobles, and the city itself can be bypassed on foot in an hour. The story “Dolls on the Throne” nevertheless calls Niar “one of the great cities of the Lands of the Crown.” Harmon, offering Polly to work with him until the summer, promised then to land her "in the north of the Crown" so that she would return home. In the city there was a temple, which [[otets-david|Father David]] called Niar, and Honey Street, from where, according to rumors, the infection went. Polly's husband, John, was the best woodcarver in Niar. He kept a workshop on the first floor of the house, and they lived on the second. Polly said that her brothers hold a tavern: there she sang, and there she was first heard by John. When Snape heard that Polly was helping her husband in the craft, he said, "You have a strange life in Niar."

## Blue-Grey Plague

In the spring of 1774, a gray sea broke out in Niar. ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) When [[kharmon|Harmon]] arrived on April 12, the epidemic lasted for half a month, and the townspeople fled in droves wherever: to Almera, down the Canaan to the capital, the Southern Way and even to Northwood. The fugitives carried everything of value, from geese and pottery circles to lacquer shoes, and looked like inexperienced travelers. In Izluchina, where the Niarians fell with a shaft, the innkeepers profited because the doomed did not stint. Harmon picked up eight refugees, among them an old Painter with a grandson, sisters Jenny and Penny, an old woman May, a baby Kroh and a young widow Polly. Priest [[otets-david|David]], on the contrary, spent the night in the house of Izluchinsky abbot, in order to go further east in the morning and in two days to be in the sea-covered Niare.

Refugees talked about the sea more than “a doctor with a barber together.” It was said that the Westerners brought the mora, a dozen free riders, not from Halliwell, or from Reis, "eyey, patchy", on hoarse horses. The first to fall ill was a laundromat from Honey Street, whose stepdaughter was walking with horses. However, the mother-in-law herself remained alive and well. Everyone had their own remedy from the sea. The old woman May advised to drink a powdered silver agate with wine. Jenny and Penny were arguing over whether the ice bath or steam room was saved. The Old Painter believed that he was healed who had time to read sixteen prayers to the Foremothers and ninety-six Forefathers, and fled with his grandson because, illiterate, he could not memorize them. He assured that the Lord will cure the disease and has already sent his “emissary” to Niar. When on the way the grandson of the Painter blue lips, companions demanded to drive the grandfather and the boy back to Niar. Polly, who saw a real mor, stood up for them: the boy just got cold.

The city was cordoned off by imperial troops and no one was released. In June 1774, in an open audience, the Niar people asked [[adrian|Adrian]] to allow them to take out their relatives, whom the doctors recognized as healthy. The emperor refused: an apparently healthy person can carry the infection. He listed the measures of the Crown: the delivery of food and water, the abolition of all work except medical and sanitary, the prohibition to leave home, to gather, to hold services and funeral processions. [[minerva|Mira]], who was present at the audience, appreciated that the bishop “did not consider it shameful to report to the common people.” Dreaming of wealth, Harmon even imagined that after a sea in the depopulated Niara, it would be possible to buy several houses for a pittance and turn them over to returning citizens.

According to David’s father, two thousand and three hundred people carried away the sea in Niar, another five hundred in the surrounding villages and four hundred in Izluchina. By the summer, the epidemic was over. David himself outlived her: “More couldn’t handle me. I’m sorry, just like I am with him.”

## After the Sea

In the winter of 1774–1775, when [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] and his mother passed through Niar, the city was bleeding. There were no emaciated faces and hungry eyes, but there was widespread despondency. It turned out that Niar was “absorbed” by tax collectors. The Imperial Tax Office did not prescribe the norm per capita, but from all over the city and from each craft shop. People died or fled, but the norm remained the same. On the road from Niar to Faunterra, the Origins saw men stealing copper wires from rail poles.

## Snowdrop Rebellion

In the spring of 1775, the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] of [[salem|Salem]] approached Niar. Three days before the city, the physician Zub invited Salem to address his "prominent friends-friends" in Niar, Izluchina and Hampton: masters, shop sergeants, councilors. ([[zub|Tooth]]) Salem agreed, but sent two of his boys with him. A few days later, a column of four thousand people came out of Niar to meet the rebels: pedestrians and horsemen, food carts, retired soldiers in chain mail, city militias with crossbows. Women and children stayed home. Even officials and the rich donated money because everyone was warmed by the idea of tax cuts:

> "The whole of Niar supported the new purpose of the uprising."

Niar donations changed the life of the army. Previously, the “feeders” who were responsible for the food of every hundred were considered the most unhappy, and after Niar, there was more money than you need for food. In the capital, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] was informed that support for the rebels had already been provided by two large cities, Lowville and Niar, and a dozen towns. Salem said that without the help of good people, the uprising would hardly have gone further than Niara. New recruits from Lowville, Niar and Izlyuchina were especially proud of the campaign. One Niar boy was among the watchmen of Tooth at the van with the captured Salem. When Three-Finger's men shot his comrades at night, Charlie Bull's apprentice stunned Niarz with his fist, preventing him from screaming.
