## Description

The bend is a small town in the northern part of the Crown Lands, standing in the bend of the full-flowing Hanai. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]; [[khanaj|Khanai]]) Hanai here is "deep to black, wayward, whirling whirlpools", and the rayon ferry crossing, the only one for a hundred miles, has always been in demand. It was not easy to travel around the city: you would have to make a ten-day hook and pay three times the cost of crossing in the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. The Trade Route passed through Izluchina, which led directly to [[labelin|Labelin]]. There was a railway track nearby, and behind the river, a couple of days' journey to the east, lay the large city of [[niar|Niar]].

In the city stood the Cathedral of St. Leonora, where from the altar “a white-marbled Pramatery looked coldly”, and there was the house of the Izluchin Abbot. For Harmon Paula, Roger Izluchin was one of four inns on his favorite path. ([[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]) In the hotel he was waiting for letters from prominent customers. The owner of the hotel knew him for a long time and called him “Roger Paula”. For the letter, the owner sent one of the maids to the pantry. Two blocks from the hotel, if you go to the crossing, turn left, and at the shoemaker to the right, there was a deaf alley with brothels, “birds”, as they were called here. Among the buyers of Harmon, which he recalled, was the family of the Izluchin burgomaster "in the North Crown". Each October, the merchant brought her four daughters six pounds of tea, and when the mayor once saved and took only five, by March the family was left without tea.

## April 1774: Letter and refugees

On April 12–13, 1774, Harmon's train approached Izliuchina and met crowds of people fleeing from the blue sea, which had broken out in Niar half a month ago. ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) Izluchina herself was not yet ill, but she looked "terrifyingly quiet" like a forest hidden in front of a horse detachment. The merchant was persuaded to go there by Snape, who did not want to overpay the greedy nobility for someone else’s ferry, and Louise, for whom the letters were “almost a divine mystery.” ([[snajp|Snipe]]; [[luiza|Louisa]]) The ferryman rejoiced at the travelers: if people go to the city, maybe the sea will bypass it.

In the hotel tavern there was a feverish drunken fun. The owner explained that the fugitives from Niar do not spare money: "When you, look, you will die from day to day - why keep the coin?" Harmon received a letter, sealed with a surging and decorated with a pair of greasy stains. It was a challenge in [[uejmar|Waymar]] to Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]], which began the story of the sale of the Light Sphere. ([[lzhe-sfera|Sale of the Radiant Sphere]]) Louise later credited herself with persuading the owner to go to Izluchina and read this letter.

In the evening at St. Leonora Cathedral, Harmon met his old friend [[otets-david|Father David]]. The priest stopped for the night at the Izluchin abbot, and in the morning was going to go to the sea, to Niar. Then David said that the Objects were silent and no one could speak to them. Back at the hotel, Harmon, not knowing why, told the servants to find among the refugees those who do not have money for the road: in his vans there would be a place for six or even eight people, if children. In the morning, eight refugees from Niar (including old men, children, several girls) were in the wagon, and [[polli|Polly]] was among them.

That same night, Joaquin Yves Hanna, disillusioned with brothels, returned to the hotel tavern, stood up for a fair-haired girl who was being molested by the craftsmen, and then disgraced himself by letting his hands go. ([[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]]) The girl was Polly. The next morning, she joined the train, and for Joaquin Izluchina forever remained "the same town where Joe met Polly."

## July 1774: Harmon's return

In early July 1774, Harmon, fleeing from under the Labelin with the genuine Light Sphere, long confused the tracks with trains. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) Then the hired driver advised him a cheap crossing on the way to the Midway, and the merchant was again in Izluchina, in the same hotel. Mora was over by then. People fled, many went to the capital for the engagement of the lord, and the owner complained of boredom. Harmon admitted to himself that he came not by chance: “Here it all began — perhaps here and should end?”

Father David found him there. He said that the Maximian monastery was burned, and asked to give the Sphere, confessing that he belonged to a secret brotherhood that was looking for the language of the Objects. According to him, two thousand and three hundred people, five hundred in the surrounding villages and four hundred in the Izluchina itself, took away the gray sea in Niar. In his farewell address, David showed a bottle of poison, assuring him that it was not his fault to be afraid. Harmon decided that David did not want to kill him, but just in case he caused vomiting, picked up the driver with the guards and at sunset, although the crossing was already closed, left for the south.

## Clues in Harmon's Plans

After parting with Joaquin in early September 1774, Louise told him to write to her at a hotel in Izluchina. To the objection that she could not read, she replied: “I will go to the priest, he will read.” In the spring of 1775, having decided to live honestly, Harmon wanted to send a letter to Izluchina, “where one of my friends lives,” and give the Sphere to Father David. He even asked the Marquis of Miral-Senelia to let him go there on a celestial ship. The Marquis suggested instead fast horses and escorts: the ship, he said, it is safer to leave in Pentago, because in the north of the Crown lands raging peasant rebellion. Harmon later admitted that the idea with David was only an “argument for conscience.”

In the summer of 1775, having drunk on the ambassadors of Adrian, Harmon sent a bill of exchange from the bank. Two hundred Hilt went to the Izlyazina for a demand for Louise, two more Hilt - to the owner of a familiar hotel with a note:

> “When my Louise arrives, tell her to go to the bank. “Harmon Paula knows what the shit was.”

## Snowdrop Rebellion

In the winter of 1774–1775, [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] and her mother, Lady Sophia, traveled from Waymar to the capital. The Origins made a hook east to resupply, and drove through Izluchina and Niar. The cities greeted her with despondency, “so widespread that it is difficult to breathe.” Iona sent word from the bend, and [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] told [[minerva|Minerva]] that her sister would be in the capital in a few days.

In the spring of 1775, [[salem|Salem]]'s [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] went through these places. The physician [[zub|Tooth]] and the scribe had in the Izluchina, as in Niar and Hampton, "sighty friends-friends" among the masters and workshop elders. On the orders of Minister Dreyfus Bourne, an army of four thousand men, gathered from the tax guard and two brigades of free shooters, met the rebels at the approaches to the Izlyachyn. The peasants, trained by veterans, unraveled the trap and turned it into a flight. At the outpost at Izluchina, Salem disarmed his opponents and spared them. Even before that, [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], who was traveling with [[veselchak|Jolly]] in the Southern Way, deliberately turned from the Trade Route to Lawville, making a hook in nine days, so as not to pass the Bench, where he met Polly. Snowdrops sold trophies to merchants, and the residents of Niar and Izluchina donated a lot of money to the rebels. The rebels who joined the campaign in Lowville, Niar and Izluchina, held out especially proudly (for more details, see the Snowdrop Uprising). ([[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov]])
