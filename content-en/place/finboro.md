## City

Finborough appears only in Fantasias, which the author calls possible futures, in the short story The Rutin Affair. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]]) It's a small free city in the South Road. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) It stands on the river bank "on the Bay of Hanoi", and get here by river barge. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) The city lives by fishing, river transportation and weaving business: the rivermen bring raw materials on barges, the weavers make matter out of it, and from the weavers, the rivermen buy sail and sackcloth. Canals flow through the city; the streets by the river are called quays, and sewage ditches are connected to the canals by pipes under the pavement. The pipe on the third quay is hammered every summer.

Since Finborough is a free city, the mayor has the right to preside over the court. There are no prisons here: there are almost no crimes, and prisoners are kept in the basement under the town hall. The city also does not have its own executioner and, in case of need, summons a master from another city. Constable Davidson complained that in Finborough "the fly will fall - you will hear."

The city is small, but it has a spark machine running from eight water wheels. The barons do not own the city, but the sparkle technique belongs to the Devo brothers: the elder Bernard, harsh and rough, and the younger Reginald, quiet and restrained. They let out the spark at a tolerable price. One day, when the guild of weavers was busy, Bernard simply “knocked out the spark”, and the whole guild stood up machines. Between the Second and Third Quays there is a brothel, nicknamed the "mouse": a sewage ditch nearby, from which rats run to the girls. There is a city and a cathedral.

## Residents

- Alois Brickman is a burgomaster, an experienced official who knows how to obey the laws and keep records. He held local elections for mayor.
- Constable Davidson.
- Clarence Kane is an elder of the river guild, an engineer who studied water engineering and attended a law course. Adrien's opponent: he considered Yanmeytsa "drovosek", ready to "lay people instead of sleepers".
- Fisherman Finch is a poor man without a family, the owner of the dog Kusaki. The girls from the arsenic fed him and let him sleep in the kitchen in the winter.
- Chuck, nicknamed Gossip Man, who calls himself a “useful city worker”: he knows everything about everyone and tells everyone.
- Kitty is a girl from a mousetrap, the mistress of Baron Bernard.

## Elections and politics

In the spring of 1776, the knight Lady [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], "our Duchess", brought the news that the election of the Lord was scheduled for the days of Abundance. The townspeople argued. The elder of the weavers hoped that [[adrian|Adrian]] would be chosen, and then “our duchess will become empress.” The elder of the rivermen objected that Adrian would raise taxes on his rails. The knight himself bypassed the Kabaki with Bernard Devo, with whom he served in the Northern Flash. Chuck, who overheard them, learned that Lady Magda would "not be offended at all" if a spark disappeared in Finborough for one day - just when Adrian's speech was brought. Bernard punched Chuck's nose for it.

Soon a talking church device was brought to the city. He was placed on the altar of the cathedral, and the townspeople listened to the voices of King Francis-Ilianus, the supreme mother of Alicia and Bishop Amessin, and then the recorded speech of Adrian. ([[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]]) The former ruler called himself the “only legitimate heir to the throne” who “ruled you for five years,” and called ordinary people to Faunterra, promising to pay for the road and housing. The speech was repeated four times. Some citizens gathered to go to help Adrian, others decided that they would come for his money, but they would vote as they wanted. Clarence Kane noted that by “joining” the Church’s appeal, Adrian gained an unfair advantage over Origin.

## “Rutine case”

### The Dog Trial

In the summer of 1776, the barons, according to the general version, reconciled: after the visit of Abbot Bernard on June 10, he allegedly went to Faunterra to inquire about politics, and Reginald made a conciliatory speech at the town hall and returned a spark to the weavers. Soon, the red dog Kusaka, who belonged to the fisherman Finch, bit Reginald in the Third Quay Deadlock (according to the burgomaster, on June 12, according to Finch, on the morning of the 10th). The baron refused the fine and demanded the trial of the dog himself: “Any villain will be punished, even a dog.” Brickman conducted the trial strictly according to the Yumin code, with protocols and witnesses. The defendant's advisor was Clarence Kane, who had provoked Reginald's spite. The girls from the arsenic confirmed Kusaki's "sound mind", and the dog was sentenced to beheading.

For execution, the burgomaster summoned the executioner [[uolter-dzhejn-dzhon|Walter Jane John]]. He arrived with the lawyer Macfried Crowe (by all indications, this is [[mark-voron|Mark]]) and was horrified to learn that the convict was a dog. Mac first considered the case “boring in the world”: animal trials, according to him, happen, like a goat from Izlyuchina, which ate bills and fell into a debt hole.

### Solve

Mack asked Chuck, Kane and the arsenic girls and noticed the oddities. Elegant Reginald, who never went to the brothel, appeared in a stinking dead end at dawn, in high boots and without a sword. The good-natured dog who knew Bernard attacked his brother. Finally, Mac guessed: the Baron wanted to check the clogged drain pipe at the gate of the mouseman, and Kusaka was there first. Bernard Deveaux's body was found in the pipe. Reginald killed his brother at dawn at the gate of the brothel when he was returning from Kitty, and he wrote the story of the trip to the capital. He told the dog not to come to the pipe anymore. Kusaka was acquitted, and Mac promised to buy him “five pounds of meat award”.
