## Origins and Name
The Snowdrops was the name adopted by an army of starving people of the Way, peasants from the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], who were later joined by townspeople from the [[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]. Its core consisted of inhabitants of Summersweet and neighbouring villages who had killed their baron. The story of the march is recounted in [[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|The Snowdrop Rebellion]].

The name emerged in late February 1775, after the fighting at an outpost near the Bend. [[salem|Salem]] found a clearing full of snowdrops, remembered that his mother had called them “flowers of hope,” and ordered his company leaders to tie the flowers to their spears so everyone could see they were peaceful people. Since they were being called rebels, he said, let it be “the Snowdrop Rebellion.” From then on, flowers on spear points became the army's emblem. They appeared at the parade in Hampton too, although, as [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] observed, they “did not conceal the forceful power of the procession.”

The Snowdrops themselves long insisted that they were “not an army.” Salem repeated this every time Sergeant Dodge asked to be promoted. Yet after eight weeks on the road, Joaquin “could call these people nothing other than an army.” Admittedly, it was a weak one: unarmoured light infantry with an enormous baggage train.

## Growth of the Army
- **Early winter, 1774/75**: around three hundred people set out from Summersweet. By the time the army reached Bakers, Salem was already speaking of a thousand hungry lads.
- **Grays, January 1775**: eight thousand people, including five thousand armed men and a thousand trained spearmen who knew how to form up against cavalry.
- **Near Lowville**: the Wanderer spoke of ten thousand people of the Way. Tooth brought eight hundred townsmen, including a hundred merchants and seven hundred recruits.
- **Before Niar**: twelve thousand. A column of four thousand people came from Niar.
- **After the Bend**: more than thirty thousand spears. Townspeople now made up more than half the army.
- **After Hampton, March 1775**: forty thousand, two thirds of them townspeople under Tooth and Dodge.

Estimates before the [[svyatoe-pole|Holy Field]] differ sharply. [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] told Minerva of a hundred thousand, of whom sixty thousand “know which end to hold a spear by.” At [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]'s council, the figure was eighty thousand. [[veselchak|Jolly]] later gave the same figure. The text does not specify how many people actually stood on the Holy Field.

## Organisation
### Command
The army was divided into hundreds and tens. Salem gave the leaders of the hundreds tall red captured hats so they could be seen from a distance. Each unit had its own place in camp and in the marching column. Carts with distinctive features, such as “the wagon with the scarecrow” or “the cart with the blind ox,” marked the divisions in the column. The strongest men and horse-drawn wagons went first to pack down the snow, while two hundred men on sledges covered the flanks. Important decisions were made by an assembly of a hundred and twenty leaders of hundreds. It was this assembly that accepted Tooth's proposal to fight for “fair tax,” by a hundred and eight votes.

### Supplies
At first, everyone provided their own food. After the first battle, Salem introduced communal provisioning: all spoils went into a common fund and were sold together, while food was strictly divided among the cooking pots. Later, each hundred acquired its own kitchen with a dozen “feeders,” who received money or spoils and purchased the food themselves. Until Niar, this was considered a thankless duty. But donations from townspeople and the spoils taken at the Bend brought in more money than was needed for food, and the feeders' positions became the most profitable. A “tail,” a travelling market, followed the army. Merchants sold stale urban stock to the naive peasants and drove straight into the middle of the camp at halts. The army had a mobile hospital with a folk healer, later joined by physicians from Lowville. The baggage train carried a field forge with a master and three apprentices, as well as a clerk who had volunteered to chronicle the march.

### Military Training
The common people were taught formation drill by the retired Sergeant Hand Dodge and other veterans, and later by Joaquin, Salmon, and Corporal Billy. Training took place every morning and evening while the women cooked. Two picked hundreds, the “toughs,” were the only men in the army with shields and helmets. People said Salem's toughs were like Duke Origin's X-cloaks, although in training they barely measured up to an average northern infantryman. The toughs were loyal to Dodge and Tooth and later arrested their own leader. In the battle against cavalry at the outpost near the Bend, the peasants formed six ranks: three close ranks with spears and pitchforks, followed by three looser ones with flails, which they used to knock riders from their horses and break their shins.

### Two Camps
Over time, two factions formed within the army. The peasants of the Way stood by Salem, while the townspeople gravitated towards the tooth-puller Tooth, who knew how to “show them respect.” Tooth and Dodge called each other “people's general” and “people's major.” The clerk explained to the townspeople of Hampton that the people's army elected its own commanders. The peasants of the Way, “sullen and inarticulate,” felt lost among the swaggering, loud-voiced townsmen who readily shouted “Fair tax!” at the first signal.

## Prominent Snowdrops
### Salem of Summersweet
A reluctant leader and an advocate of peace (see [[salem]]). Right up to the Holy Field, he sought justice, a pardon, and seed grain, rather than tax reform.

### The Wanderer
A mounted crossbowman with a mace, who called himself a former brewer from Whitehill on the Trade Road whose business had been seized by a “little lord.” He joined the march back at Pixie, suggested collecting equipment from the frozen spark troops, and proposed going to the capital. In battle, the Wanderer never missed. He commanded the scouts and conducted the negotiations at the outpost. For a long time, Joaquin could not remember where he had seen this round-bellied, limping man, and only in Iona's carriage did he realise: this was [[brat-lyudvig|Brother Ludwig]] from the monastery where the [[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]] had been stolen. The Wanderer helped release Salem from arrest but would not let him flee. He did not appear at the Holy Field. Minerva ordered him caught and hanged.

### Tooth
A tooth-puller from Lowville: thin, wiry, sharp-nosed, with a gold canine tooth. He led townspeople against the tax collectors, brought the first townsmen into the army, and proposed the slogan “fair tax.” Tooth was brave and ambitious, comparing officials to rotten teeth that needed pulling. Sergeant Dodge recognised him as a good organiser, though ignorant of military affairs. Tooth fired on General Gore's envoys. At the Holy Field, the Snowdrops handed him over to the guardsmen.

### Hand Dodge
A stocky, red-haired retired sergeant, a “veteran of the Wise River, Labelin, and Pixie” (see [[ruka-dodzh]]). He was the man who once caught Joaquin in a tavern on Pixie's square. Dodge devised the plan to turn the carts against the baron's sons' knights and made infantrymen of the peasants. He was illiterate and boastful, and dreamed of promotion. Together with Tooth, he fired on Gore's envoys, ordered Salem arrested, and was handed over at the Holy Field. He later became a “major” in Adrian's mercenary army.

### Others
- **[[dzhoakin|Joaquin Eve Hannah]]**, nicknamed Three-Fingers or the Deserter — an instructor and Salem's adviser on matters concerning the nobility. Left the army before the Holy Field.
- **Jolly** — Joaquin's squire, a gloomy joker forever predicting “coffins and spades.”
- **Corporal (leader of ten) Billy** and **Salmon** — veterans from the Way who had served with Joaquin as far back as Labelin. They helped free Salem.
- **[[charli-telenok|Charlie the Bull]]** — a tanner's son from Lowville, actually Charlie the Calf, one of the toughs in Dodge's picked hundred. He shot a spark dart at Joaquin; that night, at weapon-point, he helped Joaquin neutralise the guards at Salem's wagon.
- **The clerk** — chronicler of the march, a talker and an expert in history, Tooth's closest assistant.

## Weapons and Patrons
At first, the Snowdrops had only pitchforks, flails, axes, and equipment taken from dead spark troops. Through [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]], Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] secretly supplied them with a “holy dozen” — sixteen spark crossbows — and promised another thousand crossbows and a thousand spark spears at the Holy Field. Tooth and Dodge immediately took charge of the chest and placed it under the toughs' guard, while the Wanderer managed to take out two crossbows “for the leader's personal safety.” As Buckley explained to Minerva, the Labelins hoped that peasants armed with spark weapons would kill some of the [[dom-oridzhin|Origins]]' kairs, proving that coin could replace martial skill. Fear that the Snowdrops possessed many spark weapons made General Gore retreat and led [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] to suspect that the Puppeteer was behind the revolt. Later, under questioning by Erwin, Buckley admitted that he had acted on Duke Labelin's orders. The duke himself countered that he had given the Snowdrops “only a dozen Eyes.”

## Fate
At the [[svyatoe-pole|Holy Field]], Minerva pardoned the Snowdrops, promised compensation and tax reform, and took the leaders who had fired on the envoys into custody. According to Marquis Graysend's report, everyone who returned home received a resettlement payment of one elena per person, and no one was persecuted. But between five and seven thousand people refused to lay down their arms and turned to banditry in the Southern Way. Salem condemned them, but they did not listen.

On 1 June 1775, Minerva released Tooth and Dodge from prison so they could recruit these men for imperial service. The leaders gathered former Snowdrops into a mercenary army, but Duke Labelin outbid her, and it rallied beneath [[adrian|Adrian]]'s banners. In the summer of 1775, the former Snowdrops entered [[labelin|Labelin]] as “liberators,” beneath standards bearing the pen and sword. According to some townspeople, they had looted five towns and thirty villages along the way. Salem rushed to intercept them and tried in vain to shame them. When Adrian took the capital, “Major Dodge's toughs” dispersed through the festive crowd, keeping an eye on the townspeople. Dodge told Adrian that he and Tooth “did not like Minerva”: a proper sovereign, he said, would have gone “smash” and crushed the rebels.

For Minerva, pardoning the Snowdrops became one of her bitter mistakes. The Raven of the Crown, [[mark-voron|Mark]], told her bluntly: “You forgave the Snowdrops — and now they are in Adrian's army.” Salem blamed himself for these people's fate for the rest of his life.

## In Fantasies
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], where the author describes possible destinies for the characters, Hand Dodge is the “leader of a pack of former bandits” serving [[adrian|Adrian]], burgomaster of Faunterra, in the spring of 1776. That summer, Holy Mother Alicia told Erwin that the Snowdrop leaders, who had gone unpunished, commanded Adrian's gangs of toughs. Adrian enrolled in those gangs everyone who came to the capital for the autumn election of the sovereign.
