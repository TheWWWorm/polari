**House Lightheart** is a former Great House that ruled the Duchy of [[nadezhda|Hope]] until the [[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]. At the beginning of the series, all that remains of the once-powerful family is the last duke's daughter [[karen|Karen]] and his youngest son [[edgar-flejm|Edgar]], who changed his surname to Flame, together with Edgar's children.

## Description
The Lighthearts descended from Foremother [[pramater-elena|Elena]]. The lineage was considered exceedingly ancient: [[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]] says Karen belonged to a house “more ancient than Farway and Labelin, than even the Illustrious Dynasty itself,” and was “probably the most highborn noblewoman” at court. The Guide to the Lands of Polari identifies endurance, good fortune, and pride as the Great House of Lightheart's defining traits. According to the Guide, the Farways who succeeded them are better known for caution and skilful manoeuvring.

The house's capital was [[serdtse-sveta|Heart of Light]], the strongest fortress in the Empire. The Lighthearts also personally owned Leongard, “Hope's gateway to the sea.” According to Karen in the [[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|collector's edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark]], Leongard in those days was the Far Sea's largest port and a junction on the Golden Route from Shimmery to the Crown. The finance minister of the time supposedly used to say, “If the gods loved me, I would have been born burgomaster of Leongard.”

### Coat of Arms
According to the Guide, Hope's arms bear a bone, symbolising suicidal endurance, and a shining goblet, symbolising good fortune. Karen specifies that the cup and bone belong to the Great House of Lightheart: for centuries, they appeared “on every flag from Overon to Heart of Light,” and only the Lighthearts and their subjects were entitled to say the motto, “Death gnawed our bones.” She says the Lighthearts built the duchy, ruled it wisely for centuries, defended it from barbarians, and “died with swords in their hands.” After the conspiracy, Farway's arms rose above the capital's towers: an armoured warrior with a cross.

## History
### The Siege of Heart of Light
The novel states that during Darian the Vile's invasion in the XVI century, Hope's ruler died in battle, and his two brothers, who brought the bitter news to Sovereign Evrian, were cut down by guards in the throne room. Karen tells the story differently in her annotations. Duke Lightheart did not die then but was trapped in Heart of Light by Darian's hordes. His brothers raced to the emperor with two regiments to ask for troops, but Evrian was afraid to release his army and accused them of cowardice. The brothers drew their swords and died, taking at least half a dozen guards with them. The duke himself held out under siege for another year, pinning down the horde's forces on the southern front. The city eventually fell, and the duke died with half its inhabitants, but the horde was so exhausted that Darian dared not storm Faunterra and made peace instead (see [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]]).

### Prosperity under Jonas
In the mid-XVIII century, the house was ruled by Jonas Caroline May of Elena's lineage, Duke Lightheart. He ruled all of Hope and personally owned Heart of Light and Leongard. He received a quarter of every ounce of gold mined in the duchy, and Hope produced as much gold as all the other lands combined. Two rivers of goods flowed through Hope, from south to north and west to east, and the duke taxed these as well. His army numbered ten thousand swords. Horses and lambs were bought for it in the Steppe at next to nothing; if the Steppe's chieftains raised their prices, the duke marched beyond [[kholliwell|Hollywell]] and took livestock by force. In overall influence, only Sovereign Tellurian outranked him. The duke “was reputed to be a man of generous spirit.” His two eldest sons shone at court: one sat in the [[palata|Chamber]], while the other served as a captain of the guard. The third, Edgar, was married to the daughter of Count Flame, the strongest vassal. The duke's daughter was the most sought-after bride of her time.

According to the Guide, the Dukes of Lightheart pressed [[almera-zemlya|Almera]] both politically and on the battlefield. Half a century earlier, Almera repeatedly came under attack from Hope. Around 1750, Jonas attacked Almera in the [[zolotaya-vojna|Golden War]]. The sovereign did not intervene, having been dissuaded by his brother Manson, who was “friendly with the Lighthearts.” The [[dom-oridzhin|Origins]] came to Almera's aid, and the Almerans recovered part of what had been taken. In her annotations, Karen identifies the cause of the war as a spark workshop Jonas had repeatedly sought permission to build on the River Back. Tellurian refused, then allowed the Almerans to build a dam upstream at Nightrock. The duke seized Nightrock, but the Agathans of the two duchies drove him out.

### The Jesters' Conspiracy
Jonas intended to leave a crown to his grandchildren. Emperors had never married ladies of Elena's lineage, so he chose an indirect route and introduced his daughter to the emperor's brother [[menson|Manson]]. At their wedding, guests declared that “the earth had never borne so glorious a couple.” The duke then dropped a few hints to his son-in-law, and Manson became fired with the idea of a coup. Jonas included his two eldest sons in the conspiracy but confided in neither Edgar, who “believed rather too firmly in knightly ideals,” nor his daughter.

Events look different in [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]'s book, which Mira reads in [[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]]. It names Manson himself as the instigator, a man who had “married into the Great House of Lightheart.” Acting in the Crown's name, he tried to issue the Lighthearts a loan of half a million hilts for a spark workshop in Hope, and their wealth attracted more than two dozen nobles to his side. Aiden reasoned that, if they succeeded, the best positions would be divided among “Manson's relatives, the Lighthearts,” and betrayed the conspiracy. The trial established that the conspirators had tried to turn the house's [[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]] against Tellurian, but the Object would not obey them. By the sovereign's decree, the Dust was confiscated from the Lighthearts and transferred to the Church of the Forebears.

### The Fall of the House
When the conspiracy collapsed, the duke was in Heart of Light, beyond the sovereign's reach. His two eldest sons and daughter, however, were under arrest. Tellurian promised them freedom if Jonas came to the capital alone, without an army. The duke arrived, surrendered, and was deceived: he died on the scaffold with his two sons, having learned shortly before his death that his daughter had been “sent for execution, but elsewhere.” In reality, Karen spent twenty years in the asylum on [[ostrov-faradeya-rajli|Faraday-Riley Island]]. Aiden Almera's book says Jonas and three of his sons died. In her annotations, Karen indignantly corrects this: two were executed; the third survived unharmed and merely changed his surname.

The court's sentence stripped House Lightheart of power over Hope. The proud inhabitants of Heart of Light would not have accepted any ruler other than a Lightheart or one of their foremost vassals, the Counts of Flame and Farway. Through his third secretary, Tellurian hinted to Count Flame that he would become duke if he brought his son-in-law's head. Flame publicly condemned his former liege but did not act on the hint. The duchy went to [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]]. According to the Guide, the Farways have ruled Hope since 1755. The novel says the Crown even considered abolishing the duchy altogether.

The Lighthearts were left with Leongard alone. Gold taxes passed to Farway, trade collapsed with the loss of Heart of Light, and the Crown's and Labelins' ships began calling at [[litlend|Litland]]'s ports. Ten of his father's battalions came to serve Edgar, but he had barely enough money for one and retained the two best. To help Tellurian forget his son-in-law more quickly, Count Flame gave him the upper half of the River Flame, allowing Edgar Lightheart rightfully to take his father-in-law's surname and pass it on to his children.

## After the Conspiracy
The house's past was not forgotten. In [[vrata-doveriya|The Gates of Trust]], the prophet [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]], after visiting “Count Lightheart, ruler of Leongard,” told him, “House Lightheart's worst days are behind it.” In [[khochu-poni|I Want a Pony]], [[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]] reminded the Farways that their vassals still remembered living under Lightheart rule, that the Lightheart heirs “dream of their former greatness,” and that the Flames sympathised with them. The Guide also notes that the Farways expend too much effort keeping the Lighthearts and Flames obedient.

### In The Yanmei Hunt
In 1775, the “infamous Lighthearts” appeared among Hope's vassals at receptions in Faunterra. According to Karen's annotations, the family lived in straitened circumstances and had a bad reputation, so Erwin's relationship with [[nekseya-flejm|Nexia]] required considerable courage on his part. Iona was surprised that Henry Farway had chosen Nexia, “a Lightheart by birth,” as his envoy. At Manson's trial, Ulyana's Dust, once confiscated from the Lighthearts, surfaced again.

### In Humans and Gods
Edgar commanded the Leongard Regiment, recruited from the house's former vassals. He said the regiment had “preserved the traditions and training of Jonas Lightheart's warriors.” When [[paul|Paul]]'s brigade seized a train in Hope's deserts, desert archers attacked it. One of Paul's soldiers recognised them as “Lightheart's desert men,” a unit without equal among the sands. At the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]], Karen introduced herself to Minerva as “Karen Cecily Caitlin of Elena's lineage, Lady Lightheart,” daughter of Duke Jonas.

### In The Shadow of the Great Tree
Father David reminded the kairs that although Nexia bore the surname Flame, she came from the older Lightheart lineage. The kairs regarded her as a granddaughter of “our brothers-in-arms,” since the Lighthearts “nearly overthrew Tellurian.” It was also said that the Lighthearts were “still out of favour” in the capital. In Orwell, Edgar gave Karen a new children's counting rhyme. She realised it was the key to House Lightheart's family cipher, which she had last seen twenty-two years earlier. Later, alarmed that the mighty [[ptakha-bez-ploti|Bird without Flesh]] had fallen into Adrian's hands, Karen composed several lines using this key and sent them secretly. At the end of 1775, Edgar's regiment and Horton's kairs took up positions on the shore of the Upper [[bliznyashki|Twin]] behind [[adrian|Adrian]]. [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] called Edgar “a son of the Lighthearts executed by the Yanmei,” while Adrian accused Karen of treason and threw her into the dungeon of Main Castle.

## In Fantasies
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], which the author describes as possible futures, the house's fate is once again being decided in Hope. Henry Farway promises to return Heart of Light, “the Lightheart family's ancestral domain,” if Nexia marries Origin, but postpones the agreement. Somerset complains that Farway has forbidden him from marrying the firstborn Rose Arrowback, because then “we Lighthearts would become too powerful.” Count Arrowback wants “Count Lightheart-Flame” as his son-in-law. An editor at one of the printing houses greets Karen with the exclamation, “The legendary Lady Karen Lightheart!”

## Members of the House
- **Jonas Caroline May of Elena's lineage**, the last duke. Executed after the conspiracy.
- **Jonas's two eldest sons**, courtiers: one sat in the Chamber, the other served as a captain of the guard. Both were executed with their father.
- **[[karen|Karen]]**, Jonas's daughter and [[menson|Manson]]'s wife. Spent twenty years in captivity and returned to her husband in 1775.
- **[[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]]**, born Lightheart, Jonas's youngest son, count and ruler of Leongard.
- **[[somerset-flejm|Somerset]]** and **[[nekseya-flejm|Nexia]] Flame**, Edgar's children and Jonas's grandchildren.

## Contradictions
- In Aiden Almera's book, the Jesters' Conspiracy is conceived by Manson; in Humans and Gods, by Jonas himself.
- The number of executed sons is three in Aiden Almera's book and two in Humans and Gods and Karen's annotations.
- In The Yanmei Hunt, Nexia says her father is Count Flame and her mother “Lady Lightheart by birth,” although it was her father Edgar who was born a Lightheart.
- The Guide describes the cup and bone as Hope's banner, whereas Karen calls them specifically the Lighthearts' arms.

## Trivia
- Edgar thought the name of his pet rat, Hartley, sounded like a “hideous anagram” of Lightheart.
- In her annotations, Karen observes that grammar requires the words “great house” to be written in lower case, with only the house's name capitalised: for example, “great house Lightheart.”
