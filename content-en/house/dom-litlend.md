## Overview
House Litland is one of the Great Houses of the Empire of Polari. It rules the Duchy of [[litlend|Litland]] on the continent's eastern coast, south of Mason's Bay. The Litlands trace their ancestry to Foremother Yanmei and take pride in it: “We are of Yanmei's lineage!” Yet Litland particularly venerates Foremother Susan, the guardian of health. When the wounded in Melorange began recovering, the townspeople boasted, “Foremother Susan has paid us a visit!” The ancestral seat is Bessifor Castle, with its famous library, supposedly the finest in the South. The duchy's capital is [[melorandzh|Melorange]], a city of two hundred thousand. The house sends two representatives to the [[palata|Chamber]].

According to [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca]], every Litland has an “obsession.” Her mother adores children, her uncle books and spiders, her father loves asking for advice, and Becca herself is obsessed with horses.

## Land and People
According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, “Litland” means “little land,” although the duchy is comparable in area to Almera or Darkwater. The first settlers considered only the strip of meadows in the [[kholliwell|Hollywell]] floodplain habitable and were so afraid of the jungle that they would not settle anywhere within sight of it. Litland is covered in jungle and has a humid tropical climate with monsoon seasons. Its largest cities are Melorange, Rainforest, and Colmin.

The duchy is the Empire's principal producer of spices, dyes, and fragrances. Famous stud farms stand inland near the Shepherds' Meadows. However, flour, groats, butter, weapons, metal, and glass must be imported, and Litland has no large shipyards of its own. Hence the joke that Litland's treasury is “a leaky bucket.”

According to the Guide, the Litlanders are perhaps the Empire's most cheerful people. They dress brightly and dye their hair, sing merry nonsense songs, conceal neither anger nor joy nor tears, and feel no shame about nudity. The common people take a liberal view of marriage, while only the lords try to maintain marital fidelity to keep inheritance straightforward. Shimmerians consider Litlanders half-breeds, “something between southerners and wild westerners.” In the novel, [[menson|Manson]] observes that Litlanders know how to feel pure joy and are unashamed to show it. Melorange welcomed Adrian's army with lanterns, garlands, and ceremonial songs, while its people took the wounded into their homes and wept over the dead “as though they were their own children.”

## Members of the House
- **Duke Willas Litland** (also Willis) — a bookish man who, according to Rebecca, has read twelve hundred books. He scarcely leaves Bessifor and loves anything written on paper: letters, charters, reports, denunciations. Once, he blew on the edge of a web in an old tower and saw the spider rush towards the source of the vibration. Since then, he has ruled like a spider at the centre of a web of pigeon post: reports from all over Litland converge on Bessifor. Becca recalled, however, that everyone except the duke noticed when a bay stallion disappeared from the castle. His favourite spider Gordon — enormous, “like an olive,” black with a white cross on its abdomen — lives in a niche in the old tower. The duke told Adrian that Gordon was at least fifteen years old, that they had met eleven years earlier, and that the spider symbolised wisdom and insight. Ever since, spiders at Bessifor have been pampered like pets. The duke has four sons, “proud lords of the jungle and coast.”
- **Lord Douglas Litland** — the duke's younger brother and Litland's representative in the Chamber since Rebecca was ten. He is the father of three daughters: Cee, Molly, and Rebecca. He reminded Manson of “an old pigeon,” with bedraggled feathers and a trembling head. He loves asking for advice and calls his daughter “my own flesh and blood,” but twice disposed of her future for the house's benefit. His knight Gage, whom Rebecca trusted, died at Lacey. At the [[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]], Douglas unexpectedly helped the defence: he pointed out that after Adrian's coronation, the lords had refused to let the jester swear fealty, meaning that Manson could not have committed treason.
- **Douglas's wife** — Rebecca's mother, whose name is not given. She adores children and takes turns visiting her elder daughters, waiting for grandchildren.
- **Cee and Molly** — Douglas's elder daughters. Both have long been married, but neither has children. Rumours of their infertility destroyed Rebecca's chances of becoming empress.
- **[[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Eleonora Agatha]]** — Douglas's youngest daughter, a horsewoman and Minerva's friend.
- **Lord Brandon Coss** — Litland's second representative in the Chamber, Douglas's chief adviser, and architect of the ambush at Lacey. Rebecca broke his knee.
- **Ancestors.** According to the Guide, Duchess Paolina Litland sent parties into the jungle in search of spices in the XII century and wisely assimilated the “merry, smiling” forest dwellers. They gave the Litland lords knowledge of spices and dyes, labour, and, in the dark years of the First Horse War, refuge in the wilderness. Duke Mollis the Cunning, a famous collector of wines, ruled in the middle of the preceding century.

## Litland and the West
Litland borders Reis across the Shepherds' Meadows. Rebecca spent half her childhood in the largest of Litland's border fortresses guarding the fords over the Hollywell. There are four such fords on the lower river, each with a Litland fortress. Juliana's Law gives the nomads the right to cross them with their herds, and living beside them is the Litlands' “perpetual headache”: raids, skirmishes, and blood feuds. The knight Gage called Reis “a stretch of land teeming with raiders, horse thieves, and nomads.” The Shavans, in turn, call the Litlands “jackals” and “thieves.”

## History
### The Deal over the Fords
In 1774, the Litlands hoped to marry Rebecca to [[adrian|Adrian]]. In January, however, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] persuaded Duke Willas to abandon those hopes. He offered the Litlands all four fords as feudal holdings: the law made the meadows common land but said nothing about the crossings. In exchange, the Litlands were to recognise any bride chosen by the emperor and vote for his reforms. Rebecca's father concealed this from her for six months.

### The Ambush at Lacey
In August 1774, Douglas and Coss received envoys from the County of Reis at the Lacey estate, ten miles from Faunterra. The envoys proposed a protected trade route across the Shepherds' Meadows and Reis to the western coast, bypassing the Crown. The Litlands delayed their answer. Meanwhile, a letter arrived from the capital that, according to Rebecca's father, “changed everything fundamentally.” On the duke's orders, they demanded half the Shepherds' Meadows and then massacred the envoys at the table (the [[zasada-v-lejsi|ambush at Lacey]]). Count Damir Reis was mortally wounded, but [[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]] escaped thanks to Rebecca. Later, while analysing the Puppeteer's scheme, Erwin Origin concluded that the Litlands had been sent a forged feudal charter granting them the fords, supposedly signed by the emperor. The delighted Litlands seized the crossings, and the West turned its anger on the Crown. The Reis envoys accused Adrian of handing common lands to “his lackeys,” but the emperor himself knew nothing of the charter. It is not explicitly stated whether this charter was the letter received at Lacey.

### War with the Horde
Moran's revenge became a war. The horde crossed the Hollywell, plundered half of Litland, burned towns and villages, and crushed Litland's knights beneath Melorange's walls: the steppe riders parted before the armoured cavalry's charge and struck it from behind. The duke promised Adrian he would come to court “as soon as he settled the border dispute,” but sent his brother to swear loyalty after Evergard. Douglas came to the emperor to ask for help. Adrian defeated the Shavans on 2 December 1774, and Duke Willas held a three-day feast at Bessifor. On leaving, Adrian stationed Prince Hector's Shimmerian army in Melorange — ostensibly to finish off the remnants of the horde — together with [[uiljam-dejvi|General Davy]]'s spark regiment, ordering Davy to look after the wounded and ensure the Litlands' loyalty. When news of Adrian's death arrived, Hector wanted to take his army home; the duke begged his ally to stay but haggled over payment for his help. The guests were served honey wine from Mollis the Cunning's cellars, at least a hundred and twenty years old, in cups made from credu nuts.

In the spring of 1775, Moran again besieged Melorange. The city was holding out into its fourth month, with only two knightly battalions, a couple of thousand archers, and General Davy's imperial spark regiment fit for combat. In March, Douglas was ready to hand his daughter over to Moran in exchange for peace, and Rebecca took refuge in Davy's spark regiment's barracks. Suspiciously little news came from the Litlands: [[minerva|Minerva]] received only formal condolences on Adrian's death and congratulations on her coronation, while her letters to Rebecca went unanswered. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] refused to march to their relief — “I will not go to Litland at all” — reasoning that the Shavans came from three counties and that pleasing three lands was more profitable than pleasing one. Minerva therefore went to negotiate with Moran herself. Litland's envoys spent a month escaping the besieged city across the stormy Mason's Bay and arrived at court on the very day Minerva announced peace. The empress herself lifted the siege. Threatening to cut the horde off from the Steppe — during the spring floods, only two crossings over the Hollywell remained, Steep Rapids by Litland's border fortress and Juliana's Bridge — she demanded that the siege end, the Litlander prisoners be freed, and the horde withdraw across the river. She refused Moran the Litlands' gold but gave her “word as empress” that the Litlands would not again try to appropriate the Shepherds' Meadows. General Davy later claimed that he and Duke Litland had held Melorange and that Moran accepted the terms only after “breaking his teeth against the walls.”

### After the War
After the war, Erwin calculated that the weakened Litlands would regard Rebecca's appointment as a Crown representative as a favour from the empress: “One appointment, three votes.” Her parents ordered Rebecca to exploit her friendship with Minerva for political ends, but she honestly warned her friend. Minerva halved duties on Litland goods and organised a fair selling them on Palace Island to help the devastated duchy. In the Chamber, Douglas accused the Shavans of plunder and abducting “our horses and our daughters”; Ganta Thunderstorm reminded him of the murdered envoys in reply. When the Chamber voted to strip Adrian of his rights to the throne, the Litlands, whom Adrian had saved, did not oppose the majority: only Minerva and Rebecca raised red flags. After Rebecca's death at Rei-Roy, the Litlands considered supporting Adrian a matter of honour, particularly since, in their view, Rebecca had died because of Mira.

## Trivia
- Melorange is renowned for its inhabitants' health: the city of two hundred thousand had only three hospitals, pushed out to its outskirts.
- According to Rebecca, Melorange has so many stray dogs that no one would think of breeding them.
- During secret consultations with Coss, Douglas entrusted only his daughter with serving tea and bringing papers: he feared a servant might glimpse the documents and sell the information.
- Among the Sacred Objects that changed hands was the Third Bird: Duke Farway gave it to Duke Litland “as a sign of neighbourly friendship” when they signed a trade agreement.
- In the summer of 1775, during [[sofiya-oridzhin|Sophia Origin]]'s sea voyage, the first port visited by her ship, the Sea Arrow, was Maple in Litland. From there, Sophia visited General Davy's headquarters; he complained that postwar Melorange was in “disarray”: the knights had scattered to their fiefs, while the townspeople had been celebrating victory for a month.
