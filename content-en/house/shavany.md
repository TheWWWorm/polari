## Who They Are
In the West, Shavans are the mounted warriors of the Great Steppe, which stretches beyond the sacred River [[kholliwell|Hollywell]]. The West itself is divided into free counties: [[rejs|Reis]], Hollywell, and neighbouring [[melnitsy|Mills]] and the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]]. Imperials call Shavans savages and nomads, and their lands the Steppe. The empire's nobility looks down on the West, “tainted by the Horse Rebellion.”

According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, the word “shavan” originally meant “herdsman.” It now denotes a skilled, swift warrior capable of defending a herd and seizing part of someone else's. Shavans are the most influential and respected class in Reis. In horsemanship and swordsmanship they equal the knights of the central lands, and in archery they surpass them. Yet they have no steel armour, and many come from the common people, so knights regard them with contempt. Shavans themselves often call one another “children of the Steppe” or “sons of the Steppe.”

## Lands
- **Reis** — steppes on the western coast, south of the middle line. The land is dry and windy, with few rivers or forests. Urban culture is poorly developed: most towns stand on the coast, and only four lie inland. The largest, Rei-Roy, has about 45 thousand inhabitants.
- **Hollywell** — plains between the Sunset Coast, Reis, Mills, the Shepherds' Meadows, and [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]]. The western part resembles Reis, while the east, along Smoky Distance, contains irrigated fields, castles, and towns. The largest town is Corden. The fertile lands are protected against nomad raids by “earthen belts,” low embankments with palisades and ditches extending for tens of miles. The ditches also serve as canals.
- **The Shepherds' Meadows** — a strip of pasture on both banks of the Hollywell, about a hundred miles wide and four hundred long. Since the time of Juliana the Great, six neighbouring lands have used them in common. The nomads have long considered these meadows their own.

## Economy
According to the Guide, nine tenths of Reis's economy rests on livestock breeding. Its chief commodity is horses: smaller and weaker than Litland's or Hope's steeds, but hardy and cheap, they carry every third rider in the Empire. Shavans sell livestock to Almera and Hope by the herd. Thanks to Juliana's Bridge, the narrator says, “a whole rain of silver” poured over the Steppe. Shavans are skilled leatherworkers: patterns on a belt show its owner's achievements in battle and prosperity.

Another livelihood is raiding and the sale of captives. All the western lands secretly engage in the slave trade, but Reis leads the way. Ganta [[ganta-groza|Storm]] took human cargo south to [[shimmeri|Shimmery]]. The Guide explains that Reis values freedom above all precisely because it knows how easily freedom can be lost.

## Organisation
- **Gan and ganta.** Reis lives in communities of no more than a few hundred people. Each is ruled by a ganta, literally “one who points the way.” Gantas answer to the horde's chieftain, but, according to Neimir, a rider is no slave: he has the right to disregard a vile order.
- **The counts' authority.** The Counts of Reis rule Rei-Roy and own the largest herds, more than seven thousand horses. In practice, their power depends on personal qualities: Shavans will not obey someone they do not respect, and unite only for a major war. The Counts of Hollywell are stronger because they control the trade routes across Smoky Distance and the river fleet. Yet their authority too ends when war begins: then the Shavans follow the most respected ganta.
- **Property.** Theft is punished severely, but seizure in battle is considered normal. Land belongs to a person only if that person's house stands on it; pastures and rivers are common. Vassals are those who tend your herds; a suzerain is someone who has entrusted his herd to you.
- **Marks of rank.** Reis's warriors wear no ornaments on their clothing; their rank is visible only in the design of their belt and their elaborate spurs. Shavans do not remove their spurs even during negotiations.
- **Crawlers.** This is the Shavans' contemptuous name for settled peasants and townspeople. They call the northerners of Origin “wolves.” Holmen and Rei (natives of Reis) have long mocked one another in songs: one group despises the other for worshipping horned beasts, the other for “grubbing in the soil like worms.”

Lady [[karen|Karen]], in the [[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|collector's edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark]], writes that under [[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Code]], in the steppe lands of [[rejs|Reis]] and [[kholliwell|Hollywell]], the gains of any battle, including a raid, count as lawful spoils, whereas in the other lands only the gains of a lawful war do. She also points out that nomads consider capturing women and selling them “a fine business.”

## Beliefs
Shavans acknowledge the worship of the Foremothers. According to the Guide, however, instead of abstract gods of the Underworld they revere spirits of nature: the sun, wind, waves, steppe, and fire. Every animal species has its own forefather, the most honoured being Ramsun the First Bull and Aristad the First Horse. The ability to move is considered sacred. Everything that moves comes from the gods, while evil is whatever takes movement away: death, weakness, rust, decay, and disease. Hence the Spirit of the Worm, lord of rot and dissolution, who slowly devours the world. Only those who have lived a life worthy of a Shavan will be saved: their souls will join the Wandering Spirits, the [[orda-strannikov|Horde of Wanderers]], and depart to roam another world. Needlessly rendering something mobile immobile is a great evil, and breaking a person's legs is almost as sinful as killing them. Raids are frequent but not bloody: killing is not their aim, and is condemned.

The Holmen chiefly revere water and the sacred river. They are ruled by the Drop-God, whose body is all the water of Hollywell and Smoky Distance. Near Corden, beneath the lake's waters, stands the Cathedral of the Water Deities. The Guide jokes that faith in the Foremothers finally took hold of Reis only when Yanmei's descendants built the railway: a train is the largest moving object in the world.

In the novels, Shavans swear by the Spirit of the Steppe and speak of the Worm, who is “growing stronger and crawling out of his lair.” They pray aloud and in public. On one occasion, [[niza|Niza]] asked for a sacrificial altar in a temple “to speak with the Spirit of the Steppe,” and prayed before an icon of Sophia.

## Customs
- **Freedom and a horse** are a Shavan's greatest treasures. By ten years old, children in Reis ride off on their own, even beyond the horizon, without parental supervision. A chieftain who betrays his people loses the right to their loyalty.
- **Death.** “To lie in the dust” means to die. The farewell words “Tirya ton tirya” are spoken over the fallen, even enemies. Anyone who dies with bound legs or crippled and unable to mount a horse cannot enter the Horde of Wanderers: their spirit remains chained to the earth. Moran burned his dead rather than committing them to the ground.
- **Debt.** Children of the Steppe remember their own debts and invariably pay them if they wish to enter the Horde of Wanderers. Rubbing a Shavan's nose in a debt is like mocking a northern knight's coat of arms.
- **The law of foreign lands.** According to [[chara|Chara]], a Shavan does not feud with another Shavan in a foreign land. In the Steppe, a captive is always left with water. A Shavan may leave a war once he feels he has taken enough plunder, but not during a blood feud.
- **Rei-Roy.** Anyone coming to Rei-Roy in peace must water their horse in the muddy little Roydana River, not the Brokka marked on maps. The castle gates had not been closed for a century and a half.
- **Council.** A Shavan wishing to speak at a council scratches his neck. Flicking a finger against one's forehead means “I hear an ass talking.”
- **Curses and forms of address.** Shavans curse with “Bald tails!” and “A snake under your tail!” A respectful form of address is “worthy one.” Sayings include: “Do not eat what a jackal devours, do not ride with someone you do not know”; “The river flows, the horse gallops”; and “The wind carries away all dust.” According to the Guide, people in Reis say: “What you cannot defend is not yours.”
- **The art of war.** Shavans are light cavalry and superb archers. Their chief tactic is to swoop in, shoot, and pull away without receiving a counterblow. They dislike infantry combat and sieges, and are helpless in Litland's jungles. Sturdy armour and longbows, which outrange Shavan bows, save the empire from the steppe cavalry. [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]] therefore believed a horde equipped with [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]] would become invincible.

## Legends
- **Gett, Hanosh, and Ollai** (see [[legenda-o-gette|The Legend of Gett]]). Two brothers saved a calf touched by the Spirit of the Steppe. The wondrous Ollai gave them strength to rescue their kin from the sword-bearers. Hanosh returned home, but Gett stole Ollai, took all his power, became a bull-headed giant, and gathered a horde. Outside Fairis's walls, the sword-bearer Caderon asked where his great strength had come from. Gett could not answer because of hiccups, the enemy army burst into laughter, and Caderon cut him down. Niza said the legend showed the Shavans' two natures, whereas foreigners knew only the Getts. Erwin Origin compared Moran to Gett, and Niza reminded Magda Labelin of the legend when Adrian prepared to advance on First Winter.
- **Verilya-Wind** spent seven years seeking and taming a winged horse, then, overwhelmed by boundless freedom, fell from the saddle at cloud height.
- **Alileda**, a proud beauty of the Steppe, is said to have commanded Ganta Darian to “become strong.” When he conquered the whole world for her, she mocked him for his lack of will, and he cut her down.
- **Pus-Ganta** — in an old legend, the son of the Spirit of the Worm, made of flies and maggots. [[paul|Paul]] later adopted this name.
- **Royhar the Lazy Ox** — builder of Rei-Roy (see below).

## History
### Ancient Times
According to the Guide, Reis has been inhabited since very ancient times. It is said that in the eighth–ninth centuries its herds contained more animals than the Empire of the time contained people. Shavans also migrated into what would become Hollywell, where for centuries they feuded with farmers from Almera. In the tenth century, the Kreidor family began arming Shavans with cheap steel and setting them against one another. By the twelfth century, the Kreidors had seized power, adopted the name Hollywell, and built the Cathedral of the Water Deities. Tribes that settled near the iron mines of Mills became the slave-owning Empire of the Iron Sun, the “realm of the sword-bearers.” By the mid-twelfth century, it had absorbed half of Reis and most of Hollywell, but it fell after a slave uprising in the second half of the century. Shavan legends about sword-bearers date back to this era.

### The Horse Wars
In the sixteenth century, Ganta Darian (Darian the Foul to imperials, a count in the Guide) first revealed the West's military power. He led the hordes of Reis and Hollywell across the Hollywell, beginning the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]]: the Shavans burned Almera, conquered Hope, and reached the Crownlands. At the same time, Ganta Royhar the Lazy Ox built the stone castle of Rei-Roy, the “Place of the Lazy,” with an immense statue of a bull-headed warrior above its gates. His son withstood an imperial siege in the castle and received the title of Count of Reis from Sovereign [[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]]. The Counts of Reis descend from him, including [[yukhan-rejs|Johan]], son of Damir. After the wars, Juliana declared the Shepherds' Meadows the common property of the western and eastern lands and built Juliana's Bridge.

### Under the Origins' Hand
For the next two centuries, the Origins kept the West “in submission and fear.” According to the Guide, Reis was the Origins' vassal until 1732 and paid them tribute. Vassalage was abolished, but a small tribute remained, covering the upkeep of two Origin garrisons on the coast. In the early eighteenth century, Hollywell's Shavans fought Almera for half a year because its dukes planned to block the sacred river with a dam. Mechanics concluded that the dam was impossible, and the war was called the Foolish War. “Fighting for a dam” became a saying.

## The Horde of 1774–1775
In 1774, Count Damir Reis and his right-hand man Moran travelled to the Litlands to seek an alliance, but their hosts treacherously attacked the envoys (the [[zasada-v-lejsi|ambush at Lacey]]). The count died. The pretext for war was a forged charter of enfeoffment supposedly granting the Litlands the fords across the Hollywell. Reis's envoys accused Adrian of violating Juliana's Law, although it later emerged that he had not signed the charter. [[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]] assumed command, swore revenge, and raised a horde. According to Douglas Litland, warriors from Hollywell and Mills joined him, and the horde soon numbered at least thirty thousand riders. Rebecca later spoke of forty thousand. Over the autumn, half of [[litlend|Litland]] fell “beneath the hooves”: the horde plundered seven towns and defeated the Litland army near [[melorandzh|Melorange]]. Moran nailed deserters who made off with plunder to trees.

Defeated by [[adrian|Adrian]] in December 1774, Moran gathered his forces again and besieged Melorange. Concluding that their chieftain had betrayed them, Neimir and Chara crippled his legs. In the spring of 1775, Moran demanded a hundred hilts per rider from the Litlands and Rebecca's hand in marriage. He yielded to [[minerva|Minerva]], however: she threatened to cut the horde off from the Steppe while the Hollywell was in flood, and Moran accepted her terms — to lift the siege, release the Litland captives, and take the horde back across the river. Minerva allowed the Shavans to leave unpunished on condition that they become faithful vassals and never cross the Hollywell again. In return, the empress gave her word that the Litlands would no longer encroach on the Shepherds' Meadows. Ganta Storm represented Reis in the Chamber.

In the summer of 1775, [[paul|Paul]] appeared in Rei-Roy. Using the Object Eternity, he froze Moran and then Rebecca Litland, who had come to visit him, filling the Shavans with awe. Paul declared himself Pus-Ganta, lord of decay, and cast Wilhelm's Fingers before the Shavans. The younger count, [[yukhan-rejs|Johan Reis]], and Ganta Kort were among the first brave enough to pick up the Objects; Ganta Ondey won his Finger in a fight. The Finger-bearers became Paul's elite, the “hanida vir kanna” (“worthy to see”). Ganta Storm, Erwin Origin's ally sent to Moran, died at Rei-Roy's gates.

The horde split. Its southern half, led by Johan Reis, whom Paul had made his voice, advanced on Faunterra. Minerva deliberately leaked information about a treasury supposedly being sent by train to Arden; Johan pursued it with the vanguard, the horde broke apart, and at Patrick's Chapel Adrian defeated it and captured Johan. Adrian and his marshmen drugged the bearer of firstblood and forced him to operate the Bird without Flesh; later, when Adrian ordered him to destroy the Healer's Hand with a deconstructor, Johan dropped dead: Magda Labelin had poisoned him, leaving Adrian without bearers for the Objects. The northern half went with Paul to [[uejmar|Waymar]], plundered the town of [[lid|Leed]], and was routed in the [[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]. The Steppe has four votes in the Chamber: two from Reis and two from Hollywell.

## In Fantasies
According to the author, Fantasies presents possible versions of the future; in these, the Steppe's fate after the war is as follows.
- **The destruction of the northern horde.** In August 1775, the horde split at Fliss. Pus-Ganta led the northern part more than a thousand miles, across Smoky Distance, the Northwood forests, and the Crystal Mountains, to the wolves' capital, where it suffered a terrible defeat. Even more Shavans died during the winter retreat through lands they themselves had ravaged: hunger, frost, and kair raids finished them off. Only fragments returned to Reis.
- **Ganta Kort.** The southern part, led by Johan Reis and Ganta Kort, plundered a dozen towns in Almera and the Crownlands. After Johan's capture, Kort took the Shavans into Almera, plundered the Red Land for a month, and then made peace with Duke Farway: Kort recognised Farway as ruler of Almera, and Farway recognised Kort as first chieftain of the Steppe. The treaty was sealed by a dynastic marriage: [[dzheremi-farvej|Jeremy Farway]], the late Laura's brother, married the steppe ganta's daughter. The wedding followed steppe customs, and he lives in Rei-Roy, where people laugh at him because he cannot ride a wild horse. After driving several thousand head of livestock out of Hollywell, Kort returned to Rei-Roy by the Day of the Descent. On learning of the deaths of Pus-Ganta, Johan, and other chieftains at First Winter, he ordered the greatest funeral bonfire ever lit, its smoke visible even from Mother Mills, and sang with everyone else, thinking: “Reis, Ondey, and Pus-Ganta have lain down in the dust. I am the ruler of the Steppe!”
- **A new faith.** The Shavans quickly renounced the dead Pus-Ganta. The largest group joined Kort, who called himself a warrior of Foremother Elena: supposedly she had lived with the Spirits of the Steppe, and all great Shavans descended from her — Paul, Moran, and Kort himself.
- **Peace with the North.** In the spring of 1776, Kort kept his army under arms for fear of Erwin and sent [[nejmir|Neimir]] as an envoy. Neimir offered tribute — a thousand horses, three thousand cows, and two Sacred Objects — and told Erwin a legend in which the Shavans had suffered for weakness, not dishonour: Pus-Ganta had abandoned the weak of his own accord and gone to the Horde of Wanderers. Erwin acknowledged that it would be useful for the Steppe to remember such a legend. He ordered the ice-encased body of the Shavan who had almost killed him sent to Rei-Roy, on one condition: whenever northern envoys visited, the chieftain of the Steppe must kiss the dead man's frozen face. The ten leading gantas authorised Neimir to sign a preliminary peace agreement on their behalf. Before Minerva, the envoys fell prostrate: Ganta Kort “begs forgiveness” for having fought “the one who flies through the sky.”
- **The end of raiding.** After Pus-Ganta's defeat, the Steppe enjoyed almost a year of peace, and Laem's slave traders complained that the market had “dried up.” Only when an unrepentant hanida was stabbed to death near Rei-Roy did two gantas fight over his Finger; the victor, Ganta Farhan, brought captives to auction in Laem, including Almeran girls carried off to the Steppe a year earlier.
