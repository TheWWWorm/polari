## The Rank of Kair
A kair is a warrior-knight of the northern duchy of [[oridzhin-zemlya|Origin]]. The rank is conferred through initiation: a lord touches the warrior's shoulder with a sword, and the warrior swears to honour the precepts of the Foremothers and the laws of martial honour, after which he receives the right to wear a red-and-black cloak and serve “in the rank of knight beneath the banners of the Great House of Origin.” This is why kairs are also called “bicolours.” Initiation could take place very young: Captain [[teobart|Theobart]] received his cloak at fifteen. In northern eyes, even a duke's son remained a “softling” until he was initiated as a kair, as happened to the young [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]].

Initiation is preceded by a test. The most famous case concerns Kair [[osvald|Oswald]]: after passing, he boasted that the test could be held every morning as a warm-up. Duke Desmond ordered him to take it again the following morning, in a far more difficult form. Oswald passed that too and became famous for undergoing initiation twice.

### A Social Elevator
According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, Origin has a military “social elevator”: a commoner can become a grey and then a kair. As a result, the land is highly militarised, with immense numbers eager to serve. In the novels, the brothers Brant and Seymour Steel, for example, followed this path through service as [[grei|greys]]: “kairs in the first generation,” they were “born shepherd boys” and became the duke's swords. [[brant-stil|Brant Steel]] began as Kair Derrek's grey, and Erwin remembered him as a simple shepherd. Joaquin supposes that Steel was “the lowest-born of all kairs.”

Being stripped of his cloak is the gravest disgrace for a kair and his family. [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] was punished in this way for rebelling against Erwin in 1774. Erwin later initiated him again and personally placed the cloak on his shoulders. According to northern custom, a vassal who abandons his suzerain in trouble has no right to wear the two-coloured cloak.

## Code of Honour
The Guide describes the kair's code as a system of unwritten rules developed over centuries. No complete list exists, but there is a hierarchy of values:
- loyalty to the suzerain, meaning the execution of his will;
- protection of the suzerain's life and honour (the lord's will is more valuable than his life: a kair is obliged to kill his lord if ordered to do so);
- protection of the suzerain's family (when rescuing a lord or his children from a fire, a kair saves the lord unless ordered otherwise);
- obedience to the commandments of the Foremothers, especially Agatha and Gloria, who are particularly revered in the North, including protection of the weak and honesty with the suzerain;
- obedience to Northern Law, whose general spirit is that the North stands united against the elements, disaster, and a common enemy, values its history and traditions, and is ruled by the brave and intelligent — warriors and lords — while the lot of everyone else is to obey;
- protection of one's own life;
- protection of one's own honour (many kairs risk their lives in duels, but under the code it is more correct to swallow an insult and preserve oneself as a fighting unit of one's suzerain);
- respect for warriors, including the enemy;
- and last, as a ninth, “extra” provision beyond the sacred eight, the laws of the empire, which kairs obey only when they do not contradict northern rules.

These rules are constantly visible in the novels. During [[myatezh-dzhemisa|Jemis's mutiny]], a kair could not bring himself to cut Erwin down because killing one's suzerain is the greatest disgrace for a nobleman. Robert Origin justified Queen Madeline's execution by a direct vassal's right to execute anyone who had attempted to take Lord Origin's life. Under northern law, a lord cannot force a man to serve in the army, but may offer payment. In this way, Desmond hired forty thousand inhabitants of [[lid|Leed]] for his army.
Every kair is accompanied by [[grei|greys]], apprentices, servants, and warriors in grey cloaks (see the separate article for more). On the expedition beyond the River, the eleven kairs were generally accompanied by two greys each (Oswald had three). Under northern law, a kair has absolute authority over his grey, and a lord should not interfere in their relationship. Young noblemen also served as greys: in his youth, [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]] was [[blekberri|Blackberry]]'s grey. The best greys could earn initiation: at the tournament near Doyle, three hundred greys recommended by their masters underwent tests, and seventy-six received kairs' cloaks.

## The X-cloaks
The X-cloaks are the elite of the kairs, the duke's guard. Unlike the two-coloured kairs, they wear entirely black clothing with a single mark: a crimson saltire on the chest, resembling the letter X. Legends circulated about them. Southerners said that to earn the rank, a man had to kill half a dozen kairs with his own hands and cast their cloaks at the duke's feet, and that defeating an X-cloak with a sword was like stabbing a bear to death with a toothpick. During the war of 1774, the X-cloaks were commanded by [[dejmon-oridzhin|Damon Origin]]. Henry Horton ([[sorok-dva|Forty-Two]]) was the first to wear the black cloak with an X. Companies of X-cloaks later accompanied Erwin on campaign, with [[gordon-syu|Gordon Sue]] commanding the first, the pennant company.

## Other Units
- **The Leed Wolves** — a company of scouts, saboteurs, and interrogators from Leed under [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]; according to them, they had served the Origins for sixteen generations.
- **Battalions** — the basic unit of the northern army, commanded by colonels and prime vassals (Horton, Blackberry, Lilliday, Mine, and others).
- **The Mountain Guard** — the garrisons of the mountain forts. At the [[stvorki-neba|Gates of Heaven]], its two companies, one from Leed and one from Origin, once guarded the gorge together. In 1774, the Mountain Guard's colonel was Blackberry, “Lord Desmond's old and vicious dog,” while [[dzhoj-khemmond|Kair Hammond]], an officer of the Mountain Guard, commanded Erwin's personal guard.

## Tactics and Customs
The Origins' cavalry traditionally attacks in two waves. First come the “armoured monsters,” the strongest kairs on enormous horses in the finest armour, who smash into the enemy's formation. Lightly armed horsemen with swords then pour into the breaches. Old northern customs recounted in Leed prescribed settling disputes between lords through a “duel of the holy dozen”: each lord chose eight of his opponent's warriors at random, and the groups of eight fought one another. Erwin's army also made extensive use of hired archers and crossbows purchased in large numbers.

Tournaments were more than entertainment. At the heavy cavalry tournament near [[dojl|Doyle]], a knight received two points for unhorsing his opponent and one for staying in the saddle. Those who fell were given a second chance, so the best rode out to joust again and again, barely able to remain mounted. Henry Horton became champion with forty-two points, followed by Brant Steel with thirty-nine. The six victors later won a duel of honour for Erwin outside Labelin.

According to Robert Origin's calculations, a kair's pay was five gold coins a month, and First Winter's battalion numbered three hundred kairs. The Guide says that the Northwooders gave the kairs the nickname “wolves,” originally as a taunt: by Northwood standards a wolf is a small animal, no match for a bear. The kairs replied: “The sea is no forest, and a bear is no fish.”

Kairs are renowned for courage, endurance, and brutality: at Pixie, in Faunterra, at Roydan, and in the [[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]], they withstood the onslaught of superior enemy numbers. The greatest test of their training came with the arrival of [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]], against which swords and armour proved powerless.

## In Fantasies
The author describes Fantasies as possible versions of the future. In the summer of 1776, the X-cloaks were said to be living “snug in Sophia's bosom”: surrounded by glory, they stood honour guard in First Winter, while the arduous watches fell to other units. Out of boredom, three X-cloaks — Kair-Captain [[shram|Scar]], commander of the second company of X-cloaks, a “descendant of pirates” with an iron tooth; Lieutenant [[fittsdzherald|Fitzgerald]]; and Aubrey, Iona's personal bodyguard and “the chief mystic among the kairs” — took to hunting ghosts in the castle and town. They studied chronicles in the town library, questioned spirits in the gate tower, and eventually “caught” the shapeshifting [[dalliya-rejven|Dallia Raven]], whom they presented to Minerva for her twentieth birthday. Fitzgerald quoted Lord Desmond: “A third of war is fought with the blade, and two thirds with the shovel” (Let's Catch It).

In Nearer — Farther, kairs stood guard at Erwin's door (Hunter and Mayfield) and kept order in the Clinic of Mercy, wearing ancient battle masks over their medical ones. Kair raiding parties pursued Pus-Ganta's horde as it retreated from First Winter.

## According to Lady Karen
In the [[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|collector's edition]] of Arrow, Coin, Spark, Lady [[karen|Karen]] corrects the description of kairs:
- kairs do not wear chain mail, especially in First Winter's garrison: they prefer plate armour. They wear a surcoat over their armour, not a sleeved doublet;
- a kair's cloak has two colours: the right half is made of black cloth and the left of scarlet. This partly conceals the movements of the right, weapon-bearing hand;
- under Juliana's Code ([[yulianov-zakon]]), an initiated kair enjoys all the rights and honours of a nobleman, even if he is not firstborn and owns no fief. In other lands, nobility is inseparably linked with land;
- there is no division of “kair — horseman, grey — infantryman”: both kairs and greys may be mounted or on foot. Even a poor rider can earn the two-coloured cloak, although many kairs eventually transfer to the cavalry because it is more prestigious;
- northerners are not “exclusively swordsmen”: many kairs learn to shoot a crossbow, and every northern company should have at least three dozen missile troops;
- could Erwin, who was not a kair himself, initiate Jemis? Karen asked [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]]. He replied that Erwin could not assess Jemis's fighting skills, but those had already been recognised as sufficient for the rank of kair. Jemis had lost his cloak over discipline and honour, and in that sphere Erwin was entitled both to punish and to reward him.
