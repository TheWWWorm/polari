## Overview
Polari's imperial dynasty, the “Illustrious Dynasty,” traces its descent from Foremother Yanmei the Merciful. A saying runs: “Yanmeians are born to rule, Agathans to fight, Sophians to care for others.” The lineage of Yanmei includes not only the ruling family but many other nobles, such as [[dorian-ember|Baronet Ember]] and [[liam-shele|Viscount Shellier]]; however, only those entered in the Dynasty's genealogy have a claim to the throne. Dimples are repeatedly described as a distinguishing feature of Yanmeians.

The sovereign rules the Crownlands directly, while in the other lands the sovereign's laws are ratified by the [[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]. The symbols of authority are the [[vechnyj-efes|Eternal Hilt]], a transparent sacred dagger handed to each new sovereign and unsheathed at the coronation; the Star Crown, with its blue diamond; and the Glove of Might, or Yanmei's Glove. The Dynasty also owned other [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]], kept in the Imperial Citadel. The imperial house is protected by the [[lazurnaya-gvardiya|Azure]] and Scarlet Guards. Yanmei's diaries serve as guidance for rulers.

### Three Inaccurate Names
According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, the ruling house is called the Illustrious, Arden, and Yanmei Dynasty, but essentially lives up to none of these three names.
- **Arden.** The ancient city of Arden is considered the emperors' ancestral, titular domain. Adrian's full title is “Adrian Ingrid Elizabeth of the lineage of Yanmei, Duke of Arden, Emperor of Polari.” Yet the sovereigns live in Faunterra and Marengo, finding rest in Arden only after death, in the burial vault of the Temple of Farewell. The title “Duke of Arden” therefore has a sombre ring and is rarely applied to a living sovereign. In 1775, Secretary Ember deliberately referred to the deposed Adrian as “Lord of Arden.”
- **Illustrious.** The Third Dynasty of Miriam, overthrown in the twelfth century, was renowned for far greater splendour. Beside the Miriamians, the Yanmeians of that time appeared restrained pragmatists, “a pale shadow of the former sovereigns.” Olgard the Founder, the first Yanmeian emperor, called his dynasty Illustrious to make up for this deficiency, at least in words.
- **Yanmei.** Not all emperors were Yanmeians by blood. If a sovereign married a Sophian or an Agathan, his children belonged to their mother's lineage, and only when the next prince married a Yanmeian did he restore the Merciful One's lineage to his children. The name thus refers more to the principles of government bequeathed by the Foremother.

### Marriages and Customs
Traditionally, Polari's rulers become engaged during Sophia's Days (announcing the engagement at the close of the summer games in July) and marry in October, during the Days of Plenty (7 August and 25 October in early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark). Emperors often married Yanmeian women, reluctantly married granddaughters of Sophia and Agatha, and never married ladies of Elena's lineage. Duke Lightheart therefore sought a path to the throne through the emperor's brother, [[menson|Manson]], whom tradition did not prevent from marrying for love. After the [[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]], Tellurian made his own brother the court jester. In 1775, Minerva called the court jester “as important an attribute of an emperor as the crown and the Eternal Hilt.”

## History

### Before the Illustrious Dynasty
Before the Yanmeians, other dynasties occupied the throne, including the First Dark-Eyed Dynasty and the Third Dynasty of Miriam. The Imperial Citadel became the rulers' home more than a thousand years ago and served them for seven centuries. During the Crimson Turmoil, rebels entered through an underground passage and slaughtered Emperor Algon III the Sorrowful and his entire family, “destroying the Dark-Eyed Dynasty and severing the lineage of Foremother Miriam.” The Illustrious Dynasty, which took power after the turmoil, retained the Citadel as the seat of the throne. According to Minerva, Olgard became ruler and founded the dynasty thanks to the decisiveness others lacked during the dangerous days of the Crimson Turmoil. In the burial vault of Farewell, the tombs of the last Miriamian rulers, “killed and buried by the Yanmeians,” form a stern granite row among the lavish sarcophagi of the earlier age.

### Celebrated Sovereigns
Alanis listed the portraits Tellurian had assembled in the palace's portrait room, “only those from whom there is something to learn”: Foremother Yanmei, Olgard the Founder, Augustine the Kind, Augustine the Sophian, Garrod the Victorious, Lexian the First, Evrian the Expander of Borders, Juliana the Great, Albrecht the Second, and Prince Adrian. Christian the Lawgiver is also mentioned in the burial vault.
- **Olgard the Founder** — the first Yanmeian emperor. His code was still cited in 1775: under it, a nobleman on campaign is considered a vassal of the commander.
- **Evrian the Expander of Borders** instilled a passion for travel in the empire and almost doubled its area “without a single major war.” The First Horse War began during his reign, about two centuries ago; on learning of the defeats, he ordered the dead Duke of Hope's two brothers cut down in the throne room.
- **Lexian the First** took a keen interest in scholarship and initiated the development of the spark guilds.
- **[[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]]** ruled for half a century, ended the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]], established the empire's borders, and created a common code of laws. She moved the royal residence from the Citadel to the new Palace of Pen and Sword. It was her intention that the summer games be bloodless.
- According to legend, the fourteenth-century sovereign Eloise was overthrown but kept her coronation dress.

### Recent Generations
The sovereign **[[mejnir|Maynir]]** was remembered for his fifty altessas, a hundred slain boars, and his failure to issue a single law. His son **Tellurian** reigned for many years and was, in Karen's words, “a great emperor, though a cruel one.” At his father's bidding, he married the “marsh woman” **[[imperatritsa-ingrid|Ingrid]]**. His brother Manson instigated the Jesters' Conspiracy. Tellurian and Ingrid's two daughters died in early childhood, leaving **Adrian** their sole heir. The prince played Stratems with his father, usually behind closed doors; when spectators were allowed, generals and officers sat to the right of the table and ladies to the left. In the burial vault of Farewell, the seventh tier is reserved for “the times of Sovereign Maynir, his son Tellurian, and Sovereign Ingrid.” Empty sarcophagi prepared for Adrian and Minerva already stand there.

## Members of the Lineage
- **[[mejnir|Maynir]]** — emperor, father of Tellurian and Manson.
- **Tellurian** — emperor, father of Adrian.
- **[[imperatritsa-ingrid|Ingrid]]** — sovereign, Adrian's mother, a native of Nen-Clair; her tomb is in Farewell near Arden.
- **[[adrian|Adrian Ingrid Elizabeth]]** — a reforming emperor, an advocate of railways and universal taxation.
- **[[menson|Manson Louisa Violetta]]** — Tellurian's younger brother, Adrian's uncle, court jester after the Jesters' Conspiracy; first in line to the throne. His wife is [[karen|Karen Lightheart]].
- **Sir [[adamar|Adamar]] Susanna Elizabeth** — Adrian's cousin, second in line; killed while hunting in the spring of 1774 (at the end of May).
- **Lord Plai** (the Elder of Plai) — an elderly distant relative who lives on his ancestral estate of Plai. In early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark, Mira reflected that he was over seventy and had outlived his wife and three sons.
- **[[ledi-vo-tme|Madeline, the Lady in the Dark]]** — Queen of Darkwater, a “pure-blooded Yanmeian,” great-granddaughter of [[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]] and cousin of Sovereign Ingrid; her daughter is [[mirej-nen-kler|Mireille Nen-Clair]], and her nephew is Emmon Darklin.
- **[[minerva|Minerva Gemma Alessandra]] Stagfort** — a distant relative of Adrian; her mother fell out of favour with the Lady in the Dark by marrying a man of insufficiently high birth.
- **[[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Eleonora Agatha]]** of Litland — one of Adrian's prospective brides. In the first edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark she was mistakenly called a Yanmeian; according to the author, she belongs to Sophia's lineage.

## History in the Series
In the summer of 1774, an unknown conspirator was eliminating heirs to the throne: Sir Adamar was killed, Baron Rosbet died (officially of the grey plague), and Minerva's father had been murdered earlier. Countess Sybil Northwood leafed through the genealogy, Origins, Course, and Branches of the Illustrious Lineage of Yanmei, crossing out names: some had lost their heads after the Jesters' Conspiracy, others had died while hunting or in feuds, become monks, or succumbed to the plague. Adrian was choosing a bride from [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], [[valeri-grejsend|Valerie Graysend]], and Becca, but named “Minerva Stagfort” as his bride. In December 1774, at the height of the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], the Dynasty's Objects were stolen from a train and Adrian was believed to have died on the Beck; Minerva inherited the throne and was crowned without the Eternal Hilt. In the spring of 1775, heretics desecrated Ingrid's tomb and stole the [[kaplya-solntsa|Drop of the Sun]] buried with her, while at [[sud-nad-mensonom|Manson's trial]] it emerged that Adrian was alive; the Chamber declared him deposed and Minerva the temporary ruler.

In the summer of 1775, Adrian returned, having married [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], and took Faunterra, while Minerva moved her capital to [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]. At the end of the series, the Dynasty is divided between two claimants; [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] remarked that it had “ceased to be a dependable governing power,” and the fate of the crown was to be decided by the Chamber.

## In Fantasies

In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], which the author describes as possible versions of the future, the peace of First Winter formalises the split in the Dynasty. [[minerva|Minerva]] remained temporary sovereign until the election and ruled from First Winter. [[adrian|Adrian]] renounced his claim to the throne and acknowledged himself as burgomaster of Faunterra, retaining the right to stand in the election. His wife, [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], assumed administration of the Crownlands, the Dynasty's domain, on Minerva's behalf. Minerva formally recognised the Dynasty's debts to [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] and transferred the Imperial Citadel, the Aerial Bridge, the Admiralty, and other buildings to him, leaving the duke with more property in Faunterra than the Illustrious Dynasty itself. The election of the sovereign was scheduled for late autumn 1776. In a recorded speech, Adrian called himself “the sole lawful heir to the throne,” while the lawman Macfrid Crow (Mac; by all indications, the retired [[mark-voron|Mark]]) in the provincial town of Finborough called him “Tellurian's first heir.”

In the winter of 1775/76, Magda was pregnant and dreamed of “giving birth to a crown prince”; Fantasies does not say whether the child was born. Lady [[karen|Karen]] also recounts the marriages of the recent generations: [[mejnir|Maynir]] married whom he pleased and allowed his favourite son [[menson|Manson]] to choose his bride, but ordered Tellurian to marry the marsh woman [[imperatritsa-ingrid|Ingrid]].
