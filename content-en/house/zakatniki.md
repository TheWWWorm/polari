## The People
Sunsetters are the inhabitants of the [[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]], a free county in northwestern [[polari-world|Polaris]]. They are proud of their origins. The Sunsetters Pat and Krok recounted that their capital, Silence, was founded in the twelfth century, under the Iron Sun, by slaves who had risen against the Sword-Bearers. They “cast off their chains and went north” when Waymar had not even been thought of. According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, the slave revolt swept away the Empire of the Iron Sun in half a year. Fearing turmoil, some of the freed people travelled north over three months and settled beside a group of lakes that became known as the Quiet Lakes. There they founded Silence, marking the beginning of the Sunset Coast's history. The author of A History of All the Northern Lands, the Sunset Coast monk Goblet, wrote that no people were prouder or more devoted to freedom than former slaves.

The Guide adds that no one in Polaris guards their privacy as jealously as the people of the Sunset Coast, and therefore leaves their land “in the shadows.” [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] explained to his wife that Sunsetters were not Shavans, but were close to them in blood and culture, and that steppe chieftains readily allied with them. He therefore called the Sunset Coast “the key to the Great Steppe.”

Their northern neighbours consider Sunsetters savages and pirates. Even Count [[fleming|Fleming]]'s great-grandfather fought Sunset Coast pirates on the Seal Islands. In [[shejland-zemlya|Shayland]], hatred of the western neighbours had built up over centuries, growing with each raid and reaching its peak after the War for the Objects.

## The Counts of Rantigar and the Turmoil
The Sunset Coast is ruled by the Counts of Rantigar. During the [[melnichnaya-vojna|Mill War]], Count Rantigar tried to seize the Millers' domains, but the Origins intervened and inflicted a humiliating defeat on him. In 1774, on [[adrian|Adrian]]'s orders, he appeared at court in person, assured the emperor of his boundless loyalty, and wished the rebel Origin a journey to the Star. In the hall itself, an envoy of [[moran|Moran Steppe Fire]] slit his throat with a spur, saying: “A coward has no place at the head of a free land.” Later, when Faunterra voted to depose Adrian, flags were also raised by “the Sunsetters, who had killed their own count for submitting to Adrian.”

After Rantigar's death, the Sunset Coast was divided among three lords:
- **Corwin, the Elder Son**, the lawful heir, led an army to aid Steppe Fire, fought alongside the Shavans against Adrian, and earned a reputation as a dashing horseman.
- **Dainis**, the younger son, occupied the three main ports and sought allies in Darkwater, Shimmery, and even Folt.
- **General [[oris|Horis]]**, the count's chief commander, who had fought for him as far back as the Mill Wars. On leaving for war, the Elder Son left him to hold Silence, but the general took the capital for himself. He declared the count's sons “young and green” and drove the younger back to the seacoast. The Elder Son could not dislodge him from the city and, after negotiations with Horis, began considering peace. According to Lakht Mis, the general subdued the Elder Son and reached an understanding with Hollywell's Shavans.

In the summer of 1775, when Horis took his army to Shayland, the Elder Son occupied Silence, raised his flags, and declared himself sole ruler of the Sunset Coast. Horis responded by offering him “bonds of eternal loyalty” if he came to his aid, but did not expect him to do so.

## The War for the Objects
In 1756, the Seventeenth Gift fell in Shayland. Only a week later, while its resting place was still smoking, an army of the Sunset Coast stood at the Gift, led by Horis, whom the northerners called Oris. The fire west of the Gift was weak, and the Sunsetters approached it easily. They carried almost all the Sacred Objects out of the resting place, although they never reached the two deepest caves. The war took on a sacred character: the Sunsetters had challenged both the Objects themselves and the age-old order for dividing them.

Many remarked on the strangeness of this war. According to Count [[elias-nortvud|Elias Northwood]], whom Horis had invited to become his ally, the general had six thousand horsemen and a fortified camp with embankments, ditches, and pit traps. Advancing against him were only two of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]]'s battalions and two thousand of [[vinsent-shejland|Vincent Shayland]]'s warriors. Yet Horis retreated a day before the kairs arrived. Desmond caught him on the march, inflicted heavy losses, and cut him off from Silence. The war lasted several more months, but its outcome was decided in the first battle. The Sunsetters Pat and Krok told the story differently, claiming that the duke had six thousand warriors against three thousand. Desmond himself wrote in The Commander's Questions:
> “General Oris strikes me as a level-headed and courageous man. His hasty retreat is a strange mistake, uncharacteristic of him.”

Elias suspected that the general had learned some secret in the Gift's resting place that had driven him to flee. In 1775, Lord [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]] told Count Fleming that the Sunsetters had been the first to descend into the resting place, and Hash Eilish vouched for every word. Inside lay a dead woman in a glass sarcophagus, dressed in clothing no mortal tailor could have made. Emperor Tellurian, Prince Adrian, Duke Origin, and [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]] (accompanied by Arthur Highrock) regarded her as a new Foremother, while Adrian regarded her as a goddess, and they decided to conceal the discovery. Aiden feared that “the Fallen Foremother will spawn hordes of fanatics.”

Desmond defeated the Sunsetters and took most of their Objects, after which Horis surrendered. Part of the Origins' army remained in Silence, waiting for tribute to be paid. Horis later recalled those years with laughter as his “first capitulation to Horigin.”

## The Cult of the Fallen One
### Origins and Beliefs
Some Sunsetters follow the cult of the Fallen One. The Fallen One is their name for the Foremother from the Seventeenth Gift's resting place. [[paul|Paul]], who arrived in the world with the same Gift, told Priarch Gallard:
> “A Foremother was with me, but she died on the journey. The Sunsetters call her the Fallen One.”

He added that if the Fallen One had survived, the whole world would have grovelled before her, and he would not have been noticed at all. In a letter to the Lady in the Dark, Adrian clarified that there had been three visitors: the woman had died, while both men were probably alive.

According to [[khash-ejlish|Hash Eilish]], who had served the Fallen One for seventeen years, her fellow believers had been a minority for many years. They lived as outcasts, hid themselves, and wore masks. Vittor Shayland believed they had worshipped “him,” meaning Paul, for twenty years, but Lakht Mis corrected him: “Not him, her.” The Fallen One is connected with death. The cult's servants revere death, seek signs of her presence, and believe that the Fallen One “loves the strong.” Horis wore no armour: according to Eilish, “Horis-desh serves her. If she calls him, no iron must stand in her way.” Eilish called someone who opened the gates for the Fallen One — that is, killed many people — a “gatekeeper.”

The cult's attitude towards Ulyana the Sorrowful, Foremother of death, is ambiguous: Horis sometimes speaks her name (“Ulyana does not want to take him”). Eilish told Joaquin that she had argued with her teacher about whom Vittor Shayland had seen when he died and returned to life. Horis believed the count had met the Fallen One herself, who concealed unnecessary details beneath the guise of abstract “pure light.” Eilish herself allowed that Vittor might deliberately describe his encounter in the afterlife vaguely to please allies of different faiths, but did not blame him and believed he served the Fallen One in any case.

### Ritual Consumption of the Dead
The cult's principal rite is the ritual eating of the flesh of the dead. It is accompanied by prayer and the words “With thoughts of Her.” In the summer of 1775, during the siege of Waymar, [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] saw Horis's soldiers boiling something foul-smelling and hiding a bone. He suspected that they were eating carrion. Horis discovered that a sergeant's arm had been amputated that morning in a nearby infirmary, concluded that the soldiers had eaten it, and promised to find them and punish them severely. The issue was not corpse-eating itself: he condemned the consumption of flesh from a living person. Horis explained his teaching as follows:
> “Whoever eats living human flesh commits an outrage against nature. But whoever consumes the flesh of a dead person with due reverence and prayer worships the sacred mystery of death.”

According to him, a dead person, unlike a pig, is filled with spirituality, “close to the Fallen One and loved by her.” At that same dinner, the Sunsetters prayed and ate roast meat that Joaquin took for horsemeat. Vittor later hinted publicly that it had been dead human flesh. Northerners and Shayland's allies nicknamed Horis's soldiers “corpse-eaters.” Count Fleming called them “brothers of jackals and worms.”

### The Fallen One's “Voice”
Followers of the cult hear the Fallen One's “voice.” By touch, Hash Eilish could learn a person's past and sense whose death had occurred or was about to occur nearby. Horis could smell rain an hour before it began. The Sunsetters considered Paul a friend of the Fallen One. On the day of the [[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]], Horis sensed Paul's death and told Vittor: “We followed the call of the Fallen One. Now her voice has fallen silent.”

## General Horis
General Horis was Count Rantigar's chief commander, then ruler of Silence and the cult's spiritual leader and teacher. Chroniclers and poets called him Oris for its similarity to Origin. He himself explained: “I was Horis all my life, until I had the misfortune to cross swords with First Winter,” while in the Sunset dialect he was “Horis-desh, in fact.” His disciples called him “master.” Lakht Mis and Hash Eilish called him “our beacon and guide.”

Joaquin expected to see a stern warrior or a grey-haired sage. Horis proved to be a chubby-cheeked little man, unarmoured and almost unwrinkled, the top of his head barely reaching Eilish's neck. He was always hungry, loved jokes and embraces, and talked cheerfully and at length. But behind the clowning was a level-headed, perceptive commander. At military councils, he accurately analysed weaknesses in the defences, predicted the enemy's plans, and saw through Vittor's tricks. Richard Origin accused him of cowardice, and Horis merely sneezed in reply. He deliberately placed the “rear-men,” undisciplined soldiers, in the lower part of the defences so that their flight would lure the kairs under the Fingers' fire. Asked whether he was always like this, Eilish replied: “Wise, cunning, and happy? Yes, sir, always.”

## Lakht Mis
Lakht Mis, Eilish's companion on the embassy, is a taciturn Sunsetter with a steel prosthetic left hand, its knuckles studded with spikes. He said of himself that he served the Fallen One faithfully, but “she has honoured me with her nearness only once.” It was he who told Paul that the Sunsetters served the cult of the Fallen One and offered him their help. On the march north, Lakht Mis commanded a detachment of Sunsetters. He died in the night battle near [[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]]: defending the Chosen One's camp against the kairs, he was crawling towards the cart carrying the captive Iona's cage when a northern sword entered his back.

## In the War of 1775
### The Embassy and Alliance
In the spring of 1775, Vittor Shayland conducted secret correspondence with Horis and opened a banking office on the Sunset Coast. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] feared that Vittor would use Horis, who was respected in the Steppe, to secure an alliance with [[moran|Moran]], and hurried to send his own envoy to Moran, hoping to get there before the general. On 27 May, Horis's envoys, Hash Eilish and Lakht Mis, arrived in Shayland. That same night, they witnessed [[perevorot-v-uejmare|Iona's rebellion]]. They demanded that Vittor heal the mortally wounded Iona and let them speak with “the man from the Object.” After hearing Paul, the envoys shone with happiness and left for Silence to persuade the general to support the count.

### The Defence of Waymar
In June, Horis's army arrived in [[uejmar|Waymar]]. It contained equal numbers of horsemen, archers, spearmen, and swordsmen. When the kairs broke through the town's defences, the Sunsetters withdrew in good order to Bald Hill behind the castle, retaining 1700 of their original fighting force and losing less than a tenth. Horis held the hill and the road to the northern gates until Count Fleming came to his aid with his men from the White Sea.

During Desmond's July siege of Waymar Castle, hunger set in within the camp, and the Sunsetters' corpse-eating came to light. Fleming proposed sending the “corpse-eaters” to certain death to conserve water from the well on Bald Hill. Vittor almost agreed, but postponed the attack for a day. In that time, Priarch Gallard's army arrived, and the northerners withdrew without a battle. In his delight, Fleming shook Horis's hand. When Vittor “rose from the dead” in the castle temple after the priarch's death, Horis was the first to fall to his knees, followed by Eilish and Lakht Mis.

### The March North
Two Sunset regiments served under the Chosen One's banners. Horis lent the count five hundred “corpse-eaters” to collect taxes from Shayland's baronies. Vittor joked: “Keep them away from the graveyards.” The Sunsetters distinguished themselves at [[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]], where they attacked the Northwooders with the cry “Fallen One, receive us!” On the march to First Winter, Horis commanded one of the three corps, made up of Sunsetters and Northwooders. He deliberately slowed the baggage trains to let the Shavan cavalry go ahead.

### The Battle for First Winter
In December 1775, at the [[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]], two Sunset regiments and a regiment of bears, six thousand soldiers with five Fingers in all, entered the city almost unopposed. Horis had no Finger. He said: “I want to serve the Fallen One with my mind and heart, not my hand.” In the Cathedral of Agatha the Radiant, the general reverently declared: “I feel the presence of the Fallen One.” Then the city snapped shut like a trap. Garlands in the streets fell all at once and discharged spark; hundreds of Sunsetters died on the spot, while defenders hidden in houses opened fire from the windows. Horis estimated the ambush at about twenty thousand people, “reinforced with spark,” including an entire spark regiment. Vittor, who had carried out the reconnaissance, concluded that the soldiers had disguised themselves in townspeople's clothes. The Sunsetters fled the city, but Horis's headquarters retained its discipline.

Horis refused Vittor's order to retake the city and burn it out with the Fingers. He informed the count that Paul was dead (“Strange, Count, that Ulyana the Sorrowful did not notify you”), and said: “You are simply a man hungry for power. I do not condemn you, but understand: we did not come for power.” Before the general's eyes, Vittor killed his brother Martin, and Joaquin began shooting the count with a Finger again and again. After Vittor's tenth death, Horis placed a hand on Joaquin's shoulder and said: “Enough. Ulyana does not want to take him.” He then ordered a retreat to the [[stvorki-neba|Gates of Heaven]]. The Sunsetters withdrew from the valley in battle order and no longer wished to fight after Paul's death.

## In Fantasies
In Fantasies, which the author describes as possible destinies for the characters, Erwin Origin recounted that after the defeat at First Winter, Horis “had the courage” to meet him personally and discuss terms of surrender. Origin and the Sunset Coast signed a peace, and Horis's regiments departed with honour. The Shavans, however, did not dare surrender, and Erwin considered their punishment just: Shavans, Sunsetters, bears, and others had come to his land to plunder and kill, but he made peace with those who bent the knee. It is also stated that the powerful cult of the Fallen One spread throughout the northwest and deified both Paul and [[navi|Navi]] as “fallen gods.” Some Sunsetters even prayed to [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]], the great Finger-bearer who had fallen in battle.

## Interesting Facts
- The Sunsetter Boot, servant of Marquis Miral-Senelius, accompanied [[kharmon|Harmon]] on his flight over the [[bezdonnaya-propast|Bottomless Abyss]].
- [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]] initially took the line from Gloria's letter, “the sun of my earthly life has set,” as a hint at the Sunset Coast, but found no monastery of Ulyana there.
- Among other messages, a pigeon strip from General Horis on the Sunset Coast reached Empress [[minerva|Minerva]]'s desk.
- In Fantasies, envoys from the Sunset Coast are mentioned among the empress's guests. The monk Goblet's A History of All the Northern Lands also appears, praising the pride and love of freedom of the Sunset people.
