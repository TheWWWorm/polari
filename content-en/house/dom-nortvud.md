## Overview

The Northwoods are one of the Empire of Polari's [[velikie-doma|Great Houses]] and rulers of the northern County of [[nortvud-zemlya|Northwood]]. According to [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]], the land is ruled by descendants of free archers and northern pirates, and the Northwood people bowed to the Dynasty “only recently, less than two centuries ago.” The house traces its ancestry to Foremother Susan, to whom the Northwood people pray and by whose name they swear: “Glory to Susan!” Susan is the seventh Foremother: “Seven. The seventh Foremother, Susan,” [[navi|Navi]] whispered to [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil]] after she lost her memory.

The Northwoods pride themselves on their freedom but must keep an eye on their powerful neighbour, House [[dom-oridzhin|Origin]]. All her life, Sybil jealously competed with the Origins over lavish meals and luxurious clothes; Erwin thought the difference lay not in wealth or the number of swords, but in faith: the Origins believed in their own greatness, whereas Sybil did not. As [[minerva|Mira]] saw it, the Empire regarded Northwood as “a primeval forest full of bears and bearded boors.”

## Symbols and Customs

- **The bear.** Northwood's sacred animal and symbol of its strength; the Northwood people themselves are called “bears.” The house's arms show a bear with a raised paw: in a moment of despondency, Sybil bitterly suggested “raising your hand like the bear on Northwood's arms and growling louder” to amuse people. Imitating this gesture, Mira jokingly raised her hand while talking to Lady Iona. No hunter in Northwood will dare kill a bear unless it attacks first, so there are no bears' heads among the trophies in the halls of Bear's Fang. Its menagerie housed a trained bear named Maverick, who recognised his masters' faces: in Countess Sybil's presence, he stood to his full height and raised his right paw.
- **Green.** The tower of the countess's mansion in the capital was painted in Northwood green, as was her carriage bearing the family arms. After Sybil was exposed, the colour became unwelcome at court.
- **Nicknames.** According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, the Northwood people were the first to call kairs wolves; the nickname was intended as an insult, since by Northwood standards a wolf is a small beast beside a bear. The kairs answered with the saying, “The sea is no forest, and a bear is no fish,” implying that the Northwood people only imagined themselves sailors. According to the Guide, the kairs still surpass the bears in warfare, while the bears surpass the kairs in shipbuilding.
- **Songs.** No Northwood feast is complete without a ballad. Soldiers sang a bawdy song, “The Lady and the Bear,” about Countess Sybil and her bear. Old songs about past wars include songs of the Knights of the Bear.

## History

### Origins

According to the Guide, Northwood's forests began to be settled in the VI–VII centuries by poor people, outcasts, and lovers of freedom who refused to submit to the increasingly powerful feudal lords. Northwood became known as a free land where life was hard but independent, and “every man is his own lord.” At first, its people hunted; later, they reached the [[more-ldov|Sea of Ice]] and turned to fishing and piracy. In the VIII century, the land fell under the Order of Lightbearers and was liberated a century later, after the Wars of Despair.

By the XI–XII centuries, Northwood piracy was troubling the Dukes of Origin, beginning a long confrontation between the two lands that ended only in the XVI century, during the [[loshadinye-vojny|Horse Wars]]. Hostility towards their dangerous neighbour forced the free Northwood people to rally around their strongest warriors, eventually establishing feudal rule under counts. The series mentions the [[vtoraya-nortvudskaya-vojna|Second Northwood War]], after which the Origins acquired pastures in the west of their duchy. The Guide also describes how Northwood's army, marching to war against the Sunset Coast, crossed the Bridge of Eight Prayers in Shayland without the bishop's blessing; the bridge then burned, cutting off its return. By the XVIII century, according to the Guide, Northwood and Origin had become almost friendly neighbours, united by their northern spirit and shared contempt for the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]'s trade monopoly. The series itself says the Dukes of Origin and Counts of Northwood repeatedly went to war against the Southern Way's “bloodsuckers,” retreating each time the Crown intervened for the merchants.

### The War for the Objects (1756)

In 1756, when the Sunsetters invaded Shayland and captured the resting place of the Seventeenth Gift, their general Oris invited Northwood to join them. According to Count [[vinsent-shejland|Vincent Shayland]], relations with Northwood were then very difficult, and Shayland had to keep troops in the east for fear of an attack by the bears. Count [[elias-nortvud|Elias]] “barely restrained himself” and did not enter the war. Afterwards, he came to [[uejmar|Waymar]] for the celebrations with his young wife Sybil, three sons, and eight dozen knights.

### The Bride Substitution and Northern War (1773–1774)

In October 1773, Sybil made a secret alliance with Erwin Origin: Northwood would support the universal tax and any bride chosen by the emperor, and the countess would receive part of the port of [[uindli|Windley]]. In the spring of 1774, she arranged the substitution of the brides: her daughter [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]] became “Minerva Stagfort” at court and [[adrian|Adrian]]'s fiancée, while the real [[minerva|Minerva]] lived in the capital under Gloria's name.

In autumn 1774, after Sybil and Gloria were exposed and arrested, the entire House of Northwood was accused of conspiracy and summoned to court. [[krejg-nortvud|Craig]], detained in Waymar, was persuaded by [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] to lead Northwood's army in support of [[myatezh-oridzhina|Origin's rebellion]], while Elias remained Vittor Shayland's honourable prisoner. Thirteen thousand bears crossed [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]]. At [[bitva-pri-piksi|Pixie]], Craig and Donald were captured and thousands of bears fell; in [[reznya-v-labeline|Labelin by night]], Count Lilliday commanded the bear regiments, and Erwin believed the bears owed their success to him. After Labelin, the captured northerners, including Donald, were freed, but the Silver Fox had already taken Craig away with him.

### After Victory (1775)

The Northwood brothers demanded spoils, territories in the Southern Way, and court appointments, forcing Erwin to “make up stories” to keep them away from governing the Empire. Craig compelled him to appoint him commander-in-chief of the spark army; his bears took part in suppressing the [[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrop Rebellion]]. Meanwhile, the county descended into unrest: the war had consumed a substantial part of its budget, peasants and barons were united in discontent, and Horace, acting as governor in [[klyk-medvedya|Bear's Fang]], commanded little respect and barely suppressed a revolt in March. Minerva's advisers proposed bringing back Sybil, who would be “entirely under the Crown's control,” but her trail had gone cold.

In the summer of 1775, [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] sent [[dorkaster|Baron Dorcaster]] and Elias to Bear's Fang and proclaimed the old count its lawful ruler. Craig abandoned his ally [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] outside Waymar, besieged his own capital, and eventually recaptured it, beating his father and imprisoning him with Horace. In October, he died in the battle at [[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]], where he and Desmond attempted to defeat the “Chosen One's” army. Thousands of bears surrendered; many joined Shayland and swelled his army. Northwood fell under Elias's rule — in practice, he was the Puppeteer's puppet. Violent attacks erupted in Bear's Fang between the “grey-haired,” supporters of the old count, and the “rams,” Craig's followers; Elias gave his blessing to those wishing to join the Chosen One's army. Bears also marched with Shayland's army against First Winter.

In autumn 1775, [[minerva|Minerva]] planned to build railway branches from First Winter to Belomorye, Labelin, and Bear's Fang within two years.

## Members of the House

- **[[elias-nortvud|Count Elias Northwood]]** — a frail, grumbling old man indifferent to everything except the fleet and ships; the house's nominal head.
- **[[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Dorina Denise]]** — his second wife, born the daughter of the Baron of [[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]], who ruled the county in her husband's stead; hanged on [[adrian|Adrian]]'s orders in 1775.
- **[[krejg-nortvud|Craig]], the Fanged Knight** — eldest son and heir; died at Fox Vale in 1775. He was already married in 1756, though his wife's name is not given.
- **Horace** — the middle son, a judge of around thirty who handled the county's taxes and courts; sour-tempered and cautious, he advised Craig to go to the capital and beg Adrian for mercy. He was once gravely ill, and to celebrate his recovery the count gave the Object called Blessing to the Church.
- **Donald** — the youngest son, a good-natured giant considered a “weakling” by Northwood standards; third in the line of succession, he had no hopes of inheriting the county. In autumn 1774, he brought Erwin news that Northwood was marching; his jaw was shattered at Pixie, and he still could not chew meat in winter. He assured Minerva that he and Craig knew nothing of their stepmother's plans.
- **[[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]]** — Sybil's daughter, believed to be Count Elias's only child from his second marriage; her real father is Priarch [[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]. Executed on Adrian's orders in 1774.

## In Fantasies

In Fantasies — possible futures, according to the author — Adrian proposes postponing the election of the sovereign from spring to autumn 1776, pointing out that the North is not ready: “Northwood is in turmoil and famine.”

## Note

In one chapter of [[lish-odna-zvezda|Only One Star]], Horace is called the count's youngest son, whereas elsewhere in the series he is the middle son and Donald the youngest.
