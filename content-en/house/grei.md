## Who Are the Greys?
A grey is a warrior in the service of a [[kajry|kair]], a northern knight of [[dom-oridzhin|House Origin]]. Greys are distinguished by their grey cloaks: on the battlefield, kairs are recognised by their red-and-black cloaks and greys by their grey ones. After the capture of Doyle in Only One Star, the fallen northerners were covered with their own cloaks: black for the kairs, grey for the greys. Each kair is usually accompanied by two greys, though some have three.

In Arrow, Coin, Spark, Erwin explains to the mechanic Louis that a grey is not a squire. A southern knight's squire is his assistant and comrade-in-arms; he hunts and feasts as his master's equal, and sometimes even enters a tournament. A grey is “beneath knightly amusements.” Outside battle, his only duties are to improve his skills and serve his master. A kair gives his grey any dirty, troublesome, or exhausting task, and a grey is punished severely for arguing or neglecting his duties. Erwin's conclusion is that “a grey is his master's dog.”

Under northern law, a kair has absolute authority over his grey. When Erwin intervened in the punishment of Kair Jemis's wounded grey, Captain [[teobart|Theobart]] reminded him: “My vassal's vassal is not my vassal,” and a kair “is free to do as he pleases with his grey, just as with a horse or a dog.” Kairs enjoy testing their greys: diving into a cesspit, standing watch for two days without food or drink, crossing a castle wall at night on one leg, or walking over a lake on October ice. A new recruit is valued little, but a kair takes care of a grey who has served for several years.

## The Path to Becoming a Kair
Even a commoner can become a grey. According to Erwin, a peasant or craftsman who is strong, tough, and “as stubborn as a hundred donkeys” can put on a grey cloak, serve the required term, undergo initiation, and become a kair. For a commoner, it is a “rare, fairy-tale opportunity” to enter the upper estate. The Guide to the Lands of Polari calls this route a military “social elevator”: it has made Origin highly militarised, with huge numbers eager to serve.

Selection is brutal. Desmond Origin explained to his son that out of fifty youths, ten pass the strength and endurance test (running thirty miles without stopping and hauling a barrel of pitch up a tower). They are clothed in grey. Three die or are crippled in the first year, and another three over the following two. Four survive until initiation, and one passes. The other three fail and remain greys for ever.

The Yanmei Hunt describes the testing procedure in greater detail. The most capable greys attempt the test in their third or fourth year of service. Those who fail try again in their fifth or sixth year, and may then make a third and final attempt. If that attempt fails too, the kair is disgraced as a poor teacher, and the grey leaves the army in shame. Sensible greys therefore decline the third attempt and remain in their kair's service for life. Such a “seventh-year grey” is the equal of a kair in experience and training, only less fortunate. His kair values him more highly than a fine blade and gives him a generous share of the spoils. Some experienced greys leave for the capital's guard after failing the test.

Service began early. [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] became a grey as an adolescent and received his red-and-black cloak at sixteen. [[sejmur-stil|Seymour Steel]] first saw death “at twelve, when I became a grey.” Lords' sons also served as greys. In The Price of Fear, in 1756, [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]] wore an ordinary grey cloak like the other greys and served under [[blekberri|Colonel Blackberry]]. At the celebrations near [[uejmar|Waymar]], Duke Origin bowed to the emperor, but Richard was not granted that honour: “he was only a grey.” [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] was a grey on his first campaign, serving in a reconnaissance company, and admitted that he was “terribly scared.” According to the author, greys are often the sons of other kairs (livestream of 7 January 2026).

The best greys were initiated after victories. In Only One Star, at the tournament in [[dojl|Doyle]], three hundred greys recommended by their masters fought with blunted swords on foot and horseback, ran in armour, carried heavy loads, and fought unarmed. Seventy-six of them became kairs.

## Service
Greys stand the night watch (“a job for greys, not kairs,” according to Theobart), pitch camp, light fires, groom horses, lead pack animals, carry the wounded, and cook. At halts during the expedition beyond the River, two fires were lit: one for the greys and one for the kairs. [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]]'s grey Tommy pitched his master's tent and made his bed in twenty-one minutes, always in exactly the same way. During sieges, greys built trebuchets and swung the battering ram; on the river, they rowed the boats.

Neither kairs nor greys guarded prisoners in the dungeons of [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]: the warriors considered it degrading. In The Yanmei Hunt, the officer [[irving|Irving]], tasked by [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] with questioning Vittor's soldiers, delegated the job to the most talkative and good-natured greys: ordinary soldiers would have been afraid to speak frankly to a kair officer.

Punishments could be merciless. Erwin remembers that Desmond executed greys “for disobedience, for weakness, for mistakes.” In The Shadow of the Great Tree, an old woman in [[lid|Leed]] recalled in the wounded Joaquin's presence that in the old days the count maintained six hundred swords, “meaning kairs.” Greys, free archers, grooms, and cooks were called “the rabble,” and no one counted them.

## Greys in Battle
Most lands of Polari have no professional infantry, and greys are “head and shoulders” above the peasant militia. Desmond called the infantry made up of greys the finest in the Empire of Polari, “with the possible exception of the imperial spark troops.” Erwin saw them as a steadfast, fearless, manoeuvrable force trained to fight in formation. The kairs would never have admitted it, but victories were often won by the “despised grey greys.” In Only One Star, [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] recounts to her captive [[mark-voron|Mark]] a lesson from Lady Francesca, her tutor at boarding school: a Southern Way infantryman is a ferret, an Origin grey a wolf, and an imperial spark soldier a cobra.

Greys inherited the kairs' contempt for crossbows, and the Origins had only “a miserable handful” of missile troops. This made it all the more astonishing for the people of the Way, in Only One Star, to see thousands of greys with crossbows outside [[labelin|Labelin]]. Erwin himself later admitted that the greys “had barely learned to shoot.” At the [[bitva-pri-piksi|Battle of Pixie]], the greys fired their crossbows at point-blank range. Erwin's army contained seventeen thousand kairs and greys; after the battle, more than two thousand kairs and five thousand greys had been killed or captured. Seventy-two kairs and two hundred and ten greys died in the taking of Doyle.

Humans and Gods describes the northerners' landing in Waymar's harbour. The first to leap ashore are lightly armed greys, almost invisible in their grey cloaks. They dash from cover to cover and train their crossbows on the windows. The second wave smashes the shutters with axes and occupies the houses on the waterfront.

## Known Greys
- **Buck** — Jemis's grey, a fourteen-year-old boy. In Arrow, Coin, Spark, Erwin's horse knocked him down and broke his leg. Jemis beat him, and Erwin sent Buck home with his own grey.
- **Hank the Sailor** — Jemis's second grey. He raced three of Oswald's greys up a cliff without a safety rope and beat them by forty feet.
- **Tommy** — Desmond Origin's grey for fifteen years; during the expedition beyond the River, he served as Erwin's batman. Died in the Beyond. (Early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark call him “Lord Erwin's grey”; in the 2026 edition, following Karen's correction, he is called a batman.)
- **Gilbert** — Desmond's grey, who became a kair. After receiving his cloak, he challenged two kairs who had mocked his love for a maid to a duel.
- **[[osvald|Oswald]]** — passed his initiation twice. When he boasted of how easy the test was, Desmond replied: “Well then, Grey Oswald, you will receive your cloak tomorrow morning, after your warm-up.”
- **[[teobart|Theobart]]** — Desmond's “former grey,” in Erwin's recollection in The Shadow of the Great Tree.
- **[[brant-stil|Brant Steel]]** — a former shepherd, Kair Derrek's grey. “He was the one who made me a warrior.”
- **Willy and Denny** — Jemis's greys during the voyage aboard the Seal in Only One Star.
- **John-John** — the grey of a kair he simply called “Mine.” The only grey aboard the Seal to survive the slaughter; his kair promised to put him forward for initiation after the war.
- **Winston Flight** — in The Shadow of the Great Tree, Lieutenant Berengar's deputy, quartermaster, and grey at the Sunset Star outpost in [[melnitsy|Mills]]. Berengar offered to put him forward for initiation, but Flight stayed with him. He called himself Squire, “but never a grey.”
- Kair Seymour Steel's greys beat [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] at the card table in The Yanmei Hunt. The barber [[garri-khog|Harry Hogg]] knocked one of them senseless with a thrown steel ball.

## In Fantasies
In Fantasies, the greys on night watch heard a ghost's moans in First Winter's gate tower. In the mornings, greys run to training. Jemis taught the dog Archer to help his master in battle by staging practice fights with greys. [[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]'s nephew [[barni|Barney]] dreamed of becoming a grey and then a kair of the Leed Wolves, but enrolled in the Technical Academy instead. In A Brilliant Calculation, General [[uiljam-dejvi|Davy]] recognised northerners in town by their cloaks: greys in grey, kairs in red and black.

## According to Lady Karen
In the [[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|collector's edition]] of Arrow, Coin, Spark, Lady [[karen|Karen]] believes the author “does not know what greys are” and corrects him:
- a grey is a kair's apprentice and servant, “a journeyman under a very stern master,” but not a vassal. A vassal is a nobleman who has sworn fealty to a superior, whereas a person of any estate can become a grey, provided a kair agrees to take him on;
- both greys and kairs can be mounted or on foot: whoever has enough money for a horse rides. There is no division of “kair — horseman, grey — infantryman,” since every kair was once a grey. Many kairs eventually transfer to the cavalry because it is more prestigious;
- Tommy is not a grey but Erwin's orderly: Erwin is not a kair and therefore cannot have greys;
- the Origins' infantry is strong because of its skill, not the number of greys: even a peasant without a horse can become a grey, so brilliant swordsmen are found in the infantry too. Moreover, kairs and greys are taught to fight both in formation and outside it, whereas the infantry of other lands are trained only to fight in formation;
- every northern company should have at least three dozen crossbowmen. Against mounted [[shavany|Shavan]] archers, the North fields cavalry armed with throwing weapons and kairs and greys with crossbows.
