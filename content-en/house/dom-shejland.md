## History
### The Old Counts
About three centuries ago, a knight received from the emperor an immense, deserted region north of [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]], a rectangle two hundred miles from east to west and four hundred from south to north, reaching all the way to the [[more-ldov|Sea of Ice]], together with the title of Count of Shayland. No one wanted the misty land, which was said to be full of monsters. The count chose his own coat of arms: a signal horn, the instrument of scouts in the imperial army of the day, entwined with ivy, the symbol of the misty land where ivy grew everywhere. For a century and a half, the counts ruled reasonably well: they built towns and castles, granted generous plots of land to peasants, and established a fleet on the lake and the [[torrej|Torrey]]. The county grew rich on brightly dyed linen, for which silver from Almera and the Southern Way flowed north.

Its wealth attracted its neighbours. Year after year, the Northwooders from the east and nomads from the west sliced land away from Shayland. In the end, all that remained of the county was a strip forty miles wide, twenty miles on either bank of the Torrey, and four hundred miles long, from the lake to the sea (sixteen and a hundred and fifty miles in early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark). A chain of riverside castles helped them hold it: the Shaylanders learned to move their small forces swiftly along the river. The count's domain contains eight towns and thirteen castles. On one occasion, Twilight, with its great library, is called the capital of the land, but elsewhere the capital is identified as the counts' residence, [[uejmar|Waymar]].

### The Bankers
The present branch of the house comes from merchants. According to the Guide to the Lands of Polari, in the mid-eighteenth century the ruling dynasty “essentially changed, although it retained its name.” The Harper banking house bought up many of the counts' promissory notes and acquired a “controlling interest” in House Shayland. The count was forced to marry his daughter and heiress to [[vinsent-shejland|Vincent]] Harper, and died soon afterwards.

Vincent himself told his sons the story in greater detail. His grandfather fled [[kholliwell|Hollywell]] with his entire family when the local count decided to seize the merchants' money to maintain his army. Vincent's father was born aboard ship. Through his own intelligence, he amassed an enormous fortune and became the richest man in Shayland, but the firstborn disdained to deal with him, and he had to hire noblemen to speak on his behalf. The Counts of Shayland tried to seize his fortune three times. At last, he bought up so many of the counts' promissory notes that the Great House “essentially belonged to him.” The counts married their daughter to Vincent and named him their heir, yet even then refused to sit at the same table as the Harpers. To make the barons and knights honour their oaths of vassalage, the grandfather had to bribe half of them and intimidate the other half. For years he kept a Northwood regiment of two thousand “ravenous bears” in Waymar.

Lady [[karen|Karen]] gives a shorter account in the [[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|collector's edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark]]: Vittor's grandfather, a banker, bought up the counts' debt notes and became the house's owner in financial terms, so the counts had to marry their daughter to his son and declare her heiress to the title. Karen also points out that the Shaylands belong to Vivian's lineage, ninth in seniority, and therefore are not firstborn.

Thus [[vittor-shejland|Vittor]] is a count “in the second generation,” and the nobility looks down on the Shaylands. Aiden Almera advised Emperor Tellurian to abolish the Great House of Shayland and place its lands under the Crown's direct administration.

### The Seventeenth Gift
In 1756, the Seventeenth Gift of the gods arrived near Waymar, on the western bank of the [[torrej|Torrey]]. The Westerners believed the Gift was intended for them, and war broke out. Count Vincent called on the [[dom-oridzhin|Origins]] for help, and the Origins defended the Gift against the Sunsetters. This was the beginning of the friendship between the two families. Of the Gift's hundred and forty-two Objects, Vincent received thirty-nine. The Gift enhanced the house's standing: according to the Guide, the gods themselves had blessed Count Vincent and his children, and even the emperor began to listen to the Shaylands. At the same time, in the Gift's resting place, the young Vittor and [[martin-shejland|Martin]] learned the secret of the dead goddess and met the future [[paul|Paul]] (see [[vittor-shejland]]).

Vincent's favourite maxim was “Use what you know, sell what you have.” Another of his admonitions to his son was that lords “do not like to count,” and that if the count forgot how to count, his father would descend from the Star and “give him a proper hiding.” For many years Vincent kept a mysterious prisoner in Waymar's dungeon, first in a tower for about a year, then in the lowest circle. He died in his locked study a week after the prisoner's escape, while Vittor was talking to a priest in the temple. Erwin later concluded that Vittor had released the prisoner himself and allowed him to frighten his father, who had a weak heart, to death. Vittor became count around 1770.

## The Bank of Shayland
The source of the Shaylands' power is their bank. According to the Guide, Waymar was the birthplace of the northern banking system, which brings the counts fabulous profits. Shayland banking offices operate in towns and cities throughout the North and Centre of the empire. (Early editions of Arrow, Coin, Spark add that they are as numerous as post stations and cover four duchies and three counties.) A customer deposits money in the bank and receives a promissory note in their own name, which can be exchanged for cash at any other office. If the note is lost, the money is paid out on presentation of the customer's name and a secret word. Harmon believed the bank charged one coin in a hundred for its services, yet when he cashed a note in Labelin, he paid one twentieth of the sum.

The bank's headquarters is a hexagonal tower in the middle of Smoky Distance, two hundred yards from the shore, with a drawbridge and catapults on the roof. Objects and gold are stored in its cellars. The banking offices also supplied the count with information: news of the course of the war in 1774 reached Waymar from the branch in Labelin. The manager of the central office, [[perkins|Perkins]], became a mercenary commander and a Finger-bearer in 1775. When Shayland was declared to be the Puppeteer, townspeople ransacked his banks in the capital, while the northerners emptied the treasury in Waymar.

## The Land and Its People
According to the Guide, Shayland is divided into Lower Shayland, around the lake, and Upper Shayland, the Forest Edge, closer to Northwood. The people of Lower Shayland are adaptable cosmopolitans, while those of the Forest Edge, influenced by the Northwooders, love hunting and hospitality and consider themselves patriots of the North. They are united by a common enemy, the Sunset Coast. Shayland is a stronghold of urban culture: most of its people live in towns, and the builders' guild is powerful. Shaylanders love pubs and inns, the theatre, gambling (cards, dice, and rat races), and brightly coloured clothes in cold weather. Shayland has the greatest equality between the sexes in the empire, and Vivian is honoured here above all the other Foremothers. The Bridge of Eight Prayers, the empire's second-longest bridge, spans the [[torrej|Torrey]].

## Members of the House
- Count Vincent Shayland — father of Vittor and Martin. According to the Guide, he came from the Harper banking house. He died around 1770 in a study locked from the inside, a week after the prisoner's escape; Erwin later believed that Vittor had allowed the prisoner to frighten his father to death.
- The old Count of Shayland — Vincent's father-in-law, the last count of the previous dynasty. His name is not given.
- [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Kaylin Agna]] — count, banker, Puppeteer, and the “Chosen One.” Frozen in the [[chrevo|Womb]].
- [[martin-shejland|Martin Kaylin Agna]] — his younger brother. Killed by Vittor at the end of 1775.
- [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] — Vittor's wife, Countess of Shayland from 1774 (see [[brak-vittora-i-iony]]).
- The brothers' mother, the old count's daughter. In Vittor's recollections, she called Martin a “rat-bred monster” and later abandoned the family. Her name is not given. According to the physician Goluers, Martin's parents were already dead by 1775.

## Vassals and Retainers

- [[dorkaster|Baron Dorcaster]] — the count's “permanent guest,” a merry man with an egg-shaped head. In 1775, he alone brought his entire army to the Chosen One; he held Bear's Fang and then [[lid|Leed]]. Killed in Leed by Iona. His relative Selina Dorcaster is mentioned in 1756.

## Role in the Series
In 1774, Vittor's marriage to Iona bound the Shaylands to the Origins. During the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], Vittor manoeuvred between the Origins and the emperor, keeping Minerva with him as a bargaining chip. In 1775, it was revealed that he was the Puppeteer. After the [[perevorot-v-uejmare|coup in Waymar]], the Origins declared war on the Shaylands, and Shayland's representatives were arrested. Having become the Chosen One, Vittor led an army north composed of Sunsetters, Shavans, Almerans, and vassals, including Baron Dorcaster. To fund it, he imposed a double tax throughout Shayland. He was defeated in the [[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]].

By the end of the series, Martin is dead and Vittor is frozen in the Womb. Vittor and Iona had no children.

## In Fantasies
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], a possible future of the series, the peace agreement places the Womb containing Vittor under the supervision of [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]], and it is kept in the [[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]] pending an ecclesiastical trial. In the spring of 1776, Adrian speaks of the absence of government in Shayland: for personal reasons, the sole heiress to the title has still not assumed her rights. Erwin, meanwhile, calls Iona the Countess of Shayland and the empress's prime vassal. The narrator of one of the novellas remarks that the late Count Vincent would have been delighted with the Laemian beauty Demetra: she embodied his favourite saying, “Trade what you have.”

## Interesting Facts
- According to Vittor, Waymar has been burned to the ground three times.
- Vittor once described himself as “a simple banker, distinguished only by intelligence and, perhaps, a little kindness.”
- The Shaylands were the weakest of the territorial rulers. Harmon called the count “the weakest and lowest-born of the territorial rulers, yet a territorial ruler all the same.”
