General Horis, whom the Northerners and poets call **Oris**, is the chief general of [[graf-rantigar|Count Rantigar]] and one of the most famous people in the West. After the death of the count, he holds Silens, and in 1775 leads a sunset army to help [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]. ([[zakatnyj-bereg|Sunset Coast]]) In the novels, the story is about "Oris", then about Horis: the general himself asks to call him Horis. More about his cult in the article Sunsets. ([[zakatniki]])

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark" (collection edition)
[[karen|Karen]]'s remarks say that Rantigar's first general in the [[melnichnaya-vojna|Mill War]] was "General Horis-desh - a very famous man in the West who plays a considerable, albeit hidden role in history."

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
It is first told by mercenaries Pat and Kroc (chapter 20 "Sword - 3"): Oris was the main commander of Rantigar, the eldest son left him to keep Silens, and the general "took the capital for himself" because the count's sons are "young green", and he is an "experienced warrior". He threw his youngest son to the sea coast, the eldest also did not let him into the city. Mercenaries “more than General Oris”: “He is an experienced guy, with his experience and strength.” [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] remembers that Oris fought for Rantigar in the Mill War, and that he was the one who took the Items from Shayland. Pat and Kroc are proud of how quickly he learned about Dar and moved the army, but Joaquin notices that Oris then unsuccessfully retreated and surrendered to [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]]. He also recalls "Questions of the commander", where the Duke wrote that "a hasty retreat is a strange mistake, not characteristic of him."

In Chapter 28, "Sword - 5", Harry Hogue says that his my Lord "has conversations with General Oris" and opens a bank in the Sunset Coast. ([[garri-khog|Harry Hogg]]) In Chapter 34, "The North Bird - 6" [[elias-nortvud|Elias Northwood]] tells [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] about the 1756 war. Oris had six thousand horsemen and a fortified camp with ramparts, moats and pit-traps, against him were two battalions of Desmond and two thousand soldiers of Vincent Shayland. And yet he retreated the day before the kair approach, and Desmond overtook him on the march and cut him off from Silens. Northwood believes this to be a sign of mystery: it is possible that Oris "learned some secret" in Dar's box. He also mentions that Oris called him to be an ally. In chapter 35, “Sword — 6”, the envoy Lakht Mis says that “General Oris continues to hold the capital”, that more and more people from the Sunset Coast are rallying to his banner, and that he has “calmed” his feud with the Elder Son through wise words.

## In "Humans and Gods"
Oris sends ambassadors to [[uejmar|Waymar]], [[khash-ejlish|Hash Eilish]], and Lahta Misa (chapters 2 and 5), and [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] fears that Vittor will conspire with [[moran|Moran]] through him, respected in the Steppe (chapter 15 of Arrow-3).

In Chapter 26, Arrow-5/Sword-6, Orissa's army enters Waymar. Joaquin was waiting for a stern warrior or a gray-haired sage, and saw a "cheeked short": the top barely reached the neck of Hash Eilish, he did not wear armor, there were almost no wrinkles, and his eyes "looked not so much for the depths of human souls as for what to eat." Instead of greeting, the general shouts: “Graph, we are all very hungry!” — and climbs to hug. He said, “Don’t call me Oris.” According to him, he was Horis all his life, until he “crossed swords with the First Winter”: chroniclers and poets decided that the consonance of “Origin and Oris” would amuse the listener. In the sunset adverb, he's Horis-desh. At lunch, Vittor talks about surrender, and Horis laughs: "my first surrender to Horijin" - that is, the war for the Objects of twenty years ago.

In Chapter 31 of Sword 8 at the council, when the North took most of Waymar, Vittor checks the Bald Hill report from Horis, not Richard, which affects Richard. ([[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]]) Horis holds the hill and the road to the northern gate; his regiment lost less than a tenth of its strength and retained 1700 soldiers. Richard accuses him of cowardice, and the general only sneezes in response. Vittor then instructs him to hold the north gate and Bald Hill for two days: "Take revenge for the past war."

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
In the besieged castle (chapters 7 and 12), Horis wants to eat plenty and does not agree to half a ration. In the tent, he takes [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] with Hash Eilish and Laht Mees, tells him that the Elder Son has taken over Silens, and offers him "the bonds of eternal fidelity" if he comes to the rescue. He explains the word "backwards" (non-devoted soldiers) as follows: in each army there are those whose will is weaker than fatigue and hunger, and the commander only has to figure out how to use them, so he put them on the bottom of the descent. Having learned about the soldiers, he finds out that in the infirmary his hand was taken, promises to severely punish the guilty and explains the doctrine of the Fallen. When the castle prepares a test for the sunsets (Chapter 12 "Sword - 2"), they plan that Horis in a sortie will distract the enemy while Joaquin, Martin and Ice enter the kairam in the rear.

After the resurrection of Vittor, Horis falls to his knees first (chapter 15 "Sword - 3"). He commands one of the three corps on a march north: the Sunsets and the Northwoods go under him. In Chapter 49, "Sword - 11" he drives off the road to let the Shavans through: "I want to give way to Stepi's easy cavalry."

In December 1775, he led the sunset regiments into the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], abandoning Perst: "I want to serve the Fallen with my mind and heart, and not with my hand." In the cathedral, he says, “I feel the presence of the Fallen One.” After [[paul|Paul]]'s trap and death, he tells Vittor: "You are a simple power-lover ... we did not come for power. We followed the call of the Fallen. Now her voice is silenced.” When Joaquin killed Vittor of Perst ten times, Horis put his hand on his shoulder: "Enough. Ulyana doesn't want to take him."

## In "Fantasies"
In Closer and Further (August 1776), Erwin recalls that Horis "had the courage" to meet him after the defeat at the First Winter and to discuss surrender; The Origin and the Sunset Coast made peace, and the Horis regiments left with honor. In "Ad-ri-an!", one of the future options promises Horis a county title from [[adrian|Adrian]], and in the other, he, without advertising this, is on the side of Erwin and provides peace in the House during the siege.

## Personality and Appearance
He is short, cheeky, with a moustache, which he smoothes with the "gesture of the steppe nomad". He does not wear armor and assures that "no iron should become an obstacle" if the Fallen calls him. He likes to eat, jokes and hugs, but at the council he definitely understands positions. Students call him “master”, ambassadors — “our light and guide”. He is able to express both confidence and irony.
