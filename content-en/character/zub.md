The **tooth** is a dentist from Lowville, who in February 1775 led the townspeople to tax collectors and joined the [[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrop Rebellion]]. He became the leader of the townspeople and remade [[salem|Salem]]'s campaign "for justice" in the campaign for an "honest tax". Together with [[ruka-dodzh|Hand Dodge]], he seized power over the army, and after a pardon took the former Snowdrops to [[adrian|Adrian]].

## "A Doll on the Throne"
### Lowville
When Salem's scouts beat the townspeople of Lowville from the collectors to serve, one petty bourgeois "called the townspeople to attack" and first rushed to the shooters. He was a “skinny, wiry, sharp-nosed, angry” guy. When asked by [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] about his name, he replied: “The tooth... Because I'm a dentist," and flashed with a golden fang. The next day, the Tooth brought eight hundred men: a hundred merchants and seven hundred philistines with supplies, weapons and a camping smithy. Salem, who blamed him for the dead pickers, he objected: "rotten tooth must be pulled out", and officials and Lord's Hollowie "and there is rot." Sergeant Dodge called him a good organizer, though ignorant in military affairs. The tramp was considered an "honest citizen", and Joaquin was a man who "likes to get into trouble".

### “Honest tax”
Three days before Niar, Tooth offered to address his "prominent friends-friends" in Niar, Izluchin and Hampton. ([[niar|Niar]]; [[izluchina|Riverbend]]) He admitted that in Lawville he was calling people not for justice, but against taxes, and seven hundred people rose, "every twentieth out of the city." Justice, he called the word “painfully vague” and proposed a clear goal: a tax of a fifth of income, over which no one has the right to take. Out of a hundred and twenty centurions, one hundred and eight voted for his proposal. A tooth and a dozen Lowvilles leapt forward, and Niar sent four thousand men to meet him.

Joaquin, pretending to have a toothache, tried to talk to the doctor. The tooth noticed his white teeth, a spark dagger and a squire and refused the “lordish”: “I don’t believe you, lordy.”

### Zastava near Izluchina
At the outpost, Zub volunteered to go out to the shooters himself, leaving behind two thousand citizens. Joaquin “couldn’t deny him courage.” From the mist, the Tooth regiment attacked the cavalry. The tooth, wounded in the hand, kept the philistine in line. According to Dodge's story, he shouted, "Stand up, you sons of bitches!" and waved a spear with his shirt tied. After the fight, Dodge called him a general and Zub Dodge a major. The townspeople were reaching for the Tooth because, according to Joaquin, it “can respect them.”

### Shots and Hamptons
In the Tent of the Tooth, [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] showed the leaders sparked self-fire and called the Tooth a "tooth-bearer". Tooth and Dodge immediately took the box under the protection of their guys. In Hampton, they were introduced to the townspeople: "People's General Tooth and People's Major Dodge. We are the right and left hands of the leader of Salem!” Salem wanted to abandon three thousand Hilt collected by the townspeople, but Zub accepted them and put the money in custody. Two-thirds of the forty-thousand-strong army were now under Tooth and Dodge.

Under Hampton, General Gore ordered the Snowdrops to disperse. When he turned the horse, Tooth threw, “Why wait an hour?” and fired first. Together with Dodge, they laid down three guards and a bannerman. Salem ordered them to surrender, Zub refused, and according to Dodge, the boys arrested the leader himself. Charlie Bull, for whom Tooth was the "second father", shot Joaquin. ([[charli-telenok|Charlie the Calf]]) Veselchak decided that the murderers sent a Tooth to the wounded. ([[veselchak|Jolly]])

### Holy Field
On the Holy Field, Tooth introduced himself to [[minerva|Minerva]] as the voice of the citizens of the Crown. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) He asked for an "honest tax", called the shots "a cruel accident", and called the shooters poor people who trembled with fear. When Minerva asked who had shot himself, the crowd shouted: “Tooth! He has always been in favor of war!” The doctor "slapped something illegible, drooling", and the guards took him away.

## In "Humans and Gods"
On June 1, 1775, Minerva ordered the Tooth and Dodge of the Table Citadel to be brought to her. ([[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]]) The tooth was justified: “The finger convulsed, so he shot.” He complained that the men are fed once every two days, and from the words of the report of the interrogators denied: they were allegedly knocked out with pads. Dodge was illiterate, and Tooth read his papers aloud. He was the first to notice the letter of pardon. Minerva instructed both to bring to the imperial service five to seven thousand Snowdrops, who became robbers, and promised Gloria for a fighter. She said, “If you cheat, I’ll kill you.”

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
Tooth and Dodge gathered the former Snowdrops into a mercenary army, but [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] overbought them and placed them under Adrian's banner. In August 1775, at [[faunterra|Faunterra]], Dodge reported to Adrian about unreliable taverns and churches. The list was with Tooth, because Dodge himself is not literate. In early September, Dodge told Adrian: “The tooth and I have already discussed this issue. Don't like Minerva, she's a weakling." No more teeth in books.
