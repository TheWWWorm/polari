## Overview
Oswald was one of eleven kairs who accompanied [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] in the exploit for the River in 1774. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) He was short and stout. Next to his three greys, the braided, blond-haired and blue-eyed guys, apparently brothers, the kair looked unattractive, "like an oak snare: strong, but crooked."

In the North, Oswald was known for having been initiated into the kair twice. Then he said, “It’s easy. Do it every morning instead of every morning.” The Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]] heard this and announced that Gray Oswald would receive the cloak tomorrow morning, after the warm-up. The second test proved to be much more difficult than the first, but Oswald coped and at night dined at the ducal table on his left hand from Desmond. He participated in the siege of the port city of Seven Feet: near the campfire, Cyrene Donnell and Oswald told how they “enjoyed themselves” there when they stormed it after a month-long siege.

## In the Explorado
### Race on the Rock
When the squad was at a standstill because the old map incorrectly depicted the river bed, Erwin asked who was the best climber, and Captain Theobarth called Oswald's grey. ([[teobart|Theobart]]) Kair boasted that his guys will be on top in half an hour. [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] mockingly shouted that his only gray would pass three, and sent up Hank the Sailor. Oswald's greys climbed by all rules, killing the hooks and fearing each other, one of them fell off and hung on a rope, and Jemis' gray reached the top forty feet earlier. The Cairos were laughing. Only Oswald and Erwin did not smile, both of whom felt fooled. Later, Erwin’s servant Tommy explained to him that from the very beginning, Jemis bullied Oswald as much as he could, but it did not come to a fight: Oswald “was careful.”

### In the swamps
Oswald helped out the squad more than once. He killed with his sword the lynx that had plucked the face of the servant of the imperial observer [[filipp-loufert|Philip Lowfert]] and would have scratched his eyes. On the marshy shore Oswald with three rows went in the vanguard and was the first to notice the fang, a huge beast breaking towards the detachment. As he passed through the top, he walked in the same way as Erwin, and one of his greys Erwin entrusted to his second horse, Leocant.

When a fire broke out in the swamp, Theobarth ordered Oswald to be responsible for the Lord's safety and to keep him away from the fire. But the fire went on their side, and Erwin, tearing off the cloak, rushed to extinguish it. Oswald hesitated to keep the lord and put out the flame next to him.

### Jemis's Rebellion
The night after the fire, Jemis reproached Oswald that he allowed the "gentle" to risk himself. Oswald replied, "He's a lord. He wanted to put out the fire. Should I have kept it?” and reminded Jemise what he said about the lord. However, when Jemis offered to take Erwin's command by force and send him home, Oswald asked, "Who will lead the gentle woman in the First Winter?" The overheard Erwin realized that the kair had defected to the rebels.

Coming out to the kairs, Erwin turned to those who doubted, and reminded Oswald of the story of his double initiation. When Erwin refused to give up his sword and framed Jemis, the two kairs stood next to the lord, and the mutiny collapsed (the Jemis Rebellion). ([[myatezh-dzhemisa|Jemis's Rebellion]]) Having stripped Jemis of his cloak, Erwin thanked Oswald and Frederick for their service and instructed them to make sure that the exile immediately left the camp without combat weapons.

### Death
When a giant hole in the ground opened behind the hill, the bottom of which was lost in the fog, it was Oswald who threw a stone at it, and no one ever heard him fall. Shortly thereafter, the exploits camp was attacked at night. When Erwin woke up, he went around the tents and found bodies that had been cut so that they were difficult to distinguish. Only one dead man, Oswald's stocky kire, was found. The dying Captain Theobarth managed to say that there were about thirty attackers and they were not savages, but imperial spark infantry in red shirts.
