## Biography

### The Emperor's Bride
In the summer of 1774, Valerie Greysend, the nineteen-year-old daughter of the Marquess of Greysend of the South Road, was one of three brides from which Emperor [[adrian]] was to choose his wife; the other two were [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] and Becca Litland. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]; [[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) Her candidacy was supported by Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] and the western counties. Myra's friend Becca described her as a devout, eternally sad and languid girl, confident that "weeping eyes are a sign of deep nature." ([[minerva|Minerva]])

At the summer ball on June 2, 1774, Valerie, having made her way in line to the lord, received a mockery from the joker [[menson|Manson]] ("Greysends is now the Great House?"), Then, when the joke circled and tossed the ladies, she squealed in his hands, and in the music salon performed a song of her own composition about love for "the best man in the world" under the harps. The court had difficulty holding back laughter; only General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]] took the singing seriously.

### Suspicions of Peace
Mira was once suspected by Valerie and the Labelin behind her of plotting against the emperor, believing that the naivety of the Marquise was only a mask. She found out everything she could about the Graysends, and told Becky her version: the Graysends and Labelin allegedly dismissed the rumors that scared the lord away from Becky herself, and kept the terrible secret Alanis to bring her down on her rival. Becca, in response, called Valerie a "marquise of Wheat Bulk." The estates of the Greysends lie in the south of the duchy, in the bays of the Grey Sands. At one of Adrian's audiences, the Third Marine Guild of the Southern Way, which was patronized by the Graysends and Labelin, requested parking rights by Smile Island, the ancestral domain of General Alexis. Adrian solved the maneuver. The lords hoped to win a "prize", that is, a crown for Valerie, and then enter a new benefit into the marriage contract. He refused the guild and imposed an additional tax on it. Alexis himself, as Mira noticed, was gloomy. When asked if he would be happy if Valerie became empress, he scrawled, although he assured that the lord would not find a more worthy bride.

Mira went beyond suspicion. On June 24, at the military parade in the square of the Forefathers, which opened the summer games, she, along with Becca, bowed to Alanis as the “future Empress” right in front of Labelin and all three of the Gracends. Valerie shuddered, “as if from a blow”, the Marquis frowned, the Marquis turned her eyes away, and the Duke did not change in his face. From this check, Mira concluded that only Labelin and the older Graysends know the secret of Alanis, and Valerie herself does not know about it. On the same day, she named Maurice Labelin, Marquis and Marquess of Gracend to Duke Ayden Almer and made it clear that the secret would die with Labelin. ([[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]) Aiden reasoned that the conspirators kept Valerie in the shadows, covering Alanis as a screen to strike before the very end of the games.

### Summer Games
At the summer games, Valerie went to the arena incognito, in the veil, on a white mare, but she broke the jump when a student from the crowd shouted her name, and fell from the saddle; General Alexis took her away from the arena. On June 27, during the summer games, a tearful marquise hiding in the shadow of the supporting arch expressed "Gloria" (Mira) an insult: why the cold and indifferent get everything - the first dance, the game of strata, the place next to the lord.

In this conversation, Valerie said that the suzerain just gave her a “terrible scene”. The Duke of Labelin called it nothingness and an empty place under his mother ("This fool would not seduce even a fool!"). He said that the “daughter of a bear”, that is, Mira herself, achieved more in a month than Valerie in a year. He refused to support her four days ago, just before Sophia Day, and now only confirmed: “This is over!” He decided to sell his support to Rebecca or Alanis. For Mira, this was a shock: approaching Valerie, she still thought that she would soon “become an orphan.” Since Valerie is no longer a contender, Labelin could not be the head of the conspiracy, and she realized that she “described the death of innocent people.” Mira begged the Marquis to leave the capital immediately, but Valerie decided that she was being bullied, and ran away. When the conspiracy was revealed, it turned out that for the conspirators, the “funny Valerie Gracend” was not a rival at all.

### Marriage with Silver Fox
After Adrian named his fiancee Minerva Stagforth, Valerie's hands were asked by the Silver Fox. The Graysends considered "the best general of the Crown, the owner of strategically important islands" "a worthy consolation prize". Valerie herself found in the general something that she did not find in Adrian: he was in love with her, harsh and unhappy - and needed care. The marriage arranged by the ruler with the beginning of the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]] strengthened the loyalty of the Southern Way to the Crown. As a wedding gift, Adrian allowed the Graysends and Labelin to use the ports of the Jolly Islands. The news of the marriage reached Erwin on September 6, 1774, along with the Imperial Mail. By mid-September, the mutiny had brought Alexis a double advantage: he "married the high-born Marquise of Valerie, by whom he had long sighed", and became commander-in-chief of all the troops of the Crown. In the camp of the northerners, this news was received grimly. "The Southern Way married the Crown," said [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]], and Erwin realized that now the Southern Way would fight for the emperor.

### The Military Council
In the audience, which saw the joke Manson, Valerie appeared with her husband, held him by the hand and “carried herself with royal dignity” so that Adrian did not notice a shadow of her former feelings. Alexis complained that Origin's "non-humans" were ruining the Southern Way, and asked seven regiments to crush the rebel in a month. Valerie interrupted the emperor (“How can you say this about a traitor, a scoundrel, a vile villain?”) and demanded to give her husband “to keep the knightly oath”. Adrian put it out, "Girls don't have a seat on the military council." In Manson's eyes, she left pathetic, "leaving wet traces", as if a bucket of water had been poured on her. Remaining with the general, Adrian directly asked him to reason "without Lady Valerie."

### "A Doll on the Throne"
After the defeat of Adrian and the accession of [[minerva|Minerva]], Valerie remained at court in Faunterra and, as noted by the mistress herself, enjoyed her sympathy. Therefore, in late March 1775, Mira was surprised that Dom Labelin addressed her through an unfamiliar Magda, rather than through the Duke of Maurice or Valerie.

### In "Humans and Gods"
Minerva invited Silver Fox and his wife to the council at the same time as Labelin, Conto and the Crown Raven. ([[konto|Konto]]) In early June 1775, pregnant Valerie dined in Little Tea with Minerva, her husband, Lady Layla and Lieutenant [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]]. ([[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]]) Minerva needed to understand which side the general was on, because Alexis served both her and the secret order and would be happy to serve Adrian. But Valerie said "a frightening lot" and only about motherhood. According to her, Pramatery Sophia “brings the soul of a child into our world by the pen,” and they hold it together. She had already ordered furniture and clothes for the nursery, felt that there would be a boy, and was not going to give the child to the nurse. For her fidelity, she turned to her sister Augustine from the monastery of the Solar Agatha, who was born agate, famous for the gift of foresight, and she immediately announced: “Undoubtedly, boy!” Lady Leila was sore that the baby would spend most of the time with the nurse and that she herself had fed her daughter, but did not feel the flows of love on her part. Minerva, meanwhile, was considering taking Valerie a second maid of honor to ensure her husband's loyalty.

### The Shadow of the Great Tree
The Labelins retained their influence. In July 1775, in an inn at the station near Labelin, Baron Hugo Darryl, who was carrying Adrian's message, discussed at a table that "women can benefit" and that Valerie had already sent a secret letter: she would do anything to turn her husband's swords in the right direction. ([[derril|Hugo Derrill]]) Minerva was warned that Adrian would lure “a general with a young wife named Valerie”, and Mira herself, recalling the selection of brides, thought that “she would have ascended to the throne if she hadn’t been so stupid.” They had only one letter: Valerie, “many obliged” to the Duke Maurice and friendly with Magda, turned the “swords of the Silver Fox” to the side of the returning Adrian. Back in Faunterra, in the fall of 1775, [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], who became empress on her father's advice ("Take this fool"), made Valerie her first lady-in-waiting; the second lady-in-waiting and chalice was Shavanka [[niza|Niza]]. The pregnant wife of the commander also served as a hostage: if Magda shares food with her, Adrian will not dare poison the empress. In October, Valerie rode with Adrian's yard, which was on five trains moving along the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]] following the retreating kairs. Magda soon got tired of her talking about childbirth, midwives and rattles. When the empress staged a game in the ladies' salon - "How are gods different from people?" - the most stupid answer, as Magda expected, gave Valerie: "God dwells in the belly of the expectant mother." At the table, Valerie demanded a stool and sat down “like a horse” to make it easier to give birth. And when Magda said that she would give her child to a babysitter, Valerie patted her stomach: “Fu-fu-fu! Don't listen, bunny, Aunt Magda said a bad word."

However, in the family strategy, as Magda was surprised to admit, Valerie “brilliant general talent”. She taught that the husband in the family is like in a dark forest and the wife should lead him by the hand. The man needs to be praised as often as possible: Magda received this lesson back in [[soltaun|Soltown]], when she was angry at her husband for colluding with the Shimmerians, and Valerie explained that to stop the horse, the coachman says "tprru", whatever he thinks of her. And more often talk about children and “look into the future together,” because the future is “a man’s favorite place.” He said, “You are like a serpent.” Valerie replied that she was simply "Pramateri's granddaughter Sophia and not without feminine wisdom." On December 2, 1775, together with Adrian's court in Main, she watched the battle for the First Winter through the Bird without the Flesh, stroking her stomach and accompanying the spectacle with enthusiastic remarks ("Life itself goes to battle with death!"). ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]; [[ptakha-bez-ploti|Bird without Flesh]]) Seeing the "flowers of the Chosen One" - the children at Vittor Shayland - she suggested that when Vittor goes to prison, "adopt them with the whole yard."

### In "Fantasies"
"Fantasies" the author calls possible options for the future. In the story "Deadly Evidence" (June 1777), Lady Valerie Greysend-Smile gave birth in Winslow, thirty miles from Grace, the capital of the Greysend marquises, the double being "a pair of strong men, each six pounds in weight". Baron Winslow - her father's vassal ("daughter of my lord") - took care of her peace. He did not even want to burn the condemned at the stake, so that the nursing mother would not be disturbed by howls and stench. Her husband, a retired general, was invited to teach a course in military history at the University of Faunterra and lived in the capital. Valerie herself, according to her, flatly refused to go: from the capital unrest, milk will have a bile taste, and the “boys” will spoil the character. Her seigneur was still [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]], who "steals her teeth" from talking about feeding.

Executioner Walter Jane John investigated the murder of a quiet Farnsworth priest, stabbed in a carriage at the gates of her estate, and visited Valerie. They found a complete understanding on the basis of love for babies, and she showed him sleeping twins. According to Valerie, the priest sent greetings to her husband through her, and then sent a disturbing note asking for General Smile’s help. She replied that the general was no longer a general but a teacher, but promised to pass the request on to the bird. Farnsworth went to tea with her and was killed at the gate. Valerie herself believed that the Northerners were to blame for everything: "My Alexis and Count Kindar became friends on the basis of confrontation with the wolves." The killer's henchman, she thought, was forced to take his family hostage. Walter enthusiastically told this version to his partner.

The executioner's partner, the lawyer Macfried Crowe ("Mac"), is by all accounts a retired [[mark-voron|Mark]]. He called Valerie “Smooth Bulka” and recalled that at the court she “heard a note fool” and the first ladies of the empire laughed at her. Walter replied, "The court is a snake's cup, and Lady Valerie is a pure soul." From her words, Mac deduced that Farnsworth was connected to Silver Fox and Kindar by some secret "common cause", and linked him to a secret order.

## Personality and Appearance
Full, tall, white-skinned, with large wet eyes. Sentimental, pious, inclined to melodrama and clumsy (Becca joked that Valerie “loves and knows how to drop” things). She speaks without hesitation, especially about motherhood, and does not notice the awkwardness of the audience. Despite all the naivety, Valerie is sincerely attached to her husband and knows how to influence him. As her advice to Magda showed, she is wise in family matters.

## Relationships

### Alexis Smile
The general loved her back in the days of watching and one listened to her song seriously. Valerie responds to him with devotion and care, is proud of him and, at the request of the Labelins, influences his choice of party.

### Labelins
Maurice Labelin first promoted her to empress, then publicly refused her, but later again took advantage of her influence on her husband. With Magda, they became a strange couple: the empress tolerated her chatter and learned from her.

### Minerva
In the summer of 1774 Mira suspected her of conspiracy, then regretted. Valerie, for her part, was jealous of Gloria for Adrian's attention.
