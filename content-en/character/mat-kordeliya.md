## Who She Is

Cordelia is a Ulyanka, a nun of the Order of Pramatery Ulyana Sad, "Sisters of Death". She is thin and tall, short-haired and head-to-head dressed in black; she has a thin neck, a sharp stubborn chin, a high forehead and a "beautiful profile". Her voice is low and rough - "quiet, but with bits of stone crumbs" - and over the years of service to the Sister Death, she used to speak quietly and mournfully. According to the rules of the order, she can not dance. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] called her always gloomy and thinking about the worst.

In 1775, she was about forty — “a girl by the standards of the Chapter”, where the remaining sixteen higher mothers were gray-haired old women. In addition, she headed the least influential of the orders: the Yanmei, Agata and Sofievo monasteries have noble patrons from their families, and the childless Ulyana has no descendants. According to Erwin's guess, the Archimandrite Ellin, while still in her right mind, brought Cordelia closer precisely because she did not hide the dagger behind her back and chose the path of honest service; Elina gave her the right to speak with her voice. When the Archimedes fell into senile dementia, Cordelia, as the second priestess, actually began to rule the Pramateric branch. She was called the bearer of the diadem and the right hand of the archimatrix. Erwin believed that she was "greeting with the san of the archimatry."

Already in 1756, Cordelia accompanied Ellina, who came with fifteen higher mothers and three dozen nuns to a celebration in Shayland at the box of the Seventeenth Gift: at the wedding party at Northwood, she grimly tried to take the Archmother to bed, and she herself, as a hustle, could not dance.

## "A Doll on the Throne"

Before the coronation of [[minerva|Minerva]] Cordelia came to her to rehearse for a blessing, because from the survivor of the mind of Ellina expected anything. Fears were confirmed: after the ceremonial prayer, the archimandrite forgot to bless the empress, and Cordelia hissed: “Your Holiness!” When laying in the Fountain of the Cathedral of Agatha the Radiant, she imperceptibly corrected the hair on the back of the head of the old woman, pushing her to nod at Erwin's proposals. ([[faunterra|Faunterra]])

On Memorial Day, Cordelia accepted Minerva's confession at the Council of Mercy and imposed penances on her: a night without sleep in thoughts of the fleetingness of life and the prayers of the Sister of Death for two weeks of drunkenness and two weeks of only pure water. Minerva then demanded a clear answer from the Church: whether the Yanmei Glove, which had hoped for her hand, was a miracle or sacrilege. Cordelia dryly refused: "The confessional is not a place for politics." Then the empress threatened to show the Glove to the flock in half an hour as a sign of Yangmei, and Cordelia, breaking down, blessed her and called the Glove a miracle, asking only not to force her to repeat it under the Lord Chancellor. In exchange for her silence, Minerva secured the deposit of the Church treasury’s surplus of 140 thousand hilts, out of 960 thousand collected by the branch that year, in the banks of Ferguson, Day, and [[konto|Konto]], to be lent to the Crown; in return, she swore “by the name of Ulyana the Sorrowful, whom we both served” to help Cordelia obtain the rank of Archmother. Cordelia understood that the new archimandrite, if she did not become one, would interpret the deal as theft or a bribe.

## The “Yanmei Hunt”

In the House of Cordelia, she represented the Church of the Foremothers: without a hitch, she raised the flag for approving the rebellion of Origin and, following the bishops of the Forefathers, wished the health of the debilitated Queen Madeline. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) At Manson's trial, when Bishop [[episkop-amessin|Amessin]] called Erwin's charges against Gallard a "disgusting heresy", Cordelia replied that the Church of the Foremothers did not consider the Lord Chancellor's speech either heretical or unsubstantiated, but only asked for confirmation from Manson himself. ([[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]]) On the day of the election, the new ruler offered to consider the first one candidate - Duke [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Sophia Jessica]] - and voted for him herself. Erwin believed her to be a sincere believer in the ideals of the Church.

## In "Humans and Gods"

### Deal with Erwin

On June 1, 1775, after news of [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]]'s death, Cordelia brought to Erwin in a wheelchair Ellina, who took the Duke for her cat Murchik. While the archmatrix was asleep, Cordelia cleverly rubbed her mouth and combed her hair, and every word of hers became the word of the chapter. After hearing the charges against Gallard's prior, she presented a letter bearing the seventeen seals of the higher mothers and the signature of Ellina, summoning Gallard to Faunterra for penance along with Ulyanina Dust and Items from Erwin's register. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]; [[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) If he refused, he would face a sacred court, the Ecumenical Council, where the voice of the supreme mother is equal to the seven voices of the prelates, so that seventeen mothers and a dozen prelates constituted an absolute majority. Erwin found the idea brilliant and asked if she had made it up; Cordelia bowed modestly.

The terms of the Chapter were three: Gallard must refuse to appear; the decision of the Council will support a military expedition that will take him under arrest; a holy war must be fought honestly - a minimum of casualties among the peaceful, no terror, looting and assassins. Erwin demanded honesty, and Cordelia, rocking the sleeping Ellina’s chair, sang to him as though it were a lullaby that she could not return the 140 thousand she had given to Minerva. They made a pact: Erwin would ensure a clean victory and money, and Cordelia, entrusting the convocation of the cathedral to the mothers of Octavia (the order of Emilia, which “somewhat” depended on her) and Alice (an agate who dreamed of seeing Erwin on the throne), will go to war with him. ([[mat-alisiya|Mother Alicia]]) She agreed almost without hesitation:

> “Unlike you, I want my place on the icons.”

The council removed Gallard from office; the only one who voted against was Bishop Amessin, head of Wilhelm's Combat Brotherhood.

### The Holy Army

Cordelia was on her way to Almera with two nuns, her own horses and notable luggage, and was well-maintained in the saddle. Before visiting [[erroubek|Arrowback]] Castle, she advised Erwin to take sixteen people — “there will be seventeen with you, this number of Foremothers” — and volunteered to go with him. On June 6–7, at Beck Castle, when the news of the Ecumenical Council arrived, Erwin cheerfully announced to her that the holy war had begun and they were "on their way to eternal glory"; Cordelia frowned: “You should think about the victory over evil, not about personal glory.” In a dispute with Earl Arrowback, who offered to wait for reinforcements, she supported the kairs: “You can’t wait when evil is happening in the world.” She arrived at the dam in the tail of the count's column, along with [[ganta-groza|Ganta Thunderstorm]] and father David, and found the end of the battle. After the victory at the dam, she approved the decision to give the property of the fallen to her heirs, and the captured Almerans announced the will of the surrender, but spoke so quietly that her words were repeated in a common voice by an assistant. Erwin called her his confessor. When Captain Gordon Sue offered to go past Gallard to Fliss and sail from there to Waymar, Cordelia rejected this idea on behalf of the Church: Gallard was a heretic more dangerous than Vittor Shayland, and she insisted on his arrest. When Erwin decided to go to [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]] to help her sister, she sternly objected that the holy war had begun for the sake of the trial of “the wicked Gallard Almera.”

When Erwin sailed north, Cordelia stayed with a battalion of northerners in Almera, wanting to personally see the arrest of the former prior. According to Erwin's plan, his supposed departure for Waymar was done by a holy mother with a battalion left without a commander, "a gentle bait": Gallard left Evergard and went to Fliss to take this prize, and meanwhile in Almera, the cairns of his father's army were imperceptibly planted; the battalion called itself "the holy army", and the rumor ran through. Together with Captain Gordon Sue and Colonel Dolph Arrowback, she insisted on extinguishing a forest fire in the Beaver Forest, although most officers wanted to go to Fliss, and participated in negotiations with the Wilhelmines, with whom the Northerners extinguished the fire. Under Fliss, she abruptly rejected the offer to execute the hostages, and then climbed the siege tower and, struggling to overcome the habit of speaking quietly, announced to the townspeople on behalf of the capitol that Gallard was suspected of heresy, theft from the temple and collusion with the [[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]]. She promised the city a year without a temple to serve if the gate opened, and threatened to take it by force if not. Then day and night prayed on the forests of the tower, and nuns from the city monasteries came for her blessing, which undermined the morale of the citizens.

Before the battle, she came down from the tower and settled a dispute about the command: Colonel Dolph Arrowback, the senior in rank, did not want to obey [[gordon-syu|Gordon Sue]], but Cordelia announced that Agatha had given her a sign and approved of the captain's tactics, and the colonel accepted his plan. At the Battle of the Trench, Cordelia saw Gallard light a blue star from the top of the staff over the army, and called it a "cheap heretical trick"; Gordon Sue covered her with a shield. Gallard, for his part, was going to grab her and judge her for "the game with Origin" and Minerva's bribe. After the battle, she, in a shroud stained with blood and soot, reproached the northerners for Gallard's passing alive: he was "a banner of heretical faith", and she needed him in chains. Her father [[otets-david|David]] told her to declare the priest a “false knight” because he called himself a soldier of Wilhelm and lost. Six hopelessly wounded X-cloaks, she held a prayer "Send me a cold light", while Erwin struck them with mercy. Gallard locked himself in Fliss, and Cordelia demanded a heretic, but was determined against the plan to send a murderer to him.

## Death

In July 1775, when the horde of [[paul|Paul]] approached Fliss, the Farway army left without a fight on the Duke's orders. Cordelia announced that she would stay with Fliss, even if everyone else left, and tried to appeal to the conscience of the soldiers, but they left. With one helper, she stood at the gate and prayed in the enemy’s way. Alanis, who was traveling with Paul, recognized her and advised her to get out of the way, but Cordelia, not surprised, created a spiral, said Alanis that she was “lost”, and calmly said: ([[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]])

> "I forbid the Horde to enter the city of Fliss."

Paul replied to Gallard standing on the wall that the sacred rank of two ladies did not allow him to "show impoliteness." Gallard then excommunicated Cordelia and her helper from the Church, declaring that Cordelia was sowing discord between the branches and, for personal gain, had started a war. Paul thanked him and broke both women with a hand movement - Cordelia across the chest, an assistant below the waist.

## Aftermath

Crown Raven [[mark-voron|Mark]] predicted to Minerva that with Cordelia's death, the capitol would "fall into chaos": Elina "counts the bugs in her head", and the other mothers would fight for her place. Erwin, who loved Cordelia more than anyone in the higher clergy, was comforted that she had died so gloriously that she would be received with honor on the Star. Gallard later claimed that the Ecumenical Council was made "at the instigation of the higher mothers, especially Alice, Octavia and the now deceased Cordelia."

## In "Fantasies"

In the Phantasies, her death (it is said that she was killed by the Ganta, that is, Paul) is called one of the reasons why both branches of the Church were left without chapters: Cordelia died fighting against the Ganta, and Gallard was on his side, and the flock did not understand on whose side the gods. The captain, according to the narrator, made the right bet as Cordelia fought for Origin. King Francis-Ilian explained that the Foremother branch "by the hands of Cordelia" had given the Empress a loan "for a monstrous sum" and therefore became poorer; Bishop Amessin reminded Minerva in 1776 that the Chapter was dissatisfied with this loan and "someone" calls it a bribe, and Erwin, arguing with his mother Alicia, recalled that he could say the word "without the Parody".
