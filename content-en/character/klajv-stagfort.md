Sir Clive Maria Bella, Lord Stagforth - [[minerva|Minerva]]'s father. In the novel [[strela-moneta-iskra|Arrow, Coin, Spark]], he dies in the first lines of the first chapter of Iskra and continues to live only in the memories of his daughter.

## Service and marriage
According to Mira, nineteen years before the action, Clive, a poor knight, served at court as captain of the scarlet guard. At the ball, he met Gemma Alessandra of the Yangmei family. She was a haughty beauty who kept herself apart and did not love people. He was kind and open, loved games and tournaments, although he did not win at them, joked and laughed a lot. He cared for Gemma according to all the rules of old ballads, and most importantly - he knew how to talk to her so that she was not bored. Gemma accepted his offer.

A week after the engagement, Clive lay down with a pulmonary disease and spent two months in a monastery hospital. At that time, there was a plot in the capital. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]) Five other captains of the Red Guard were recognized as traitors and executed. Clive saved the disease: the court found no evidence of his guilt. But suspicion left a stain on his name, and he had to leave the guard and the capital. Before leaving, he suggested to the bride to break off the engagement with the “despicable and poor exile”. Gemma replied that it was not for that reason that she had been praying for him to recover for two months so that he would now escape. The wedding was played in the North, in Stagforth, and a year later Minerva was born.

Among the papers Clive Mir later found a letter written to him a week before the plot by Captain John Corvis, one of the main conspirators. Ivy House host [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]], Corvisa's fiancée, told Meera that Clive advised her to go north to Prelesia, where she would be safer. At first she threw Mir in anger that Sir Stagforth "was an Emilian", and the guest - from the highest nobility, which means that the daughter of a knight can not be. Mira herself calls her father "Clive Stagforth's sire of the Emilia family."

## Life in Stagforth
Gemma died of consumption when her daughter was three years old. Clive raised Mira alone and taught everything he knew: literacy, mathematics, history, secular manners, dancing, horseback riding, stratemes, and even a little tactics and strategy. ([[stratemy|Stratems]]) He told his daughter about it in a confused and scanty way. He did not understand politics. Father and daughter together fed squirrels and went sailing. He listened to her confessions about his first love and talked about his mother. For the sake of jokes, they corresponded, being in different rooms of the same castle. He called his daughter “Little Mia.”

There was no prison at Stagforth. Clive did not like to punish and always awarded the rod: he announced in front of everyone who was punished and for what, and ordered to immediately execute the sentence in order to quickly end the unpleasant. Mira thought that if they had a dungeon, her father wouldn't put anyone in it anyway.

Clive was a great player in execution. Once in the Bear Fang, he called young [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] to the game and lost to him three times in a row. ([[klyk-medvedya|Bear's Fang]]) For the sake of entertainment, he learned to throw knives and from ten yards easily got a dagger into the apple. His letters and documents were kept in a special box: certificates of knighthood and rank, marriage record, old letters. After his death, Mira took the box to the capital.

## Death
On March 21, 1774, Clive's sire troop traveled to the Bear Fang, where [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] was invited by his father and daughter. In the Pre-Less, near the Northwood border, they were ambushed by at least a dozen men without coats of arms, uniformly dressed and armed with crossbows and axes. Clive died instantly: the bolt went into his high and out through his left eye, the knight fell five steps from his daughter. Most of the squad was also killed. The world was saved by the warrior Huron and the servant Lisha. After the funeral, she spent two more nights in Stagforth and then left with the Countess of Northwood.

At an audience in May, [[adrian|Adrian]] called the murder "an ordinary feudal scheming." He instructed the Earl of Shayland to find the culprits and ensure Minerva's entry into the rights to paternal flax. Later, refusing to put Sybil on trial, the emperor admitted that he was "sorry for poor sire Clive Stagforth".

### Who Killed Sir Clive?
The books never give a definitive answer to this:
- Sybil Northwood claimed that the killers' target was Mira, the heiress to the throne.
- Mira was suspected alternately by Sir [[adamar|Adamar]], [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]], and finally Sybil herself. Driven into the tower, she demanded that the countess confess to the substitution and “the murder of her father.”
- Mira herself noticed the strangeness: there were only two girls in the squad, she and Lisha, and both survived, although they were easy targets.
- In A Doll on the Throne, Mir recalls that his father was killed by "the mercenaries of the Duke of Almer."
- After her arrest in October 1774, the countess was charged with the murder of Sir Clive and four of his servants. The murder was not proved, and she was convicted only for cheating the emperor.
- In The Shadow of the Great Tree of the World, Navi is told by Navi that Sybil is responsible for the deaths of at least ten people and one of them was her father. ([[navi|Navi]]) In Humans and Gods, when she met the imprisoned Countess, she wanted to say "you killed your father", and later in an unsent letter, she still wrote this.

## Father's instructions
Mira constantly remembers his words, and in her eyes they become life rules:
> “Love those who are dear to you. Love every minute, don't waste time. Someday a person will leave, and it will happen suddenly.”
> “Never swallow insults. You can forgive me if you want. But to endure and to conceal in oneself is not.”
> “A man is not happy around. If the gods have given something generously, there will be a hole in something else.”

On suspicious coincidences, he said: “Where I shot from the bow, by pure chance there was a squirrel.” He also taught the rule with execution: if all the exits from the situation are bad, break the situation itself.
