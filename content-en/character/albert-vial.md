## Biography
Albert Vial served as Minister of Finance under the old emperor Telurian, who greatly appreciated him: everything that the minister undertook was done slowly, but only qualitatively. The impetuous [[adrian|Adrian]] repeatedly clashed with him, but did not dare to remove him from office. Under Viala, the imperial budget was reduced every year to a surplus, which was regularly returned to the treasury, and Adrian rightly believed that with the replacement of the minister, the surplus would disappear.

### In "Only One Star"
In the autumn of 1774, after the arrest of the Northwoods, the emperor ordered Minister Viahl to revise the war budget and not to count on the revenues of the counties of Northwood and Shayland, but only on revenues from the Lands of the Crown, Almera and Hope. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]])

### "A Doll on the Throne"
In December 1774, when the northerners of [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] took the capital, Vial left it. In a letter to the Lord Chancellor, [[minerva|Minerva]] listed the fugitive officials: the Minister of Finance, the Treasurer, the Ministers of Trade and Crafts, and the First Deputy Minister of Taxes. The protection agents took Vial off the ship at Grace, from where he was about to sail south. According to the staff, it took him a good week. He, along with former Deputy Minister of Taxes Frank Morlin-Mei, were found guilty of ravaging the treasury and imprisoned in the Holy See. ([[prestolnaya-tsitadel|Imperial Citadel]])

On the morning of February 1775, Minerva went down to the Citadel. Prisoners, left behind and gags, prepared to interrogate army interrogators General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis]]. Vial firmly reiterated that they were innocent, did not hide, and did not change names, and Morlin-May explained that the tax collection for December was detained by the Minister of Taxes Dreyfus Bourne. ([[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]]) Minerva replied that she knew about their innocence. She ordered them to be untied and asked another to teach her everything they would tell their son if he wanted to master their craft.

The prisoners were transferred to comfortable cells with desks, bookshelves and a view of Hanai. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) Minerva refected and teased with them and was tormented by guilt in front of her teachers. She understood that the money almost certainly settled at Bourne, but the Lord Chancellor would want to find another scapegoat, and it is much more difficult to prove innocence than guilt. The Citadel's "mentors" led her through the five steps of Pramaterie Eleonora, the patroness of the dealers. For the first lesson, "to determine the added value", Mira for two sleepless nights dismantled the reigns of all fifty-three emperors of the Brilliant Dynasty. With her own added value, she semi-sarcastically called a beautiful name, and Vial quite seriously agreed with this: the name of Minerva, Who Brings Peace, is associated with the beauty of peaceful life in the people. When Morlin-May timidly asked if they would be released soon, Vial was outraged. The haste, he said, is not to the Empress's face, and his honour demands a full justification.
> “I’m willing to spend a year in jail for that.”

He recalled that eight generations of his ancestors owned a merchant fleet and none received accusations of uncleanness. Maureen-May also refused a pardon.

Mentors helped Minerva in other matters. They advised to generously pay for the military expedition of Origin against the Shavans, promising to find funds for it, and made calculations for tax reform. In the spring, after negotiations with the Steppe Fire, the empress came to the Citadel and offered them freedom. Vial objected that they could not leave without a court order. Minerva then showed them Bourne's confession, snatched from the schooner Fast, and offered Morlin-Mei the vacated post of Minister of Taxes. Until midnight, former prisoners discussed with her the details of the reform.

At a secret gathering of associates, Minerva thanked Vial, Morlin-Mei, and banker Conto for the spring fair, the introduction of paper appropriations, and tax reform. ([[konto|Konto]]) Suspicing that her success was being driven by a hidden player, she checked everyone's motives. Viala she considered beyond suspicion: Sophie, a native of the Southern Way, he hated the Duke of Origin, who conquered his native land, and left the post out of contempt, not out of fear. In addition, both financiers left the capital just before her arrival and could not know in advance what she would need. While Mira was talking about the duke, Vial's lips crooked with contempt.

### The “Yanmei Hunt”
In the spring of 1775, after Minerva had foiled the massacre at the Holy Field, Erwin, after listing Minerva's approximates (kair called them "sighs" and added Vial and Morlin-Mei's mentors), ordered everyone to be brought to him. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) Playing the role of a scoundrel, he announced summoned that they would go "travel to distant lands" and find out the destination already on the high seas. Vial, who portrayed infallibility, asked only: “Where, my lord?” Only [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]] and [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] were released, and ministers were told to leave the palace. Already in this scene, Vial is named Minister of Finance: after his release, he apparently returned to his previous post. What ended the expulsion is not directly stated, but in the summer of 1775 Vial still served as Minister of Finance in the capital.

### The Shadow of the Great Tree
When Adrian returned to [[faunterra|Faunterra]] in the summer of 1775, Vial stayed in the city and came to see him. The joker [[menson|Manson]] ahead of him at the door and drove the minister from his chair. Vial bowed low and congratulated the lord on the wedding, and Adrian thanked him for not running away. The minister filed a suspiciously thin folder and reported that Minerva and Treasurer [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] had taken the entire treasury out of the capital the day before yesterday: forty-four percent by bank transfers, the rest in cash. He could not interfere, because the treasury was led by Origin, and there was a whole battalion with him. To the anger of Adrian Vial timidly objected that from the point of view of the law nothing was stolen: the transportation was carried out by order of Minerva, signed under her authority. He also noted that under her, budget revenue had risen by sixteen percent compared with the previous year. The treasury is in excellent condition, just not here. He also added that the Minister of Taxes Morlin-May fled with Minerva, taking books, seals and senior officials, so that there can be no immediate receipts.

## Personality and Appearance
Vial was born in the midst of luxury, never needed money or the highest mercy and treated public service as a matter of pride: in his family all were rich, and he became a minister alone. His appearance breathed with beauties: round "Sophian" cheeks, bare sideburns, eyebrows so thick that they seemed no longer gloomy, but eccentric. Minerva remembered his loose, soft white hand, “as if created to comfort a child.” He was in no hurry: the fees for lunch in the cell took him up to half an hour, and the guards waited. He spoke slowly and significantly and, according to the narrator, announced the served tea in such a tone as if he announced the descent of the flagship.
