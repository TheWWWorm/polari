## Who He Is

Darius Hightower is the emissary of the church court and the "eye of the archimatry". The Church is ruled by a chapter of seventeen high priests, where the path is closed to a man; after the chapter, the most powerful organ is the ecclesiastical court, the right to execute and pardon priests of any rank, except for the highest mothers themselves - "a sword capable of demolishing the head of anyone, even the bishop or the abbot." The edge of this sword and serves Hightower. This is the highest post available to a man in the Pramaterina branch.

The Archpriest constantly praises his emissary - resolute, quick, executive and ruthless to sin. However, the priests of the Chapter do not like that he is a man. Exactly one year later, on the following days, the new composition of the court was to be approved, and a group of mothers pinned in his place the Agathan abbess from Aleridan, whose only advantage is sex. Therefore, the archimandrite told him to marry and have children: a married man is “more understandable than a single man”, and then no one in the capital would vote against him. He himself, as the abbess later guessed, wanted to introduce into the chapter “a faithful and intelligent woman” — his protege.

## In "Confidential"

### Arrival

The story takes place in an unnamed provincial town, in the modest monastery of Sophia the Wonderworker (sixteen nuns and twenty-two novices), on the eve of Sophia days; the year is not specified. The author’s foreword calls it a story about love, “a little about intrigue inside the Pramatery Church” and “about the past of one of the main cycle characters.”

The reason for the arrival was theft: the young man [[devid-atterton|David Atterton]] brought out of the temple a small Sacred Object of the monastery, which, according to legend, connects the hearts of lovers, if you look through it to the Star and call the name of your beloved. The Abbess only reported the case to the Chapter, and it struck her that for the sake of such a small matter the emissary himself appeared in the province. Hightower showed her contempt from the first minute: he ridiculed her jewelry, refused the best room (he lived in a friend’s apartment), rejected the blessing, looked through financial books and threw that her palms were “oiled with glue, to which silver sticks.” After hearing the story of the theft, he immediately decided: “Hang”.

### Bride

In fact, the emissary was looking for a bride - Rose Wingram, the daughter of her longtime friend, the winemaker Baron Wingram. He examined her without desire and interest, asking only about what could affect pregnancy and childbirth: he banned wine, ordered her to walk “to develop hips” and ride in a carriage. When Rose honestly admitted that she did not love him, he replied that he himself does not like her: for passions there are altessa, and feeling is a risk.

> “If I love my wife, through her enemies will be able to manipulate me.”

He saw her protest as proof of disinterestedness. Rose, on the other hand, loved David and asked the abbess to become her confidante under the "Miriam right" - the girl's right to choose a man for the first night; in fact, she wanted to penetrate the camera to David and give him the keys. The abbess recorded the petition, but when she saw the name of the prisoner, she forbade Rose to enter it.

### The requirement of confidentiality

Hightower noticed how cleverly the abbess manages the chaos of festive weddings, and, imbued with respect, hinted that he could take her to the capital as an assistant - that is, lead her to the chapter. He paid eight ephemera for Rose to receive the subject's blessing at the wedding ("The gods will bind her to me, and she will not dare to betray"), and then demanded to say whether the bride had used Miriam's right. The secret of confidentiality is equal to the secret of confession, and the abbess made a condition: let the emissary demand information in writing. He deduced in the book of confidence, "I demand information about the privacy of Rose Wingram," on the back of the sheet where Rose's rejected petition was recorded. The abbess answered the truth: Rose is clean. She also advised to justify David, so that the bride could see in the groom and severity, and mercy, and Hightower approved of the "move of her thoughts."

### Court

On the eve of the trial, Sister Lucille stated instead of Rose that the maiden did not wish to marry and would prefer to serve Pramateri Sophie; the abbess cunningly locked Rose in the basement, and the emissary, warned of the risk, ordered to keep the girl there until the wedding, fearing that she would hide in the cave monastery of Ulyana, from where the novice could not be legally extracted. He led the trial in the main nave, hastily and without listening to the witnesses. Rose broke out of the basement, broke into the temple, and her seminarian friend Colin Baker revealed that David had carried the Item to the well because it caught fire; when the abbess opened the chest, the Item burst into flames in front of everyone. This gave David an excuse, but the young man refused to lie at the altar and admitted that he took the Subject for love, calling the name Rose Wingram. Hightower understood everything and sentenced him to be hanged "at the first sound of the evening song."

### Blackmail

The emissary came to the abbess to find out who had arranged the "fraud with the Object", and explained the sentence as follows: David was only a coward, once he did not dare to lie, and "the poor son took a nap on the Baron's daughter", which violates the first commandment. Then the abbess showed him the book: the record gave the impression that Rose had used Miriam's right, and on the back was the demand of the emissary. According to her, the chapter would see that he forced her to violate the secret of the confession, found out the name of the opponent and dealt with him, abusing power, and Rose’s child would be suspected of being from a executed thief. She demanded David's acquittal and canceled the wedding, refusing to give the book to anyone other than the archimandrite.

Hightower, furious, promised to cancel the wedding and justify the “small puppy” — but only so that the book would lose its power — and vowed to destroy the abbess:

> "I'm so deep you can't see God's light in your life."

He left, contemptuously giving her the title of "holy mother", which he had previously refused.

## Personality and Appearance

Skinny ascetic, "curved from head to toe": black cassock, fallen cheeks, bony fingers, a veiny neck, a heavy forehead over the cheeks of the eyes, thin lips, a beard with a wedge. He despises luxury, jewelry and profit, does not appreciate anything that is given for nothing. Picky to trifles (in a day I found thirteen flaws in the monastery), determined, quick and merciless to sin; considers feelings of weakness and risk, people are humiliated. He is able to appreciate someone else's mind and even praise him - but only so far it is beneficial to him. He is accompanied by black swordsmen.

## Relationship to the main cycle

Which of the characters of the main cycle is meant in the foreword is not directly stated. Colonel Hightower of Adrian's entourage is mentioned in The Only One Star, who was awarded a helmet with a gold pen and sword engraving and, together with other commanders, persuaded the lord to forget about the train; his name is not named, and no connection with the emissary has been established.

More notable is the abbess' cat name Murchik, and in the last scene she promises him that he will still "become the most well-fed cat in the capital." Surviving from the mind of the archimandrite Ellin in “Humans and Gods” also calls his cat Murchik (and even confuses Erwin with him). ([[ellina|Ellina]]) Perhaps the heroine of “Confident” is a young Ellina, but this is only a guess; if so, the events of the story take place long before 1756, when Ellina was already an archimper.
