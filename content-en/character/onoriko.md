## Who He Is

Onoriko-Meysor, or simply Rico, called himself an "architect of happiness", and enemies - a pimp. He picked up brides and violass rich people [[lajem|Laem]]: one alchemist found altessa, the other married a daughter and those, according to him, “fit into the scientific community.” ([[alter|Alter and Altessa]]) He wore a black moustache and a white hat, was a charming talker, showered with Shimmerian proverbs ("Even a mountain lion can refuse to hunt if the deer is too much a pimple") and got stuck in debt. His wife, white-blooded Vanessa-Lilit, was taller than his kind. The house in Laem belonged to her since she was a virgin, and Rico could not mortgage it. Rico's main rival in the craft was Timeret's pimp.

## "The Skyship and the Girl" (August 1774)

In Laem Square, where the fireman-inventor [[gortenzij|Hortensius]] vainly urged the townspeople to climb on his "cursed" balloon, Rico noticed a seemingly wealthy stranger without a marriage bracelet: the merchant "Horam Pauline Robert of the Lands of the Crown". Actually, it was [[kharmon|Harmon]]. Rico took him to Valerion's expensive teahouse, lectured him on how a man without a woman in Shimmery was like a knight without a horse, and then brought him to dinner. Vanessa played a model hostess, but after the guest left, she attacked her husband: Horam, in her opinion, is poor, and they themselves barely make ends meet. Rico himself dreamed of buying althessa shavanka [[niza|Niza]], which he saw on the ship of the storm Shavan. Vanessa wouldn't let me.

Rico tried to borrow money from friends and moneylenders. He was given at best a gloria or agate, and for trinkets and mechanical watches offered two deer. Then he conceived a plan: Horam buys an altessa more expensive, Rico receives a tithe from the seller and with this money buys Nisa, and then searches for Horam a rich white-blooded bride. At the last auction in the tea room Exila Harmon first left, saying that to sell women "against his nature." After learning from Rico that an unsold shrewd Niza would be thrown overboard, he returned and bought it for five ephemera. The tenth of Rico was four Greeks. Vanessa decided that her husband brought a miser to the house, who bought "the cheapest whore in the whole kingdom." In a quarrel, Rico hotly contrived that they would buy the celestial ship Hortense, fly to the capital and sell it to the emperor. Vanessa kicked him out of the house: “Pay off your debts. I’ll put you back on the doorstep.” Three nights later, Rico, an unkempt and beggar, asked Horam for a loan of twenty, then ten ephes and was refused. But the story of the heavenly ship lit the eyes of Niza, and Harmon took him seriously: “Maybe you will not reach. But we can try.”

## In Melison

Harmon bought the ball and settled with Niza and Hortense in an old estate near Melison in the Lion Mountains. Rico was carrying goods from Laem: silk and cotton, ropes, acid and alchemical utensils to produce "light gas". Local mountaineers despised Laemians, but Rico forgave: a poor Laemian, expelled from the house by his own wife, "not at all that rich." Almost all of Rico's salary was put in a trunk for Vanessa and spent more on trips than it should have been. Harmon guessed that he was giving the difference to his family and creditors. He became bored with Harmon: “words say that water pours.” He sucked on his wife and only occasionally threw “hopeless dog looks” at Niza. When Harmon asked for advice, Rico offered to throw a ruinous ball, and then hire a ship to Litland and buy trophies from the victorious Shavans, including captured women: to trade, in his words, "not people, but happiness." Harmon refused. When the ball was launched, Rico volunteered to climb the turret and removed the loop that held the shell off the hook.

In March 1775, after a quarrel with the Second of the Five, Harmon gave Rico a hundred ephesas and sent him to his wife. Rico had to approve Vanessa with gifts and through her family, the cousin of her older sister's husband, achieve a reception at Pentago from the Second of the Five. As soon as the word “one hundred” sounded, Rico’s eyes were “sparkled with inspiration.” Meanwhile, [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] was looking for the Sphere thief. Through Timerett, a cab driver and Laem merchants, he learned that Horam was courting Rico. Buckley ambushed Vanessa's house. On the holiday of Spring Dawn, Rico came to his wife with gifts, and he was captured at the doorstep.

## Death

Buckley's men brought connected Rico, Vanessa, and the children to the Melison estate. Buckley, who called himself simply Moe, checked the hired killer Rodrigo, the "boy in the hat", and demanded to prove that he would not stop "no moral things." For Vanessa and children, Rodrigo asked for a hundred ephemera: white-blooded noblewoman - "dear game". Then Buckley asked how much is the architect of happiness. They traded in fifteen ephesses, and Rodrigo slit Rico's throat with one stroke in front of his wife and children. Harmon was forced to give all the money. Vanessa and the children and Harmon were thrown without water into the basement under a locked barn, where they spent almost two days. They were saved by Nisa. ([[niza|Niza]]) In the morning, the servants found the “tortured corpse of Onoriko” and in horror spread a rumor through the city that Niza had eaten the hearts of the owners and turned into a mountain cat. The townspeople with the Constable not without difficulty buried Rico with abundant prayers.

## Legacy

In the basement, Vanessa threw it to Harmon: “You dragged Rico into your mud! He was killed because of you!” He agreed. Harmon considered him one of the "clean souls" who died because of him, and recalled a "funny and kind guy" who was "cut throat in front of his wife and children." In the summer of 1775 he sent the widow a bill for four hundred ephes. The second of the Five at the first meeting reminded Harmon Onoriko: the same swift, ardent and gambling southerner, only "ten times more rigid."

## In "Fantasies"

In "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]" describing a possible future, the story "Amicable" takes place in Laem in August 1776. Widowed a year earlier, Vanessa-Lilit became famous for her tragedy: half of Laem sympathized with her, the other stuffed in the groom. She continued her husband’s work. The story notes that Onoriko-Meysor was a mediocre pimp, and Vanessa achieved much more: with her help, five prominent nobles married, and two dozen got althes. She herself recalled that she did not want to marry a man below her birth and only then learned “how caring and passionate Onoriko will be.” Harmon Paula, who became the Minister of Aeronautics, sent the widow money, which, according to Sheriff Halintor, helped the family to survive the loss of the breadwinner. Vanessa's new lover was Halintor himself. The widow helped Princess Demeter to break off her engagement to [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]]: she informed her that the nephew of Captain Hyder, Leed Barney, would be sold at auction, and sent for the boy as an intermediary in a white cloak. ([[hajder-lid|Hyder Leed]]) Then she brought Barney to the palace.
