## Biography
Leyla Agnes Francesca, Lady Talmir, was born and raised in the capital. She is the daughter of a weak baronial family, according to Pramati, a deer. In her youth, she was a court lady and, according to an old servant, so beautiful that all the neighbors looked at her. Leila was the bride of the guard captain Sir John Mireille Sophia, Lord Corvis, a member of the Shutow Conspiracy, and lived in a house in Linden Square at the Church of Courage. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]) On 19 October 1755, on the eve of his departure for Aleridan at the behest of Duke [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]], Corvis wrote to Sir Clive Stagforth, the father of [[minerva|Mira]], and asked that he take care of the "most dear" person to him if he himself died. ([[klajv-stagfort|Clive Stagfort]]) He swore Layla wasn't involved in his affairs. Soon the captain died under torture.

Clive came to get Layla and asked her to go north to Shayland, where he promised to find her shelter and protection. She argued with him and replied that she was not a rat to hide, and did not want to rot on the outskirts. Neighbors considered her a witch who knocked the groom down for "black deeds", and set fire to her house, but the hostess was no longer there. Leila took refuge in the post town of Pale Meadow seven miles west of [[faunterra|Faunterra]]. There, under the name of Greta Eloise, she kept the poor living yard “House with Ivy”, converted from her own house for twenty years. Rejected by her family, she raised her daughter and spent years saving two gold a month for her boarding school. Over time, she stopped believing that she would ever make the right amount of money and that any money “could remake her daughter.”

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"
On 9 June 1774, Mira found a letter from Captain Corvis to her father and then sought Layla along with Secretary [[itan|Ethan]]. The house of the Church of Courage was burned down, and the owner of the neighboring tavern told about the “witch” who lived there with his fiancé. In the almshouse of St. Teresa, the chatterer Martin Bondar posed as her servant and spread fables. A real servant of Ashby caught up with them on the street. He told how a knight from the North persuaded the mistress to run, and suggested where to look for her: in the Pale Meadow.

On June 20, 1774, Mira traveled from the capital to Pale Meadow and, along with Sir Vanden, found the House with Ivy. Skinny mistress, early old, with a knot of hair on the back of the head and huge eyes, at first denied everything and drove away the guest. Mira noticed that the maid who hugged the guests was the daughter of the hostess. When Mira recalled that Layla's fiancé died on the torture table, and the culprit is bathed in luxury, the hostess slammed the shutters. Then Mira threw a copper into her crack "for invaluable help." An enraged Leyla called it "small rubbish" and accused it of lying: Sir Stagforth was an Emilian, and the guest was clearly from the highest nobility. Mira revealed her full name and told her about her mother. Leila remembered that Clive had talked about the engagement, and told the main thing. A month before his death, Corvis met with Aiden and learned a secret of the duke - or maybe they learned it together - from those "for whom they kill without hesitation." How, Leila didn't know. The only thing Corvis told her was that the Duke was “hiding for his daughter.” Leila refused to even stutter about the money, but asked to write to her if Aidan fell.
> “Any lord is capable of murder. And you will. Such a breed.”

These words of peace were remembered more than once. In a conversation with Aiden Almera herself, she, posing as "Gloria", used Layla's name for a bluff. She stated that Leila passed defamatory information to the Duke Labelins and Gracends, and she herself recently died from the blue sea.

## "A Doll on the Throne"
### The First Freylin
In February 1775, after a humiliating scandal in the theater, the Empress Minerva without a retinue, with only two guards, came early in the morning to the “House with Ivy”. The hostess in the moth-eaten twenty-year-old fur coat of blackberries just drank yesterday’s tea “burdu” instead of coffee. Mira drank the same burda and offered her the place of her lady, demanding an answer immediately, without hesitation. Leila warned that she had long ceased to be a lady: she drinks bitter, smells tobacco, is not ashamed of strong words, despises mannerism and intrigue and hates the lords of the [[velikie-doma|Great Houses]].
> “That’s exactly how I need it.”

Mira explained that she wants to learn what Leila knows: to lose her family, home, loved one and name and still survive, preserving her soul. In the carriage, Leila first advised her to surrender to the Lord Chancellor, because "the cemeteries are full of people who believed in their own strength." Seeing her stubbornness, she admitted that Mira would perhaps be able to master finance, as she had previously mastered conspiracies. Leyla advised to respond to [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]]’s letter with a request because the male nobleman “mumbles” when the girl asks for something. From this council came Minerva's idea of asking Origin for agents to find fugitive officials.

The court met the "life-beaten Yelenovka" with ridicule, and Erwin's vassals considered her appointment a spit in the face of the Northern Princess, whom they preferred "it scared". ([[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]]) When Iona asked about Leila’s past, she quipped about the “nuances of hotel business.” Iona then confessed to her brother that the lady almost managed to piss her off. Erwin, however, was the only one to approve of Minerva's choice and to compare Layla to Kair [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]]: she also chooses who to respect. Leila had already calculated and ferociously drove away the three lackeys Erwin had bought. He predicted that when she was strong, half of the yard would be in front of her.

### Counsellor
Leila solved dozens of tasks for the Empress. The main thing was to arrange secret meetings: in the official schedule, she was looking for excuses to bring Minerva with the right people. For two days of searching for money, she organized meetings with everyone who could at least theoretically help. At the tasting in the winemakers' guild, Leila kicked the wine-loving mistress under the table, so that a shoe flew off, and Mira came to her senses just before the bankers' speech. At her request, Leila dropped a fan so that the banker Conto, returning him, could imperceptibly sit down to the empress in a carriage. ([[konto|Konto]]) Layla's advice to "never drink from grief" Mira remembered:
> “Drink only on joy, then there will be more joy.”

Leila did not understand the war ("my knowledge is reduced to the tactics of placing glasses on tables"), but advised to pit two scoundrels, Origin and Shavans. When Minerva returned from dangerous negotiations with the Steppe Fire, the maid of honor chastised her for her recklessness, and then hugged her like a younger sister. Erwin told Minerva he didn’t know two things about her, and one of them was where she found Leila Talmir.

Labelin envoy [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]] offered Minerva a deal. Erwin, who opposed the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]], was to be disgraced by the massacre of the peasants, and Minerva had already issued Buckley a deposit. She then asked Leila what to do. She deliberately asked what embarrassed her, until the empress cried out that the price was twenty thousand peasant lives. Then Layla clapped her hands three times: she hoped to hear exactly that. She advised to warn the Lord Chancellor. Mira went to the Holy Field herself instead of writing. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]])

At a meeting of associates Minerva called Leyla and Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] those who did not allow her to fall without strength, and the only ones whose motives did not cause her doubts. Leila and the captain were also present at the exposure of General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]], and when Minerva escorted the guests, Leila announced: “You can’t get rid of me, Your Majesty.”

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
In the spring of 1775, Erwin, playing the villain, gathered the Minerva’s “tails” to send them by sea. Leila was blazing with hatred, said she had a daughter, and threw to the Duke that his mother was from the Dukes of Almera, and that sounded like the worst insult. Erwin immediately "remembered" that Layla and Shatterhand were present at the Lady's memorable conversation with Smile, and released them both. Minerva understood this hint and later thanked Erwin for leaving them at large. She sent Leila to check the paycheck archives for traces of "Object X."

One morning, Leila herself turned to Erwin, a "malicious thunderbolt" who, in his opinion, would rather ask for help from [[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]]. She took the word of silence from him and brought it to the bedroom, where Minerva, drunk since the evening, stood on all fours above the pelvis, and two hours later the Chamber opened. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) Erwin ordered her to sober up and take her to [[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]], and Leyla recalled that the mistress is supposed to accompany the retinue when visiting the queen. Later, at a crucial meeting of the House, Erwin realized that he did not suspect a trick either in this drunkenness of Minerva or in her other actions.

## “The ones we forgive.”
In the interlude "[[te-kogo-my-proshchaem|Those We Forgive]]", in May 1775, on the day of Minerva's return from negotiations with the Steppe Fire, Leila, drinking for courage, demanded from Minerva five thousand Hilt and a small linen presented on behalf of the Crown, and quickly and without explanation. The conversation eavesdropped through Ronald Wigham's fireplace. When Minerva asked if she was being blackmailed and if she was looking for where to pay more, Leila snapped at the cry: “You will grow up and find out how it happens!” Minerva refused her land and money.

While the mistress and Erwin were on a campaign against the Snowdrops, Leila made an "inspection" of the palace cellars with the yard minister and warehouse keeper Bertie Crane. With her keys, she opened a locked box of kairs and found in it the body of Znacharka, a servant of the Puppeteer, who was killed in April. ([[znakharka|The Healer]]) Having sent the satellites, she was alone with the deceased for three minutes. On the day of Smile's exposure, Leila showed Minerva a syringe with a thick dark liquid from the corpse's veins. She overheard Minerva's conversation with Erwin that the Objects may be obeying people with "special blood," and suggested testing this theory: pour blood on a faithful person or pit the Northerners with someone else's. Minerva, who suffered from anemia and survived the bloodletting of [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]], almost fainted, guessed about eavesdropping and decided that Layla was bargaining for the land. Freilina replied:
> “Your Majesty, there is something in the world besides intrigues and conspiracies. It happens that a person needs help.”

Shatterhand asked to forgive Layla, but Minerva said she was too tired to forgive. However, later, "by the grace of the Lord," Layla got hers. Wigham overheard her talking to her pregnant daughter, a rude commoner, whom the maid of honor had secretly brought to the palace. Layla gave her the address of a matchmaker, five thousand hilt to a groom who would turn a blind eye to her position, and a gift to the flax just given: two villages and eight hundred acres on the bank of the Hanai. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) She wanted if not a daughter, then at least the granddaughter became a noblewoman. The daughter replied that she herself was born out of wedlock. Leila slapped her, said she did not intend to forgive, and promised to see her again only five years later, when it was time to give her granddaughter to boarding.

Minerva jokingly thanked her for her long-standing eavesdropping: she has since had a map of the auditory vents. Leila noted that "the temporary will last longer than you think", announced that the throne belongs to Minerva by right, but advised not to steal the hands of the murder of Origin. Through a bribed assistant birder, she recognized the addressees of the palace letters. Pregnant [[valeri-grejsend|Valerie]] she besiege the question, as she ordered a baby dress, not knowing the sex of the child. When [[menson|Manson]] was disgraced at Minerva's court, Leyla also got: she blushed "like a girl" and asked to banish the joke. She said, “The Lord has done a good deed. You, my lord, do not understand this.” Manson, in response, called Minerva's advisers "an old innkeeper, a ruby soldier, and a horse racer." Leyla usually spoke in favor of conscience, duty to the state and the strength of the throne. To the alarm about the brigade, she advised to leave it to Origina: let her "sweat", this is "unbearable care".

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
Leila woke Minerva, who was asleep in the carriage between countless cases. She kept records of the papers that the empress signed in the evenings in the hops without looking. According to this record, for example, there was a forgotten order to move Davey's regiment north. Her servants, together with the protection agents, recorded the seditious whispers of the court, which split after the return of Adrian. In the summer of 1775, when Faunterra was caught on both sides by Adrian, coming from the north, and the horde of Johan Reis, coming from the south, Leyla was the first to offer to give up the empty capital: let the "men" playing war face their foreheads there. Minerva left the capital in August, and Leila followed. The Minister of the Court remained in Faunterra, and in Patrick's Chapel the Lady took care of the comforts of Leyla and Inge Price: bypassing the whole town, the lady obtained for her an entire floor in the lady-burgomaster's house. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) On Mira's orders, she gave [[navi|Navi]] a sleeping pill. When Adrian's regiments approached at the end of August and Minerva sent Robert off with the treasury, Layla looked unambiguously towards Hanai, still open to flight. At night alarm, she was the first to find herself next to the mistress, and Mira told her to drag a sleepy Navi to the ship with at least a fiber. In October 1775, Leila was already at Minerva in the First Winter.

In the First Winter, Leila unexpectedly advised Minerva to think about marrying Erwin. Then the Origins believed it, and now they are weak, and they can be taken “under the heel”. Amber, having created a secretariat, entrusted her with the recruitment of courtiers. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) Since the newcomers had no experience, the department also became a court school, and Leila was its main mentor, to whom Robert Origin sent and mobilized teachers of Elena-u-Lake boarding school. ([[pansion-eleny-u-ozera|Elena-by-the-Lake Boarding School]]) On the morning after the Battle of the First Winter, Minerva appointed her interim minister of the court and commissioned her to turn the entire court into a "service of mercy" for the incinerators. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]) Leila herself suggested adding to the tasks the analysis of blockages. When Mira rushed to the monastery to the wounded Navi, she left Leyla in her place.

## In "Fantasies"
In [[fantazii|Fantasies]], which the author calls possible future options, Leila is still the first lady of the family. After the Battle of the First Winter, together with Amber, she represented the Minerva side in negotiations with the Origins and [[magda-labelin|Magda Labelin]]. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) They decided that Minerva would remain a temporary mistress until the election, and Adrian recognized himself as mayor of Faunterra.

In the summer of 1776, Leila became the "leader of black souls" who hated the upcoming twentieth anniversary of Minerva. She considered it a bad omen if a pretender to the throne "eats from the hand of the mother of another pretender." Minerva joked, the ministers were overwhelmed with work, and the officers of the guard did not believe that the mistress could be mistaken. The "Old Assassin", who could influence her, left for Faunterra in the winter. Then Leila called Amber for a walk, as if to choose a gift. She explained to him that the Origins had given the orphan maternal care, brother and sister, and family holidays and thus made Minerva a hostage, and they only need her votes in the election. Amber deliberately made a fool of himself to hide how deeply he was dedicated to Minerva's plans. Layla prepared her gift long before: a handkerchief embroidered with her own hands. It was the only home-made gift.

On the day of the holiday, Leila seized the Glove from Minerva in advance so that she would not fly to the castle with a sparrow, and forbade her to spend the night in the castle. The next morning, she dressed the mistress herself, not allowing Iona to do this, and wrapped the short Minerva in high-heeled shoes: “It’s time for your majesty to grow above himself.” At the party, Leila tried to contain her fervor. Minerva called for the help of the guards, and Captain [[uitmor|Whitmore]] began to drink frailin toasts "to the bottom". Before the fight, Leila took Minerva to change into an evening dress. “Lady Leila, you are poisoning my holiday,” said the lady.

Leila didn't know the extent of the reproaches. She jealously noticed that dressing the lady was a privilege of the lady, not Iona, and suspected that the Origins were picking up Minerva dresses and advice. She brought a bunch of reports from her spies about Erwin's meetings with Alicia's mother and the treasurer and about the kair night in the library. Even before Minerva, she learned that by her birthday, the ambassadors of Ganta Cort would arrive, and advised not to let the Duke alone. To do this, she got “ears” in the city and in the secretariat. Minerva replied that the Duke was honest with her, and refused money to expand the spy network. Leila replied that she would try to meet the current budget. In the affairs of the heart Minerva addressed not to her, but to Amber: Leyla is a “fang in a skirt” and would not forgive her frivolity.

## Personality and Appearance
Sharp, cynical, superstitious and perceptive, Leyla in public did not hide contempt for court mannerism and for the feudal lords. She could not lie: there was “too much nobility” in her. She was feared at court by everyone except the kairs, and she herself, according to Wigam's observation, was afraid of only one Origin. In the Pale Meadow, she looked older than her forty years. Her hands Mira described as thin fingers of almost perfect beauty, with noble wrinkles from the hardships experienced. Despite all the harshness, Leila was unconditionally devoted to Minerva and, as the Empress herself became convinced, free from any suspicion.
