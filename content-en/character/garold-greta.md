## Overview
Harold Greta Lucia of the Inesa family is a knight, castellan of [[uejmar|Waymar]] Castle and commander of its garrison, vassal of Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]. He appeared in many scenes in the castle throughout the four novels, but always remained a figure of the background: respectable, thorough and a little boring servant.

## Appearance and Personality
[[minerva|Minerva]], who lived in Waymar in the fall of 1774, described his voice as catish: bassy, purring, measured. Castelian and outwardly resembled a cat: a wide face, round eyes, gray moustache. His real name seemed too faceless to Mira and all the time flew out of her memory, so she called him Sir Kotofey or Sir Murmur. Iona saw him on the night of the coup as a mustache, round-faced, self-satisfied, cat-like. Harold was short-sighted and brought the paper to the very eyes.

He liked to talk at the table about military science and tell long instructive stories. The world seemed unbearably boring. Baron [[dorkaster|Dorcaster]]'s daughters imitated him: "Uh-uh... uh... mmm-daaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa

## Biography
### Letter to Harmon
On March 2, 1774, Harold wrote from Waymar Castle to the merchant Harmon Paule, who had long been buying goods. ([[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]) Suzeren wanted to quickly and quietly sell “one product” and asked for advice from the castellan. Harold named Harmon, his longtime supplier, and the Earl wished to meet with the merchant. Harmon knew Harold well and knew how to negotiate with him, and with the Count himself had not previously dealt with. Later, during interrogation in captivity, Harmon confirmed that the count appealed to him on the advice of the castellan. When the merchant left the castle, Harold said goodbye to him so simply that Harmon realized that the castellans did not know that the Bright Sphere was for sale (see Sale of the Bright Sphere). ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]; [[lzhe-sfera]])

### Garrison and count's table
In the autumn of 1774, Harold met the returning count with a ceremonial construction: a hundred infantry in chain mail stood five lines, and the arrows saluted from the walls. Then he announced that the festive meal was ready. At the count's table, the castellans sat with their wife. Mira found them to be an extreme beneficent couple, so powerful and powerful that she was lulled into a dream from a single glance at them. The wife of the castellan threw away the baronial daughters when they began to bleed.

Once at lunch, Harold relayed news of the war received through the Count's bank point in [[labelin|Labelin]]. In seven weeks, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] moved a hundred miles into the South Road and took twenty castles and twelve cities. ([[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]) Harold drew a knife pen on a tablecloth triangle from [[uindli|Windley]], [[dojl|Doyle]], and White Stone, aimed at the Labelin, and reported that Labelin had collected fifty thousand against eighteen thousand northerners. Mira, investigating the disappearance of Lindsay's servant, briefly included the castellan in the list of suspects: Bernadette's servant, who was afraid to name Lindsay's lover, served him. ([[lindsi|Lindsey]]) Mira admitted that the appearance of the castellans has nothing to do with love intrigues, but “in a quiet corner there are devils.” Later it turned out that Lindsay was killed by [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]].

Once Mira drew a diagram of the caves of the Ulyanovsk Monastery at the table on a napkin, and the castellans became interested in this.

In a letter to Iona in the winter of 1775, Vittor mentioned a castellan who was knocked down to feed the garrison better, despite the skids. The wife of the castellan, according to the count, did not have a soul in the winter garden.

### Death
In the spring of 1775, Harold was still sitting at the count's table. On the evening of May 27, [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] began a coup. ([[perevorot-v-uejmare|Coup in Waymar]]) At 10:15, she looked out the window and called out to the castellan, who was easily noticed by the rich coat of arms: "There were questions with the provisions." Harold went up to the donjon with his squire. Iona gave him a sheet, as if with a list of supplies on the road. A close-knit castellan brought the paper to his eyes and noticed that it was a page from a book. Then Iona stabbed him in the throat right through the page, and Seymour's cairn twisted the squire's neck. At 10:20 the Castelians were dead, and his deputy, Captain Leus, remained in command of the garrison. Iona soon killed him too.

After the coup, the castellan became Sir Randol. He had taken office only a week earlier, and had to report to the earl on the position of the garrison after the landing of the northerners.
