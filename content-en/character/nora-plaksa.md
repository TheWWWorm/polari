## Origins
Nora is a commoner, judging by her "peasant thoroughness". For the sake of her lover, squire Jerry, she once ran away from home. In her memory, Jerry was noble, literate, strong and affectionate, "like a tiger." He had green eyes, he sang, called her "sunny," read her aloud, pointing her finger at the page, and put her on his shoulders in tournaments. Over time, Nora got bored with him, he fell in love with another and, when she "became a burden", sent her to the caves. Nora tried to keep him caresses, pleas and sobbing and even said that she could not live without him.

## In the "Plax"

### Abode
The monastery of Foremother Ulyana Sad was under the ground in the west of the Duchy of Almer. ([[almera-zemlya|Duchy of Almera]]) The underlings dug tunnels and took clay in trolleys, lived without the sun, and could only pronounce five words a day. For extra words punished, and informers rewarded. Prioress Virginia begged Ulyana for a “cold light” — wisdom and peace. Mira later recalled Abbess Delia, who would have considered it a sin to live in a spacious room. In "Only One Star" the monastery is described as an extensive network of natural and man-made caves "in the west of [[blekmor|Blackmore]] County"; the first year novices were not supposed to see the sun.

For the first six weeks in the caves, Nora wept day and night, for which she was nicknamed Plaxa. She was considered a fool and a “powerless sheep.” On the forty-fifth day, during the evening prayer, Plaxa had her own dream - to die. She systematically considered the possible methods: a blunt little shovel with which she had not even managed to cut her palm, kitchen knives the novices were not allowed near, a cistern a foot and a half deep, and fire. Eventually, she stopped at the stone.

### Mia
In August 1774, Sister Jen put her in a pair of new — exhausted Mia: no one else would want to be Plaxa's partner, and the new girl did not have a candle burner or a piece of cheese to redeem herself. Nora was jealous, hoping that the patient would be given more bread and candles. When asked if she had a pestilence, Mia replied that she had been poisoned: “Put in coffee.” Nora thought it was a lie, because coffee costs two agates a cup. When Mia fainted, Nora kissed her on the lips, hoping to get infected. Later, she reported to the Prioress that Mia had said seven extra words, received a piece of ham for it, and Mia was left without lunch.

Judge Cindy, the fourth daughter of a junior judge in the city of Clermie, who was respected by the novices, instructed Nora to find out from the new news from above. Mia came to life a little, she began to carry buckets and ask about the trolleys. Every day, Nora gave her one sentence about Jerry, and one night, through an outlet between the cells, Mia guessed that he had sent Nora to the caves. When Mia was late for the meal and the refectory door was locked, Nora secretly took out bread and cheese for her, but Cindy took the food and raised Nora to laughter.

### Stone
Over the dead end of their tunnel, Nora found a huge boulder in the vault. She began to dig it so that the stone was held on two clay supports and fell on her. Mia decided and threatened to tell her sister Jen. Nora responded by threatening to betray her desire to run and knocked out the supports. At the last moment, Mia pulled her out from under the falling stone. Nora clung to her neck, but immediately let go when the footsteps were heard, and Mia lied to the sisters who arrived: “There was a collapse. Nora saved me.”

### Escape
Then Mia whispered to Nora that up there was August, apples, cherries and weddings. She explained that the trolley is not unloaded by people: she herself overturns on the gears outside the monastery. If you're late for dinner, the refectory doors are locked, and the runaways are caught too late. When asked what would happen if Nora did not like life at the top, Mia shrugged her shoulders:
> “I will kill you. I can do it."

Mia put Nora in an almost full trolley, gave her a breathing tube from the pages of the prayer book and buried her in clay. Nora thought Mia was gonna lie down next to her. But then Mia was caught by the Prioress of Virginia and sent her to the cell without light and lunch. Unsuspecting novices poured a few more buckets on Nora.

At sunset, the trolley carried Nora out onto a huge pile of quarry land. Blinded by the unusual light, she rolled down and in the twilight went down the path up the slope of the quarry, on the other side of the carriages. Halfway through, she cried — for the first time in three months without shame. At first, she blamed herself for not sneaking out to run with Mia, then blamed Mia, who saved her for some reason. Looking at what five words she would say to her — “Forgive me, fool,” “I will remember you forever,” “I believe in everything you do,” Nora realized: Mia knew what she was doing, and believed that Plaxa’s life was worth something.
> “I promise I will keep your gift.”

On the road she was picked up on a cart by a mustache farmer and laughed at her appearance. “Better help me than laugh,” Nora replied, all the words being spent on it, and the peasant put her on a cart and fed her.

## Mentions later
Mia is [[minerva|Mira]], the future empress of Minerva, imprisoned in the monastery by the will of the Prior Gallard. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]) She was later taken to [[uejmar|Waymar]].

In "Only One Star", Mira recalls that her first partner in the dungeon was Nora, "deeper than goats and weeping like a baby." Nevertheless, in the darkness and cold of the World, she felt almost native. Later, in a rush of forgiveness after the news of Adrian's victory, she wonders where "crying Nora" is now, and wishes her to find honest mutual love. Immediately she twitches herself for bragging about generosity, because “Nora left me in prison, and I forgave her.” Later, during the siege of the palace by the northerners (taking Faunterra), Mira tries to reach out to the ice of Hanoi with the Power Gauntlet and reminds herself that she is no longer needed by the maid, not by the "bad cry of Nora", but by the whole city. ([[bitva-za-faunterru|Capture of Faunterra]])

In A Doll on the Throne, Mira, feeling sorry for herself, ironically calls Nora "the master of tears, the master of self-deprecation" - the only person who would appreciate her tears.

## Personality
Through the eyes of Mira Nora is simple-minded, envious, not too smart and lives with feelings and self-pity. For the sake of small profit, she reports to her neighbor, but is also capable of secret kindness: this is how she brought the bread of the locked Mia. The experience in the monastery changes her. Nora realizes the value of someone else’s self-sacrifice and goes free with a promise to save the gift of life.
