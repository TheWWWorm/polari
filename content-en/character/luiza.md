## Origin and character
Louise was born in a peasant family on the bank of the Hanai. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) Much later, she was happy to tell Salem of Summerswith about her native village, her parents, her livestock, and her field. ([[salem|Salem of Summersweet]]) By the beginning of the cycle, Louise, along with her husband [[vikhor|Whirlwind]], a dark-haired patched peasant, had served merchant [[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]. The whirlwind was his stableman and loader, Louise cooked, and on occasion traded with the cart. They've been riding with Harmon for six years. Their children grew up at the curb: ten-year-old cunning Sarah and the son of Wihrenok.

Spouses are always fighting. The whirlwind grumbled under his nose so that his wife could hear. Louise rarely answered, but "loud and tasty", could run an apple or mug into her husband and moved to another cart for half a day. Harmon found these fights to be fun. He considered Louise, along with [[snajp|Snipe]], the most sensible in his entourage and attracted to reflection when deciding where to go.

Louise was illiterate and could not even write her own name. The correspondence seemed to her “almost a divine mystery.” But she quickly counted in her mind, knew how to knock down the price and kept in mind the cost of each thing. She was straight and straight. Joaquin once remarked that she was angry with the “heat”.

## In Harmon's cart
### Spring of 1774
In the spring of 1774, Louise rode with a convoy of Harmon in his usual trading circle. On March 21, at the fair in Smallden, while the owner was looking for a new guard in the Market Square, she, under the supervision of Snip and Wihrenka, traded from the cart at the inn, and in the evening handed Harmon the day's proceeds. ([[smolden|Smolden]]) [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] hired her and her children to do sword exercises. Across the north-east of Almera and the Six-mile forest, the train reached Izluchina on April 12–13, took refugees from [[niar|Niar]], among them the blond [[polli|Polly]], who sat in the rear van to [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] and Louise and then helped her cook, and at the end of April moved along the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]] to [[dymnaya-dal|Smoky Distance]]. ([[shestimilnyj-les|Six-Mile Wood]]; [[izluchina|Riverbend]]) When Whirlwind talked too much to Polly, the "sensible Louise" instantly weighed her husband's nappy. In May, while Harmon and Joaquin and Polly were driving to [[uejmar|Waymar]] for the Earl of Shayland, the rest of the entourage was waiting for them, and then the train was again driving along the South Road: through [[soltaun|Soltown]] to [[labelin|Labelin]], where it arrived in early June. On the road, Louise asked about rich brides from Harmon's stories and noticed how one roadside village differs from another: where cows are black, and where spotted, where the church is brick, and where plastered.

Louise was one of the first to convince Harmon to come to the fear-infested sea of Izliachina for a letter (Snipa convinced the economy on the crossing, and her - a conversation about letters): "It is not good to leave the letter unanswered." ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]) A letter from Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]] and marked the beginning of the story with the Light Sphere (see Sale of the Light Sphere). ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]; [[lzhe-sfera]]) When [[ben-vomak|Sir Benjamin Womack]] stopped the wagon in the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]] and told everyone to undress under the pretext of checking for the gray sea, Louise seems to be the only one of all to have kept her cool. ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) She calmly explained to the knight the signs of illness and recalled the count's letter. After [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]]'s fight with Womack, Louise supported his question why the owner was so frightened (she herself had not seen Harmon's "soul go into the heel"). She demanded a share in the promised profit, recalling that persuaded to go to Izlyachina: Harmon promised each of three gold, Louise four, and Joaquin five. Uzhina in Labelin, Vihor and Louise admitted that they dream of saving money, buying a piece of land with a hut and settling in the village, only they could not decide where it was better: in Almera or in the Southern Way.

### The Monastery and the Death of the Whirlwind
In mid-June 1774, Harmon was kidnapped, and the retinue was left without a master. When Joaquin and Polly rescued him from a pit in the ruins of the fortress in late June, Louise and Polly rushed to hug him and lament over him. It turned out that the Sphere from Harmon was kidnapped by monks. Louise was the first to lose patience, wondering what the fat-beer looked like, and Wihrenok remembered that he had seen him. Then Louise went for exploration to the Maximian monastery. According to the plan of the owner, at the end of June she portrayed a beggar pilgrim: the widowed mother of two “spiny-rats” with a drinking father, whose role was played by Doxet. ([[doksett|Doxett]]) Louise herself offered to tie the hem, as do hikers, and kiss coins before handing over. She was afraid to lead the children to the monastery, and Harmon allowed them to leave behind the gate, reaching with them only to the entrance. When she returned, Louise described the monastery as an ordinary monastery. The villains in the monks recognized [[polli|Polly]] who walked with them.

On July 4, 1774, when the men went to the ruins of the fortress behind the returned Sphere, Louise and Polly wished them good luck, and Louise kissed her husband. The [[vikhor|whirlwind]] went with them: a few days before, he had already accompanied Joaquin, who played the “Sir Desmond”, to the monastery with a fake letter, and then he was told to keep silent so that the rude rural proverb would not give out a disguise. During the retreat, the arrow wounded him in the thigh, and [[brat-lyudvig|Brother Ludwig]] cut off Whirlwind's fingers, and he was bleeding. Louise wept over her husband, but immediately ordered him to be carried in a van and served water, rags and wine. While the train was busy with the wounded, Snip tried to kidnap the Sphere, and Harmon, shooting at him, killed Polly. The merchant fled, taking all the money. The whirlwind died of rotten blood after two days of torment.

## After Harmon's escape
Louise left a seriously injured Joaquin, who for two days lay without feelings. When he woke up, he heard her whisper:
> “You should stay... At least you are. If you die, I will never forgive you.”

When he was able to walk, the train was already far from Labelin, closer to the sea. When asked about Polly, Louise replied that she had been buried and that they had prayed for everyone. The goods were left in the vans, and Joaquin helped Louise sell them at a fair in the Greysend lands. There were almost four ephes. Then she offered to go to the port, buy the southern silks and sell them in the Crown, and called him in a share: she would bargain and count, and he would talk to the lords and write letters. According to her calculations, the two of them would help out the Hilt twenty a year. Joaquin contemptuously refused, and Louise burst into flames: three good people and his beloved died, and he "all is mute." After making peace, she gave Joaquin half of the proceeds and told him to write to a hotel in Izluchina if necessary: the priest would read the letter to her. The next day, Joaquin helped her hire a reliable security guard at the market and said goodbye to her. A month later, by September 1, he was already under [[aleridan|Aleridan]], and the money received from Louise had long been spent.

## "A Doll on the Throne"
### Meeting at the Snowdrops
By the spring of 1775, Louise had succeeded. The war, she said, proved to be a glorious time for merchants: cheap becomes more expensive, and expensive becomes cheaper. She got dressed, dizzy, and “rounded up.” She was traveling the old route of Harmon, only changed hotels. The children helped her, and guarded the wagons two hired brothers-peasants, Bud and Het. Louise called them "bad rams." Their sister Sally served as her cook.

In March 1775, shortly before Hampton, Louise was trading in the "tail", a vagabond market under the army of the [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]]. There she, wearing a sheepskin tuloupe and a red scarf, called Joaquin by his full name from the van, threw herself around his neck and asked for news. Joaquin brought her to Salem, and he accepted Louise as his sister, because they both grew up over Hanai. Salem admitted that she looked a bit like his wife. When Salem asked her opinions about the campaign, Louise responded with peasant directness:
> "The Empress is a young lady of eighteen years. I remember being eighteen—there was only one thing on my mind. And this is one thing - believe me, it is not justice at all."

Salem invited Louise to visit Summerswith. She told Joaquin that the chief was kind, but simple, like her late Vortex, and he could not defeat the nobility. ([[vikhor|Whirlwind]]) Louise urged Joaquin to go with her to [[uejmar|Waymar]] and sell cheaply bought helmets and mail for a third of the proceeds. He refused while he could help Salem, and Louise called him hotels on her way.

### On the road to Leixiti
At the end of March 1775, after leaving the Snowdrops at Faunterra itself, Joaquin and Veselchak met with Louise at the agreed place and moved with her vans to Smoky Distance. Joaquin accepted her offer for the first time. On the marshy road from Faunterra to Leixiti, escort [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] overtook the train. Bad and Het did not give way, and Cyre [[sejmur-stil|Seymour Steel]] put them and Sally on their knees and told Joaquin to kill one of the brothers. Otherwise, he threatened to kill them both. Louise bowed, offered reparations, insisted that “this is not done in the Lands of the Crown,” and finally exclaimed Joaquin, who was staring at the massacre. Joaquin managed to save the brothers, but he himself was captured by Iona, and Louise remained on the roadside with the vans.

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
After consulting with Veselchak, Louise decided to split: he went to Faunterra to seek the intercession of the Duke of [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], and she rushed to Waymar to ask the Earl of Shayland. Louise was only three days behind Iona's equestrian escort. What to do in Waymar, she did not know, but was not going to “stand aside”. Released by Iona on May 5, 1775, Joaquin remembered that Louise was going to Waymar, and decided to wait for her in the city. In the following days, he inquired about her arrival at the lake port authority. Soon Louise met him there, called him “lucky”, which “all the Foremothers kissed”, and, as a ticket, noted the joy of a long feast.

For almost a week, from about May 15 to 20, Joaquin traded with her in the Waymar market. Louise brought from the Lands of the Crown glass, porcelain, tea and cutlery and laid them out according to the precepts of Harmon: bright and expensive ahead, for bait, in the second row is fashionable, “almost the same”. At first, Joaquin angered customers with excessive honesty. In the evening, Louise chastised him, but he laughed and said that she was good: polite people wanted to kill him, and she screams good. Gradually Joaquin mastered the craft. Louise was going to sail with a new product to the Southern Way, and in Labelin she set up a meeting with Veselchak. However, on the last evening, Joaquin was captured in Seymour's one-eyed caback, and after being massacred in a caback, he was taken to the tower of the Earl of Shayland, where he was patronized by barber Harry Hog. ([[garri-khog|Harry Hogg]]) In order not to put Louise in danger, he did not go to her hotel, but only handed over the news with a cab driver. Sitting in the tower, he did not understand why he was not returned to the hotel to Louise.

## Further Destiny
In [[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]], June 9–10, 1775, as Joaquin was preparing for the Battle of Waymar, he saw the Port Authority, where he inquired about Louise in May, and was afraid that she was still in town. ([[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]]) Then he found out in the market that Louise had been looking for “her companion” for a long time and went even to the castle. She was told that the “servant” was taken to prison, but she did not believe, because he himself wrote to her that he was alive and had found a new service. She was driven away from the castle, and a week before the war in Waymar, she sold the last product, bought a new one and sailed away by boat. Joaquin was ashamed and decided to find her and the Merry Man as soon as the war was over.

In “Shadow of the Great Tree” Veselchak told Harmon that Louise “lives — does not bother”: she has her own business, a caravan and servants. ([[ten-velikogo-dreva|The Shadow of the Great Tree]]) Harmon, tormented by guilt for the [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] and Polly, sent two hundred Hilts to the Izlyachina to be demanded for Louise. He sent a letter to the owner of the hotel: “When my Louise arrives, tell her to go to the bank. “Harmon Paula knows what the shit was.” Harmon, going through the dead through his fault, recalled that because of him, Whirlwind died, and Louise and her children remained to their fate. Joaquin in November 1775 among the “good people” who “gads” broke his life, called Louise.
