## Overview
Gallard Almera, the elder brother of the Duke of [[ajden-almera|Aiden]], led the Pro-Fatherly branch of the [[tserkov|Church]] in the rank of Prior and Archbishop of Faunterra. He was severe, with a granite face in deep wrinkles, bulging with bile and black eyes falling under the thick eyebrows. He was loud and loved long speeches from the pulpit, but did not tolerate dialogue. Gallard opposed all innovations: printed books were considered soulless, spark shops were considered sinful, and the heretical experiments of Adrian with Objects were considered sacrilege. He did not like women and believed that the wife should obey her husband, "standing lower - standing higher."

### Youth
As a child, Gallard was a solid mixture of stubbornness, self-confidence and willpower, he adored to prove his case and restored the entire castle of Aleridan against himself. The father decided that the best ruler would be the youngest son, and sent Gallard to the seminary. Gallard did not argue: he was smiling high rank and the role of truth in the last resort. Later, Aiden bribed, blackmailed and threatened his brother with Archbishop Faunterra's mantle, hoping to find an ally in it. Gallard got along with the emperor Tellurian, but the young reformer Adrian made him a staunch opponent of the Crown.

### Seventeenth Gift
In 1756, while still a bishop, Gallard accompanied the imperial court at [[uejmar|Waymar]], to the site of the Seventeenth Gift. The Almer brothers provided their fleet for the carriage of the yard. Ramir-Aslan was then sick, and Gallard, who was marking his place, showed piety in every way. At a party in Waymar, he went into a dance with [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]], mercilessly stepping on her feet. It was later revealed that Gallard and Sybil were lovers and that the real father of her daughter [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]] was Gallard, not the old Earl of Northwood. According to Navi, Gloria was conceived “in that month and year” when the nobles gathered for the celebration of the Seventeenth Gift, and was named after Pramateri, whose name is consonant with Gallard. To the young Vittor Shayland, the bishop made it clear that he knew of the tribute of the Objects received by Desmond Origin, and of some particular Object found in the Lodge of the Gift. Gallard himself later claimed that he was the first to see Dar, but was deceived by four villains, including his own brother.

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"
### Conversation with Erwin
In early December 1773, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] met with the priory at the altar of black marble in the Cathedral of the Forefathers. Under the guise of the story of “two brothers from a noble family,” Erwin convinced Gallard to bless the suzerain for the free choice of the bride: another, more pious wife will keep him in the bosom of the Church, and the counselor will gain influence over the lord. Gallard, according to Erwin, wanted power and influence most of all and in this he was like a brother. In farewell, the archpriest warned Erwin not to side with the heretic.

### Summer Ball
On June 2, 1774, at a ball at the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]], which opened the summer season, Gallard, in a blue-silver robe, came to the throne and uttered a lengthy opening prayer. Mira, still under the name Gloria, described him to Becke as a man similar to the sculpture of the Forefather Wilhelm the Strict by a provincial master.

### Sybil Conspiracy
Gallard was complicit in Sybil's intrigue. Under the name of Gloria, [[minerva|Minerva]] Stagforth, heiress to the throne, was brought to the court. She was poisoned to pass off the real Gloria as Minerva and make the emperor's bride. Sybil, apparently, put the imaginary "Gloria" in the wife of an unmarried Gallard, and he came to her for lunch to look at the girl. He read the countess for a bookcase in the refectory and a sparkle chandelier and taught that a woman should obey her husband. Alanis he called “a spoiled, spoiled creature.” Mira deliberately stated that she would support the husband-emperor in all his reforms, and the priest in anger accused her of pandering to the heretic.

Mira came to the conclusion that it was through Gallard that the secret of his brother about the Shutov conspiracy became known: the abbot of the pro-patristic monastery, who kept a letter from the Aleridian jailer, shared it with his spiritual father. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]) So [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] was eliminated from the brides. The conspirators exposed by Mira told her that the poison in her blood would kill her anyway, but Mira threatened to shoot herself and take them with her. Then Gallard offered to send her to the underground monastery of Ulyana Sad in the west of Almera and, together with Sybil, swore "the life of our daughter", betraying the secret of Gloria's paternity.

## In "Only One Star"
### Regent
After the exposure of Aiden, Gallard left Faunterra for [[aleridan|Aleridan]] to gather an army of poor knights and mercenaries. When Evergard died, his men were the first to appear on the ashes, and Gallard declared himself regent under young Lord Albert. ([[pozhar-evergarda|Fall of Evergard]]) His mercenaries hunted the wounded Alanis, and the Earl of Blackmore tried to give her to the arch. Soon the archpriest obtained an oath of loyalty from three-quarters of the vassals of the deceased brother.

Gallard immediately began to restore Evergard and strengthen it against the Persians: long-range ballistics platforms were built on the towers, the moat was expanded, and the inner road was blocked with barriers. He revived the burning of heretics, canceled by Julian the Great. Joaquin saw several people caught under Blackmore being burned at the Cathedral Square of Aleridan by a court verdict; the crowd whispered that the parish was apprehensive because it was afraid of a vassal uprising. Gallard swore an oath of allegiance to the Emperor. Adrian, however, was not deceived: according to him, the priest was with him “for the sake of power.” The barons, dissatisfied with the archpriest, threatened to throw him off and choose the one who would avenge Aidan as regent, and Gallard reported to the capital that he was keeping the Red Land under complete control. In the autumn, Erwin, learning that Alanis was alive, dictated to her a letter to her uncle: if the prince sent at least one regiment to help the emperor or let the troops of Hope pass to the capital, the resurrected Duchess would declare herself, and Gallard's power would lose its legal basis.

### Meeting with Adrian
In December 1774, Emperor [[adrian|Adrian]] traveled by train to Aleridan. Gallard met him on the platform with a retinue of six bishops and hundreds of knights and brought the news of the theft of the dynasty's heritage: a train with objects taken from Faunterra was attacked by unknown. The priest declared it a sign that the gods deprived the lord of mercy, and demanded to moderate the reforms. Adrian besieged him: Gallard rules Almera and stands at the head of the Church as long as the emperor allows it. Adrian then asked why the Almer company had missed the rebel trains, and reported that Alanis was alive and in the palace with Origin. Gallard demanded the execution of Alanis and Erwin as rebels, and Adrian promised that Alanis would never rule Almera. Priarch explained the passage of trains by the arbitrariness of the bribed company commander and vowed that the Almer battalions would march into the capital tomorrow. He also reported that [[minerva|Minerva]], who had fled from Waymar, was under his supervision in Aleridan, and offered to take her to Vladyka; Adrian told me to bring her in. The guardians of the parish watched the inn for several days, where Mira was hiding with Lieutenant Shatterhand's guards, and then the monk with the soldiers of "his eminence" together with the captain of the azure guard Hugo Grace appeared behind her. Mira had by then suspected that Gallard, Sybil's former lover, was a secret ally of the Northerners and even the "main spider" of the conspiracy. Adrian confirmed to her that the priory was indeed an ally of Erwin and Sybil, but now, for personal reasons, wished the death of all the defenders of the captured palace, among whom was Alanis, and therefore stood firmly on the side of the Crown. That same evening, in the palace of the Priory, Manson, who had no room at the table, received a letter impregnated with dust; a sign was formed from the dust, and Manson understood the order: “You will kill the emperor.” ([[menson|Manson]])

Later it turned out that the duchy went to Gallard in a deal with a mysterious interlocutor: he recalled that Gallard received a “whole duchy”, and he received only four Subjects. ([[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]])

## I want a pony and A Doll on the Throne.
### Hostages
In the winter of 1774–1775, Gallard's grandchildren [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]], Laura and [[dzheremi-farvej|Jeremy]] lived in Evergard as hostages. Behind them was the monk Brother Clement, and in the lower courtyard of the castle after each execution put a new pillar for burning, as a reminder. The priory personally experienced [[laura-farvej|Laura]] with questions about faith. At first, the priest put Laura in the wives of his young nephew [[albert-almera|Albert]]: the boy was too young to confirm the marriage, but could take an oath. Laura's marriage to Albert he demanded and in negotiations for an alliance with Lord Finley Farway. Farway achieved in exchange for the completion of Aiden's railroad expansion from Aleridan to the Heart of Light, a loan of two hundred thousand to the Church of the Forefathers for the army of Hope, and a promise that Laura's first-born would be blessed as the rightful heir to Almera. When Laura informed him that Jeremy was a “freethinker” who did not believe in the holiness of the Forefathers, Gallard put the young man to the test with prayers and sent him to a distant monastery for eight years of obedience. Laura, on the other hand, offered a “more mature groom,” that is, himself. He explained to Laure that cruelty does not consist in punishing criminals, but in tolerating them.

### Statham Ultimatum
General [[stetkhem|Statham]] brought him Empress Minerva's order to end the terror, free Albert, and hand over power to Alanis' legitimate heiress. Gallard called the decree a fake, written by the hand of Origin, and in response threatened with publicity: he knew that Alanis's companion had visited Back Castle the day before the Adrian train crash. The prince said that he would not give Almer "self-absorbed trash", but is ready to recognize the new power if Erwin leaves him the duchy. As a result, Gallard became the viceroy of Alanis: he ruled Almera on her behalf, after his death the duchy devolved to her or to Albert, the Earl of Blackmore was put on trial, and the Earl of Arrowback received his spark shop.

Laura Gallard admitted that he executed the hostess of the music salon in Fliss, urging the mayor to go to the side of Alanis, and with her several officers and barons. He said he wouldn't have done it if he had killed his niece in time. After listening through the auditory outlets of the conversation between the priest and an unknown interlocutor, Laura and Jeremy learned that Gallard had long, even before Evergard, been associated with the owner of the Rings. Gallard himself demanded the right of the interlocutor to “speak with the Objects”, arguing that the head of the Church should own them. Laura, however, did not extradite him, deciding that "Gallard is not a scoundrel."

### Searching for the Blue Gang
In March 1775, Almera was scoured by detachments of the archpriest - monks with soldiers who were looking for a "blue gang" in guard uniforms, thieves of the dynasty's heritage. One such patrol, led by his brother Hemish, stopped Marc-Crown and Ded in Arrowback County, and later the people of the parish arrested them on the road from Fliss "in the name of Archbishop Gallard Almer." ([[mark-voron|Mark]]; [[ded|Grandfather]]) Major Backfield, who was also looking for witnesses in Almera, turned his eyes to the agents of the parish, pretending that he had come to Gallard with a certain business proposal. ## The “Yanmei Hunt”
In March 1775 Gallard became engaged to Laura Farway and through this marriage made an alliance with [[nadezhda|Hope]]. Alanis found out about this, looking for hired killers. Together with Farway, the archpriest promoted the project of the “sacred guard”, and Bishop Gregor Amessin spoke in the House of Representatives from the Church of the Forefathers. In May, the priory wrote to [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] bypassing Erwin, suggesting the creation of a sacred guard based on monastic orders to capture the Puppeteer under the worldly supervision of the Duke of Farway. Desmond and Lady Sophia defended Gallard before his son as a zealous minister of the Church, unable to turn the Rings against the innocent. Father David told Erwin that the parish, braiding a network of informers among the monastic brothers, he himself learned about the secret order of Lady-in-the-Darkness, helped him for some time, but about a year ago suddenly cut off all ties; he did not know about the fake Order Items. ([[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]]) Erwin declared Gallard the main suspect in that he is the Puppeteer: The objects of the Dynasty disappeared in his land, the burning of Evergard was beneficial primarily to him, and Bishop Aleridan asked the archpriest about the "fate of the apple". At [[menson|Manson]]'s trial, Erwin read an intercepted pigeon letter from Bishop Amessin to Evergard: "Your Eminence, what about the apple?" The apple turned out to be Ulyanina Dust - an object that breaks the will of any mortal. By decree of Telurian, he was seized from the Lighthart House and handed over to the Church of the Forefathers. ## In "Humans and Gods"
### Excommunication and war
[[mat-kordeliya|Cordelia]]'s mother Erwin presented the evidence collected. As early as 1772, Ulyanina Dust was taken from the vault of the Wilhelmina Cathedral in Aleridan to the palace of the Priory, and from there, under heavy escort, was sent to Fliss and loaded onto a ship. Wilhelm's Fingers were until then considered small objects, and they were kept in two dozen Almeran temples. Over the past two years, no one has seen them: the archpriests removed them from the churches. According to Erwin, for these Rings and Dust Gallard received from the Puppeteer the burning of Evergard and the Duchy. Cordelia called it a “monstrous deal.”

At the end of May 1775, a captive [[alanis-almera|Alanis]], whom Paul asked how to persuade her uncle to side with the brigade, suggested: teach Gallard to manage the Objects and call him chosen, for he dreams of divine power and dreams of becoming a new Forefather. The ecumenical council of the two Churches summoned Gallard to the ecclesiastical court, and when he refused to appear in Faunterra, he suspended him and deprived him of his dignity. Erwin declared war on him. The threat of trial caused Gallard to lose a few days, and then he moved his best regiment of General [[veksler|Wexler]] against the North, bought, in the words of the Almeran knights, "a barrel of silver". On June 6–7, 1775, the Beck Erwin Dam, allied with Earl Arrowback, destroyed half of this regiment with a blow from the rear; later, the Almeran troops were defeated in the forests at Fliss. In the ranks of the feudal battalions, the parish had its own people who informed him about the movements of the northerners.

### Battle of Breakdown
On June 19, 1775, Gallard personally led his army to battle at the town of Oblivion. He believed that Erwin had sailed to Waymar, and went to grab Cordelia's mother to judge her and force her to confess to colluding with Origin and slandering the priory. His army looked like an icon: on the banners sparkled spirals and faces of the Forefathers, in the center were the monks of the Wilhelmine Combat Brotherhood, on the flank were heavy knights. Before the attack, the archpriest raised a staff, and at its summit a blue star shone, inspiring the army with the cry "With us Wilhelm!". Mother Cordelia called it a “cheap heretical trick.” Gallard shelled the cliff with fire shells from Fliss' ballistics, but at the decisive moment, the Origin's cavalry entered the field, and the archer lost. He lost Aleridan, and with him the treasury, lost his infantry, archers and wagons, and locked himself in Fliss with only a thousand knights. Meanwhile, [[henri-farvej|Farway]]'s troops occupied Aleridan. Farway, through [[edgar-flejm|Edgar Flame]], suggested to Erwin that after Gallard's execution, he recognize the rights of his widow Laura and agreed to marry her to Albert. On the advice of David's father, Mother Cordelia decided to declare him the false knight of Wilhelm: Gallard publicly called himself a tool of the Forefathers and lost the battle.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
### Alliance with Paul
When [[paul|Paul]]'s horde approached Fliss, Farway's army, which was besieging the city, left without a fight. Gallard, from the gate tower, welcomed Paul as a true friend, "excommunicated" Cordelia's mother, who blocked the horde's path, and Paul killed her. The priest recognized Paul as the Forefather and told the council that the young Shaylands had learned the “good news” of the Seventeenth Gift, but were intimidated by the villains. He thanked Paul for turning the "sinner Aiden" into a fire.

Paul promised him two Rings at once and six more after the next battle, and instead of Aleridan remaining at Farway, offered any city the Lands of the Crown; Gallard chose the sacred Arden. The temples and guilds of Fliss gathered four carts of silver for the Shavans, and Gallard promised a thousand ephes for each khanid. Paula and Alanis he settled in the summer ducal palace. Later, Gallard and Laura came to Paul's ship and, on the list of questions recorded by Laura in a notebook, "calculated the theological nuances": how to glorify the Forefather, whether he agrees with the previous doctrine, why he hid for so long. Paul encouraged the priest’s arguments about the secondary role of women and that the clergy of both branches should unite “under the power of a man,” and Laura begged to give her husband the first to pass the test of the chosen one, recalling that he was supposed to enter the bed of the Seventeenth Gift, but Aidan deceived him. Gallard sent pigeons to bishops and abbots with the "good news" of the Forefather. Paul himself told Vittor that the test provides a way to “easy and clean” remove the power-hungry prince: he will die and not rise again, and everyone will decide that the gods judged. With a fleet, a battalion of knights, and a battalion of the Combat Brotherhood, Gallard went to [[uejmar|Waymar]] to aid Vittor. He brought ten thousand poods of provisions to the starving city as a gift, and six thousand Shavans came with him.

### Death
At the "elector's test" on 2 August 1775, Gallard ceded the championship to Wittor Shayland. ([[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]) He and Vittor were poisoned by the "family gun of the house of Nan-Claire" from Alanis powder. Vittor was resurrected thanks to the Absolute, and Gallard died excruciating blood and worms. Dying, he did not let the monks and plucked worms from his wife's hair. The body was infested with parasites, and Vittor, after the all-night prayer service, gave it to the fire of the Rings. Most of the ashes were sent with honors to Aleridan, and the small urn was left in the castle chapel. The Sheilanders shouted "Glory to Gallard the martyr!" The deceased received the title of great martyr, and Vittor allocated a plot for the Cathedral of St. Gallard the Forerunner. Laura's widow prayed every day for his soul to rest. In Fantasias, Henry Farway called him a "holy martyr" who in his last moments thought only of Laura. ([[fantazii|Fantasies]])

## In "Fantasies"
The death of Gallard, which occurred a week after the death of Cordelia’s mother, split the Mother Church: two prelates died fighting for opposite goals, and the flock did not know whose side the gods were on. Bishop Amessin and his Wilhelmines stubbornly stood for the sanctity of Gallard and Paul and glorified the martyrdom of the priory even after the inglorious death of both "saints"; the freed rank of the priory became the subject of struggle between the prelates. Henry Farway called the deceased “a holy martyr” who fell in love with his granddaughter Laura.
