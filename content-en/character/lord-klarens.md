## Who He Is

Lord Clarence Madeleine Fiona of the Ludmila family served as the ruler of the imperial stables and lived at the court. He was the alter of Countess [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]]. At the first meeting of Mir, it seemed that the lush mustache, rounded cheeks and a slightly inflated nose gave him the appearance of a "satisfied with the life of a cat." ([[minerva|Minerva]]) As soon as the countess arrived in the capital, Clarence hugged her and kissed her right on the lips, sat next to her at the table, hugged her shoulders in a masterly manner and began to rudely courteously say: "My lioness, why have you been missing for so long in your north?" After escaping from the table Mira, he threw: “What a gloomy child ... It's not like you at all! I think she went to her father.” Later, he visited the countess more than once, and they were alone on the upper floor. Knight [[vanden|Vanden]] explained to the stunned World that it was "everyone knows". Count Elias had long been cold to women, and the connection with the alter, allowed by writing since the time of Pramatery Sophia, did not bother him.

Clarence spoke untidy and mocking. He called the archivists of the Court Shrine of Per "priests of parchment", and their vault "saray filled with paper". The countess did not allow Mira to ask the alter about the heirs of the lord, and she conspired: she asked Clarence to show her the life stories of the emperors of the Dynasty. He held her in the Sanctuary and gave her an “altar tour” because he knew the chief archivist well. Sybil grumbled at the same time that it was a strange habit to scold books in the spring. Clarence objected: the erudite girl is a rarity, “the grooms immediately notice such.”

## Warnings to the Countess

Shortly before the summer games (according to Clarence, a month remained before them), Mira overheard Clarence and Sybil talking in the Countess' bedroom. Walter spoke with unusual seriousness. He reproached Sybil for "dangerous acquaintances": with the king Henry Farway, whom the first adviser considered his man and who "slept with the Duke of Almera"; with Lady Catherine Catherine, an althess of the Shimmer prince; with General William Davey, the "second general of the Crown", who had long drunk with the younger Origin. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) On the eve of the summer games, he said, “all dogs have long known whose food they eat.” He jokingly called himself the "faithful dog" of the Countess.

The last straw, he considered friendship with the disgraced parish of [[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]: because of her, the Countess’s enemies would be [[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]] and all his allies. Clarence knew that Sybil had met him in May, talked at the ball, and that he would come to visit her the next day. The countess exclaimed, “Are you following me?” He recalled that Gallard was in Waymar for the 17th Gift celebrations, when young Sybil was there. And then whispered “just a thought” about the Countess and Gallard. Sybil laughed, “The prankster! He invented it!”

At the annual ball at the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]], Clarence invited the Countess to dance.

## Death

In the summer of 1774, Vanden told Mira that Clarence had been killed in a duel by a Northwood baron. He believed that the connection of the horse with the Countess, although approved by the writing, stains the name of Count Elias Northwood. The whole city thought so. Vanden described the fight in detail and not without pleasure. For two days the countess did not go to the World, as they said, not wanting to share her sorrow. In "Only One Star", Mark clarifies that Clarence was killed in a duel "back in June".

Mira later remembered that conversation. All the names that Clarence called - Catherine, Davey, Farway - were connected with Erwin's plan, and in the absence of Erwin (who left for Zazrezhye), Sybil, according to Mira, contacted all members of his coalition. ([[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]]) And in a whisper, Clarence, according to Mira, suggested that Gloria's real father is Gallard: Sybil's connection with the parish has been dragging on since Dar arrived in Shayland eighteen years ago. Mira concluded: “If he had said it loud, it would have been completely different.” Whether the Countess is involved in a duel, the text does not directly say.
