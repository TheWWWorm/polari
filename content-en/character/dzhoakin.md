## Origins
Joaquin Yves Hanna is from the Sad Hill in the Holmogore Bishopric of the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. Parts of the name "Yves Hannah" are given to him by his mother and grandmother. He is the son of a knight, but he is neither an original nor a knight. The village of his father is “one-sided”: the father’s shield went to the elder brother, the middle one serves as the elder armorer, and Joaquin, the youngest, was not found. In his early youth, he vowed not to return home until he had a reason to be proud. The father read aloud to his sons "Questions of the commander" Duke Desmond.

By the beginning of the cycle, Joaquin had served Baron Broyfield in Almer. According to him, they “disagree in matters of honor”, and later, in his own thoughts, he admitted that he was “disgracefully expelled”. Even earlier, he did not stay long in the army of [[graf-rantigar|Count Rantigar]] at the War of the Mills. ([[melnichnaya-vojna|Mill War]]) He later admitted to [[polli|Polly]] that he had only spent two months there and had not been in a single battle. He dreamed of a castle on a sea cliff and his own coat of arms: a heart pierced by a sword, and below the catapult.

## In "Fantasies"
In "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]" the author tells about the possible fates of the heroes after the cycle. Joaquin does not appear there, but in the story “A Composition on a Theme” he, by all indications, is a “knight” from an essay led by Mrs. Gale, a teacher at a rural school. And Mrs. Gale herself, by all accounts, is the survivor [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]]. Children call her a “gargoyle”: she walks with a cane, does not remove black velvet gloves, her upper lip is cut open, a scarlet hole in her mouth. At the same time, she teaches children manners “deserving of the nobility”.

Mrs. Gale asked the class a story about a knight and a lady, and then anonymously wrote the essay of a student [[salli|Sally]] in the drawer of her desk. In her paragraphs the lady rotted in the dungeon, the knight nursed her, "admired her kindness" and swore, "I will never leave you." She then revealed the truth: the lady lacked two fingers, teeth, nails and pieces of skin; the knight was there while his friends tortured her, then left for the war and returned when he lost. He said to her, "One can forgive everything to one's soul," and showed his hand: he also did not have two fingers. This coincides with the fate of Joaquin: he lost two fingers in the battle at Arrowback, tortured Alanis in Waymar along with Vittor's men and left with the losing army of the Chosen One. When Sally asked for advice, Mrs Gail replied, "It's your turn, not mine." Sally wrote the last line, “Lady killed a knight,” and the teacher admitted, “A lucky ending.” So, in this possible fate, Joaquin returned to Alanis after the war and was eventually killed by her.

## Personality and Appearance
Joaquin is tall, broad-shouldered and very strong, two inches taller than the X from the Duke's personal guard. He has brown good eyes and a beard "in a noble manner." As a child, he never got sick and, according to the author, therefore did not have a tendency to think: for the first time he began to think, only lying down in Beck Castle. He is an excellent fighter on swords and daggers, and later the best shooter from Perth. He is arrogant, flamboyant, naive and prone to self-deception. He desperately wants recognition, and he easily takes the side of the one who is kind to him. Therefore, in serving Vittor, Joaquin sincerely saw the righteous in the Shaylands and the wicked in Iona and Alanis.

The chapter "Sword" in "A Doll on the Throne" is based on the opposition of the "old Joe" who would rush into any fight, and the "present", tired and cautious. In Lowville, at the outpost and under Hampton, they take the upper hand in turn, and you can see how bravado and experienced fear struggle in it. Deep down, Joaquin is kind and conscientious. The violence eventually forced him to give up his weapons.

## Relationships
### Polly
First girlfriend to die from Harmon's shot. He later dreamed of finding “a good girl like Polly.”

### Alanis Almera
The subject of enthusiastic love, then bitter resentment and hatred. He saved her life, served as her bodyguard, and in Waymar cut her face. Iona, Alanis' fellow student in boarding school, easily guessed his feeling.

### Harmon
Former owner. Joaquin considered him guilty of Polly's death and called him a "walker", but then traded according to his will.

### Louisa
She went out after the monastery, repeatedly called in companions and rushed to rescue him from captivity (see Louise). ([[luiza]]) Louise is one of the few people who spoke to him about “warmness.”

### Jolly
A loyal squire and friend. He saved Joaquin's life under Hampton, and after his capture, Iona sought his helpers in the capital. Not finding Joaquin, Veselchak went to Salem in Summerswith, where he promised to visit, and was next to Salem on the day when Joaquin killed him.

### Salem
A friend whom Joaquin respected as none of the "great" and saved from arrest, and then killed without recognizing.

### Vittor Shayland
Suzeren, who gave him the Perst and the knighthood, but held him on a chain of pills. Joaquin sincerely considered him a righteous man, until he saw how the count stabbed his brother. Then he turned the pen against him.

### Martin Shayland
The chief and the partner: together they guarded Iona. Joaquin sympathized with Martin, even finding him over the strangled Laura Farway. He considered the surrender of Leed to the horde a disgusting act, but Martin’s foot journey through the cold to the army, according to Joaquin, “redempted everything.”

### Iona Origin and Hash Eilish
Iona held him captive twice, and then became his captive. Hash Eilish, a sunset girl from Silens, was his mistress in Waymar.
