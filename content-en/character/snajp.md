## Biography

Snip served as a guard in [[kharmon|Harmon]]'s circuit long before [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]] was hired. He got a little less than the new mercenary and more than the old Doxet. ([[doksett|Doxett]]) Harmon knew that Snape was a deserter and that a stake or gallows was waiting for him at home. Snape didn't say anything about himself. The lord landowner for several years kept him in the army, not letting go to his wife and young children. When Snape escaped, the lord took two-thirds of his wife's clothes: without her husband, they say, you can not cultivate a third, and if the husband returns, he will still be hanged. Since then, Snape hated lords-landowners and did not believe in the good deeds they do. Sir Ben Womack's squire determined at one glance that the "smooth murmur on goats was born in Blackmore", and guessed quite accurately. ([[ben-vomak|Benjamin Womack]])

Harmon Snipe was needed as a fighter, but the merchant dreamed of a warrior "not rotting like Doxet, and not a deserter like Snipe." In Smolden, while Harmon was looking for a new guard, Snip and [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] were guarding Louise and the goods. On the way, he alternated with the owner of the head van and slept in its back half, along with a load of dishes. In the evening, an offended Joaquin lay there, and in the morning, Snape woke him with a pull in the ribs.

## In the campaign of 1774

In the Six-mile forest, the road was blocked by the "forest lords" led by old Sedym and demanded a fare for the bridge. ([[shestimilnyj-les|Six-Mile Wood]]) When the fight broke out, Snape jumped off the goat with an axe and fought with the robber holding the horses. Harmon shot him on the crossbow, and Snip hit him on the side below his ribs the next time. Then the deserter checked whether he had given everything to Sedoy, and picked up the abandoned axes and onions.

In Izlyachyn, it was Snip, along with Louise, who, as the most sane people on the wagon, helped Harmon decide whether to enter the feared city. ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]; [[luiza|Louisa]]) Snape was convinced that otherwise he would have to overpay in the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], and he did not want to fill the pocket of the greedy nobility.

On the way from Izluchina, the grandson of the refugee Painter had blue lips, and the train decided that the boy had brought a gray plague. ([[sizyj-mor|Blue-Grey Plague]]) Snipe with an axe blocked his grandfather and grandson from the rest and demanded to expel both: “Small around him all the time. Both are sick.” The boy, who asked for mercy, he threatened: "You'll talk - a hitch." When [[polli|Polly]] approached the baby, Snipe threw, “Oh, stupid. Now you have to be kicked out too.” He lowered the axe only when Joaquin drove the sword into the floor of the van and told everyone to go to bed. In the morning it turned out that the boy was just cold, and Snip only shrugged his shoulders.

When Harmon showed people the diploma to the county land, Snape said: “Earth, so now we are trading ... We are helping the Lords.” He did not believe that the money would go to Lady Iona's Hospital. During a skirmish with Sir Ben Womack, the squire put a spear to Snip's neck and drove him off the goat unarmed. But, taking a moment, the deserter grabbed an axe from the van and knocked out a spear from one of the hunters, and then drove the defeated squires with a threat to take up the crossbow. After the skirmish, Snape praised Joaquin: "The right decision, good boy." Then he was the first to question how much Harmon would actually earn. He drew from the merchant a confession that the profit would be a hundred ephes, and threw: "Lords lie like dogs. That's not news. “You are a merchant!” Harmon promised three golds each.

When Harmon asked his companions about their dreams, Louise and [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] talked about breaking free, and Snape muttered, "Well... I'll think of something..." Savings would have been enough for a piece of land, but not to pay off the charge of desertion. Joaquin, who boasted that he was “almost” a noble family, Snipe threw: “I found something to be proud of, stupid head.”

## Salvation of Harmon

While Harmon sat in a dungeon under the ruined fortress, Snip and Joaquin took turns with Doxet and Whirlwind's son, Whirlwind, following the ruins. ([[vikhor|Whirlwind]]) Together with Joaquin and Doxet, he freed the master. He was guarding a bound jailer, and then he led him to torture, pushing him with an axe, and, grinning, he held his head while the atrocious Harmon beat the jailer with a hammer. When he heard that there was a “thing” of the count in the barracks, Snip was surprised: “You said the count sells paper.” He dissuaded the emaciated master from the outing, but agreed when Harmon promised each of the twenty ephemera. Snip hit a guard at the barracks, throwing an axe at him; he got hit with a buck. In the battle at the barracks, the torchman threw fire in his face. Snape lost his eyebrows and burned his nose, but his eyes were not injured.

Then Snip in the livery of the Labelin guard went with Joaquin and Vhirm to the monks in the monastery of St. Forefather Maximian. Joaquin gave him his feral mare Lady. Harmon allowed him to throw a curse for the sake of persuasiveness: the deserter's "grip, angry voice" was suitable for a night performance. Snip scolded the gatekeeper with a "cattle", and threw it to the abbot: "Dumbs in sluts. Let's get out of here, Desmond. In the morning we will bring the guys and search this lair.” On the way back, he noticed that the abbot had spoken of the return of the Subject, although Joaquin claimed that it was a commodity.

On July 4, 1774, the detachment was waiting for the monks in the barracks of the destroyed fortress. Snape picked up a table top with a dagger and laid siege to the restless Whirlwind: “Sit down and shut up, or I’ll hit you.” When the monks gave the Sphere and the Whirlwind shouted “It seems, it was...”, Snape banged him under the ribs: “Shut up, dub.”

## Death

In the chase of the monks of [[brat-lyudvig|brother Ludwig]], Snipe and Joaquin fought off three riders. Two of them were put down, but the third stunned Snape with a blow to the helmet, and he dropped the axe. At a halt in the grove, Harmon gave Snip the only surviving sword to finish off the Whirlwind's wounded horse, and put Whirlwind to his saddle. ([[vikhor|Whirlwind]]) On the way, the farmer lost his senses. Snape drove him to the wagon, pressing him, and let him go with the words "He doesn't hear": Whirlpool was dead. After recovering from the blow, the deserter helped wash Joaquin's wounds with hot wine.

Then Snape put on the road coat, took the axe, dagger and the sum of brother Ludwig with the Light Sphere and went to the horse. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) Harmon grabbed the crossbow. Snape replied calmly, “No, I’ll cut it.” The owner promised to pay with money if he returned the Item. Snipe replied:

> "Ha-ha. Fair enough. All you did was lie. A letter to the ground, a hundred ephes, aye. You're a jackal, Harmon. Worse than any lord."

The object, he hoped, would be enough for him to return to his wife and children. On the threat that the Sphere was being sought by the Baron, Snip countered that the Baron had been sold a fake: "I understood everything, not stupid." [[polli|Polly]] came out of the van with the jug. Snape grabbed her, put a knife to her throat, and told Harmon to drop the crossbow, promising to release the girl a mile from the camp. He put Polly in the saddle, pressed his left hand, like the Whirlwind recently, and finally shouted to Joaquin: "Don't stay with this. If you survive, get away from him.” As soon as he turned away, Harmon pulled the string again. The wounded Joaquin shouted that he would not shoot. With a hundred paces, the bolt sewn Snape through, "left of the spine, below the shoulder blade," and Polly entered the chest. The deserter died without a sound. Harmon took the Sphere out of the bag. Louise later told Joaquin that "three good men" had died.

## Personality

Always gloomy and silent, Snip could not say a word for days and looked so gloomy that the oncoming peasants took him for a robber and scurried out of the way. Sometimes some minor event brought him a dreaminess: he sighed quietly, scraped his nails bristles, and in the evening drank a couple of cups with Doxet and said: “M-yes, life...”. He liked to watch the [[vikhor|whirlwind]] fight with Louise. Snip was sarcastic: when Joaquin, frightened of Polly's pregnancy, left to "clean the mail", the deserter threw him in the wake that "you don't have to polish the dagger." While the owner went to Waymar, Snip with Louise was sold without him, and the deserter honestly gave Harmon the proceeds. Good fight with the axe.

## Legacy

Harmon later listed Snip as one of the "scumbags" he had buried, as opposed to "pure souls" like Polly and [[vikhor|Whirlwind]]. He compared it to other killers he knew, and all of them, including Snip, “appeared to be kittens” next to the Hunter. In May 1775, over the abyss, Harmon himself cited Snip as an example of a mercenary marquess Booth, convincing him that the Objects of happiness do not bring: "There was such a Snip, hung on the Object - and died with a bolt in his chest."
