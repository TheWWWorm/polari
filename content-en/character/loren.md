Sir Lauren of Hope is an unknown knight who became the champion of the knightly tournament in Aleridan in the summer of 1773. ([[turnir-v-aleridane|Tournament at Aleridan]]) It tells the merchant Harmon guard [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]]: Harmon saw the tournament with his own eyes. ([[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]) Loren is not in the book, only this story.

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"
On the first day of the tournament, less prominent warriors came out on the list. Two became favorites of the public: Duke Almer's guard Sir Karl the Handsome and Sir Lauren. He “did not look like a prominent figure”: a bald-faced warrior of average height in a gray cloak and an oiled chain mail, with a brown shield. However, in the fight “it seemed as if it was carved from a granite block”: the impact of the spear did not even make him shake in the saddle. In sword fighting, he acted "with a leisurely, confident pressure", did not resort to fints and rounds, pressed the enemy, knocked down and put a blade to the observation gap of the helmet. The first day he and Carl won together.

On the last day, the winners of the nobles had to fight against the winners of the unknown. Lauren, whom Harmon calls "gray", met with the Fang Knight, the first son of the Earl of Northwood ([[krejg-nortvud|Craig Northwood]]). The first and second spears broke without success; on the swords Lauren was stronger, and the Northwooder was counting on a third attack. His blow smashed Loren's shield into chips, the knight "swung back", but miraculously kept in the saddle, and in return struck his opponent out of the saddle under the weight of the patch.

In the final before sunset, Lauren had to fight for the title of champion with the emperor's cousin Sir [[adamar|Adamar]]. Adamar left in all its glory and dedicated the fight to the Duke's daughter, Lauren "did not say a word, just in the shortest way went to the position." In the equestrian fight, Adamar hit harder and more accurately, Lauren almost flew out of the saddle, but resisted; the stands shouted: “Strong Lauren.” Then the fight went on swords, and speed with Lauren's dexterity equalized the chances with a high Adamar in heavy patches. Lauren knocked off Adamar's helmet, but lowered the sword and let him lift the helmet. Adamar managed to knock him down, retreated and said, "Get up, Sir Lauren. I'll pay you back." The fight lasted another ten minutes; tired Adamar lost his balance, and Lauren hit a shield in the shoulder knocked him down and put a blade to his pick.

After winning, Lauren got on a horse, rode around the stands and stopped in front of the balcony of the nobility: “Beautiful lady Alanis,” his bassed voice roared, “I dedicate my victory to you.” Sharp-tongued [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] slyly noticed that she had not heard such words from him before the fight. Lauren replied, unabashedly, "Milady, the true knight serves the lady with deeds accomplished, not promises." Alanis smiled and threw him a flower, and the Duke of Aiden invited the champion to a gala dinner. ([[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]])

Joaquin excitedly exclaimed that the tournament was won by a low-born warrior. Harmon corrected: Lauren was a knight, but not a lord, and a man of low birth often has more courage than spoiled lordly sons; “The nobility of the heart is more important than the nobility of blood.” Then he noticed that at the ducal table poor Lauren among the originals, rightly, had a hard time. The fate of the knight in the books is not named.

## Personality and Appearance
A bassed voice, a braided figure of medium height, a gray cloak, an oiled mail and a brown shield. In battle, he acts slowly and methodically; before the final, according to Harmon, he "didn't say a word".
