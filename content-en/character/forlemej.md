Forlemey is an adjutant of the joker [[menson|Manson]], who for many years fed, dressed and took care of him. Every morning he starts the same way: "Your porridge, Kolpak." Almost everything that is known about him is shown through the eyes of Manson himself in the chapters of “Kopak”, as well as in the testimony at the trial. ([[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]])

## In "Only One Star"
### Prisoner and Babysitter
"It's hard to believe, but Forlemey is a nobleman and a guard." He was assigned to Manson seventeen years ago, under the old emperor. Food joke had to be served by a reliable military, so that no one "soft hearted" it did not poison. For what guilt Tellurian so punished Forlemey, "now you will not remember." Manson still maintained his Yankee pride and vowed not to speak to his jailer. Over time, the guard for faithful service was “renamed” to adjutants, and the vow lost its meaning, but the tradition remained: “the adjutant says, the jester is silent.” One day, six years later, Manson asked what would happen to dinner. Forlemey stared at him and dropped, “Ultimately fooled!” I haven't spoken to him since.

Every morning, Forlemey serves the table according to all the rules and always says “Your porridge”, no matter what is served. The joke he imposes only as much as he can eat, and eats the rest himself directly from the bowl. Looking at him, Manson himself begins to eat. Then the adjutant wears a jester, grumbling, "Here's an old moron," "a cock without feathers." Once at court, where Manson was ridiculed, this rumbling seemed to him a “problem of kindness and care.” Under Adrian, no one dared to talk like that with a joke. No one but Forlemey: "Perhaps he deserved that right."

### Adrian's campaign
In November 1774, in the camp at Secret Bay, Forlemey dressed Manson in a wide-brimmed hat instead of a cap, so that the wind did not freeze the "last brains", and he was the first to notice the Shimmerian sails: "Here they waited." After the Battle of [[melorandzh|Melorange]], he brought to his senses an unconscious jester and told him how the battle was going. In Litland, he brought Manson to see the spider in the bank and told the story of the old castle spider Gordon. From Heart of Light to [[aleridan|Aleridan]], the jester slept in the compartment almost all the time, and Forlemei only woke him up to feed him.

### Letter
In Aleridan, in the palace of the Priory, Forlemei, along with tea, brought Manson an envelope: "To you a letter, Kolpak." ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]) On duty, he read it first: “A girl writes something. “I saw you at the summer ball.” There was gray dust left on the paper with his fingerprints. When the aide-de-camp left, Manson went to the sheet and saw the sign of Ulyanina Dust with the order to kill the emperor. ([[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) The night before the crash, the sentinel did not let the jester to Vladyka and offered: “Okay, I’ll call your Forlemey. ([[krushenie-na-beke|Beck Train Disaster]]) You will tell him, and he is the Lord.” Manson refused: “Forlemaeus can’t!”

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
### Witness at trial
On May 25, 1775, at the third meeting, Francis-Ilian summoned Forlemey along with the guardsman [[sanders|Sir Sanders]]. Seeing him, Manson escaped from the guard and rushed around his neck: “Forlemey! Gods! You..." The adjutant said, “What have you done? Skinny, boney... You are my poor man!”

Sanders said he pulled Forlemey out of the river in the crash. Then they were picked up by a fisherman, and the priest sent to the Wilhelm monastery, where both were ill for a long time. Forlemey confirmed everything and added one thing: "the cars were burning, all in smoke and fire." Neither on the balcony, nor in the case of tea, he was not ("This would not have happened then, because I was looking at the cap well. He was like a child to me.” But he noticed a change in the joke back in Aleridan: “For sixteen years I was with him, day after day... As the letter was filed, so something clicked in Manson." He described the letter as ordinary, from the girl from the summer ball, "If there was something terrible, I would not give the letter to the cap." Accusations of manhood he called “slander and slander”: “the cap to the ladies is like a bear to a raspberry!”

[[mark-voron|Mark]] asked if Forlemey believed that Manson had killed the lord. “I don’t believe it!” he replied. Then the Raven told him to look the joke in the face. Manson held the former adjutant's gaze for only a few seconds and lowered his eyes. "Are you?... Cap, why?... How could you?” exhaled Forlemey.

In his testimony, printed in The Voice of the Crown, Manson said that he and Adrian sailed, helping each other, "like Forlemey with a guard." The boatman whom they later met, he considered "the same fisherman that Forlemea saved."

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
In September 1775, in Faunterra, Manson, receiving a salary from Minerva, hired the "old day-keeper" Forlemey: "Forlemaeus to me - as a native. He was out of service, you can’t help him!” He paid him a month in advance, although, according to [[karen|Karen]], the daycare was of little use: she took care of her husband herself. In the autumn, Forlemey traveled with his spouses to the south, in [[soltaun|Soltown]] he packed their luggage and was unhappy when Karen gave his own servants. In the Southern Way, when Manson again began to wonder bumblebees from the mouth of Adrian, Forlemey was the first to understand what happened: “Kolpak, my dear you! The old one is back, right?” According to him, the joker again “salad in the brain”. Karen sent him to look for the bubble that Manson was sending for in the hallway to whisper to her husband that she had sent her brother an encrypted wave.

## Personality and Appearance
Forlemey is "clay and faded, like a hundred times erased uniform." He has dull eyes, bristly cheeks, a protruding lower lip and a fluffy but "powerless" mustache hanging below his chin. He grumbles, speaks colloquially and jokingly treats him like a capricious child, but is sincerely attached to him. Manson says the same thing.

## Trivia
- The service life is named differently. In November 1774 ("One Star"), Forlemey joked "seventeen years", and at a trial in May 1775, he himself said: "Sixteen years I am with him."
- In "Only One Star" he is an "adjutant", in "Shadow of the Great Tree" - "dayling" and "ordinary". Manson he invariably calls "Kolpak".
