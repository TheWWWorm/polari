## Burgomaster of the capital

Baron Escher was mayor of [[faunterra|Faunterra]] during the reign of [[adrian|Adrian]]. [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] characterized him as a "humanitarian" of a kind who is "famous for devotion": "Adrian trusts Escher." When [[minerva|Mira]] saw him for the first time in December 1774, she was faced with a “yellow-faced and sharp-nosed cannibal”. He did not respond to the Lord, but he did.

## Removal of Items

On the sixth of December 1774, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] stormed the [[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]] (see the [[bitva-za-faunterru]]). On the seventh of December, learning of this, Esher ordered the Dynasty’s Objects removed from the capital and loaded onto a train; Captain [[uitmor|Whitmore]] commanded the company guarding them. The train went to [[marengo|Marengo]], the summer palace of the emperor. On the way, the guards were poisoned with water, and at Orwell’s train attacked by unknown and stole a wagon with Objects: stolen almost all the property of the Dynasty, except for twenty Objects at the University and the Yangmei Power Gloves (for more details, see [[persty-vilgelma]]). Alanis, seeing the rails, guessed: “This is Escher... Escher was taking out the Items while Backfield was banging his head against the walls of the palace!" Together with Major Backfield, Escher tried to clear the capital of the northerners and sent letters to the great lords asking for military assistance, and [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] told Minerva that Escher had removed the Items from under the nose of the Kairs. ([[krojdon-bekfild|Croydon Beckfield]]) The siege of the palace was led by Beckfield, who recruited, according to Escher, "everyone who is capable of holding a weapon." According to Escher himself, “dignified philistines are difficult to convince to fight ... "Origin is an origin." On the train to Marengo, as he sadly reported to Mira, one hundred and fifty selected warriors were killed. When it came to the train itself, the mayor mumbled, blushed and left to explain to the general. Then he hurried to assure the lady that the Objects were looking for: the police were mobilized, and Sheriff Faunterra himself left the city with five hundred constables. Erwin, entrusting Mark with the investigation, admitted that Escher "was so disgruntled" that he took the Items away before the Northerners got to them.

## Suspect

### Evidence

The investigation that [[mark-voron|Mark]] studied in A Doll on the Throne almost entirely pointed to Escher. For the evacuation, he chose exactly the train that stood at the station for a day without a driver, and there was enough time to poison the tanks with water. The bandits needed at least half a day to prepare an ambush at Orwell, which means that they received orders in advance. After the abduction, Escher developed a "boiling activity": he sent a hundred constables to Hope and ordered another hundred to interrogate the metropolitan apothecaries. Finally, the station protection agent, who kept a book of observations at the station, was stabbed, and the book disappeared. Deputy Sheriff Fairfax was ready to "at least present the case in court tomorrow." Red's protection agent, recounting the same version, said that the burgomaster was familiar with the gang leader and the Items now lie in Darkwater, "in Baron Escher's chests."

### Resignation and Escape

Erwin, becoming Lord Chancellor, himself suspected Escher, displaced him and handed the scarf to burgomaster Alanis Almere. But he didn't arrest the baron until the evidence showed. Escher took advantage of this and fled from the capital: he sat on a schooner going to the South, in [[darkvoter|Darkwater]]. There was a Lady-in-the-Dark rule that "Origin can't stand," so it was hard to get a fugitive out of there. ([[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]]) Proponents of the prosecution considered it a mistake of the duke and expected that the mistress would demand the extradition of the fugitive, and with him the Objects would return.

### Mark's Doubts

Mark didn't believe the reports. Against Escher said everything, “except two strange things. “Except for three.” He reasoned that the speed and cunning of the plan do not resemble either ordinary bandits or a burgomaster:

> "Rich, well-fed, cautious Yelenoff... He’s not the kind of guy who would put everything at stake, including his own head.”

The Guards captain Whitmore was also sure: "Burgomaster Escher is definitely innocent." They deliberated in the town hall half that night, and when the captain left, "the pharmacies were already on fire." In a conversation with Grandfather, Mark admitted that he did not think about Escher’s guilt at all, but was looking for answers to questions that no one before him had explained. Such plans, he noted, “are not built overnight.” Mark came to the conclusion that the Items were stolen by a brigade with the Wilhelm's Fingers (which, as it turned out, was commanded by [[paul|Paul]]). Mark himself proceeded from the fact that Escher, like Captain Whitmore, learned about the evacuation of the Objects only on the night of the assault.

## Lineage

Alanis and the narrative in "Only One Star" call Escher a cannibal, and Mark in "A Doll on the Throne" is a "Hellenic".
