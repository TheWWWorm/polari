## Overview

Count Elias Northwood is the head of the Great House of Northwood and the ruler of Northwood County. ([[dom-nortvud|House Northwood]]; [[nortvud-zemlya|Northwood (County)]]) By the beginning of the events of the cycle, he is a deep old man: in the year of his wedding with [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil]], he turned sixty, and he was four decades older than his wife. From his first marriage, he has three sons - [[krejg-nortvud|Craig]], Horace and Donald; [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]] was considered the only child of Elias and Sybil, although in fact her father was [[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]].

The count himself was interested only in ships - river and sea, their equipment and driving qualities. Sybil was in charge of all the other affairs of the county, and Elias did not delve into them unnecessarily. According to [[minerva|Mira]]'s observations, he hated to leave his castle and even went to the city once a month to inspect some vessel.

## Appearance and Personality

Elias wore snow-stained hair to his shoulders, intercepted by a northern tradition with a silver hoop; otherwise he looked like a skinny, bile-haired, brusque old man with few teeth. Mira in Waymar called him “yellowish”, and Inge Price, seeing how the old man is planting his rich-son, mockingly remarked: “The skeleton commands the bear.” ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]]) By 1775, the count was tormented by a dry cough, which he took away with endless cups of hot tea.

Sybil, recalling his prediction of [[adrian|Adrian]]’s ingratitude, admitted: her “cloudy grumbling Elias” hates people, says only abominations about them — and always gets to the point. The Count considered it the first duty of the Lord to take care of his people, and not to pursue fame, and came to old age fatalism: a person, even if he is a genius, even a king, only rushes to where his stream of history carries. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] believed that "Elias is tired of life."

## History

### Marriage to Sybil (1756)

In August 1756, when [[uejmar|Waymar]] celebrated the arrival of the Seventeenth Gift, the Northwoods sailed there last: Count Elias with a young wife, all three sons and eight dozen knights. Count [[vinsent-shejland|Vincent Shayland]] explained to his sons that Elias was deliberately emphasizing his non-interference in the recent war to Vladyka Tellurian: the sunsets called Northwood to their side - to beat Shayland and divide the Objects - and the count "struggling". Elias himself laughed that Tellurian did not wait for him and went to the bed of Dar, “but I made him hurry.” When Martin Shayland suggested that the count was delayed because of his young wife, Vincent countered: so Elias himself would say, "because he is proud that he is still able to have fun with someone in his sixties." To young [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]], the Earl introduced Sybil as "the first beauty of the North", his wife. At the feast, Vittor seated Bishop Gallard Almer next to him; the count was tired at the second dance and watched with complacency as the cavaliers nearly fought for his wife while Gallard led her in the dance. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]) According to [[navi|Navi]], that's when [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]] was conceived.

### Bride Swapping (1774)

In March 1774, when Sybil sheltered an orphaned World in the Bear Fang and announced that the murder of Sir Clive Stagforth was the beginning of a conspiracy against the throne, Elias only snarled: "Conspiracy. ([[klyk-medvedya|Bear's Fang]]; [[klajv-stagfort|Clive Stagfort]]) “Another dirty conspiracy.” Sybil went with Mira to the capital, and the count went with the children to the wedding in the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], having instructed his wife: The dynasty does not like to feel obliged; when heads fly, Sybil's head simply will not be among them - that's all the gratitude, and Myra Adrian "and will not notice at all."

The wife's relationship with the stableman, Lord Clarence, ended with a Northwood baron summoning Clarence to a duel and stabbing him, believing that this connection stained the name of Count Elias. ([[lord-klarens|Clarence]])

### The Prisoner of Waymar (Autumn 1774)

In the autumn of 1774, Adrian summoned the Count and his sons to [[faunterra|Faunterra]] by order. On 15 September, Sybil assured the emperor that her lord husband with the "glorious sons" was already on his way to the capital to take an oath in person: the Northwoods would descend on [[torrej|Torrey]] to Waymar, cross the Smokey Dahl and be in Faunterra three weeks later. But in Waymar, they were detained by vassals of Vittor Shayland; Vittor's men also brought Mira, who was secretly abducted from the monastery. In early October, she was taken to a refectory where three Northwoods, Elias, Craig, and Horace, were seated. Wittor deliberately put her at the same table with people who "used her for their game". Seeing her, Elias muttered "Your Highness..." and began to justify the delay in the journey, as if talking with the bride of the emperor. From this Mir concluded that the old count knew nothing about the intrigue of his wife, and only the heir Craig was initiated into it. When Craig tried to send a letter, his father stopped him from the window: “We are in a trap. If you pull, you will break your paw.”

Arriving Vittor showed the Northwoods a dove letter to Adrian: Sybil and Gloria are arrested, the whole House of Northwood is accused of conspiracy and summoned to court. Elias decided that he was being blackmailed, and [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]] offered to support his brother's rebellion. ([[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern Uprising]]) The Count refused to put fifteen thousand of his soldiers "under Adrian's spark spears" and ridiculed her calls as cheap manipulation:

> "Honor, dignity, victory, glory... What precious empty sounds! The Lord has to take care of his people.”

Then Iona turned to Craig and easily persuaded him to his side. Elias shouted to his son that he would die with the whole army, and, as Mira realized, it was with these words that he finally pushed Craig to the rebels. The exhausted old man only asked: “Let me leave the ships...” Vittor promised him books, visits to Waymar lakeyards, and sailing trips — in any direction except the Bear Fang. Iona assured Craig that his father would be held in Waymar for the rest of the war with all due respect. At the common table, the count rarely appeared and avoided Mira: she was a living testimony of his humiliation. Mira herself, being in the same position with him, felt sympathy for the gall old man and "some sense of kinship" and tried to talk to him, but without success. Her secret letter, which stated that "Count Elias is captured in Waymar, Northwood is ruled by Craig", was intercepted by Shayland's men.

### At the Villa at Torrey (Spring 1775)

In the spring of 1775 Elias no longer lived in the castle, but in a coastal villa ten miles from Waymar. On May 27, 1775, he was visited by Iona. Kyr [[irving|Irving]] learned from the old servants that at the time of the appearance of the Waymar prisoner in the castle, a Northwood spy was acting, exposed and hanged two years later, and Iona came to the count for information. She caught him demonstrating the model of the ship in the pool: a spark machine with a pendulum in the body gave a current from the rolling of waves, and flashlights lit up on the masts. Elias, now and then coughing, explained that the full sample is not yet available, but such an engine will allow the ship to go without wind - in a narrow channel, between reefs and among ice floes.

The count spoke coldly to Iona: she and her brother “used my sons as a fighting hammer, and I was simply swept out of the way.” He refused the offer to return power to him: he had enough politics, which he played at thirty, forty and fifty years. Craig he called the bear, which the gods cursed, from birth giving strength, and his hope called Sybil: she had enough intelligence to rule and charisma to cope with Craig. Appointing Horace as his heir would mean signing his death sentence: two-thirds of Northwood warriors adored Craig.

Elias then shared the long-held conclusions of his spies. During the War of the Objects, he fed spies in all the Great Houses and learned about the honorary prisoner in the Waymar tower, which was visited by Count Vincent himself. Elias suspected that the prisoner was a vassal of the Origins, whom Vincent was preparing for a conspiracy, and later allowed another version - that it was the surviving bodyguard of Wittor or Martin, who saw some secret. He said that the sunset general [[oris|Oris]], who had called Northwood into the allies, had a noticeable advantage, but retreated just a day before the kairs approached, and linked this retreat to a secret found in the Dara lodge. Finally, the old man’s remark that the poisoned letter can not be trusted (“If you want to kill a man with one blow, use a blade”), led Iona to guess about the death of Count Vincent.

### Shayland's puppet (summer - autumn 1775)

In the early summer of 1775, Vittor Shayland sent Baron [[dorkaster|Dorcaster]] with a detachment and Wilhelm's Feather to the Bear Fang, taking Elias with him. The old man tried all the way to escape, then die. Dorcaster interrupted Craig's supporters, pinned to the count of the healer with soothing drops, and sent word across Northwood that "the glorious Count Elias had regained legitimate power." When Craig besieged his own capital and threatened to burn it down, Elias tried to commit suicide by jumping off the tower, but Dorcaster caught him and tied him up. In the negotiations, the baron offered Craig the division of power, the fleet to the old man and the dry son, the line along the [[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]], and eventually, in the name of "a little alive from the soothing drops," Elias surrendered the castle.

Returning, Craig beat his father - the old man had a broken nose and a twisted jaw - and walled him up with Horace in the dungeon. After Craig's death at Bald Dole in October 1775, they were pulled out into the light. Elias' supporters - sailors, artisans, and furriers - called themselves "sedentary" in honor of the old count and branded the clothes with a white stripe, and Craig's followers were beaten as "drums". In front of David’s father, the frightened count rode to the crowd on a white horse, blabbed “Glory to Susan...”, said that he was mourning for his son, and when the bears asked for the army of the “Chosen One”, he blessed them with a trembling hand. ([[otets-david|Father David]]) From that time on, Northwood came under the rule of Elias, "essentially a puppet of the Puppeteer."

## Relationships

- **[[sibil-nortvud|Sybil]]** is the second wife to rule the county for him. The Earl appreciated her intelligence and charisma: according to him, Sybil was Northwood's hope, able to cope with Craig. At Faraday-Riley Hospital, semi-insane Sybil murmurs: "Poor my innocent Elias... They ate you too.”
- **[[krejg-nortvud|Craig]]** is the eldest son and heir. His father did not believe in his mind, he said that the day would be happy for Northwood when Craig died, and in 1775 he was beaten by him and thrown into prison.
- Horace is the middle son, in 1774 a thirty-year-old judge who was in charge of collecting taxes and courts of the county; he shared his imprisonment with his father and went with him to the crowd. Once the count gave the Church a gift of the Blessing on the occasion of the recovery of Horace from a serious illness.
- Donald is the youngest son, the third in line of succession; he went to war with Craig and, according to him, he and his brother did not know about the plans of his stepmother.
- **[[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]]** was considered his only child by Sybil; in fact, Gallard's daughter Almera. Mira realized that the count knew nothing about the substitution.
- **[[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]** - in 1756, a young man who "can flatter wisely", in 1774 - a jailer, in 1775 - the owner who returned the throne to the count.
- **[[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]]** took away his son and army; later the count gave her a lesson in politics and unwittingly led to the solution of Vincent's death.
