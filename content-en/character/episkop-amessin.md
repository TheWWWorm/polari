## Who He Is

Gregor Amessin is the Bishop of [[aleridan|Aleridan]] and head of the War Brotherhood of the Forefather Wilhelm, warrior monks wearing futanas on top of armor. The prelates of the brotherhood "contest with each other forever," and only the "iron hand" of Amessin can unite them. In 1775, he was a representative of the Church of the Forefathers in the House; his mother Alice called him "the right hand of Gallard Almer", and [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] and [[minerva|Minerva]] called him "the handyman of Gallard" and an ally of the Puppeteer. ([[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) His voice is powerful; he says "hard and dry, as if he were knocking with a hammer", and flattery is not given to him: compliments "fell out of his mouth like lumps of dry dirt". Minerva was like the late Gallard.

## The “Yanmei Hunt”

At the spring session of the House in May 1775, Amessin gave a lengthy speech about the wave of atrocities of the past year - the shooting of people by the Persians, the burning of Evergard, the theft of the Objects of the Dynasty, sabotage on the bridge, the murder of Vladyka Adrian and the assassination of Queen Madeline. On behalf of the Church of the Forefathers, he proposed the creation of a "sacred guard" subordinate to the House and Church, not to the Crown, and to take as its basis the Order of Wilhelm; most of the Lords agreed with him.

Erwin, meanwhile, intercepted his pigeon letter from Faunterra to Evergard, sent to Gallard on May 11, the first day of the House's work, when Minerva announced a census of Items: "Your Eminence, what about the apple?" Having presented this note to the House, Erwin explained that Ulyana Dust has the shape of an apple. ([[ulyanina-pyl|Ulyana's Dust]]) Amessin rose up and called the Lord Chancellor's speech "disgusting heresy", unworthy of controversy, but Mother [[mat-kordeliya|Cordelia]], on behalf of Pramatery, replied that she did not consider it either heretical or unfounded.

## In "Humans and Gods"

Amessin was present in the capital at the Ecumenical Council and the only one of all the participants voted against the deposition of Gallard; the decision was made without him, and he immediately left Faunterra. The Northerners felt that he remained on Gallard's side and the Wilhelmines "would never go against their master"; while the bishop was on the road, the Brotherhood battalions acted each on their own.

In the summer of 1775, crowds of poor preachers gathered in Faunterra, threatening to punish the gods for "greed, lust and laziness." Mother Alice asked Minerva to disperse the gatherings, but the sheriff showed that the preachers had permits signed by Amessin: the bishop himself was not accused of anything, and his signature was legal. Minerva refused to use force.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree

After Gallard's death, Amessin's pigeon ribbon from the fighting fraternity was among four reports that Vittor Shayland had died, returned to life, and [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] killed the priory. Among the allies of the "Lord Father" Vittor, who were waiting in the First Winter, was also called "Bishop Amessin of the brotherhood of Wilhelm." The essay “Abbot Amessin” on the resurrection of the Chosen One spread among the clergy of the Forefathers and through the channels of the Maximian Order came to [[adrian|Adrian]]. From it, Adrian realized that [[absolyut|the Absolute]] worked only after the death of the carrier, and not from fainting, which means that a spark will help against him.

## In "Fantasies"

"Fantasies" the author calls the possible fates of the heroes.

According to their account, Amessin led the monks of the brotherhood to the First Winter and personally blessed the archers with the Rings, breaking Wilhelm's taboo. The monks were defeated, and the other bishops of the Forefathers, "who were fed up with the domination of the Wilhelmines", accused him of heresy. Amessin countered that the Origins were also fought by two ring-bearers, including the Northern Princess, and he himself did not take the Ring in his hands. His Wilhelmines stood for the sanctity of Gallard and Paul; the bishop glorified Gallard's martyrdom and attacked "vicious Iona".

In the spring of 1776, together with [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]], the mothers of Orlando and Alicia and the Second of the Five, he came to the show of [[nikolas-olli|Nicholas Ollie]]'s speaking device. ([[mat-alisiya|Mother Alicia]]) There he exchanged quotes from the Scriptures with Alicia, “evil pressed his lips” to the joke of the king and announced the will of the Church: within six months all finger-bearers should take penance, and reward will be assigned for the heads of unrepentant ones. He demanded to sell the device to the Church ("Money does not matter!"), gave up the right to buy the chapter, threatened that the Church would not be considered "with some burgomaster", since even Iona Origin "kneed before us", and offered the scientist protection from a dozen monks of the Combat Brotherhood. The king later explained that the bishop was not going to pay: there was no money from the highest ranks of the Church. The recorded address of the prophet ended with the voice of Amessin: “I, Bishop Amessin of the Order of the Forefather Wilhelm, assure all that has been said.”

In August 1776, Amessin came to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]] as an ambassador of the Forefather's branch with his mother Alicia. Erwin wanted to "spit the bishop in the face." Without resting from the road, Amessin demanded peace from him with Steppe, accused him of "feudal pride" and threatened to punish the gods if a new war broke out, and Erwin in response asked if he did not claim the rank of primate. In the clinic, the bishop urged Minerva to repent for the battle with the Gauntlet of Power, interpreting Wilhelm's taboo "widely and metaphorically". Erwin suggested that he fall to his knees, because under his flag, too, fought fingernails, and Mother Alicia admitted that the mistress, who did not kill anyone with her hand, can not be exacted.

The next day, Amessin came to Minerva for construction, apologized for the pressure and, on behalf of the Church of the Forefathers, offered her votes in the election. In return, she was supposed to sign a list of candidates for the archdiocese, where both Francis-Ilianus and Amessin himself stood: The forefathers wanted to elect the archpriest before the election of the lord, so that the “solid spiritual leader” would bring the common people to their senses. He hinted that the Chapter was dissatisfied with Cordelia's loan, and demanded to sign immediately, otherwise the list would be burned. Minerva signed, receiving a service in return: the printing shop of the Wilhelm Order in Almer, whose brothers “know how to keep secrets”, will print a secret circulation for her. Mother Alicia, for her part, made it clear to Erwin that she would like to see Amessin removed from the list. In negotiations for peace with the Shavans, the bishop “is softer than Alicia, but also with pressure” told Minerva that elections would fail without peace if she did not curb the willful Erwin.

### "Ad-ri-an!"
The election of the archpriest took place “hurry, ahead of time”, even before the election of the ruler. In the Cathedral of Wilhelm, four cardinals counted the cards of one hundred and twelve prelates: Amessin received forty-one, Nellis-Lyon (Francis-Ilian) - thirty-nine. The voice of the Crown announced that Wilhelm's new parish and fighting brotherhood would "ensure the security of the House meeting." The monks of Amessin rose throughout the city and replaced the city guard. In October 1776, Amessin in the mantle of the Priory, together with Magda, met Minerva on Vokzalnaya Square. He advised her to withdraw her candidacy so that Adrian would "lean toward mercy", and said he would vote for Adrian himself. The world his "spider fingers" reminded Gallard. In the Chamber, his place was contested: "the current arch-rival fought on the side of the Kuklavod". According to Adrian's plan, the "arrest" of the Priory was to leave the Chamber with him. Amessin announced that the Church of the Forefathers recognized Adrian as emperor, and headed for the exit, but Minerva with the Glove lifted him into the air. If the ruler is Adrian, she said, then her consent to the election of the archpriest is invalid, and with him his rank. "I'm staying," Amessin replied.

On the third day of the meeting, four cardinals confessed before the House that Amessin had bribed them. Cardinal Morgret Amalia Heather of the Katrina family received four thousand golden Hilt and counted Amessin five votes cast for Francis-Ilian. The second Amessin promised the estate and the castle, the third was cheaper, and the fourth called the fee so shameful that the hall sighed. All four confirmed that most Amessin received only by deception. Mother Alicia announced that he was deprived of the mantle of the priest, and demanded arrest. Minerva carried the mantle on the shoulders of Francis-Ilianus, and Amessina was escorted away by an azure guard. To the cardinals, the prophet promised mercy, and to the "former bishop of Amessin" - a bonfire in memory of Gallard, who "resurrected the tradition of burning heretics."
