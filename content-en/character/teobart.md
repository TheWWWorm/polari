## Biography
Theobart is an old kair in the service of the [[dom-oridzhin|House Origin]], whom [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] had known since childhood. The sixth son of a poor cyre, he received a red and black coat at seventeen, and at twenty-three became the armorer of the Duke of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] himself. They went through several wars together. It was said that Theobarth twice saved the great lord's life and one of these debts the duke had already returned. In the [[pervaya-zima|first winter]], he had a wife and three daughters. He was not a hothead and hoped to live to old age, get a piece of land from the Duke for faithful service and leave his daughters a good inheritance.

### Appearance
Erwin described him as a wrinkled stern face, a gray beard, a shaved skull and cold but clear eyes without a hint of cunning.

### As a child, Erwin
Theobart accompanied Desmond when he once, as a child, Erwin, went with his son to visit Duke Aiden Almere in [[evergard|Evergard]]. A dozen years later, in Humans and Gods, Erwin recalls the way on the dam from Beck and how Theobarth hurried and urinated from it into the pond, evoking the frogs.

### In Shayland in 1756
In Humans and Gods, there is a memory of August 1756, when the Seventeenth Gift came to Shayland. At the Origins' camp near [[uejmar|Waymar]], the Duke of Desmond ordered the skinhead Theobart to show the Sacred Objects to young [[vittor-shejland|Vittor]] and [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]]. Vittor refused the examination and asked to escort them out of the camp.

## Expedition for the River
In 1774, Theobart led a detachment of kairs and greys, who accompanied Erwin on an expedition for the River. ([[ekspedicija|Expedition beyond the River]]) There were about 40 armed men in the squad. Erwin believed that his father had placed Theobart with him as a “nanny”, experienced, reasonable, skinhead and very quick in handling the sword.

### Clashes with Jamie
At the very beginning of the way, Kair [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]] began to argue with Erwin because of a wounded gray named Buck. Theobart listened silently to the argument, and then repeated the order himself: "Follow the order, cairn." Jemis smiled. This captain, out of good intentions, showed everyone that the Kairs obeyed him, not the Duke's son. Erwin thought it was "the worst thing that could have been invented." In retaliation, he defiantly did not listen to the advice of Theobart about the place of the night and ordered to put himself a separate fire. He also assigned Jemis to the night watch, although the captain noticed that it was the work of the Greys, not the knights.

### Counsellor and Commander
During the campaign, Theobarth sent kairs to hunt and chose places for the camp. He called the abuse of a mechanic Luis, for example, a dead badger in his bed, “pardoning pranks”: people are bored, and it is useful for the victim to learn to be cool. ([[luis-mariya|Louis Maria]]) When an avalanche came down in the mountains at night, he confirmed Erwin's decision to stay put and take cover at the edge of the glacier. In the fishing village of [[spot|Spot]], Erwin gave the villagers half the supplies, and the captain warned him that the detachment would not turn back when the food ran out: the Duke's order was to go east until there was room for a spark shop. He admired the lord’s “generosity” and he understood the hint that generosity was at someone else’s expense.

In the swamps of the Soft Fields, the squad attacked the fangs. Theobart shot and screamed the beast at himself and lured him into a thin net of swamp grass next to the wormwood where the donkey drowned. He himself jumped to the side, and the fangs failed and drowned. Theobarth, who praised him, turned away: to praise the commander means to evaluate him. Instead of a statutory reply to Erwin, he said, "Don't risk your life, my lord," because every kair of the detachment would have to answer for his death to the duke.

One night Erwin found a poisonous snake in his tent and built a detachment. Theobarth stood between the lord and Jemis and took charge of all the kairs. It turned out that the snake for the poison caught a young guide [[kid|Kid]]. The captain recalled the Northern law, but Erwin forgave the boy. Theobart then whispered to the lord to apologize to Jemis for the suspicion. Erwin refused, but apologized to the captain himself for not believing his surety.

Later, the detachment crossed the area of "bad air", where the grass dried up. Theobart offered to bypass it, but Erwin listened to the guides and led the people straight. When a fire started in the back and the swamp gas broke out, the captain instantly divided the rearguard into two wings. The Kairs trampled a strip of raw grass in the path of fire, and the humans and animals Theobart sent forward.

### Mutiny and Intelligence
When Jemis tried to take Erwin's power and send him home, Theobart was not in the camp. Erwin initially thought of calling the captain, but realized that if the rebels obeyed Theobart, it would become clear who the real commander was. He did it himself (see Jemis rebellion). ([[myatezh-dzhemisa|Jemis's Rebellion]]) Returning from the intelligence Captain Erwin reported only that Jemis tried to raise a riot and was punished.

[[reka|The river]] has no place to build the dam. Theobart could not find anything to object to when Baron Philip Lowfert suggested returning. Beyond the River, the scouts found a dried-up body, and Theobart, keeping his cool, noticed that something had dried up the man before the wolves and worms reached him.

### The Dara Lodge
On 8 June 1774, at Dar's looted lodge, Theobart demanded a report from Kair Frederick, who was searching in the lodge, and reported to Erwin on the descent. According to him, the first group reached the bottom and found human bones, but not Objects, and urged Erwin not to lose hope.

### Death
On the night of June 9, 1774, the camp was suddenly attacked. Theobart was not on the watch, and the attack caught him in a tent. Without armor and boots, in one shirt, he grabbed a sword, ran to the glade and immediately got hit in the stomach. In response, he stabbed the enemy and crawled back into the bushes before he was noticed. All night and morning, he clenched his wound and waited for one of his kairs to survive and be able to take revenge.

In the morning, he was found wounded by Erwin. He at first thought that savages were attacking, but the captain pointed bitterly to the bodies: spark spears, swords, mails, uniformed scarlet shirts, guard boots, and speech, "as in Faunterra," are "the people of the Crown." He told Erwin to write down everything that happened, to nail the note with a dagger to a tree higher so that it would not be pulled by wolves, and then shoot himself with a crossbow: "It's easier than cutting your throat." Erwin said he would survive. Then Theobart asked for a stroke of mercy, and after being refused, he threw: “Nezhenka... Sheep." Erwin put a dagger on his chest and walked away, not wanting to be a witness. The captain whispered a prayer and killed himself.

## Legacy
Theobart's last words were both an injection and a challenge for Erwin. As he made his way home, he would say, "I'll survive," and thought he would survive Teobart's misfortune: let him know he was wrong. In a moment of despair, he admitted that Theobart was right and the best way out was a crossbow, but still continued on. He later told Jemis, and then his father, that the dying Theobarth called the assailants the people of the Crown, and backed up these words with boots, shirts, and spark spears of the slain. In "Only One Star", [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis]], going over those who are able to tell Erwin the truth before leaving, noted: "Captain Theobart is dead."

In The Shadow of the Great Tree, Erwin, looking at the names of all those he lost in two years, sees a stone statue similar to Theobart. He remembers him as his father's "former Grey", who brought him alive to the River and died in despair. Erwin gave the rich flax to Theobart's family, but doubted it would fix anything. Later, when he saw [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]] with his head shaved, he compared her to Captain Theobarth.

## Personality
Theobart was an experienced, cold-blooded and short-tempered warrior who was able to make decisions in battle without fuss. To the son of a suzerain, he was treated with respectful care, which often hurt Erwin's self-esteem. He expressed his objections not directly, but in the form of information (“night watch is a matter of grays”) or emphasized omissions. He sincerely considered Erwin to be a “tender”, and his last words expressed bitter disappointment.
