## Overview
Aiden Ellis Gwenda of the Agatha family ruled the Duchy of Almer. ([[almera-zemlya|Duchy of Almera]]) He owned fifty cities, six navigable rivers and twenty million acres of land in the heart of Polaris. He was a peer of [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] and a distant relative in Agatha's blood.

The Duke's appearance was recognizable: a hawk's nose, grey sideburns, angular cheekbones, prominent lower jaw. Mira, seeing him for the first time, found him exactly as she imagined the author of the book about the Shutov conspiracy: majestic, intelligent, dispassionate, narcissistic, dressed in silver and gold and ruthless enough to torture the man who saved his life.

## Youth and the Path to Power
The old Duke of Almera, who died shortly before the Shutov plot, had two sons. The younger, Aiden, was as arrogant and arrogant as most of the Agathans, but in addition cunning, far-sighted and perceptive: from childhood he easily managed the nannies, father's guards and the Duke himself. The elder, [[gallard-almera|Gallard]], was stubborn and fiery and restored the entire castle of Aleridan against himself. The father reasoned that the best ruler would be the youngest son, and sent Gallard to the theological seminary. According to the "Guide to the Lands of Polari", Almer's prosperity is due to the policy of the last two dukes, Astor and Aiden: having received the right to spark shops, they made them the engine of industry, and their banks lent artisans and manufacturers.

In the [[zolotaya-vojna|Golden War]], around 1750, the Lighthart House successfully attacked Almera, and the emperor did not intervene. ([[dom-lajthart|House Lightheart]]) To help the Almerans came the Origins, and later Aiden admitted to Desmond: "You saved my father in the Golden War."

Aidan gained influence through the Shutov conspiracy. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]) Captain John Corvis, his vassal from [[aleridan|Aleridan]], knighted by the Duke himself, informed Aiden in advance of the plot being prepared. Aiden did not report to the emperor at once, leaving himself time to choose whose side it is more profitable to take, and then at the decisive moment rushed to the capital, “save the lord”, became the first adviser to the emperor Telurian and the strongest feudal lord of the Empire. Corvis died under torture and was unable to testify. Later, Aiden wrote the book “The Fool’s Conspiracy: Persons and Roles”, and the court knew the official version of the events from it. [[menson|Manson]], once a member of the conspiracy, hated him fiercely.

After becoming the first advisor, Aiden gained unprecedented trading privileges and rights to a second spark dam for his land, and Almera became the richest land of the Empire. Adrian recalled that Tellurian had given Almera a third spark shop. Aiden also planned to expand the rail route from Aleridan to the Heart of Light: add two tracks and triple the number of trains. After his death, the project was frozen, and in 1775 it was resumed by Gallard and Farway. Hoping to find an ally in his brother, he bribed, coaxed, blackmailed, and threatened Gallard with the mantle of Archbishop Faunterra and the parish priest of the Proto-Patriarchal Church. The soldiers spoke highly of the Almeran army: the Duke did not allow the knights to relax, regularly organized tournaments and maneuvers and twice sent them on trips to the West for experience. One of his festivities was a knightly tournament in Aleridan in the summer of 1773. ([[turnir-v-aleridane|Tournament at Aleridan]])

## Seventeenth Gift
In August 1756, the Duke of Aiden accompanied the Emperor Thelurian to the site of the Seventeenth Gift near [[uejmar|Waymar]] (the flashback for "The Price of Fear" in [[lyudi-i-bogi|Humans and Gods]]). The Almer brothers provided their fleet to transport the court across the Smokey Dahl to drive next to the lord; Ayden did not take the family, as the emperor also rode without his wife. Aiden, who was then marking the first advisers, constantly accompanied Telurian and inserted his remarks into the conversations.

When Desmond Origin was guarding Dar's lodge, Aiden, under the lord and prince, politely asked to change the guard of the northerners to the imperial guard. Desmond burst into flames, recalling that Almera's troops had not come to Shayland's aid. Aiden replied that Desmond had saved his father in the Golden War. By this he reminded the lord that the northerners were wayward, and the emperor took his side.

Later in the cave, the Duke became one of the few witnesses to the mystery: in the bed of Dar, a dead woman was found, similar to the Mother. Aiden insisted that the find should be hidden. In his opinion, the Church is the main source of danger, and the “Fallen Mother” will give rise to hordes of fanatics, and fictional ones will be added to the seventeen true Foremothers. The young [[vittor-shejland|Vittor]] and [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]] were caught there, and the emperor ordered Aiden and Desmond to be killed. Desmond refused; Aiden said "Badness" with contempt and drew a sword over Martin, but the execution was stopped, and the matter ended with Desmond's oath to vouch for the silence of the Shaylands.

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"
### Union for the Daughter
In the spring and summer of 1774, the duke and his daughter lived in [[faunterra|Faunterra]]; in Almera itself, according to rumors, they expected that at the summer games the lord would choose Alanis as a bride. A cold and vain first counselor sought the marriage of his daughter [[alanis-almera|Alanis]] to the emperor [[adrian|Adrian]]. He supported the Lord's reforms and played with him in the strata. ([[stratemy|Stratems]]) For the sake of his daughter, Aidan assembled a coalition. He promised Desmond Origin freedom from the new tax for the entire Alanis reign, and Nadezhda - to reduce the expectation of the right to a spark dam. ([[nadezhda|Hope]]) On September 2, 1773, at a reception at the Pera and Sword Palace, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] demanded more payment from him. ([[dvorets-pera-i-mecha|Palace of Pen and Sword]]) Aiden angrily refused, saying that Erwin's father had lost his mind if he asked for it. Erwin considered this an excuse to act independently and gathered Alanis’ own coalition against the marriage.

### Disclosure
When the murders of the heirs to the throne began, [[minerva|Mira]] and [[mark-voron|Mark]] came to the Duke's secret. On June 24, at a military parade that opened the summer games, Mira dropped a note at Aiden's feet on a page from his book: "I know it's a lie." In the carriage, Aiden traded with her: Mira called the name of Lady Layla Talmir, the bride of Corvis, and claimed the place of the first lady-in-waiting under the future empress Alanis and a lazy possession. ([[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]]) The Duke agreed.

However, Mira and Mark revealed his secret. The defence, dressed as Duke Almer's guardsmen, captured a former Aleridan jailer who had seen Corvis tortured. According to Mira, the recent murders of the heirs were a matter of the same handwriting: the threat of regicide, and then “salvation”. For the murders, Aiden hired crooks from the slums: the luck of the assassination was not mandatory, because the goal was to scare the court and the Chamber. The two heirs to the throne, according to the conclusion of Mira, Aiden was killed; but Mira's father, Clive Stagforth, was killed not by him, but by Countess Sybil Northwood. Exposed in July 1774, Aiden, warned by his spies, fled with his daughter from the capital to [[evergard|Evergard]]. His gray-haired coachman Jonas Holly Martin, when questioned by Mark, assured that Lady Alanis knew nothing, and “all the crap” began with Gloria Northwood, who allegedly blackmailed the Duke.

## In "Only One Star"
In the summer of 1774, Aiden, accused of treason, dug into Evergard on the outskirts of Aleridan: he doubled the garrison, filled the warehouses with supplies, hoping to hold out until winter against any troops, and never left the castle walls again. Meanwhile, in the capital, [[mark-voron|Mark]] interrogated his people, including the coachman and the messenger, and the Crown was preparing a trial of accomplices. In August, the emperor summoned the Duke to the capital through a “wave”, threatening otherwise to deprive him of the title and deliver it by force. Brother Gallard left Faunterra for Aleridan to gather an army of poor knights and mercenaries, hoping, as it was believed in the capital, to declare himself ruler, as soon as a court deprives Aidan of the title. At the same time, the young traveler [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], despite the objections of his friend [[berk|Burke]], who recalled that the Duke was a state criminal, decided to enlist in the service of Ayden Almer. In the First Winter, Treasurer Robert reminded Erwin that it was no longer possible to borrow money from Almera, as Desmond had done before: Aiden was "locked in a siege." On the night of September 2, 1774, a detachment with the [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]] destroyed the citadel in an hour. The Duke’s body was found on the ashes, on his head the killers wore a hood. Alfred's middle son was killed. His brother [[gallard-almera|Gallard]] seized power in the duchy, declaring himself regent under young Albert.

Adrian openly took Aiden's death upon himself, stating in a letter to the Great Houses that his word "sounded in Evergard" and would be repeated for anyone who, like Aiden Almer, would go by deception and intrigue. Later it turned out that it was not he who burned Evergard, but [[paul|Paul]]'s brigade, which served the Puppeteer. Erwin suggested that the Puppeteer offered Adrian a deal: spectacularly kill his enemy Aiden and scare the other lords.

## Posthumously
In the summer of 1775, Gallard told Paul that Aiden was among those who had once silenced the Seventeenth Gift, and thanked Paul for having set "Sinner Aiden" on fire. Laura Farway consoled her husband by the fact that Gallard was the first to enter Dara's bed, but Aidan pushed him away. Alanis' daughter in Waymar, at the test of the chosen one, called the late father her guiding beacon.

In "[[fantazii|Fantasies]]" veterans remember him: the girl's father named Sally served the Duke of Aiden and wore a "red cherry", the badge of the best gunner of the company, until the Shavans of the Gnome-gants burned his hand with the Perth. Manson jokingly composed how the ghost of Aiden arrives at his son Albert, engaged to Evelyn Farway, and asks, "Son, why are you getting married?"

In "Ad-ri-an!" (Autumn 1776) The narrator calls him "the late seigneur" of Sir [[khamfri|Humphrey]], his guard's lieutenant, [[dzhonas|Jonas]]'s coachman, and Murriel's fortune teller. Together with the mute [[sofi|Sophie]], they avenged the death of the Great House of Almer, and by all indications their detachment killed Adrian in Faunterra.

## Family
- His wife is Duchess Alicia. Her name is known only from early media publications, where she sits next to her husband at the 1774 summer games parade; in the 2026 edition, Aiden comes to the parade with her son Alfred and daughter Alanis, and the duchess is not mentioned.
- The daughter is [[alanis-almera|Alanis]], the heiress; in the main cycle she is considered to have died after being tortured in Waymar (1775), in the Fantasies she lives under the name of Mrs. Gale.
- Son Alfred (14 years old in 1774) had a fine sword and practiced with a knight's spear; he died with his father.
- The youngest son, [[albert-almera|Albert]] (calculated by Erwin, about seven years in 1773) survived the death of Evergard. He grew up under the tutelage of his uncle and was engaged to Laura Farway, and in Fantasies in 1776 he was engaged to Evelyn, daughter of [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]].
- The elder brother is [[gallard-almera|Gallard]], a priory of the Proto-Paternal Church.

Some believe that Adrian, who disliked too strong vassals, used the Duke's long-standing guilt as an excuse to undermine Almer's house.

- Alanis said in 1775 that Erwin, as ruler, was "only slightly inferior to my late father", although Aiden was tougher.
- As a child, Erwin traveled with his father to visit Duke Aiden on the road from Beck to Evergard.
- When Maurice Labelin asked Aiden to catch a detachment in Almera, who burned the monastery in the Southern Way, the Duke replied that "crimes against the Church are a matter of the Church" and Gallard's parish took up the matter; nothing has changed since then. Labelin called Aidan a "rare scoundrel."
- Lady Leila Talmir told Minerva that if the Duke of Aiden were in her place, her bones would "already rot in the ground."
