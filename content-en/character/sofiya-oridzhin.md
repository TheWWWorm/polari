## Biography

Sophia Jessica Augusta of the Agatha family is from [[aleridan|Aleridan]]. Becoming the Duchess of Origin, she learned to "wear ice on her face" and sewn on the chest of a tramp, but well remembered herself as a child - "the daughter of a bright, beautiful Almera." At the age of seventeen, she was married to [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]]. She later recalled that he embodied all the stereotypes about officers, northerners, and Origins together. In her first year, she could not believe that she had married a living person, and not a ceremonial portrait: Desmond, according to her, lived as if he was a character in an instructive book. Immediately after the wedding night, the husband went to war for a year. The eldest son, Richard, was nursed by a nurse and four kire, so he learned the word "sword" before "mother" and "milk". Sophia compared the native Aleridan and the First Winter: in both cities, the nobles are proud that Agatha’s blood conquers time, but Aleridan rushed into the future, and the First Winter was forever frozen in the past. The Duchess gave birth to three children: [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard]], [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] and [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]]. The most soulful of all were Erwin and Iona, in which she saw her own “spark”.

### In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"

In the first winter, the Duchess was the soul of social life. Without it, the castle would live only tournaments and military initiations. She encouraged music and painting, arranged singing competitions, opened theaters, pestered the bishop with the demands of sermons on the right topics and annually distributed at least five hundred ephes to half a dozen hospitals founded by her, although medicine itself was considered a dirty craft and did not appear in the chambers. One day, the entire family met with [[minerva|Minerva]]'s father and Countess Sybil Northwood. Mira remembered that while the men drank hanty, Lady Sybil entertained the Duchess and Iona.

Since [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard]], who sailed from Lilliday to the Sunset Coast, disappeared around 1772, Sophia has only worn black with platinum and has had a continuous mental dialogue with the gods. Erwin and Iona noticed that something had shifted in her mother’s soul: she sought solace in [[svyashchennye-predmety|Sacred Objects]], considering them to be “incarnate light,” and the holy fathers to be “a chain of ghosts.” At the end of March 1774, when Erwin arrived home, she turned the small refectory into a music parlor for Iona's wedding to [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]: she outweighed tapestries, rehearsed with musicians, and called her son to the sanctuary to teach her how to look at Objects with her eyes.

Shortly after Erwin left for the exploit, [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond]] fell ill with a [[kamennaya-khvor|stone disease]]. The disease was kept secret. The Origins did not come in the summer of 1774 to the Sunny Games in Faunterra, and the capital’s nobility wondered where the Duke and Duchess went. Sophia fed her husband with a spoon, moistened his stone eyes and translated his creaking whisper, better than anyone in the castle parsing the words. Fearing that the relatives of the Origins, Count Fleming and Baron White, would rebel (White collected banners in the Belomorye, Fleming sailed to the West), and the cairns would not obey the woman, she summoned Iona with her husband in the First Winter. In August 1774, when Erwin returned from Beyond, she met him at his father's bedside, "a gray-haired woman tormented by misfortune," as his son thought. Her son’s account of Dar’s Lodge and Adrian’s sparklers brought her into a state of trembling (“This is a cursed Gift. The gods couldn't send him." When Erwin demanded the emperor be overthrown, she turned pale, but did not say a word. Desmond's abdication in favor of her son was made with her.

### In "Only One Star"

In the autumn of 1774, while Erwin was preparing a hike to the capital, Sofia remained with her sick husband in the First Winter. Treasurer Robert estimated that, out of the ransom that Vittor Shayland had paid for Iona, she had spent one thousand two hundred ephesions on opening the theatre in Lilliday and had given six hundred to the bishop for new frescoes in the Rock Chapel. On September 6, when the news of Evergard's death came, she would have cried out to her son if Lady Origin could afford it: "Poor Alanis... Neither the walls nor the troops offer any more protection." Erwin broke down, and she, wrinkling tears, returned to talk about the theater: she wanted to put in the First Winter a full-fledged tragedy about the Seven Ships.

On September 10, 1774, on the last evening before the army's performance, Sophia wore aloe instead of a mourning dress and called her son to her father "for moral training". Desmond said "We are proud of you", and she "translated" his words with her own suggestions and instructions: the army is fed up with idlers, he gave Damon a mocking position, you need to dress warmly and take good books on the march. Her husband’s last phrase, “We believe in you,” she repeated verbatim. When the army left, Desmond, Sophia, and Castelians Arthur Hyroc remained in the First Winter. To the captured Crown Raven [[mark-voron|Mark]], whom Desmond sent on an expedition for the River, Sophia issued a travel document. Count Fleming, who passed to Adrian, threatened to throw Desmond and Sophia at the feet of the lord of the head.

### "Speaking to God"

In January 1775, the mountain guard brought the news that Erwin had taken the capital, and Adrian was killed. Soon in the First Winter returned cyre [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]] and Mark with the Blessed [[gvenda|Gwenda]] and sailors from the "Sealing". Desmond by that time did not accept anyone and lay behind the curtain door of the next room, so the guests were met by Sofia herself. After hearing Mark, she agreed that Adrian was innocent: "We will keep his name from false accusations. The House Origin will not be slandered." Mark offered to ambush the Messengers of the Lord of the Rings when he demanded the [[golos-boga|Object]] bracelet back. ([[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]) Sophia, when the bracelet lit up, took it from Gwenda’s hands and called it: “I am Sophia Jessica Augusta, Lady Origin.” She offered a deal: a bracelet in exchange for Desmond's healing. The voice said, "Go to Faunterra. Bring a bracelet and a husband.” So Sofia thwarted Mark's plan, and he called her a fool in front of everyone, that he was later forgiven.

In the winter, the Origins moved to the capital with the whole detachment: in front of the large covered sleigh with Desmond and Sofia, about a hundred kire, Jemis, Mark, and in the tail of the column of sailors who were promised a schooner and service in the Origin fleet. Desmond told his wife in the First Winter what Erwin would say later: The puppeteer cannot be trusted.

### "A Doll on the Throne"

The railroad was destroyed, and the road was on horseback. In [[labelin|Labelin]], Iona, who came from Waymar, joined her parents. Further, the Origins drove south through the war-torn [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]], following in the footsteps of Erwin's battalions, past fresh cemeteries and looted villages. Sophia gave money to the hungry peasants for which there was nothing to buy, and by the grace of her caught thieves, cutting off the wires of the railway, got away with flogging. For supplies, the detachment made a hook to the east, to Izluchina and [[niar|Niar]] in the Lands of the Crown, and from there along the rails it went to [[faunterra|Faunterra]]. ([[izluchina|Riverbend]]; [[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) Sophia did not warn her son, wanting to surprise him, so at Cathedral Square, the motorcade was met by the sheriff, guardsmen and [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]], whom the Duchess embraced as a “poor child”.

At the first dinner at the palace in February 1775, she proudly questioned her son about his victories, and then spoke about the deal with the Puppeteer. Erwin became enraged: his mother “exchanged” the only way to find the enemy for the illusion of healing. Alanis stood up for her: the Puppeteer might want to cure Desmond in order to quarrel with his father and son. Sophia asked when Erwin would announce Adrian's innocence, and her son forbade her and everyone at the table to mention the owner of the Rings.

At a reception at Minerva, Sofia gave the lady amber with a frozen blue flower, a symbol of the fragile beginning of the reign, said that her husband’s illness brought the couple closer together, and offered her advice to the young empress, “only not about my son.” On the day of the premiere, Minerva deliberately took her with her to the carriage and learned everything about the theatrical etiquette of the Grand Crown. In the box Sophia admired the "fresh interpretation" of the play and argued with Iona, and the second act was held in the box of her son.

Soon the beggar-Signer with a healing Object, sent by the Puppeteer, broke into the palace. ([[znakharka|The Healer]]) She treated Desmond and demanded that on her next visit she be received by Sophia herself and vowed to let her go. Desmond and Jemis considered this a bait for Erwin, and Erwin promised not to let either himself or his mother enter the Sign. As Desmond got better, Sophia warmly supported Iona ("I wasn't wrong when I made the deal!"), but her husband cut her off: "Sophia, don't indulge your daughter's naivety." Upon learning that Znaharka had died while trying to get Iona and Alanis to sneak her in, Sophia was inconsolable, though she did not shed a tear. Robert's offer to sell the Hand of the Sorcerer almost brought her to sobs. ([[ruka-znakharki|Healer's Hand]]) She herself tried to revive the Object by praying to the three Foremothers and indulging it with incense on the altar, but without success.

### The “Yanmei Hunt”

In the spring of 1775, after Saint Paul, Erwin decided to play the role of a villain at court. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) He was prompted by the accidental phrase of his mother, who did not know about the failure of his hunt for the Puppeteer: "When the actor enters a creative dead end, the best drug is a change of role." At the same time Erwin came to serve as a guard Colonel Arden, who guarded Sofia in those days when she was an infant Almer. Desmond, to whom Iona had given [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]] a potion, was getting better in the meantime.

In May, during the Manson trial, the parents suddenly moved from the palace to the Origin mansion in the capital, and Erwin came to them to find out the reason. ([[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]]) Desmond and Sophia reproached their son for looking like a diversionary maneuver, and showed a letter from the Prior of Gallard proposing to create a sacred guard for the capture of the Puppeteer under the supervision of Duke [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]]. ([[gallard-almera|Gallard Almera]]) When Erwin named Gallard the Puppeteer himself, his parents didn't believe him. Sophia advised the transfer of the investigation to the guard and praised Farway as a fair man. Then Erwin asked if she knew Mireille Nan-Claire (Sophia met her many years ago at the court, before breaking up with her mother), and told her to go to [[folta|Folta]] and find out why the daughter of Lady in Darkness broke up with her mother. ([[mirej-nen-kler|Mireille Nen-Clair]]; [[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]]) Desmond forbade, but Erwin recalled that he was suzerain. Sophia agreed.

### In "Humans and Gods"

The embassy sailors considered opal: the Duke “broke out” his mother for thirty-nine lands. Sophia herself played the grief of separation, and secretly rejoiced. After a year spent at her husband's bedside, her son's order gave her freedom, and Desmond, thanks to Martin's potion, was on the mend and did not intend to die. Erwin gave her twelve kairs led by kair Glenn and a guide: a mysterious old man without a name, a judge ([[ded|Grandfather]]), with a student (Granddaughter). ([[vnuchok|Grandson]]) They left Faunterra by train, and on 29 May 1775, Captain [[dzheff-bamber|Jeff Bamber]]'s Sea Arrow schooner with the former Seal team climbed to [[marengo|Marengo]]. When they started talking about the attack on Arden on the waterfront, Sofia, without blinking an eye, lied that she knew everything, and ordered to leave. The sailors called the old man Skipper, and the student Jung. In swimming, she composed plays in a cabin built once for the althessa of the Shimmerian king.

The schooner had a rough history. Nineteen years ago, it was built in an Orcadian shipyard commissioned by the Shimmerian king as a gift to the althesse. Then she served as a courier ship of the Shimmery yard, more than a year lay on the reefs and changed several owners, including the Salttown merchant Hobart-Synthal. ([[gobart-sintal|Gobart-Sintal]]) In the Northern Flash, Erwin spared her as ships were sinking on Soltown's fairway, and in the spring Iona persuaded her brother to give the schooner to the sailors from the Seal.

Five days later, the schooner arrived at Maple, the port of [[melorandzh|Melorange]] in [[litlend|Litland]]. At General William Davey's headquarters, Sophia handed him Erwin's package: Minerva's order to remove Melorange's defenses and a letter calling the spark regiment north to Main. ([[uiljam-dejvi|William Davy]]) The general was offended that the victory over Moran was attributed to Minerva. From Davy Sofia with the judge learned about the attack of heretics on the temple Farewell and that Adrian is alive. She dispelled the general's hesitation with a scene with animal masks (sheep, hare, toad, bat, seagull) and the direct question of whether he wanted Adrian to find him. Davey honestly warned that he was serving the Crown. They left the headquarters an hour before the news of Iona's supposed death and the holy war arrived, and walked around Melorange for several days until the judge recalled the case.

In the port of Maple stood the ships of the Admiralty of the Volta. In mid-June, Mireille Nan-Claire herself boarded the Sea Arrow with hunting captains Lycaine and Colistad and invited the Duchess to hunt for the gurken, the king of sea monsters. ([[likejn-i-kolistad|Likein and Colistad]]) Sailors and kairs discouraged her, but Sofia, judging that Mireille would not reveal the secret in the first conversation, accepted the invitation. In the morning, the Guardian, the White Wolf, and the Sea Arrow went east, to the High Seas, seven hundred miles from the shores of Polaris. For a week of conversations, Mireille became Sofia’s closest person after her husband and children. However, Mireille masterfully became a “mirror”: instead of answering, she turned into a listener, and the Duchess told her about her husband, about children, about Erwin the “white crow”, having learned nothing.

In the voyage, a sailor nicknamed the [[potomok|Descendant]] claimed that the sea water dissolved the rat thrown overboard. The team laughed at him, but Sofia, who knew that “according to all the laws of dramaturgy” the eccentric, whom no one believes, would be right, asked Mireille about “predatory water”. Having figured out her method, she herself lured the interlocutor into a conversation, promising to tell about the northern wonders - emerald bears, fangs and runners - only after the story of predatory water. Mireille explained that it is a black badger, a predator of a million tiny creatures. When the Voltians mistook the maktuka whale for prey and saw the gurken beneath it, the squadron turned to land. On the way to the island of the Eastern Mark, Mireille told Sophia about her vow and about the “key in the castle” - the [[pervokrov|firstblood]], behind which there is a danger worse than heretics with the Rings. The expedition landed on East Mark Island.

### The Shadow of the Great Tree

After learning the secret of the Great Tree at the end of July on the island of the North-Eastern Mark, Sofia decided to reveal it to her son personally in the First Winter. She approached the case as a playwright. You can not keep the secret alone, otherwise the evil forces will finish off the character to leave the audience in darkness, so she took an oath from the judge and with Mira to reveal the secret if Sofia dies. The monologue should be pronounced at exactly the right moment, and the ideal scene she saw the castle of the First Winter, and sometimes the first day after the victory of the Origins over the Puppeteer. It was impossible to entrust the secret to pigeons, and even it should not be mentioned, so only a short note went to the capital (an excerpt is given):

> “You can’t trust Queen Madeline, or the entire order, because they are false and dangerous. I have reasons to extend the mission until December."

The note was intercepted by protection agents, and Minerva handed it to Robert, who replied, "Aha."

Sailing on the islands of Metok with sea monsters took several more months. From the North-Eastern Mark, the squadron went south along the boundary of the disastrous waters. She was attacked by a mirage who copied the Sea Arrow itself along with the crew and Sofia, and dolphin-like screamers singing brought the sailors to bleeding from their ears. On the island of South-Eastern Marks at night, “skeletons” crawled ashore, inside of which, as it turned out, glow beetles lived. A few days later, a young gourmet appeared and grabbed the Guardian with a water funnel, and a storm came after him, taking away nine sailors. The squadron then fell into the draught of dead water, where scurvy began on the ships. People were saved only on the island of South Mark, where lemon trees grew. There were so many monsters that Sofia rubbed the calluses, sketching them in an album for children. On the way back, on the island of cannibals in the archipelago of Thousand Shards, the savages, seeing the dried tentacle of the gurken, decided that the winners of the monsters could only be gods. Sophia pulled out a short wand and played their goddess, the Mother of All Mothers: she was sung in prayers and given to kill someone's blood. Returning, she discovered that “fifty-two is not age at all,” because Agatha looked like a girl at ninety.

In early November, the squadron entered [[uindli|Windley]]. After five months of wandering, Sofia regretted parting with the team and the Voltians. To the sailors, she distributed generous bills and letters about the eternal friendship of the House Origin, and went ashore with Skipper, Jung, Mireille and the Kairs. She refused to listen to the bad news (“In this play, the forces of good will win!”). The road to the north was blocked by the troops of Adrian, and the Duchess went around, through the Beyond: fjords over the sea, a ridge on the isthmus, the Hem of the Servant, the trail of the White Wanderers, the grotto of Kosuli. ([[grot-kosuli|Roe Deer Grotto]]) It was the same mountain path that Erwin took two years ago, only in the opposite direction. Two greys crashed, exhausted Mireille had to carry. The fifty-two-year-old Duchess was a day late. On December 3, 1775, the day after the battle, her detachment reached the monastery at the grotto of Kosuli on Mount Agatha. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]) There she was told about the victory and that Desmond recovered, and Erwin and Iona performed feats. In the morning, repeating her monologue on the road and preparing to hug her husband, whom she had not seen for almost two years, she drove up to the gate of the castle. The sentinel enthusiastically confirmed the victory, said that Iona learned to speak with the healing Object and saved hundreds of wounded, and when asked where to find Desmond, turned pale: “He died. Richard is dead.”

The mystery of the Tree to children and Minerva was told by Mireille, to whom Sophia, crying, gave way. Mireille showed Erwin Sofia an album with sketches of monsters and the last sheet on which the Duchess depicted the First Tree over all Polaris. When Erwin doubted, Sophia assured: “Son, I saw them all myself.” After seeing her children and Minerva come to the same conclusion together, she fell in love with them. Over the embarrassed Minerva, who was teased by her brother and sister with her new passion, she took the helm: "Two older ones are mocking the younger one, I have a duty to protect her."

## In "Fantasies"
"Fantasies" the author calls possible options for the future. In them, Sofia is the Dowager “Duchess Mother” in the First Winter.

- **Negotiation.** In January 1776, after the battle, Sophia, together with the judge ([[ded|Grandfather]]), represented the Origins in negotiations with [[magda-labelin|Magda]], Dorian Amber and [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]], while the exhausted Erwin was only present. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) So they agreed on the election of the lords and the provisional power of Minerva.
- **Taking care of Minerva.** After the death of her husband and son, while Iona was on a penitential journey, Sophia brought down her mother's love on Minerva ("Mia, the goldsmith"): the best bed in the theater, sweets, rare wines. Nexia noticed that the “Duchess Mother” and Erwin were courting the mistress in defiance.
- **Mourning and the Judge.** Sophia lifted the mourning for the one hundred and seventieth day after Desmond's death, although he said during his lifetime: "Seventeen days of mourning is enough with the head." A week later, she put on mourning again: her husband’s spirit appeared to her and reproached him with an unusual sarcasm that she had forgotten him too quickly and was in a hurry to “get healthy.” At a spiritualistic session with Iona and Minerva, she admitted that she had dreams of marrying a judge, but they did not appoint an engagement, and now everything is postponed “for a year, if not more.” According to the spirit, she was in her sixth decade. Later, the Kairs announced that all the ghosts that frightened Iona, Shatterhand, Sophia, and Minerva were Dalia Raven's shape-shifting ("Let's Catch Him"). ([[dalliya-rejven|Dallia Raven]])
- **Cultural life.** Sofia was in charge of the cultural life of the First Winter and was preparing a "fairy play based on a trip to the ends of the earth"; the news about it reached the capital scientists.
- Minerva's twentieth birthday (August 1776). The whole holiday was arranged by Sofia: painting the gate (the dawn sun above the sea), towers in flowers, a fountain in the yard, tapestries, fireworks, an unprecedented cake, a day broken into "acts", and toasts by lot, where everyone had to mention the word (she fell "explorada"). She forbade formal gifts ("The girl wants a celebration, not a ritual!") and promised Iona that her birthday would be twice as bright. In the evening, she staged a play about love in an ice maze; Minerva ran away from the performance with Erwin, and Leyla noticed that Lady Origin "does not think of herself as a great playwright" ("Closer - further").
- **Clinic.** Sophia opened a clinic of Mercy in the First Winter ten years before the orphanage for the sick old and poor; in the summer of 1776, she began to treat Iona for free there.

## Personality

Sofia is a cheerful, talkative and theatrical nature. The codename of its chapters, "Mask", seems to refer to this passion for drama. Any event she mentally plays out as a play, distributing roles and choosing a moment for the “monologist”. She can be considered frivolous, but she is astute, knows how to keep a secret and is capable of bold actions, whether it is a deal with an invisible enemy for the sake of her husband or a months-long voyage for the edge of the known world.
