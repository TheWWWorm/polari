## Life in Summerswith
“That’s the strangest thing: Salem didn’t want to hurt anyone,” begins the chapter in which he first appears. Salem was a poor peasant from the village of Summerswith in the [[yuzhnyj-put|Southern Way]]. His white hut under the straw roof stood on a hill above the thresholds of Hanoi. ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) The buckwheat field gave birth to just enough to pay the baron's dues and reach the spring. So lived his father and grandfather, and Salem was pleased with this. Friends recalled that once he was a cheerful, “soul full”, and after marriage he became a thoughtful silent.

Salem's wife, Clementa, was the miller's fifth daughter. She grew up in prosperity and was burdened with poverty, called her husband a “slug” and constantly reproached him. They had no children. It was said that Clementa drinks a potion, hoping to escape with someone richer. Salem still loved her, although he hid tenderness, because she angered her wife.

## War
In the autumn of 1774, when the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]] began, Clementa persuaded her husband to sell the entire harvest in [[labelin|Labelin]], where prices soared. Salem borrowed a cart from a neighbor, but halfway through, the soldiers took the grain and put it in the militia of Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]]. For several weeks he stood among fifty thousand men who blocked the city. More than death, Salem feared he would have to kill someone. After the surrender, the northerners dismantled the prisoners according to their skills, and Salem honestly said that he did not want to kill. An officer in red and black threw up, "Get out," and Salem went home satisfied that he had done no harm to anyone.

Trouble was waiting for him at home. The corporal of the imperial forages took away the remnants of the harvest and Clement. Ponsch's neighbor said she left on her own; Salem punched him, then locked himself in a hut and beat the dishes until he knocked down the skin on the knuckles. Following the foragers, the retreating spark army raked out all that remained. In return, the elder left the Crown bill for a “full refund.”

## The beginning of the campaign
In the winter of 1775, thirty men led by Salem went to Baron Summerswith to ask for help. The old greedy baron accepted them only because he was waiting for a doom, and ordered them to be driven away. There were only two guards in the castle. They killed the chief and his son, but the men overcame them, and the blacksmith Blake killed the baron with one blow, throwing himself on the peasants with a dagger. Salem was the first to understand that the former life is over: the sons of the baron will not forgive. He offered to go to the Duke of Labelin to ask for justice, hoping that only Blake would be executed. Peasants took horses, wagons and supplies from the castle. A couple of dozen men came from the neighboring villages, and three hundred people moved along the way.

In Pixie, a rider with a crossbow and a mace joined the campaign, a brewer who called himself the Tramp. He showed the peasants a chain of frozen bodies of sparklers along Hanoi, and the sale of the armor and weapons removed from them provided food for a long time. When the sons of the baron with fifty riders attacked the column, Salem came out to them empty-handed. The eldest son rushed at him with a sword, and he was knocked off the horse by the Tramp's shot. The knights got stuck in the carts deployed across the road. Half of the attackers fell into the snow, including all three sons of the baron. Salem served loaded crossbows to the Tramp in this fight. The carts were deployed by a retired sergeant, Ruka Dodge, "a veteran of the Wise River, Labelin and Pixie", who immediately volunteered to teach the peasants how to build. ([[ruka-dodzh|Hand Dodge]])

Details of the campaign are described in the article Rising Snowdrops. ([[vosstanie-podsnezhnikov|Snowdrop Rebellion]])

## The Chief involuntarily
The city of Bakers met the peasants with a locked gate and threats. Salem only asked to be allowed to trade. The tramp obtained food as a threat, and Salem forbade him to do so: “We do not rob, but buy.” Yet, after reflecting on the Wanderer's words about power, he allowed Doge to teach people. Against his will, Salem began to control a growing crowd. He divided people into hundreds and tens, distributed hundreds of high red hats, marked the column with carts with signs, strictly considered and divided food and arranged a mobile hospital. He led eight thousand people to the walls of Grays. There, Colonel Sir Walter Day of the Marquess of Graysend demanded that they separate. Salem, bowing low, bargained for permission to buy food from the townspeople. Upon learning that the Duke had gone to the capital, a campaign on the advice of the Tramp moved to [[faunterra|Faunterra]] to the Lady.

Salem recruited three advisers: Sergeant Dodge knew the military, the Tramp knew the townspeople, and [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], whom the peasants called Trehpal, knew the nobles. “Together we know everyone who is in the world,” he explained. When the Lowvilles, led by Tooth's dentist, killed the tax collectors, Salem coldly told Tooth that the lesser evil could not be answered by the greater. It was the wisdom of a priest from Summerswith. Later, Zub persuaded hundreds of people to replace “justice” with “honest tax”. Salem hesitated, but gave in. After the battle at the outpost, he released all the prisoners, leaving only their money for food for the hungry, and thanked Joaquin for breaking his pledge not to fight for the sake of saving people. At the same time, seeing flowers in the clearing, Salem came up with the name of the uprising:
> “If they want to call us rebels, we will be the Snowdrop Rebellion.”

The more townspeople came into the army, the less they listened to Salem. At a meeting with Labelin agent [[moger-bakli|Moger Buckley]], he refused to oppose the Duke of [[ervin-oridzhin|Origin]]: he spared him and thousands of his fellow countrymen near Labelin. He took the sparks only after long hesitation, and the casket immediately took the Tooth and Dodge. In Hampton, the city's officers honored the "people's general" and "people's major", and Salem, embarrassed, sat aside. He did not know that “his mouth speaks the voice of the people.” He tried to refuse from the donated three thousand ephesas: the army, according to him, had enough money. The tooth insisted, and the money was taken “on receipt”.

## Arrest and the Holy Field
In March 1775, at Hampton, when General Gore demanded to disperse and extradite the baron's assassins, Zub and Dodge shot his guardsmen and standard-bearer with their own arrows. Salem ordered them to surrender, but Dodge's men arrested him. Joaquin, who stood up for the chief, was thrown off with a spark arrow. Salem was tied up and locked in a van with the treasury, and the townspeople were told that the chief was "not in himself". During the night, Joaquin with Jolly, Tramp, Salmon and Billy outwitted the guard and freed him. Salem hugged every savior, but refused to flee. Joaquin called him to go on horseback to the Southern Way, first to Summerswith, and then to his own, and urged at least to take the Summerswith, since the Tooth now commands everything. The tramp objected that the leader needed an uprising, and Salem agreed with him:
> “You yourself say: forty thousand lives are at stake. As long as there are chances, my place is here.”

Joaquin did not want to break his vow not to fight again and left the army with Veselchak. Salem hugged him in farewell, promised to pray for him and invited him to visit if he survived. Later, Joaquin, being captured by [[iona-oridzhin|Iona Origin]], assured her that Salem was a kind and honest man, and the water behind his back mutated Tramp, a monk of the villainous order brother Ludwig, and asked to persuade Erwin not to destroy the Snowdrops.

On Holy Field, thirty miles north of the capital, in the spring of 1775, before the formation of the northern army, Salem went to [[minerva|Minerva]] without weapons. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) He bowed to her to the ground, but spoke directly, looking into her eyes. He asked not to be called the leader: trouble came to him earlier than to others, only everything. The tooth reduced the case to taxes, and Salem listed everything that was “not true”: the robbery of fodder, the murder of the baron, the attack of his sons, the sparkle in which the boy died, and the hope for Buckley’s weapons. He took all the blame for the death of the ambassadors, and the lord presented the forage bills to the foragers. Minerva was deeply touched by his faith. The new tax minister, Morlin-May, announced the tax reform, and Minerva promised triple reimbursement and forgiveness to all but the shooters. When she asked if Salem had fired, the Snowdrops replied in one voice, "No!" With tears in his eyes, Salem told the lady that now they know: the truth exists.

## After the pardon
The pardoned Salem returned home to Summerswith. Marquess Gracend reported to Minerva that all those who returned were paid for the spruce per capita. Soon in Summerswith came Veselchak: Joaquin at Smoky Distance took the Northern Princess prisoner, and Veselchak, who tried in vain to rescue him in the capital, reasoned that Joaquin would sooner or later come to Salem, as he promised. He did not wait for Joaquin, but remained with Salem, who “walked quite gloomy”: part of the Snowdrops wanted to work and went into robbery. Salem condemned them and urged them to come to their senses, but without success. In July 1775, when the former Snowdrops under the leadership of Zub and Dodge entered the Labelin mercenaries [[adrian|Adrian]], Salem, along with Veselchak rushed across the column and urged the "brothers" to come to their senses. Mercenaries treated him with respect, but simply pushed him into the alley, and Veselchak was given by the teeth. Salem blamed himself for the fate of these seven thousand and thought that he could go to the Empress alone, without balamut. He said that he owed Minerva not only his life, “but another eighty thousand.”

In the inn near the station Salem and Veselchak met [[kharmon|Harmon]], who was riding a cupola at the embassy of Adrian in the capital. Salem told Harmon that the soldiers who took his grain and took his wife came from Adrian, and Minerva forgave the Snowdrops and imposed an honest tax. After listening to the ambassadors' conversations, Harmon suspected that the casket sent as a gift to Minerva should kill her. Harmon drugged the ambassadors with sleeping pills, Salem and Veselchak helped drag the sleepers upstairs. Fearing a trap, Harmon sent Salem to the corridor with a train ticket to warn Minerva in case of trouble, and together with Veselchak opened the casket. Inside were two Light Spheres, genuine and fake, an Object Belt and a letter from Adrian to Lady Madeline. The three of them went to the capital. In the car, Salem, for the first time riding on the rails, endlessly clicked the spark lamp switch. In Faunterra, he directly at the secretariat announced to the official that they had brought two Sacred Subjects and had come to the empress to "save". They were then captured by Crown Raven [[mark-voron|Mark]] and taken to Minerva. Mark later admitted that Salem and Veselchak are “pure as water.” Harmon, who became the contractor of the House Origin for the construction of celestial ships, called them in as assistants. Salem objected that he was a peasant, but he had nowhere to go: the way to Labelin was closed. Harmon promised to make them masters and pay by hilt per month, and they swore to Gloria the Intercessor to serve him faithfully, not to run away and not to betray his secrets. Joaquin was considered dead by that time.

## In the first winter
In the late summer of 1775, when Minerva moved the court to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], Salem, with Veselchak and Harmon, sailed from Faunterra down the Canaan and beyond by sea. Harmon did not remember how to build the ball, and the assistants were scratching their heads with him. On the ship, they were joined by a [[sorok-dva|forty-two]] cuir, and Salem and Veselchak dragged Harmona to a test of all sorts of smelly substances in order to find a smell for the ball tissue, and even dragged him into the booty quarter to smell shoe glue. Salem's first judgment of the drawing on the napkin was, "Smear here." At the port of [[uindli|Windley]], where the flotilla arrived in September, Salem tracked down Harmon during unloading and brought him to the ship, and [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] approved the estimate of the ball that was to be built for the day of the Descent, when the approach of the enemy armies was expected.

In the First Winter Salem worked in the "Ministry of Air Intelligence", which Harmon and Kair Henry Horton arranged as a mixture of an alchemical laboratory and a sewing workshop. Salem hired workers and made sure they were paid. On the day of the Descent, December 2, 1775, he soulfully made three straw stuffed animals, a "merchant", a "young man" and a "lady", dressed them in worn-out old age and painted their faces with coal, wondering if the gods would not demand air sacrifices. On the plateau south of the First Winter, Minerva, who arrived on a boat with a Glove, ordered to put a stuffed animal in the basket instead of people and start the ball after the third fall of the stone: the ship was to distract the enemy. Salem, who still adored the lady and seemed to sense her approach from afar, noticed her boat first. When Minerva, after the altercation, gave way to her favorite [[navi|Navi]], Salem jealously remarked: “An obedient woman is happiness for a man.” As the ball floated over the valley, Salem made a toast to Gloria the Intercessor: "Let him take care of all the good people." The ball of stuffed animals was soon shot down by [[paul|Paul]].

## Death
On December 2, 1775, the day of the Battle of the First Winter, Forty-Two invited Harmon and his friends to take shelter at the outpost of the mountain guard at the pass, and he himself returned to the besieged city. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]) Salem said, "Brother Forty-Two, I'd rather be with you," and Veselchak went too. To Kair's objection that the First Winter was not their city, they replied that they had been accepted here as their own, they founded a guild and opened a shop, which means that they became citizens of the First Winter. During a street ambush, [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]], who now served as Earl of Shayland, broke into the workshop with canvases of colored fabric and vats for chemicals (he himself took it for a dyeing shop). Seeing the spear pointed at him, he immediately hit the "weaving" of Finger of Wilhelm, and the penny-taker fell, "broken like a branch." ([[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]) Joaquin only realized who he had killed when the second penny man, Veselchak, recognized him. Upon leaving the city, Joaquin said to General Horis, "A fine man has died... ([[oris|General Oris]]) I killed him." Horis replied, "No, not you," but Joaquin couldn't believe it. Both did not speak of defeat; a little later, Horis informed the Earl of Shayland of [[paul|Paul]]'s death.

## Personality and Appearance
Salem was red-bearded, with a lush cap of curly hair, with kind and sad eyes. Joaquin had not seen such eyes in any general or duke. A tooth on his eye gave him thirty-five years. Salem was weak in vision and never learned to stay in the saddle, so he rode on the goats of the cart. He deeply believed in the Pramatery, especially in Gloria the Intercessor, and revered the Lady: “Not without reason, the Foremothers put her over everyone.” He wanted neither power nor blood. He said he was “like a millstone around his neck.” The city leaders considered him "blessed", and the young Charlie the Bull - "underdog". Only once, for the wine from Harmon, Salem admitted that he was still offended by his wife.
