## Who He Is

Vanden was one of two knights who accompanied Countess [[sibil-nortvud|Sybil Northwood]] on her way to Faunterra in the spring of 1774 and "had time to get acquainted somehow" with Mira. ([[minerva|Minerva]]) Mira lived at the Countess under the guise of her daughter [[gloriya-nortvud|Gloria]]. He wore a sword and a doublet, and his northern features were so prominent that the postmaster mistook him for a courier. The coachman-capitalist directly distinguished him, a northerner, from himself, a native of Faunterra. The countess, choosing Mira, said about him: "He is better than others."

## Next to Peace

### The first days in the capital

It was from Vanden Mir that she asked who Lord [[lord-klarens|Clarence]] was on the first evening. The knight simply explained, "The imperial stableman is the alter of the countess." When asked if Count Elias was against it, he replied that the count had long been cold to women, "so it is calmer for him, and she is pleased."

### Horseback riding and the night of fear

When Mira was going for a horse ride with Lady Becca Litland, the countess gave her Vanden escorts: "he is better than others." ([[rebekka-litlend|Rebecca Litland]]) The girls jumped from the guard, and in the forest of the World faced suspicious people; from them she was rescued by Becca, and later Mira decided that these people were sent by Sir [[adamar|Adamar]]. Later, she and Vanden agreed not to tell the Countess that she had escaped from him. Vanden admitted that Sybil would "put his head on the peak" if he found out. Mira replied that the peak would be two.

At night, in a fever and in fear of new killers, Mira took in a weapons dagger and a crossbow. Vanden, standing on the watch, was surprised, but he remained silent. She thought that of all households she can only trust him: he accompanied her on an ill-fated day. At her request, the knight sat in the hall next to her room all night, and then himself, without servants, brought her hot wine, with red eyes from insomnia. While telling the Countess of the assassination attempt, Mira made her promise not to punish Vanden. Sybil kept her word and didn't say a word.

### Trip to Pale Meadow

On June 20, 1774, weakened after bloodletting, Mira decided to find Leyla Talmir and asked Vanden to accompany her to the place of Pale Meadow seven miles from the capital. He doubted: my lady is unhealthy, “mother will be unhappy.” Mira replied that the countess would be even less pleased if he let her go alone. Vanden obeyed. In the hotel "House with Ivy" he stood up for the girl, when one of the visitors pinched her: grabbed the brazen hair and impressed her face into the table. When Mira became ill from pain in the liver, the knight rushed to the rescue and all the way pale with excitement. He then routed her through the crowd in the square where Mira met [[mark-voron|Mark]]. Mark called Vanden and took Mira to talk.

### Recent meetings

Later, Vanden told Mira why the Countess did not come to her: Clarence was stabbed in a duel by a Northwood baron, who stood up for the honor of Count Elias. He described the fight in detail and, it seems, not without pleasure, so Mira decided that the knight did not like Clarence. He also advised her not to drink coffee, "this shit": from him, they say, all the disease.

When Mira realized that the countess was poisoning her, and rushed to run up the tower staircase, two warriors chased her. Vanden ran first. The world seemed to be running slower than it could. A little later, defending herself in her room, she bitterly thought that the first person to shoot would be him.

## The Disappearance

In "Only One Star", [[itan|Ethan]]'s secretary, among the suspicious actions of the Countess, called this: she sent Vanden, "the knight who guarded Lady Gloria", - where, he does not know: "To Northwood ... or underground." Mark said to Sybil, “If your Sir Vanden was around, he would have kicked me out.” In the face of the Countess ran only a “light shadow”. Vanden's further fate is not revealed in the books. Lady [[karen|Karen]] in the collectible edition of Arrow, Coin, Spark writes that shortly after the July 1774 events, Sybil sent him to the North — because he had heard too much. ([[strela-moneta-iskra-kollektsionnoe|Arrow, Coin, Spark (Collector's Edition)]]) Karen even jokes that the author seems to have gone to Vanden in Northwood to describe the scene with Minerva and Gallard in such detail.
