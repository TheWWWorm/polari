## Overview
Simon Lilliday is the eldest son of the Earl of Lilliday, who is called the Snow Count in the North, and the brother of kair [[dzhemis-lillidej|Jemis Lilliday]]. The Lillidays are the prime vassals of the [[dom-oridzhin|House Origin]], a legendary family that is rumored to rule the North even when the Origins were not in sight, and their ancestral city of Leed, the ancient capital of the North, is famous for gunsmiths, winemakers, the Leed Orge and earls, "mercilessly fair as the axe of the executioner". ([[lid|Leed]]) According to Erwin, this family serves the House Origin eleven generations and four times was related to him by marriage. Once Leed argued with the First Winter for the primacy in the North; the war was decided by the fight between the Earl of Lilliday and the Duke of Origin in the yard of Fort Superior at the Squaw of Heaven, after which Lilliday admitted himself a vassal of the First Winter. ([[stvorki-neba|Gates of Heaven]])

Soldiers from Leed took the Snow Count to war: his corps of three battalions fought in [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]]'s army, and in July 1775 was defeated by [[paul|Paul]] in the Glorious Watch, where the count died without being given Shavans alive. When the war came to Leed in November 1775, Simon ruled the city.

In Arrow, Coin, Spark, Erwin refers to his younger brother, Jemis, as "the heir of the noble family of Lilliday" and "the hereditary count of Lilliday." In "Amicable" ("Fantasies"), Jemis explains the family tradition: men of this kind always have several sons, the first son heads the city and the garrison, the second - the expeditionary army, and both are called counts. Leed and surrounding lands are owned by the elder brother.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
### Leed refuses to run.
After losing the Battle of Lysium Dol, [[desmond-oridzhin|Desmond Origin]] retreated to the mountains and sent couriers to the duchy with orders to take supplies and livestock and leave, leaving the enemy with nothing but empty walls. ([[lisij-dol|Fox Vale]]) Almost everyone listened. But when Desmond came to Leed, all seventy thousand residents remained on the spot and poured into the streets to meet him. At the head of the city's elders, with bread, orge, flags, and music, Desmond was met by Simon Lilliday.

The duke asked in anger why the city was not deserted. Simon calmly replied that all four couriers had arrived and reported Shayland's attack, "We're not afraid of him." When Desmond admitted that he could not protect Leed and went into the First Winter tomorrow, Simon put his hand on the hilt: "We know, my lord. We don’t see any reason to run away from these assholes.” The elders supported him: “Lead does not give up without a fight!”

### The Lead Army
On the advice of General [[stetkhem|Statham]], Desmond hired citizens into his army with money captured in Shayland banks: by effes per month, eternal glory and the opportunity to get even with the Puppeteer. Under northern law, a lord cannot force a man to serve, but can offer a fee, and the honor of the Lydians did not forbid to serve the duke for good money. Within a day, forty thousand men of Lydia stood under the banners with the uncircumcised, and of them were shelves of laundries and cooks, battalions of blacksmiths and carpenters, companies of winemakers and harlots. Desmond appointed Simon Lilliday as general of this army. Thirty thousand people remained. The generals silently offered to burn Leed so that the enemy would not get his supplies, but Desmond said no twice.

Before leaving, Simon gathered the remaining townspeople in the square and gave the order:

> "By the power of the Earl of Lilliday and by the name of the Mother of Gloria, I command you not to resist the enemy."

He warned that together with the Puppet Master, the traitor [[rikhard-oridzhin|Richard Origin]] would come and call himself their ruler, and ordered him to be welcomed as a lord, to be treated with bread and an orge, to shout "Glory to Richard" and to do everything necessary for survival. Soon Leed was occupied by [[vittor-shejland|Shayland]]'s army, and the Chosen One and his entourage settled in Lilliday Castle.

### Fate of Leed
Simon's order was at first justified: the ancient Leed opened the gates and obeyed Richard, so the Chosen One protected the city from looting and kept most of the troops behind the walls. However, [[martin-shejland|Martin Shayland]], having taken possession of the Voice of God, gave the city to the horde. Shavans looted churches, smithy, shops and wealthy houses, killed and maimed seven hundred people, took away half a thousand women and, leaving, shelled the city with the Persians, so that several blocks burned to the ground. Most of the houses and towns survived.

### In the first winter
The Leads came with Desmond in the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]]. [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] explained to [[minerva|Minerva]] that a column of women, old people and children are the leaders hired into the army. After the battle for the First Winter Minerva ordered to mobilize them, and take the company of prostitutes to the nurses at the expense of the treasury. ([[bitva-za-pervuyu-zimu|Battle for First Winter]]) She then told Jemis that his brother was in town: Lord Lilliday manages the refugees from Leed and solves their problems if possible. Jemis should have helped him, and then taken up the plan to protect Leed, because the retreating enemy army could have re-robbed the city.

## In "Fantasies"
In Fantasies, which the author calls possible futures, Simon does not appear himself. In "Amicable" (August 1776), Jemis tells Princess Demeter that Lidom and the surrounding lands are owned by his elder brother, and the army, which was ruled by his father, the Duke is in no hurry to pass to Jemis. Before the trip to Shimmery, Jemis laid a chapel in memory of his father and undertook to restore the half-burnt Leed.
