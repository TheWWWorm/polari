## Who He Is

Doxet is an old soldier who by the spring of 1774 had already served fifteen years as a guard at the merchant [[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]. He was listed as a guard more from the old memory: Harmon considered him more of a driver than a fighter. Doxet's salary was less than that of [[snajp|Snipe]] and later hired [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]]. His main weakness was wine. The main talent was endless tales about his own glorious military past.

## In "Arrow, Coin, Spark"

**Smallden.** The novel begins with Doxet's booze. On the eve, as soon as the detachment was located in an inn in [[smolden|Smolden]], the old man asked the owner for “deserved” money and received two silver agates. One he hid in his boot, the other he drank. In the morning he was found sleeping on the road in front of the hotel: he could not overcome the three steps of the porch. Harmon poured a bucket of water over his head, but Doxet only flashed "Master..." and fell asleep again. Because of this, Harmon went to the market square without protection. The void behind his left shoulder led him to believe that Doxet was “exploited” and needed a new guard. So Joaquin Eve Hannah appeared in the wagon. The novice Doxet met with a confused curiosity: he first crept up to him, circled around and tried to call him “son.”

**Six-mile forest and Womack cheese.** When Harmon led the convoy through the Six-mile forest, Doxet brought a captive from the rearguard, a frightened young robber, pushing him with the edge of a spear. ([[shestimilnyj-les|Six-Mile Wood]]) In a skirmish with Sir Ben Womack's squires, he and [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] armed himself with an axe. ([[ben-vomak|Benjamin Womack]]) After Joaquin's victory, Doxet took out a bottle of excellent horde from the unknown even to the owner of the cache and poured the hero a full cup. Even earlier, in May, when Harmon returned to the Southern Way, it turned out that the four agates left by Doxet had already been “well-drinked”.

**Release of the owner.** In June 1774, Harmon was kidnapped and thrown into the dungeon of the ruined fortress near [[labelin|Labelin]]. Doxet went to rescue him along with Joaquin and Snape. He guarded the entrance to the dungeon, pulled the exhausted merchant out of the stone bag and put his shoulder with the words “Poor master...”. In the battle at the barracks, he received a sliding hit on the helmet with a mace and was stunned.

**An observer who forgot everything.** Later it turned out that on the eve of the liberation, Dokset and Vikhrenko were watching the fortress. They saw a bald, bubbling rider, "neither let him take a brewer", accompanied by two swordsmen, take a leather sum out of the barracks. In it lay the true Light Sphere. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) Where the riders went, Doxet could not remember: “where they turned, op, and disappeared from sight.” Harmon, grabbing him by the breasts, asked him how much dryness he had vomited. Doxett was not particularly embarrassed.

**Reconnaissance at the monastery.** For the sortie in the Maximian monastery Harmon invented a masquerade. Louise portrayed a poor widow with children, and Doxet was given the role of her prodigiously drinking old father. ([[luiza|Louisa]]) For persuasiveness, the owner poured him a couple of glasses of Orja and told him to "look like a complete fool." Returning, the old soldier made the most accurate observation of all: the monastery “smells with a military camp”, the brethren walk in a formation and established ways, and there are no idle servants there. Then Harmon instructed him and Snip to find retired soldiers in the Labelin, drink and bribe them and get four camels with dolphins and wheat sheaves of the House of Labelin, boots and swords. They also had to find out at the station whether the Duke had gone to the capital. Harmon acknowledged that Doxet "has a strange nose for old soldiers, especially for those with whom you can drink", and for the first time this sense of smell came in handy.

**Death.** On July 4, 1774, after the monks gave the Sphere, Doxet gave the rein to the master with a happy smile. Then the deception was revealed, and the monks of [[brat-lyudvig|Brother Ludwig]] chased the detachment. Doxet's horse languished and fell behind. “In a rush of half-hearted courage,” the old drunk turned to meet his enemies and threw up a spear. Brother Ludwig cut the stick, and the second monk struck the mace. Doxet's helmet cracked, and he fell into the dust. Harmon saw only a chance in his death: a wounded mare without a rider wrapped up in the middle of the road, and the chase mixed for a moment.

## Legacy

The surviving companions remembered Doxet. Louise, reproaching the recovered Joaquin, who was again eager to fight, said that “three good men” had died. Harmon in the spring of 1775, responding to [[niza|Niza]], how many people died because of the Sphere, remembered Doxet and [[vikhor|Whirlwind]] and corrected the "two" on the "four". Joaquin, counting the fallen friends, began with the same two names: "First Doxet and Whirlwind." Harmon recalled that Doxet found pride in any trick, and in Melison, dreaming of a wine cellar, corrected himself: he will not drink every day - "I am not Doxet."

## Character and fables

Doxet remembered the details of his campaigns vaguely and composed them on the move. For example, about the battle with swamps on the bank of the Sidar, he told everything in different ways. At first, it was as if he had axed five swordsmen in chain mail. A year later, it turned out that he had stabbed the squire with a spear and hurried the knight. And the next spring it turned out that he himself almost drowned on the crossing with a crossbow bolt in his shoulder, and the bolt had a plumage of pure silver. A frequent listener was Wihrenok. Doxet explained to the boy that the arrow can be caught “with his left hand and closer to the plumage” and that the count from the duke is easy to distinguish by the coat of arms. He recounted how, at the Battle of Lowville, an enemy stallion treated him with a hoof on his forehead so much that the helmet was imbedded and not removed from his head. In other fables, he remembered serving in the army of the Earl of Blackmore, intelligence in the Shepherd's Meadows, changing the clothes of the buffoons at the Lion's Gate in Shimmery and even sergeant rank. As far as this is true, the text does not say.

## Trivia

- According to David’s father, the monks tried not to kill: Brother Andrew “did not finish off the one whom he stunned with a blow to the helmet.” ([[otets-david|Father David]]) It was Doxet who received the hit on the helmet on the night of Harmon's liberation. In the second fight, Doxet's helmet cracked, and he died.
- Harmon, in general, deliberately hired people “in his own way, but in something spoiled”: they were cheaper and served unquestioningly. Doxet in his eyes was a “rotten” warrior, and Snip was a deserter.
