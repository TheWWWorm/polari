## Who He Is
Hortensius is a master hypnotist from [[lajem|Laem]] who put water-heating ovens to white-blooded ladies. In his youth, he went to open lectures at Laem University and read books in the library. He came up with his ball almost by chance: he noticed that a sheet of paper thrown into a fire first takes off and only then flashes. Then he put the bag on the furnace pipe, saw him rise, and estimated how much hot air it takes to lift a person. He proudly called the invention “the heavenly ship”. He is a verbose man, loves the chiming of the Shimmers, and even more likes to count expenses: [[kharmon|Harmon]] noticed that Hortensius speaks of any matter in coins.

## In "The Skyship and the Girl"
In the summer of 1774, Hortensius called the townspeople to the Laem square to ride on the ball. Three months of negotiations didn't work. The last blow to the case was made by the merchant Fidel-Corel. He went up in the air on the condition that “there will be no fear”, a beat hour with screams hung over the city, and a week later demanded from the inventor three hundred gold. According to the merchant, the flight deprived him of male power, and the bishop confirmed this with a theological interpretation. Svata Onoriko Hortensius called everyone a pimp. ([[onoriko|Onoriko-Maysor]]) When [[kharmon|Harmon]], who called himself Horam, asked about the ball, Hortensius corrected him: “This is not a ball, my lord! It is a heavenly ship.” Rico, in a quarrel with his wife, hotly contrived that they would buy this ship, fly to the capital and sell it to the emperor. Harmon took the fiction seriously.

## The “Yanmei Hunt”
### Balloon in [[melison|Melison]]
Harmon suggested that Hortense rework the balloon so that he could fly to Faunterra. The inventor decided that he was laughed at, and swore, but Nisa asked him, and Harmon promised to pay “as much as you need.” ([[niza|Niza]]) Hortensius named three hundred ephesas to untie, and Harmon agreed. The inventor moved to the Harmon estate near Melison in the Lion Mountains. Harmon considered the ship his business, since his money went to him, but Hortensius built it.

In a test flight, Hortensius and Niza climbed half a mile. At the suggestion of Niza, the master discovered that the direction of flight can be changed by choosing the desired height. He remembered the flying gas from the university books. Rico brought a master alchemist from Laem, who called the gas hydrogen and promised that they would burn with it quickly, but still suggested the way. Instead, the master sent a student who needed a topic for a credit work. Since then, Hortensius and the student worked together, and Harmon was politely escorted out of the workshop.

The ship was expensive. Hortensius asked for money every day: for potions, fabric, ropes, a basket, a special stove and bubbles for hydrogen. By the spring of 1775, Harmon had spent 423 Hilt, two Helens, and five Glorias. To the reproaches of the master replied: "The path of science lies through unknown lands." On March 8, 1775, Hortensius reported that the ball was ready, and Niza first climbed into the basket alone. The messenger of the Second of the Five, the Marquess of Miral-Senelius, wanted to buy a ship, the blueprints and the oath of Harmon and Hortense no longer to build balls for anyone else. On hearing of male impotence, the Marquis replied that his master's people visited Hortense's wife and made sure that in the autumn she and her husband conceived a child.

### Departure and return
When Harmon was threatened by Labelin men, he hid the ball with Hortense on a cloudy night. Later it was discovered that he was lying on the roof of the estate. Harmon sent the inventor with the money to his uncle and aunt in the remote town of Iserinsky County, as if to protect from the townspeople who are evil on Niza. Hortensius did not argue and took a student with him to go was not afraid. After Rico's murder, the Melisonian servants initially decided that Nisa had killed Hortense as well, but the cab driver confirmed that the inventor had simply gone to his family.

Harmon summoned Hortense back when the Second of the Five demanded for patronage to descend on the ball into Bottomless Failure. ([[bezdonnaya-propast|Bottomless Abyss]]) The master burst into tirade and listed the dangers: from monsters and non-toppers to the lair of [[temnyj-ido|Dark Ido]]. Still, he flew. Together with Harmon, Niza and the assistant to the Marquis Booth, he reached Proval for almost two weeks. Over the Fail, Booth with a dagger ordered to descend. Harmon showed him the Light Sphere, and Hortensius decided that the Object had come to life over the abyss: “This is the eye of God!” ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]) When Harmon sprinkled blood on the Sphere, the master shouted that now they were all cursed, but after making sure that the Object was intact, he fell silent.

### Hunter and the Second of Five
In May 1775, Char's archer set fire to the basket with an arrow. ([[chara|Chara]]) Hortensius released hot air and landed the ship by the river, and then, together with Harmon and Nizah, put out the fire. The fugitives were captured by the Hunter, in fact [[adrian|Adrian]], and his Shavans. The hunter Hortensius presented himself with a flowery speech "Hortensius of Laem" and responded with delight to his questions about the lifting power. The ship was by then called the Double Sphere and could lift four men twenty miles without landing. In the form of troops at the fortress, Hortensius was outraged that “a little soldier” is “two or three, well, from strength four.” He also warned the Hunter against taking the second prisoner.

Hortensius then led the ball to the Abode-of-the-Abyss with Harmon, the Hunter, and Chara on board. The second of the Five knew Hortense and Horam from the descriptions, so Neymir could not take the place of the master in the basket. ([[kuindar|Quindar]]; [[nejmir|Neimir]]) The abduction failed: the ball hit the wall, Kindar jumped on it, and the ship carried over the Fail. Hortensius prayed with Harmon, and then all the ropes moved to the plateau. Harmon and Hortensius jumped last. The loose ball flew away. “My ship! My beautiful ship...” cried Hortensius. Prisoners, half-naked and tied, were brought to the monastery, and the master cried silently all the way.

When the Hunter's men captured the monastery, the Hunter ordered Chara, Horama, and Hortensia to be given two thousand ephes. The bill was counted by the master Ganta Biray. ([[ganta-biraj|Ganta Birai]]) Hortensius assured Harmon that the money was honestly earned: the Hunter stole them, and they got them at risk. Part of the amount he was going to set aside "daughters on a dowry" and demanded from Harmon another 420 coins for the departed ship. Harmon replied that then Hortensius himself owed him for the loss, and he threatened the lawyers.

## In "Humans and Gods"
Harmon, becoming Adrian's cupbearer, envied Hortensia: he "took his money - and his aide to Laem." Members of the order in conversation with [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] explained that the celestial ship of Master Hortense rises only a mile and therefore will not reach the Star. His invention in the order was known for a long time.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
Harmon wrote to Niese that if they had a child, no one would be afraid that flying would take away male power: "A similar misfortune has already happened to Master Hortensius." He explained to the masters who had to build a new ball that only two were trained in the craft of ballers: himself and Hortensius. In fact, all the secrets remained in the head of the master who lived in the town near Laem. At the end of the summer of 1775, Harmon sent him a letter: promised money, fame and a place at the court, and in case of refusal threatened to send a company of "northern wolves". Soon the court moved to the [[pervaya-zima|First Winter]], and Harmon realized that Hortense would not be lured there. In Ruilde he hoped to meet the master on the Shimmerian ships, but he was not there. The ball was built from memory. Harmon recalled how Hortensius taught him: the most important thing is the ratio of the weight of the basket and the volume of the ball.
