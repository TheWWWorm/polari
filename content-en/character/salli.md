## Who She Is

Sally is a girl from the evening class of a parish school in the poor town of Almera. ([[almera-zemlya|Duchy of Almera]]) There are 13 children in the class. Emma is the prettiest, Red from Third Street is the most daring, Rupert, nicknamed Sasquire, is the strongest, and Paul the Needle is the sweetest, and his voice is the best. Sally has no visible talent, and in dictations she makes a lot of mistakes. She knows how to fantasize.

Sally's father once served Duke Aiden Almer and wore a "red cherry", the badge of the company's best shooter. ([[ajden-almera|Aiden Almera]]) According to Sally, the "Ganta Puss" (Ganta Puss) burned his hand with a Perst. ([[paul|Paul]]; [[persty-vilgelma|Wilhelm's Fingers]]) The Duke of Farway-Almer pays retired soldiers a pension of four gloria per month. My father drinks half of it, and Sally's been running the farm for over a year. She works part-time sewing clothes for her father's co-workers, and runs for her father to the mower in Mace's cellar.

## In "Fantasies"

### Composition

A strict teacher, Mrs. Gale, whom the children call gargoyles, asked the class an essay: "the lady was in trouble, and the knight saved her." Sally wrote three pages about Lady Alice and the wounded knight Pollack, secretly dedicated to Paul. When she stayed in class, Emma, Rupert, and Red with the Third tried to read her essay. While running away, Sally mistakenly grabbed someone else's sheet. There was only one line on it: “The lady was rotting in prison. The knight came to save her." Sally decided that it was Paul who wrote it, and began to write for him. She did not dare to read her own essay aloud, and Mrs. Gale threw it into the urn.

Soon, a new paragraph appeared under her lines, written in the same handwriting as the first line. The knight nursed the lady, "loved his kindness" and swore, "I'll never leave you." Sally accepted this as Paul's confession and began to answer. Correspondence went through her desk drawer. The co-author added that the lady “could not forget the past.” Then Sally herself came up with the idea that the lady lacked two fingers cut off by the executioners.

### Sheep or not.

Mrs Gail accused Sally of being timid, calling her "victim" and "sheep." One day, Sally intercepted Bartleby's father's letter to the teacher and purposely let the rain blur the ink. In Mace's cellar, the gargoyle caught her with a bottle of cheap scab for her father and said it was Sally who was sleeping with her father, since the money was in her hands. Hearing about the hand burned by the Perst, the teacher paused and lowered her gaze. At home, Sally refused her father for the first time: one bottle a week, will not be anymore. Her father grabbed her by the breasts, but then apologized. Sally felt proud: “I am not a sheep!”

Before the arrival of the auditors, Paul suggested that the gargoyle should be fired on purpose. Sally refused. Emma and Rupert caught them eavesdropping and forced Paul to sing Sally Serenade on his lap. Sally did not ask for mercy and sent Emma "into the darkness", and Paul escaped. Sally herself admitted to Mrs. Gale that she was eavesdropping. The teacher dismissed the offenders and said that they were not fired from hard labor.

### Last line

In an open lesson, the auditor asked Sally about math and mannerisms. Instead of apologizing, she asked for permission to read a note from the desk and read the last paragraph. The knight was there while his friends tortured the lady, went to war, returned when he lost, and showed his palm: he also did not have two fingers. Sally realized that Paul was not writing. The auditors were satisfied with the school. When Paul called her to meet “where usual,” Sally replied, “No.”

Left alone with Mrs. Gale, Sally asked for advice on how to complete the essay. The teacher replied, "It's your turn, not mine." Sally wrote, "Lady killed a knight." Mrs. Gale called the ending successful and left the composition to her as a souvenir.
