## Who He Is

Gobart-Synthal is a Soltown merchant shipbuilder, one of four possible buyers of the Light Sphere, who was named Harmona by Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]. ([[svetlaya-sfera|Radiant Sphere]]; [[kharmon|Harmon Paula Roger]]) In the spring of 1774, he owned a merchant fleet of fifty ships, and thousands, if not tens of thousands of ephesas passed through his hands. He became rich on trade with the [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] Kingdom. Its galleons circled the eastern coast of Polaris and carried marble, silver and steel to the south, and returned with silk, tea, spices and coffee. He was a senior merchant of the Second Marine Guild, which enjoyed the bays of the free cities of [[soltaun|Soltown]] and Fishtown.

Hobart-Synthal was proud of his friendship with the southerners and put it on display. He wore turbans, scarves and silk sleeveless, drank coffee and spicy tea. Even the name he took a double, in the Shimmer manner, "dozen to this from birth dull Gobart romantic-mysterious vestige." Later, Harmon clarified for [[adrian|Adrian]]: Hobart-Synthal only pretended to be a Shimmerian, but in fact there was a “normal traveler”.

## Appearance and home

Harmon saw a short, snug man with a sleek gray beard, wearing a red-and-gold silk robe and a snow turban, with a quick, tenacious look. In the autumn of the same year, Erwin Origin appeared to be a “funny shorty” in huge scarlet balls, sharp-nosed shoes with no backs, a yellow shirt and a tiny blue vest. On his chest hung a medallion of red gold, the sign of the guild's senior merchant. On his shoulder sat a crested bird with a monstrously long tail, which at the right moments hollered sadly.

He lived in a floating house. One of his galleons he anchored at the waterfront of Salttown Bay, removed his masts and superstructures and turned the ship into a palace, set so that the central street of the city rested directly on board. In the reception hall, the wall facing the sea consisted almost entirely of windows. The floor was covered with soft carpets, a fountain murmured, and in pink trees walked mottled birds with crests and long tails. The guests were met by a dwarf servant in scarlet glaze, and the owner himself took them on the terrace, sitting on a pile of pillows. Later, talking about him, recalled his love for strange birds and other wonders.

## Trading for the Light Sphere

In late May or early June 1774, Harmon, dressed as a successful travel merchant and wearing a bracelet of the Merchant Guild of the South Road, wrote a letter to Hobart-Synthal, "full of flattery and meaningful hints," and received a reception. After an unhurried conversation over the Shimmerian coffee, which Harmon hated, he told the parable of a traveler with a priceless product that offers the merchant to name the price himself. Hobart-Synthal began with fifty ephesas and raised the stake: a hundred, three hundred, a thousand. When Harmon hinted at the number “of five figures”, the shipkeeper jumped up and told him to get out “in the dark”, but suddenly he himself guessed what goods could cost such money:

> “Sixteen thousand gold coins. A thousand in honor of every Holy Mother."

Harmon asked for forty-five thousand. Gobart-Synthal called the twenty-five thousand "the ultimate price": this is all he could collect without selling ships. When the guest got up to leave, the merchant promised to borrow another seven thousand by the day after tomorrow and give thirty-two if the goods were shown immediately. Harmon, whose Sphere lay under his shirt, replied that he had no goods with him and that he should pray and ask the gods. Gobart-Synthal said, “Tell the gods I’ll add a thousand more ephes.” Harmon left and already knew he wasn't coming back.

Harmon explained the failure to his people by the buyer’s stinginess, but he himself understood that it was not a matter of price. The shipowner, he reasoned, was too angry during the trade to plot a hoax, and appeared to be really willing to part with the money. Harmon just couldn't give up the shrine. Later, dreaming of future wealth, he thought to start, “following the example of Hobart-Synthal”, his own merchant fleet. And at the sight of torture crosses in Baron Darryl's castle, I thought: shouldn't I have given up the Subject to a Soltowner? The Duke [[moris-labelin|Maurice Labelin]] learned that the Sphere was first offered to the "shipman from Soltown", considered it an insult and cut the price by five thousand (see Sale of the Bright Sphere). ## The Northerners

In early October 1774, during the [[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern War]], [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] took Saltown. Hobart-Synthal, along with the non-citizens Aksinion and Yus-Fairy, did not flee the city and received the northern lords in his floating palace, by then three-story. He sat the guests at a table the size of a cart, called the dancers and scattered in the eastern praises of "the glory that shines like a ruby." Erwin, in order not to lose his head, broke a jug and asked for red pepper oatmeal. Merchants complained about the Duke of Labelin: he took from them a third of the profits, gave lucrative contracts to his favorite Gracends and forbade the construction of ships anywhere except his shipyards. Aksinion said that Labelin bought Sacred Objects and last summer paid an “unknown merchant” for the Bright Sphere forty thousand. This rumor later became for Erwin one of the threads in solving the story with the Sphere.

Erwin caught the merchants at a simple reception: he offered a quarter instead of a third, and when they were confused, gave them a fifth. He promised not to give preference to the Graysends and allowed the ships to be ordered at any shipyards, not only at the First Soltown Shipyards he captured. In return, he demanded fifty thousand Hilt immediately, "a little more than one Sacred Object." He gave the merchants thirty-four ships taken from the escaped merchants, and ordered a dozen ships to be sunk at the entrance to the harbor to narrow the fairway. To the reproaches of the generals, why he did not take the fleet from the three fat men who owned together two hundred ships, Erwin replied that the northerners are not merchants: Hobart-Synthal and his friends will trade and pay taxes.

## "Sea Arrow"

Gobart-Synthal bought a black schooner, Sea Arrow, from the wayward Lord Captain Courtney Benefit, who picked her up on the reefs, hired a crew of Northwooders for her and sent her on a voyage. Black ship with gloomy bearded "bears" on board was mistaken for a pirate: it six times turned to flee oncoming ships, twice he was chased by warships of the Crown, and one merchant surrendered to him without a fight. The owners of the frightened ships demanded to get rid of the “cursed vessel”. The merchant offered to simply repaint it, but he did not find a profitable way to get rid of the schooner. When Erwin ordered a dozen ships to be sunk on the fairway, Hobart-Synthal gave the Sea Arrow to his heart. The duke of the schooner liked it so much that he left it afloat. She survived the war and stayed at the House Origin.
