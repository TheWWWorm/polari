## Who He Is

Jeremy Farway is the son of Finley Farway and the grandson of Duke [[henri-farvej|Henry Farway]], ruler of [[nadezhda|Hope]]. His mother was a kind of Agatha the Radiant, so he and his sister [[laura-farvej|Laura]] are the only "Agatov children" among the twelve grandchildren of the Duke. Jeremy is two years older than Laura. He looks like his father and he knows everything. He doesn’t try to be good. He calls his sister a “chicken.”

From his father, Jeremy adopted both views and the word “freethinker” itself: he does not believe in Pramatery. He said, “I pray only to the gods, and not to show off.” The father was proud of his son's courage. The grandfather held traditional views, but respected the flexibility of the mind and also praised the grandson: if free views spread, the Church and the Crown will lose some of the influence, and the Great Houses will strengthen. Therefore, in the [[serdtse-sveta|Heart of Light]], Jeremy was considered a smart and brave future politician, and Laura was considered a cute fool.

## In "Only One Star"

In the autumn of 1774, after the rebellion of Origin, [[adrian|Adrian]] invaded the Heart of Light with an army. ([[myatezh-oridzhina|Northern Uprising]]) He wanted to look at the “young shoots of the great branch” — Laura and Jeremy. The emperor took the boy by the hand and asked what he was dreaming about. Jeremy replied, “Commander, Your Majesty. Strategist." Adrian noticed that it was a dangerous role. He then demanded six battalions from Henry and took both of his grandchildren to the capital.

## The Shadow of the Great Tree

In the fall of 1775, the Ganta Court made an alliance with Henry Farway and sealed his marriage: "the daughter of the Ganta for Farway's grandson." ([[ganta-kort|Ganta Kort]]) The name of the grandson is not mentioned in the novel. That it was Jeremy is said in Fantasies.

## In "Fantasies"

In the short story "The Miserable Little Things" (1776), Henry Farway complains to Lady [[karen|Karen]] that his offspring are unhappy in love. Jeremy, Laura's brother, married the daughter of a steppe gauntlet: the only way to make peace. The wedding was played in the Steppe, according to local customs. Now Jeremy lives with his wife in Rei-Roy and sends letters to his grandfather, "complete homesickness." Henry puts him on a par with Laura and his daughter Evelyn: they all seemed to sacrifice their happiness for the sake of family and hope.
