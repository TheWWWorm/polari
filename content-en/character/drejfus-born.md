## Biography
Under [[adrian|Adrian]] Dreyfus, Born managed the tax service and passed through his hands more than a third of all proceeds to the treasury of the Empire. He got rid of his deputy, the honest Frank Morlin-Mei, and the transparent tax accounting system created by that. Anticipating the change of power, Bourne delayed tax collections and did not let them into the treasury. Morlin-May claimed that it was about December fees and that he was fired for protesting. Minerva later reasoned that the receipts were blocked much earlier, probably on the day when the fighting began in the capital. When, in December 1774, officials fled the northern capital, Bourne did not think to hide.

### Deal with the Lord Chancellor
In February 1775, shortly after his coronation, [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin Origin]] laid out three "difficult questions" for [[minerva|Minerva]]. ([[koronatsiya-minervy|Minerva's Coronation]]) The second touched Bourne, "a round cheeky man in an expensive gall." Erwin explained to her how the tax pyramid is arranged: each level, from ordinary collectors to the capital department, pocketes part of the tax. His first wish was to stab Bourne on the spot, but now money is needed more than justice. Bourne offered him a guaranteed million ephes per year without question about how much the collectors were stealing, and immediately handed over one and a half hundred thousand. Sister, [[iona-oridzhin|Iona]], Erwin at the same time explained that under Adrian, the money of the Empire was run by “a kindred of low-breed dogs” — the Minister of Taxes and Fees, the Treasurer, the Collectors — and looked after them by the financial adviser to the lord Count [[vittor-shejland|Vittor Shayland]]. Later, Erwin admitted that Bourne "was useful for a while", but he was not going to tolerate him forever.

In February 1775, the drunken Minister of Railways Liam Shelier in the intermission of the performance pulled Bourne by the elbow to the empress. ([[liam-shele|Liam Shellier]]) Schelier said Bourne boasted: “The Lord Chancellor will not hang his tax secretary!” According to Schelier, Bourne bought his post from the Chancellor for one hundred and twenty thousand gold. Bourne burrowed the "Dumb" and disappeared into the crowd. So Minerva found out she had a tax secretary appointed without her knowledge.

### Snowdrops
Bourne's collectors pressed out of the Crown Lands to pitch forward. ([[zemli-korony|Crownlands]]) When in Lawville the insurgent [[podsnezhniki|Snowdrops]] drove out a squad of pickers, the captured picker confessed to [[dzhoakin|Joaquin]]: they did not have paper from the empress, they were sent by the Minister of Taxes by his oral order. It was ordered to take a third of the income, but the collectors rowed everything clean. In February 1775, Bourne appeared to Erwin in the small throne room of the palace along with the treasurer [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]] and demanded that the rebellion be suppressed by the troops. Erwin, demonstratively drawing something in a black notebook, refused: he would not kill eight thousand honest peasants, and the treaty was about a million a year “and no problems.” Bourne got under the eyes of the Agathans and asked for permission to go. [[alanis-almera|Alanis Almera]] imposed a “fine” on Born: tax collection in February, March and April was to grow by ten percent. Alanis called the minister a “dog girl”, which is necessary to deal with men.

In March 1775, Born assembled units of tax guards, hired two brigades of free shooters, and deployed an army four thousand strong against the rebels on the approaches to [[izluchina|Riverbend]]. The farmers of [[salem|Salem]] defeated him. The prisoners confessed to the Snowdrops that they were sent by the Minister of Taxes, not the Empress. Bourne himself did not appear at the advice of the Lord Chancellor and sat in the tax office in [[marengo|Marengo]]. Erwin was furious: he asked the Minister of Taxes why he was spending his time on it again and why Bourne wasn’t even on the council?

### Conversation in the "Ladies' Party"
In March 1775, Minerva summoned Bourne to the Ladies' Party Hall, where Adrian had previously exposed the Northwoods' substitution, and hung a portrait of Julian the Great there. ([[yuliana-velikaya|Juliana the Great]]) She asked what a fair Juliana would say about his affairs. Bourne said he was not a painter. Minerva caught him in the delay of taxes and urged to think back: a million should go to the Chancellor, but every ephemeral over it will be spent on the hungry and wounded. The minister wished her “great female happiness and childbearing power” and condescendingly noticed that he himself had three sons, the youngest of them her age, so that he would not tolerate moralizing. Then he turned away, playing with a cane. Minerva refrained from the temptation to order the guards to cut off his head: Origin would have appointed another such scum in Bourne's place.

### On the schooner "Straight"
In March 1775, on the day of the opening of navigation on Canaan, the Lady invited Born on board the schooner "Striking". ([[khanaj|Khanai]]) He grumbled that urgent matters were waiting for him in Marengo. On the cargo deck were eight barrels of “unusual wine”. The guard opened the first one, and it was a bound blonde woman with a gag, the wife of Minister Margot. Bourne himself tore the lids off five more barrels and found three sons, a brother and a sister there. The two remaining barrels were indeed with wine. The minister offered a ransom, raising the price from fifty to one hundred and fifty thousand, but Minerva replied that it was not about money. She demanded to sign a confession of fraud and embezzlement, write in the exact amount, later repeat the confession in court and declare Morlin-Mei and Finance Minister [[albert-vial|Albert Vial]] innocent. When Bourne referred to the Lord Chancellor, General [[aleksis-smajl|Alexis]] hit him in the stomach:
> "Forget the Lord Chancellor, nothing. The Empress is before you!”

The schooner had to leave with the hostages to the port, the name of which only Minerva knew. Bourne was told to return every coin stolen from the beginning of the rebellion, not to Robert Origin's coffers, but to the Crown's special account at Conto's bank. ([[konto|Konto]]) The verdict on such charges meant ten years of hard labor in the mines. The hostages were ordered to untie and feed. Minerva herself then experienced a belated fear of what she had done. On the day of the massacre of Bourne, she was tormented by hesitation and remorse; later, thinking of Moger Buckley, she no longer experienced a drop of doubt.

### Aftermath
Bourne's confession befell Vial and Morlin-Mei. Morlin-May took the post of Minister of Taxes and Duties and announced to the Snowdrops on the Holy Field that the former Minister confessed to the monstrous thefts and was put on trial. ([[svyatoe-pole|Standoff at the Holy Field]]) At the end of March, after returning from St. Paul, Minerva received Robert Origin: he asked for clarifications about the interaction with the new Minister of Taxes. She promised that the banks would give him just as much as he received from Bourne. The huge sums seized from the former minister, together with the profits from the fair and the printing of appropriations by the end of May 1775, filled up the personal treasury of the mistress. Erwin later recalled to Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] that the lady had “hid all the relatives of the previous tax minister in the barrels.” Bourne did not appear again.

### Role in Erwin's designs
Erwin admitted to Minerva that he had long wanted to get rid of the "scumbag" without breaking his word, and her exposure had to be very useful. Bourne served him as a convenient weapon. At the end of March 1775, revealing to Iona his plan, Erwin showed her a notebook with a picture: Dreyfus Born riding on a mountain of golden ephes. In portraying a careless mot in front of the [[vittor-shejland|Puppeteer]], Erwin spent state money, not his own, and in advance kept Bourne on the role of a scapegoat. Later, at a meeting of associates, Minerva recalled that the idea of involving the army in police work, including to take Born’s relatives hostage, was suggested to her by Silver Fox. ([[aleksis-smajl|Alexis Smile]]) In the Janmei Hunt, revealing to Erwin that Alexis was serving Lady in Darkness, Minerva said that the general had told her how to handle both Steppe Fire and Dreyfus Bourne. ([[yanmejskaya-okhota|The Yanmei Hunt]]; [[ledi-vo-tme|Lady in the Dark]])

## Appearance
Fat and round, with pink bristly cheeks, a bald top and a rounded belly. The image of good-nature was broken by a mouth compressed into a line of curves and small close-set eyes. He was short-sighted. He wore a checkered vest and a gilded coat, like a successful merchant, a chain from a pocket watch, a cane with an elephant head and four bracelets: according to southern fashion, an engagement and another one for each child.
