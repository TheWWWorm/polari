Conto is a banker from [[faunterra|Faunterra]], the owner of the First Credit Bank. In the books, he is called only “Mr. Konto” or “Banker Konto”, the personal name and kind of text does not name. Conto became one of [[minerva|Minerva]]'s closest assistants in money matters: with his appropriations, her financial policy began, and her personal money went through his bank.

## "A Doll on the Throne"
### Paper money
In early 1775, the bankers came to the young empress three: Conto, Mr. Ferguson and Mrs. Day. Prior to that, according to Amber's secretary's note, they had already offered credit to the Lord Chancellor, but he refused. ([[dorian-ember|Dorian Ember]]) The three of them called themselves the masters of the banks First Credit and Fergusson and Day, the "best in the Lands of the Crown", and offered the Crown two hundred thousand ephes at twenty-five percent per annum. Dei and Conto gave Mira a hundred ephes in color, and Conto explained that it was not bills of exchange, but allotments, “tender securities.” In any branch of the bank they can be exchanged for silver or gold. When asked what it was, money from paper, he replied: “From very beautiful paper, Your Majesty.” According to him, such papers appeared five years ago, when printing equipment allowed it. Ferguson called the competitor a hypocrite and admitted that the philistines do not yet trust the allocations.

### Fair and secret account
In March, at a tasting at the Konto Wine Guild, a "low funny man with a round bald head," instead of a report on his accomplishments, joked: "You know, I just made a lot of money. It's terribly boring - just numbers on paper..." When Mira moved the April fair to Palace Island and told the bankers to send the bill, Ferguson and Day nearly opened their mouths, and Conto "glittered a trick" in his eyes, and he clarified whether Her Majesty decided to take a loan from them.

After the reception of [[lejla-talmir|Leila Talmir]] at the request of Mira, she "armored" a fan, and Conto, returning him, was in the Empress's carriage. That's where he figured out her plan. The Crown will take from the merchants on the agate with each gloria of revenue and will not lose on the cheap sale of appropriations. In three weeks, about half a million paper ephemera will go into circulation, of which about two hundred thousand will remain with people, and the treasury will become as much richer. Mira asked to open an account that no one will know about, “except you and me.” Conto immediately realized that it was an alternative execution, not accountable to [[robert-oridzhin|Robert Origin]], and agreed. In farewell, he advised to pay the fair from his own funds, and not from other people’s loans, so as not to get “dependent on anyone.”

The money, hidden by Minister [[drejfus-born|Dreyfus Born]], Mira was ordered to be returned "to the special account of the Crown in the Conto Bank." One hundred and forty thousand ephesas from the church treasury, she asked Cordelia’s mother to place in the banks of Ferguson, Day and Conto. ([[mat-kordeliya|Mother Cordelia]]) At the fair, the banker congratulated the lady: “paper money at a discount is a complete triumph!” Ferguson and Day were envious: the allocations were printed "with the coat of arms of the Crown and the venzel of the Conto Bank." Then Mira demanded that their branches open in every town of the Crown Lands, where there are no Konto branches. [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]] later admitted to Mira that he knew about her personal account with Conto: he arranged such an alternative treasury for himself back in January.

Through the banks of Conto, Ferguson and Day went and tax reform: the filing was now paid in the bank branches, not collectors. Mira promised Robert Origin that the banks would transfer to the treasury every month as much as he received from Bourne, and the surplus would remain to her. At a closed meeting of the "most faithful" assistants in the execution room, Mira thanked [[albert-vial|Vial]], Morlin-Mei, and Conto for the spring fair, appropriations, and tax reform.

## In The Voice of Winter
Enraged that Minerva had thwarted his hunt for the Puppeteer, Erwin thought, "I'll put pressure on Conto's provost and close all her accounts." Later, going over Minerva's tail, he named Leila Talmir, Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]], "Banker Conto" and Minister of Paths.

## “The ones we forgive.”
When Leila Talmir asked Minerva for five thousand ephes, she readily agreed: the venture with paper money brought an unexpected profit, and the next day the “Conto Bank” had to pay the lady this amount.

## In "Humans and Gods"
Becoming a temporary ruler, Mira secretly summoned Conto one of the first. He confirmed that her personal treasury was “full of money”: to the income from the fair and allocations were added the amounts withdrawn from Bourne. Bank Conto, he said, twenty years, and now he was waiting for new investors: after the speech of the Lord Chancellor, Shayland’s clients will take the money. Of the sixteen best hired squads of Polaris, the banker boasted, nine keep money from him. He named the Burning Sun Regiment, the St. Strauss Brigade, Oliver Gold's Ringing Coin Brigade, and the Sea Rat Battalion, though they all had already served under contracts. He also remembered the Black Flag pirates hired by [[vittor-shejland|Shayland]]: money came to their account from other sources. Mira instructed him to hire detachments to "maintain order" on Palace Island. To pay them was under the guise of spending the ladies on dresses, and to talk about the case - only as patterns and tailoring. When Conto left, Mira was told to trace where he was going and who he was talking to. Later, he brought the estimates of two brigades, but the lady demanded a discount of “at least a third.”

## The Shadow of the Great Tree
In July 1775, when the horde was going to Faunterra, the "First Credit" began to export reserves to [[labelin|Labelin]]. Conto reported to Minerva that the bank's gold was going to the South Road and from there to Laam. ([[lajem|Laem]]) He himself was going to sail to [[shimmeri|Shimmery]] a week later and was sure that the court would move there: Laam is the only one of the five great capitals that the war "will never get." Mira felt pushed again, and did the opposite. Half of the contribution she ordered to give her bills, and the rest to buy houses, not in Laem, but in the cheaper Faunterra. In August, the banker reported that large sums were spent on defense, and for the rest of the World, she became the mistress of two small streets.

In the autumn Minerva left for the First Winter, and the Crown Lands were still paid in the offices of Conto and Ferguson Day, and the money flowed north. [[adrian|Adrian]] could not put pressure on the bankers: Conto, Ferguson and Day "had long left the capital." In November, Ferguson and Conto told the lady that they were going to the First Winter themselves, "for the development of the local banking network."

## In "Fantasies"
In the fantasy The Device (Spring 1776), Eugene's doorman returned a stolen notebook to Professor [[nikolas-olli|Nicholas Ollie]]. In the note, he said that the “mistress” asks the professor “to go to the Conto Bank and get a greeting of monetary value from her.” By all indications, Eugén is Inji Price, and his mistress is [[minerva|Minerva]], but her name is not named in the text. ([[inzhi-prajs|Inzhi Price]])

## Personality and Appearance
Short, round-faced and bald, with a lush moustache. Conto courteous to the chivalrous: Ferguson and Day interrupted his verbiage. He likes to joke and often laughs at his mustache. He has a sharp sense: he guesses Minerva’s plans before she explains them, and willingly plays along with them. But when the empress asked to secretly hire an army, the banker “lost his handkerchief” and recalled that her accounts are monitored by protection agents and Origin.
