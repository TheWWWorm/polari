**Albert Cantor** is the President of the Supreme Court of the Empire of Polari. In the “Yanmei Hunt” he is presented as a “thunder judge”, who became famous after the Shutov conspiracy. ([[shutovskoj-zagovor|Jesters' Conspiracy]]) In May 1775, he presided over a panel of eight judges at Manson's trial. ([[sud-nad-mensonom|Trial of Manson]])

## The “Yanmei Hunt”

**Before the trial.** The author says that Kantor became famous after the Shutov conspiracy. [[menson|Manson]] then "avoided Cantor's punishing hand": Emperor Thelurian pardoned his brother to make him a court laughingstock. At the second sitting, [[mark-voron|Mark]] asked the judge if he had sentenced Manson to be stripped of his nobility in the 1755 Manson Conspiracy case. Cantor replied, "No, sir. You can not deprive a person of the title of nobility, because it is given with blood. Lord Manson was discharged from the admirals and sentenced to death. The absentee decision of December 10 last year, which condemned [[minerva|Minerva]] and [[ervin-oridzhin|Erwin]], [[frantsisk-ilian|Francis-Ilian]] called judicial arbitrariness. Kantor reminded him that the decision was overturned on April 12 after a review of the case and has no force.

**First meeting, 17 May 1775.** When Manson turned down a lawyer, Cantor insisted that the court appoint him an adviser: "no deviation from the procedure is unacceptable." Francis-Ilianus volunteered as an adviser. Cantor carefully asked if the king knew the laws, but found no reason to deny: "Your Majesty, the court accepts you as an adviser to the accused." When the prophet offered to recognize Manson as insane, the judge replied that he was smart enough to survive the train crash, commit murder and escape, but “the principles of Pramatery Yumin are sacred,” and ordered a medical evaluation. Francis-Ilian then demanded that Manson be immediately found guilty so that he would leave the court through mercy. Kantor recalled that the adviser should help the defendant. Manson rushed at the judge, but the bailiffs caught him a step to the table when the fist "was already flying to Cantor's nose."

Cantor then called witnesses of "moral appearance" - Vimas's lackey and Captain [[shatterkhend|Shatterhand]] - and then questioned the Almeran fishermen. Francis-Ilian’s request to postpone his examination of witnesses to the next meeting he rejected: “The court sees no reason for this.” Erwin saw in this calculation: the judges will go to sleep in the belief that the joke is guilty. He said Cantor "played this meeting as if on notes." When the prophet at the identification by portraits showed that the fishermen did not recognize Adrian, the judge had to squeeze: “Bring in the record, not a single witness did not identify the ruler of Adrian.” Then Francis-Ilianus, recalling the decision of December 10, ensured that Minerva had to decide whether to dissolve the panel. Cantor got up, and it became clear how old he was and subdued by diseases. Kantor referred to the Julian Law (section on the judiciary, chapter on the supreme court): neither the Chamber nor the sovereign has the right to dissolve the supreme court during the hearing. ([[yulianov-zakon|Juliana's Law]]; [[palata|Chamber of Representatives]]) If Minerva is dissatisfied with his work at the end of the trial, he will immediately resign. Minerva admitted that "the evil old man Kantor was right", and rejected the petition.

**Second meeting, May 21.** Cantor warned Francis-Ilian for trying to manipulate the empress and Mark for trying to deliver a verdict instead of a trial. He allowed Mark to ask him two questions. He wanted to prove that Manson was a nobleman in a military campaign. When Francis-Ilian and Manson were about to leave the hall, Cantor shouted: “The charge is still pending! The defendant must remain in place.” Later, when the adviser did not name the witnesses, the judge proceeded to deliver a verdict. Minerva chose both the court and the defense for the rush and gave the defendant a day on the witness list. Cantor assured her that he valued justice above all else, and explained his haste by taking care of the lord and the lords. Erwin called Cantor "the only biased hero of this drama."

**Third meeting, May 25.** Cantor "suffocated with surprise" when Mark admitted that Manson had struck Adrian with a blow of mercy, and asked, "The accuser justifies the defendant?" Listening to the guardsman [[sanders|Sanders]], he forgot to interrupt the monologue and came to his senses only at the end.

**Fourth meeting, 29 May.** When Manson confessed that Adrian was alive, Cantor interrupted him with a bell ringing: “Respondent, have you been making enough noise? Are you able to speak humanly?” Then he asked how the joke could be sure. When the House removed Adrian from the throne, Erwin asked Cantor to explain the order of the transfer of power. The judge hoarsely outlined two interpretations: Manson, Adrian's heir, or Minerva's heirs should receive the crown. The only precedent he called Lord Keilor, who in the XVI century was deposed for violations of the feudal code. The Lords of the House elected a new ruler who was not a direct heir.

## Personality and Appearance

Cantor is old and sick, says the hoarse, at meetings he rings a bell. He strictly follows the procedure and the law. Minerva called him an “evil old man.” In seeking the unanimity of the judges, Kantor “sacrifice speed”, but “killed doubts”.
